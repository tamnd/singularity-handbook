## This edition

The second edition of Volume 0 and the first edition of Volume 9 went with the milestone S1 of the project. At S1 the project had these results in Lean, in addition to the statements and the fidelity checks of S0.

1. Resolution in dimension 0, in each characteristic (Theorem 2.3.1).
2. Resolution of integral varieties in characteristic 0, from the Lean proof of Hironaka's Main Theorem I in resolutionorg/hironaka (Theorem 2.3.2).
3. Resolution in characteristic 0 for all varieties, from the named hypothesis that reduces the problem to integral varieties (now Theorem 2.3.3, and Theorem 2.3.4). At S1 the Lean proof of the hypothesis was planned, so the map gave this result the status "proved-mod". Release 0.2.5 of the Lean package proves the reduction (Theorem 2.3.3), and the status is now "proved".
4. The bridge to google-deepmind/formal-conjectures (Proposition 2.5.4), and Hironaka's theorem in the form of formal-conjectures, with no hypothesis (Corollary 2.5.5).

Volume 9, Chapter 1 gives the complete proofs of these results, with examples, and it explains how each Lean proof follows the argument.

The open case has this status.

Edition 0.4.28 adds Theorems 3.9.10 and 3.9.11 to Volume 7, Section 3.9. If $\rho$ stays, the old point is in the first case and no component of the divisor leaves, then the new point is in the first case, and $\tau_\rho$ does not go down. The theorems have no hypothesis on $p$, and both have Lean proofs for polynomials. So at a step where $\rho$ stays, $\tau_\rho$ can go down only from the second case to the first case, or from the first case with $p \mid |r| + d$ to the first case with a leaving component. Volume 9, Section 3.10 gives the result as the tenth research report of the milestone S7, with these two kinds of steps and what they mean for a secondary invariant. The map is at the release 0.4.33.

Edition 0.4.27 adds Example 3.9.9 to Volume 7, Section 3.9. It is an example for $p = 3$ in three variables. Here $\rho$ stays, the old point is in the first case, $p$ divides $|r| + d$, and the new point is in the second case. So the hypothesis of Proposition 3.9.7 on $|r| + d$ is necessary. The example has a Lean proof over $\mathbb{F}_3$. Volume 9, Section 3.9 gives the result as the ninth research report of the milestone S7, with the data of a test that records the divisor at each step. At each step of the test from the first case with $p \mid |r| + d$ to the second case, a component of the divisor leaves. The map is at the release 0.4.31.

Edition 0.4.26 adds Proposition 3.9.7 and Corollary 3.9.8 to Volume 7, Section 3.9. If $\rho$ stays, the old point is in the first case and $p$ does not divide $|r| + d$, then the new point is in the first case, and $\tau_\rho$ does not go down. So Theorem 3.9.1 holds with no hypothesis on the new point. The section also lists the combinations of the cases at a point where $\rho$ stays, and $\tau_\rho$ can go down in two of them only. Both results have Lean proofs for polynomials. Volume 9, Section 3.8 gives the result as the eighth research report of the milestone S7, with a table of the combinations and the data of a test of sequences of blowups. The map is at the release 0.4.29.

Edition 0.4.25 adds Examples 3.9.5 and 3.9.6 to Volume 7, Section 3.9. Both examples are for $p = 2$, in five and in four variables. In each example $\rho$ stays, both points are in the first case, $p$ divides $|r| + d$, and $\tau_\rho$ goes down. So the hypothesis on $|r| + d$ in Theorem 3.9.1 is necessary, and the pair $(\rho, -\tau_\rho)$ is not a resolution invariant in the first case. Example 3.9.5 has a Lean proof over the field with two elements. Volume 9, Section 3.7 gives the result as the seventh research report of the milestone S7, with what it means for route R1. The map is at the release 0.4.27.

Edition 0.4.24 adds Volume 7, Section 3.9, on the codimension $\tau_\rho$ from the first case to the first case.
