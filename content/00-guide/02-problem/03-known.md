The case of dimension 0 is elementary, and it is the first theorem that the project proves in Lean. We give the proof, because it shows how the definitions of the last two sections work together. Volume 9, Chapter 1 gives the results of this section again, with complete proofs, examples, and an explanation of each Lean proof.

**Theorem 2.3.1 (dimension 0).** Let $p \ge 0$. Then $\mathrm{Res}(p, 0)$ holds. More exactly, a reduced scheme $X$ of dimension at most 0 is regular, so the identity of $X$ is a resolution of $X$.

Node: SG0000001W · Lean: `Singularity.resolutionInDim_dim_zero`

*Proof.* Let $x \in X$. The dimension of the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is the length of the longest chain of points $x = x_0 \rightsquigarrow \dots$ that generalize $x$, that is, the coheight of $x$ in the specialization order. A chain of points of $X$ gives a chain of irreducible closed subsets of the same length, so this length is at most $\dim X \le 0$. Thus $\dim \mathcal{O}_{X,x} = 0$.

The ring $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is reduced because $X$ is reduced. In a local ring of dimension 0 the maximal ideal is the only prime ideal, so it is equal to the nilradical. The nilradical of a reduced ring is $0$. Thus the maximal ideal is $0$, and $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is a field.

A field $K$ is a regular local ring: its maximal ideal is $0$, so $\dim_K \mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2 = 0 = \dim K$. Thus each local ring of $X$ is regular, and $X$ is regular. By Proposition 2.5.1, the identity of a regular scheme is a resolution. $\square$

The proof does not use that $X$ is of finite type over a field, and it does not use the characteristic. The Lean proof follows the same three steps, and it uses the Mathlib lemmas `ringKrullDim_stalk_eq_coheight` and `Ring.KrullDimLE.isField_of_isReduced`.

The case $p = 0$ is Hironaka's theorem. The project does not prove it again. The Lean proof of Hironaka's theorem in resolutionorg/hironaka, the base, has Main Theorem I in this form: let $X$ be an integral variety over a field $k$ of characteristic 0. Then there is an ideal sheaf $D$ on $X$ whose support is the singular locus of $X$, such that the blowup $\pi : \mathrm{Bl}_D X \to X$ is regular. The next theorem shows that this blowup is a resolution in the sense of Section 2.1.

**Theorem 2.3.2 (integral varieties in characteristic 0).** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic 0 and let $X$ be an integral variety over $k$. Then $X$ has a resolution.

Node: SG0000001Z · Lean: `Singularity.Base.integralResolutionInDim_zero`

*Proof.* Let $D$ and $\pi : Y = \mathrm{Bl}_D X \to X$ be as in Main Theorem I. We examine the four conditions of a resolution.

1. $Y$ is regular, by Main Theorem I.
2. $X$ is of finite type over a field, so $X$ is locally Noetherian. The blowup of a locally Noetherian scheme along an ideal sheaf is proper. The base proves this.
3. Let $U$ be the complement of the support of $D$. Then $U = X \setminus \operatorname{Sing}(X) = \operatorname{Reg}(X)$. The blowup is an isomorphism over the complement of its centre, so $\pi^{-1}(U) \to U$ is an isomorphism. The base proves this.
4. The inverse image $E = \pi^{-1}D \cdot \mathcal{O}_Y$ is an invertible ideal sheaf. The base proves this. The support of $E$ is $\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{supp} D)$, so $\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{Reg}(X)) = Y \setminus \operatorname{supp} E$. It remains to show that the complement of the support of an invertible ideal sheaf is dense.

Let $V \subseteq Y$ be a nonempty open set, and let $y \in V$. There is an affine open set $\operatorname{Spec} A \ni y$ on which $E$ is generated by one nonzerodivisor $g \in A$. Let $W$ be the inverse image of $V$ in $\operatorname{Spec} A$. Then $W$ is open and nonempty. Let $\mathfrak{q} \in W$, and let $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$ be a minimal prime of $A$. Then $\mathfrak{q}$ is a specialization of $\mathfrak{p}$, and an open set contains each generalization of its points, so $\mathfrak{p} \in W$. A minimal prime contains no nonzerodivisor, so $g \notin \mathfrak{p}$, and $\mathfrak{p}$ is not in the support of $E$. Thus $V$ meets the complement of $\operatorname{supp} E$. $\square$

Main Theorem I of the base is for integral varieties. A variety in the sense of this handbook is reduced, but it can have more than one irreducible component. The step from integral varieties to reduced varieties is the next hypothesis.

**Hypothesis 2.3.3 (reduction to integral varieties).** Let $p \ge 0$ and $n \ge 0$. If each integral variety of dimension at most $n$ over a field of characteristic $p$ has a resolution, then $\mathrm{Res}(p, n)$ holds.

Node: SG0000001Y · Lean: `Singularity.ReductionToIntegral`

The argument is standard. Let $X_1, \dots, X_r$ be the irreducible components of $X$ with the reduced structure, and let $Y_i \to X_i$ be resolutions. Then $\bigsqcup_i Y_i \to X$ is a resolution. A regular local ring is a domain, so a point in two components is not regular. Thus $\operatorname{Reg}(X)$ is the disjoint union of open subsets of the sets $\operatorname{Reg}(X_i)$. The project keeps this step as a named hypothesis until its Lean proof is complete. It is not an axiom: each theorem that uses it takes it as an argument.

**Theorem 2.3.4 (characteristic 0).** Assume Hypothesis 2.3.3. Then $\mathrm{Res}(0, n)$ holds for each $n$.

Node: SG0000000D · Lean: `Singularity.resolutionInDim_zero`

*Proof.* A field of characteristic 0 in the sense of `CharP k 0` has characteristic 0 in the sense of `CharZero k`. Apply Theorem 2.3.2 and Hypothesis 2.3.3. $\square$

The status of SG0000000D on the map is "proved-mod", because the proof takes SG0000001Y as a hypothesis.

**Theorem 2.3.5 (de Jong).** Let $X$ be a variety over a field $k$. There is a regular variety $Y$ and a proper, surjective and generically finite morphism $Y \to X$.

Node: SG0000001K · Lean: `Singularity.exists_regular_alteration`

Such a morphism is an *alteration*. An alteration is weaker than a resolution, because it can have a degree greater than 1 over the generic point. For many questions of cohomology an alteration is sufficient, but it does not answer the problem of this handbook. Proposition 2.5.2 shows that the statement of the project does not accept an alteration as a resolution.
