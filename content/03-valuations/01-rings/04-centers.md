## Centers

A valuation of a function field is a birational object. A model of the function field gives it a geometric place, the center. In this section we define the center and we prove that it exists and is unique on a proper model. Schemes here are integral, and we view all their rings of functions as subrings of the function field.

**Definition 1.4.1 (center).** Let $X$ be an integral scheme with function field $K$, and let $V$ be a valuation ring of $K$. A point $x \in X$ is a *center* of $V$ on $X$ if $V$ dominates the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{X,x} \subseteq K$. The center of a valuation $v$ is the center of $\mathcal{O}_v$.

**Lemma 1.4.2.** Let $A \subseteq K$ be a subring with $\operatorname{Frac} A = K$, and let $V$ be a valuation ring of $K$. Then $V$ has a center on $\operatorname{Spec} A$ if and only if $A \subseteq V$. In this case the center is unique, and it is the prime $\mathfrak{m}_V \cap A$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a center. Then $A \subseteq A_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq V$, and $\mathfrak{m}_V \cap A = \mathfrak{m}_V \cap A_\mathfrak{p} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}A_\mathfrak{p} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}$. This proves uniqueness. Conversely, let $A \subseteq V$ and $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{m}_V \cap A$, a prime ideal of $A$. The elements of $A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ are units of $V$, so $A_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq V$. An element $a/s$ of $A_\mathfrak{p}$ with $a \in \mathfrak{p}$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}_V$, so $\mathfrak{p}A_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_V$. Thus $V$ dominates $A_\mathfrak{p}$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.4.3.** Let $X$ be an integral separated scheme with function field $K$. Then each valuation ring of $K$ has at most one center on $X$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Let $x$ and $y$ be centers of $V$. Choose affine open sets $U = \operatorname{Spec} A$ with $x \in U$ and $W = \operatorname{Spec} B$ with $y \in W$. Since $X$ is separated, $U \cap W$ is affine, and it is a closed subscheme of $U \times_\mathbb{Z} W$ by the diagonal [Har, Chapter II, Exercise 4.3]. So its ring $C$ is generated by $A$ and $B$ as a ring. Now $A \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{X,x} \subseteq V$ and $B \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{X,y} \subseteq V$, so $C \subseteq V$. By Lemma 1.4.2, $V$ has a center $z$ on $U \cap W$, and $z$ corresponds to $\mathfrak{m}_V \cap C$. As a point of $U$, the point $z$ corresponds to $\mathfrak{m}_V \cap C \cap A = \mathfrak{m}_V \cap A$, which is $x$ by Lemma 1.4.2. In the same way $z = y$. Thus $x = y$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.4.4.** Let $k$ be a field, let $X$ be an integral scheme that is proper over $k$, and let $K$ be its function field. Then each valuation ring $V$ of $K$ with $k \subseteq V$ has exactly one center on $X$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Uniqueness is Proposition 1.4.3. We prove existence when $X$ is projective. The general case follows from the valuative criterion of properness [Har, Chapter II, Theorem 4.7], and it is [Har, Chapter II, Exercise 4.5].

Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_k$ be a closed integral subscheme with homogeneous coordinates $x_0, \dots, x_n$. We renumber the coordinates so that $X$ meets the open set $D_+(x_0)$. Let $J$ be the set of indices $j$ such that $x_j$ does not vanish on $X$. Then $0 \in J$, and for $j \in J$ the function $x_j/x_0$ is a nonzero element of $K$. Choose $i \in J$ such that $v_V(x_i/x_0)$ is as small as possible. For $j \in J$ we have $v_V(x_j/x_i) = v_V(x_j/x_0) - v_V(x_i/x_0) \ge 0$, and for $j \notin J$ we have $x_j/x_i = 0$ on $X$. So the ring $A_i = k[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i]$ of the affine open set $X \cap D_+(x_i)$ is contained in $V$. This open set is not empty, so $\operatorname{Frac} A_i = K$. By Lemma 1.4.2, $V$ has a center on $X \cap D_+(x_i)$. $\square$

For a valuation $v$ of $K/k$ and a model $X$ as in Proposition 1.4.4, we write $c_X(v)$ for the center. Let $X' \to X$ be a proper birational morphism of integral schemes proper over $k$. Then $c_{X'}(v)$ maps to $c_X(v)$, because the local ring of $X$ at the image is contained in the local ring of $X'$ at $c_{X'}(v)$, and the inclusion is local.

The center can be a closed point, the generic point of a subvariety, or the generic point of $X$. The valuation is trivial if and only if its center is the generic point. The *dimension* of a valuation $v$ of $K/k$ is $\operatorname{trdeg}(Kv/k)$. If $x = c_X(v)$, then the residue field $\kappa(x)$ is a subfield of $Kv$, so $\dim \overline{\{x\}} = \operatorname{trdeg}(\kappa(x)/k) \le \dim v$.
