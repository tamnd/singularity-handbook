## Centers

A valuation of a function field is a birational object. A model of the function field gives it a geometric place, the center. In this section we define the center and we prove that it exists and is unique on a proper model. Schemes here are integral, and we view all their rings of functions as subrings of the function field.

**Definition 1.4.1 (center).** Let $X$ be an integral scheme with function field $K$, and let $V$ be a valuation ring of $K$. A point $x \in X$ is a *center* of $V$ on $X$ if $V$ dominates the local ring $\mathcal{O}_{X,x} \subseteq K$. The center of a valuation $v$ is the center of $\mathcal{O}_v$.

**Lemma 1.4.2.** Let $A \subseteq K$ be a subring with $\operatorname{Frac} A = K$, and let $V$ be a valuation ring of $K$. Then $V$ has a center on $\operatorname{Spec} A$ if and only if $A \subseteq V$. In this case the center is unique, and it is the prime $\mathfrak{m}_V \cap A$.

Lean: `Singularity.exists_center_iff`, `Singularity.eq_of_ofPrime_le`, `Singularity.eq_centerIdeal_of_ofPrime_le`

*Proof.* Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a center. Then $A \subseteq A_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq V$, and $\mathfrak{m}_V \cap A = \mathfrak{m}_V \cap A_\mathfrak{p} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}A_\mathfrak{p} \cap A = \mathfrak{p}$. This proves uniqueness. Conversely, let $A \subseteq V$ and $\mathfrak{p} = \mathfrak{m}_V \cap A$, a prime ideal of $A$. The elements of $A \setminus \mathfrak{p}$ are units of $V$, so $A_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq V$. An element $a/s$ of $A_\mathfrak{p}$ with $a \in \mathfrak{p}$ lies in $\mathfrak{m}_V$, so $\mathfrak{p}A_\mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_V$. Thus $V$ dominates $A_\mathfrak{p}$. $\square$

The Lean statement does not use the hypothesis $\operatorname{Frac} A = K$.

**Proposition 1.4.3.** Let $X$ be an integral separated scheme with function field $K$. Then each valuation ring of $K$ has at most one center on $X$.

Lean: `Singularity.Scheme.eq_of_isCenter` (the Lean proof uses the valuative criterion of separatedness, not the affine argument below)

*Proof.* Let $x$ and $y$ be centers of $V$. Choose affine open sets $U = \operatorname{Spec} A$ with $x \in U$ and $W = \operatorname{Spec} B$ with $y \in W$. We first show that $U \cap W$ is affine, with ring generated by $A$ and $B$. This is [Har, Chapter II, Exercise 4.3], and we give the argument. Let $\Delta : X \to X \times_\mathbb{Z} X$ be the diagonal. Then $\Delta^{-1}(U \times_\mathbb{Z} W) = U \cap W$, so the morphism $U \cap W \to U \times_\mathbb{Z} W = \operatorname{Spec}(A \otimes_\mathbb{Z} B)$ is the base change of $\Delta$ to the open set $U \times_\mathbb{Z} W$. Since $X$ is separated, $\Delta$ is a closed immersion, and so is its base change. A closed subscheme of an affine scheme is affine, and its ring is a quotient of the ring of the affine scheme [Har, Chapter II, Corollary 5.10]. So $U \cap W = \operatorname{Spec} C$, and the map $A \otimes_\mathbb{Z} B \to C$, $a \otimes b \mapsto ab$, is surjective. Here we view $A$, $B$ and $C$ as subrings of $K$. Thus $C$ is the subring of $K$ generated by $A$ and $B$. Now $A \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{X,x} \subseteq V$ and $B \subseteq \mathcal{O}_{X,y} \subseteq V$, so $C \subseteq V$. By Lemma 1.4.2, $V$ has a center $z$ on $U \cap W$, and $z$ corresponds to $\mathfrak{m}_V \cap C$. As a point of $U$, the point $z$ corresponds to $\mathfrak{m}_V \cap C \cap A = \mathfrak{m}_V \cap A$, which is $x$ by Lemma 1.4.2. In the same way $z = y$. Thus $x = y$. $\square$

**Proposition 1.4.4.** Let $k$ be a field, let $X$ be an integral scheme that is proper over $k$, and let $K$ be its function field. Then each valuation ring $V$ of $K$ with $k \subseteq V$ has exactly one center on $X$.

Lean: `Singularity.Scheme.existsUnique_isCenter` (the scheme $X$ is given over $k$ by a proper morphism)

*Proof.* Uniqueness is Proposition 1.4.3. We prove existence from the valuative criterion of properness [Har, Chapter II, Theorem 4.7]. The morphism $X \to \operatorname{Spec} k$ is of finite type, and $X$ is Noetherian. Let $\eta$ be the generic point of $X$. The inclusions $k \subseteq V \subseteq K$ give a commutative square of the morphisms $\operatorname{Spec} K \to X$, with image $\eta$, and $\operatorname{Spec} V \to \operatorname{Spec} k$. Since $X$ is proper over $k$, the criterion gives a morphism $g : \operatorname{Spec} V \to X$ over $k$ whose restriction to the generic point $\operatorname{Spec} K$ of $\operatorname{Spec} V$ is the given morphism. Let $s$ be the closed point of $\operatorname{Spec} V$, and let $x = g(s)$. The morphism $g$ gives a local homomorphism $\varphi : \mathcal{O}_{X,x} \to \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec} V, s} = V$. The composite of $\varphi$ with $V \subseteq K$ is the map of $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ to the stalk at the generic point, which is the inclusion $\mathcal{O}_{X,x} \subseteq K$. So $\mathcal{O}_{X,x} \subseteq V$, and $\mathfrak{m}_{X,x} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_V$ because $\varphi$ is local. Thus $x$ is a center of $V$. This argument is [Har, Chapter II, Exercise 4.5].

When $X$ is projective, we give a direct proof that does not use the criterion. Let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^n_k$ be a closed integral subscheme with homogeneous coordinates $x_0, \dots, x_n$. We renumber the coordinates so that $X$ meets the open set $D_+(x_0)$. Let $J$ be the set of indices $j$ such that $x_j$ does not vanish on $X$. Then $0 \in J$, and for $j \in J$ the function $x_j/x_0$ is a nonzero element of $K$. Choose $i \in J$ such that $v_V(x_i/x_0)$ is as small as possible. For $j \in J$ we have $v_V(x_j/x_i) = v_V(x_j/x_0) - v_V(x_i/x_0) \ge 0$, and for $j \notin J$ we have $x_j/x_i = 0$ on $X$. So the ring $A_i = k[x_0/x_i, \dots, x_n/x_i]$ of the affine open set $X \cap D_+(x_i)$ is contained in $V$. This open set is not empty, so $\operatorname{Frac} A_i = K$. By Lemma 1.4.2, $V$ has a center on $X \cap D_+(x_i)$. $\square$

For a valuation $v$ of $K/k$ and a model $X$ as in Proposition 1.4.4, we write $c_X(v)$ for the center. Let $X' \to X$ be a proper birational morphism of integral schemes proper over $k$. Then $c_{X'}(v)$ maps to $c_X(v)$, because the local ring of $X$ at the image is contained in the local ring of $X'$ at $c_{X'}(v)$, and the inclusion is local.

The center can be a closed point, the generic point of a subvariety, or the generic point of $X$. The valuation is trivial if and only if its center is the generic point. The *dimension* of a valuation $v$ of $K/k$ is $\operatorname{trdeg}(Kv/k)$. If $x = c_X(v)$, then the residue field $\kappa(x)$ is a subfield of $Kv$, so $\dim \overline{\{x\}} = \operatorname{trdeg}(\kappa(x)/k) \le \dim v$.

### Examples of centers

**Example 1.4.5 (two centers).** Proposition 1.4.3 needs the hypothesis that $X$ is separated. Let $k$ be a field, and let $X$ be the line with the origin doubled [Har, Chapter II, Example 2.3.6]. It is the union of two open sets $U_1, U_2$, each isomorphic to $\mathbb{A}^1_k = \operatorname{Spec} k[t]$, glued along $\mathbb{A}^1_k \setminus \{0\}$ by the identity map. The function field is $K = k(t)$. Let $0_1 \in U_1$ and $0_2 \in U_2$ be the two origins. Both local rings $\mathcal{O}_{X,0_1}$ and $\mathcal{O}_{X,0_2}$ are the subring $k[t]_{(t)}$ of $K$. Let $V = k[t]_{(t)}$ be the valuation ring of $\operatorname{ord}_t$. Then $V$ dominates both local rings, so $0_1$ and $0_2$ are two distinct centers of $V$. By Proposition 1.4.3, $X$ is not separated.

**Example 1.4.6 (no center).** Proposition 1.4.4 needs the hypothesis that $X$ is proper. Let $K = k(x)$, and let $v(f/g) = \deg g - \deg f$ for nonzero polynomials $f, g \in k[x]$. This is a valuation of $K/k$, with $\Gamma_v = \mathbb{Z}$, $v(x) = -1$ and $\mathcal{O}_v = k[1/x]_{(1/x)}$. Since $x \notin \mathcal{O}_v$, Lemma 1.4.2 shows that $v$ has no center on $\mathbb{A}^1_k = \operatorname{Spec} k[x]$. On $\mathbb{P}^1_k$, the open set $\operatorname{Spec} k[1/x]$ is the complement of the origin. Its ring is in $\mathcal{O}_v$, and $\mathfrak{m}_v \cap k[1/x] = (1/x)$. So the center of $v$ on $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ is the point $\infty$. The residue field is $Kv = k$, and the dimension of $v$ is 0.

The converses of Propositions 1.4.3 and 1.4.4 are also true. Let $X$ be an integral scheme of finite type over $k$. If each valuation ring $V$ of $K$ with $k \subseteq V$ has at most one center on $X$, then $X$ is separated. If each such $V$ has exactly one center on $X$, then $X$ is proper over $k$. These are [Har, Chapter II, Exercise 4.5]. We do not use the converses.
