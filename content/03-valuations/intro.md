# Introduction

This volume treats valuations of fields. Valuations enter resolution of singularities through the method of Zariski [Zar40, Zar44]: one resolves a variety along each valuation of its function field, and then one patches the local solutions. Zariski carried out the method in characteristic 0 up to dimension 3, and Cossart and Piltant carried it out in characteristic $p > 0$ in dimension 3 [CP08, CP09, CP19]. In dimension 4 and characteristic $p$ it is one of the routes of Volume 9. This volume gives the theory that the route needs: valuation rings, the Zariski-Riemann space, the Abhyankar inequality, the defect and key polynomials.

Chapter 1 gives the basic objects. Section 1.1 defines Krull valuations and valuation rings. Section 1.2 relates the rank to the prime ideals of the valuation ring. Section 1.3 proves the extension theorem of Chevalley. Section 1.4 defines the center of a valuation on a scheme. Section 1.5 proves that the Zariski-Riemann space is quasi-compact. Section 1.6 computes the monomial valuations and the arc valuations of the plane. Chapter 2 proves the Abhyankar inequality, in the form for fields and in the local form of Abhyankar [Abh56], and it studies the case of equality. Chapter 3 studies the extensions of a valuation to a finite extension of fields: the fundamental inequality, the defect with the Artin-Schreier example, and the key polynomials of MacLane [Mac36]. Chapter 4 defines local uniformization, proves the reduction of Zariski to finitely many models, and states the known results with their sources.

The reader we have in mind knows Volume 1 and the first chapters of a book on schemes, such as Hartshorne [Har]. We use the theorem of Tychonoff and the valuative criterion of properness without proof. The standard source for the classical theory is Zariski and Samuel [ZS2, Chapter VI]. For defect and henselian fields we follow the book draft of Kuhlmann [Kuh-book].

We prove the main results of Chapters 1 and 2. These are the theorem of Chevalley, the quasi-compactness of the Zariski-Riemann space, the Abhyankar inequality in both forms, and the structure of the value group and the residue field in the case of equality. In Chapter 3 we prove the criterion for defect by best approximation and all claims of the Artin-Schreier example, and we compute one MacLane chain by hand. We cite three facts in Chapter 3: the fundamental inequality for more than one extension, the theorem of Ostrowski, and the fact that a complete valued field of rank 1 is henselian. We prove parts 1 to 3 of the theorem of MacLane-Vaquié on augmented valuations. We prove part 4 when $\nu$ is discrete of rank 1 (Theorems 3.3.12 and 3.3.36) and when $\nu$ has nonzero support (Theorem 3.3.46), and a weak form for each $\nu$ (Theorem 3.3.44). For a valuation with support 0 and values that are not discrete, we cite part 4 from [Vaq07]. In Chapter 4 we prove the reduction of Zariski to finitely many models. We prove local uniformization only in two easy cases: transcendence degree at most 1 (Proposition 4.1.7) and valuation rings that are essentially of finite type, which include the divisorial valuations (Proposition 4.1.8). The proofs of the other known cases are planned for Volume 5.
