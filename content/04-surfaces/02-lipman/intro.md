# The theorem of Lipman

This chapter states the theorem of Lipman: each reduced excellent Noetherian scheme of dimension at most 2 has a resolution, and one obtains it if one normalizes and blows up the singular points, in turn, until the scheme is regular. Section 2.1 defines rational singularities, proves the formal parts of the reduction to the rational case, and shows that rationality can be checked at the local rings. Section 2.2 states the theorem, proves the parts that are formal, and says which steps of the proof we only cite. Section 2.1 also proves that the curves over a rational singularity have $H^1 = 0$ and meet in at most one point. Section 2.2 also proves that the process is local and that the length of $H^1$ does not go down along it. Section 2.3 computes examples in characteristic $p$ in charts. It resolves $A_n$, $D_4$ and $E_6$ with their dual graphs, it shows how $D_4$, $E_6$ and $z^2 + x^3 + y^5$ behave in characteristic 2, 3 and 5, and it shows that the cone over a plane cubic is not rational. Two of them study $H^1$ of a resolution: it is not 0 for a singularity whose exceptional curve has genus 1, and it is 0 for the singularity $xy = z^2$.
