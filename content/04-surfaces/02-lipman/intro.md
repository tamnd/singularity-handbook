# The theorem of Lipman

This chapter states the theorem of Lipman: each reduced excellent Noetherian scheme of dimension at most 2 has a resolution, and one obtains it if one normalizes and blows up the singular points, in turn, until the scheme is regular. Section 2.1 defines rational singularities, proves the formal parts of the reduction to the rational case, and shows that rationality can be checked at the local rings. It also proves that the curves over a rational singularity have $H^1 = 0$ and that two of them meet in at most one point. Section 2.2 states the theorem and proves its formal parts, among them that the process is local and that the length of $H^1$ does not go down along it. It says which steps of the proof we only cite. Section 2.3 computes examples in characteristic $p$ in charts. It resolves $A_n$, $D_4$ and $E_6$ with their dual graphs, and it shows how $D_4$, $E_6$ and $z^2 + x^3 + y^5$ behave in characteristics 2, 3 and 5. Two examples study $H^1$ of a resolution: it is not 0 for a singularity whose exceptional curve has genus 1, and it is 0 for the singularity $xy = z^2$. The cone over a plane cubic is a second singularity that is not rational.
