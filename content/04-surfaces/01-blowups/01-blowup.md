## Blowups

The blowup is the main operation of the resolution of singularities. It replaces a closed subscheme by an effective Cartier divisor, and it does not change the scheme outside the subscheme. In this section we recall the definition and the universal property, we compute the blowup in charts, and we compute the blowup of a regular surface at a closed point. All schemes are Noetherian.

### The definition

**Definition 1.1.1.** Let $X$ be a Noetherian scheme and let $Z \subseteq X$ be a closed subscheme with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}$. The *Rees algebra* of $\mathcal{I}$ is the graded $\mathcal{O}_X$-algebra $\mathcal{S} = \bigoplus_{n \ge 0} \mathcal{I}^n$, with $\mathcal{I}^0 = \mathcal{O}_X$. The *blowup* of $X$ along $Z$ is the scheme $X' = \operatorname{Bl}_Z X = \operatorname{Proj}_X \mathcal{S}$ with its projection $\pi : X' \to X$. The closed subscheme $E = \pi^{-1}(Z)$ of $X'$, with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}\mathcal{O}_{X'}$, is the *exceptional divisor*. The set $Z$ is the *center* of the blowup.

The algebra $\mathcal{S}$ is generated in degree 1 by the coherent sheaf $\mathcal{I}$. Thus $\pi$ is projective [Har, Section II.7], and in particular $\pi$ is proper.

**Theorem 1.1.2.** Let $X$, $Z$, $\mathcal{I}$ and $\pi : X' \to X$ be as in Definition 1.1.1, and let $U = X \setminus Z$.

1. The ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}\mathcal{O}_{X'}$ is invertible. Thus $E$ is an effective Cartier divisor on $X'$.
2. The morphism $\pi^{-1}(U) \to U$ is an isomorphism.
3. Let $g : T \to X$ be a morphism such that $\mathcal{I}\mathcal{O}_T$ is an invertible ideal sheaf. Then there is a unique morphism $h : T \to X'$ with $g = \pi \circ h$.

Lean: none yet

Part 3 is the *universal property* of the blowup. It characterizes $\pi$ up to a unique isomorphism. For the proof see [Har, Section II.7] and [Blow]. We prove part 1 for an affine $X$ in Lemma 1.1.3. For part 2, note that $\mathcal{I}|_U = \mathcal{O}_U$, so $\mathcal{S}|_U = \mathcal{O}_U[t]$ and $\operatorname{Proj}_U \mathcal{O}_U[t] = U$.

### Charts

Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring and let $I = (f_1, \dots, f_r)$ be an ideal of $R$. We write the Rees algebra as the subring $S = \bigoplus_{n \ge 0} I^n t^n$ of $R[t]$. For $f \in I$ we let $R[I/f]$ be the subring of $R_f$ generated by $R$ and the fractions $f_1/f, \dots, f_r/f$. It consists of the fractions $a/f^n$ with $n \ge 0$ and $a \in I^n$.

**Lemma 1.1.3.** Let $R$ and $I = (f_1, \dots, f_r)$ be as above, and let $X' = \operatorname{Bl}_I \operatorname{Spec} R = \operatorname{Proj} S$.

1. The open sets $D_+(f_i t)$ for $i = 1, \dots, r$ cover $X'$.
2. The map $a t^n/(f_i t)^n \mapsto a/f_i^n$ is an isomorphism from $S_{(f_i t)}$ onto $R[I/f_i]$. Thus $D_+(f_i t) \cong \operatorname{Spec} R[I/f_i]$.
3. In $R[I/f_i]$ we have $I \cdot R[I/f_i] = f_i R[I/f_i]$, and $f_i$ is a nonzerodivisor on $R[I/f_i]$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* 1. The irrelevant ideal $S_+$ is generated by $S_1 = I t$, and $I t$ is generated as an $R$-module by $f_1 t, \dots, f_r t$. Thus each homogeneous prime that does not contain $S_+$ does not contain some $f_i t$.

2. An element of degree 0 of $S_{f_i t}$ has the form $a t^n/(f_i t)^n$ with $a \in I^n$. The map is well defined and it is a ring homomorphism. Its image is the set of the fractions $a/f_i^n$ with $a \in I^n$, which is $R[I/f_i]$. For the injectivity, assume that $a/f_i^n = 0$ in $R_{f_i}$. Then $f_i^k a = 0$ in $R$ for some $k$. Thus $(f_i t)^k \cdot a t^n = 0$ in $S$, and $a t^n/(f_i t)^n = 0$.

3. For each $j$ we have $f_j = (f_j/f_i) \cdot f_i$ in $R[I/f_i]$. Thus $I \cdot R[I/f_i] = f_i R[I/f_i]$. The element $f_i$ is a unit of $R_{f_i}$, so multiplication by $f_i$ is injective on $R_{f_i}$, and hence on the subring $R[I/f_i]$. $\square$

If $f_i$ is a nonzerodivisor of $R$, then $R \to R_{f_i}$ is injective, and $R \subseteq R[I/f_i] \subseteq R_{f_i}$. If $R$ is a domain and $f_i \ne 0$, then $R[I/f_i]$ is a subring of the field of fractions of $R$. In that case each chart is a domain, all charts contain the generic point, and $X'$ is integral when $I \ne 0$.

### The strict transform

**Definition 1.1.4.** Let $\pi : X' \to X$ be the blowup along $Z$, and let $Y \subseteq X$ be a closed subscheme. The *strict transform* of $Y$ is the scheme-theoretic closure of $\pi^{-1}(Y \setminus Z)$ in $X'$.

Lean: none yet

In a chart the strict transform is easy to compute. Let $X = \operatorname{Spec} R$, let $B = R[I/f]$ be a chart, and let $Y = V(J)$. On $\operatorname{Spec} B$ the set $\pi^{-1}(Z)$ is $V(fB)$ by Lemma 1.1.3. Thus the strict transform of $Y$ in $\operatorname{Spec} B$ is the scheme-theoretic closure of the open set $D(f)$ of $V(JB)$. Its ideal is the kernel of $B/JB \to (B/JB)_f$, that is, the ideal $(JB : f^\infty)$ of the elements $b \in B$ with $f^k b \in JB$ for some $k$ [Stacks].

**Proposition 1.1.5.** Let $\pi : X' \to X$ be the blowup along $Z$ and let $Y \subseteq X$ be a closed subscheme. Then the strict transform of $Y$ is isomorphic over $Y$ to the blowup $\operatorname{Bl}_{Z \cap Y} Y$, where $Z \cap Y$ is the closed subscheme of $Y$ with ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}\mathcal{O}_Y$.

Lean: none yet

This is [Har, Section II.7]. Thus we can compute the blowup of a hypersurface as a strict transform in the blowup of the affine space. The next lemma does this computation.

**Lemma 1.1.6.** Let $k$ be a field and let $F \in k[x, y, z]$ be nonzero with $F(0) = 0$. Let $m \ge 1$ be the least degree of a monomial of $F$, and let $F_m$ be the homogeneous part of degree $m$ of $F$. Let $\pi$ be the blowup of $\mathbb{A}^3_k$ at the origin. The chart $R[I/x]$, with $R = k[x, y, z]$ and $I = (x, y, z)$, is the polynomial ring $k[x, y_1, z_1]$ with $y = x y_1$ and $z = x z_1$. In this chart

$$F(x, x y_1, x z_1) = x^m F_1(x, y_1, z_1),$$

where $x$ does not divide $F_1$ and $F_1(0, y_1, z_1) = F_m(1, y_1, z_1)$. The strict transform of $V(F)$ in this chart is $V(F_1)$, and the exceptional divisor in this chart is $V(x)$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* The ring $R[I/x] = k[x, y/x, z/x]$ is generated over $k$ by three elements, and its field of fractions $k(x, y, z)$ has transcendence degree 3. Thus the three generators are algebraically independent, and $R[I/x]$ is a polynomial ring in $x$, $y_1 = y/x$ and $z_1 = z/x$.

Write $F = \sum_{d \ge m} F_d$ with $F_d$ homogeneous of degree $d$. Then $F_d(x, x y_1, x z_1) = x^d F_d(1, y_1, z_1)$. Thus $F(x, x y_1, x z_1) = x^m F_1$ with $F_1 = \sum_{d \ge m} x^{d - m} F_d(1, y_1, z_1)$, and $F_1(0, y_1, z_1) = F_m(1, y_1, z_1)$. This polynomial is not zero, because $F_m(x, y, z) = x^m F_m(1, y/x, z/x)$ and $F_m \ne 0$. Thus $x$ does not divide $F_1$.

By Lemma 1.1.3, the exceptional divisor in the chart is $V(x)$. By the discussion after Definition 1.1.4, the ideal of the strict transform is $(x^m F_1 : x^\infty)$. The ring $k[x, y_1, z_1]$ is a factorial domain and $x$ is a prime element that does not divide $F_1$. If $x^k b \in (x^m F_1)$, then $F_1$ divides $x^k b$, so $F_1$ divides $b$. Thus $(x^m F_1 : x^\infty) = (F_1)$. $\square$

The charts $R[I/y]$ and $R[I/z]$ are the same with the roles of the variables exchanged. The intersection of the strict transform with the exceptional divisor is $V(x, F_m(1, y_1, z_1))$ in the chart. It is the part of the projective tangent cone $V(F_m) \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ in the chart.

### The blowup of a regular surface at a point

**Theorem 1.1.7.** Let $X$ be a regular Noetherian scheme, let $x \in X$ be a closed point with $\dim \mathcal{O}_{X,x} = 2$, and let $\kappa = \kappa(x)$. Let $\pi : X' \to X$ be the blowup of $X$ at the reduced point $x$, with exceptional divisor $E$. Then the following hold.

1. The scheme $X'$ is regular.
2. The divisor $E$ is an effective Cartier divisor, and $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1_\kappa$.
3. The conormal sheaf $\mathcal{I}_E/\mathcal{I}_E^2$ is isomorphic to $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)$.

*Proof.* *Step 0: reduction to a local ring.* By Theorem 1.1.2, $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $X \setminus \{x\}$, and $X \setminus \{x\}$ is regular. A regular local ring is a domain (Theorem 1.3.3 of Volume 1). Thus $x$ lies on only one irreducible component of $X$, and we can choose an affine open neighbourhood $\operatorname{Spec} R$ of $x$ with $R$ a Noetherian domain. Let $M \subseteq R$ be the maximal ideal of $x$, let $A = R_M$ and let $\mathfrak{m} = MA$. Each point $e \in E$ lies in a chart $\operatorname{Spec} R[M/f]$ with $f \in M$, and its prime $P$ satisfies $P \cap R = M$. Thus the local ring of $X'$ at $e$ is a localization of $R[M/f] \otimes_R A = A[\mathfrak{m}/f]$. Also $E$ is the scheme $\operatorname{Proj} \bigoplus_n M^n/M^{n+1}$, and $M^n/M^{n+1} = \mathfrak{m}^n/\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}$, because $M$ is maximal. Thus we may replace $X$ by $\operatorname{Spec} A$. Then $A$ is a regular local ring of dimension 2, and $\mathfrak{m} = (u, v)$ for a regular system of parameters $u, v$. By Lemma 1.1.3 the two charts $B = A[\mathfrak{m}/u]$ and $B' = A[\mathfrak{m}/v]$ cover $X'$.

*Step 1: the chart $B$.* We show that $B \cong A[T]/(uT - v)$, with $T \mapsto v/u$. Let $C = A[T]/(uT - v)$. First, $u$ is a nonzerodivisor on $C$. Let $g, h \in A[T]$ with $u g = (uT - v) h$. Reduce modulo $u$. In $(A/uA)[T]$ we get $\bar v \bar h = 0$. The ring $A/uA$ is a regular local ring of dimension 1 (Proposition 1.2.6 of Volume 1), so it is a domain. Also $\bar v \ne 0$, since otherwise $\mathfrak{m} = (u)$ and $\dim A \le 1$. Thus $\bar h = 0$, so $h = u h_1$. Since $A[T]$ is a domain, $g = (uT - v) h_1$. Thus $u$ is a nonzerodivisor on $C$, and $C \to C_u$ is injective. Now $C_u = A_u[T]/(T - v/u) \cong A_u$, and the image of $C$ in $A_u$ is $A[v/u] = B$. Thus $C \cong B$. In particular

$$B/uB \cong A[T]/(u, v) = \kappa[T].$$

By Lemma 1.1.3, $\mathfrak{m} B = uB$, so $E \cap \operatorname{Spec} B = \operatorname{Spec} \kappa[T] \cong \mathbb{A}^1_\kappa$. This is the case $d = 1$ of Proposition 1.10.4 of Volume 1.

*Step 2: $B$ is regular.* The ring $B$ is a finitely generated $A$-algebra, so it is Noetherian. Let $P$ be a prime of $B$. If $u \notin P$, then $B_P$ is a localization of $B_u = A_u$, and $A_u$ is a localization of $A$. Thus $B_P$ is regular by Corollary 1.6.9 of Volume 1. If $u \in P$, then $B_P/uB_P$ is a localization of $\kappa[T]$ at a prime ideal. It is a field or a discrete valuation ring, so it is regular. The element $u$ is a nonzerodivisor of the domain $B_P$. By Proposition 1.2.8 of Volume 1, $B_P$ is regular. Thus $B$ is regular. This is the case $d = 1$ of Theorem 1.10.6 of Volume 1. By symmetry, $B' \cong A[S]/(vS - u)$ is regular and $B'/vB' \cong \kappa[S]$. This proves part 1.

*Step 3: $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1_\kappa$.* The charts meet in $\operatorname{Spec} B[1/T] = \operatorname{Spec} B'[1/S]$, because $B[u/v] = A[v/u, u/v] = B'[v/u]$, and there $S = T^{-1}$. Thus $E$ is glued from $\operatorname{Spec} \kappa[T]$ and $\operatorname{Spec} \kappa[S]$ along $\operatorname{Spec} \kappa[T, T^{-1}]$ with $S = T^{-1}$. This is $\mathbb{P}^1_\kappa$ with homogeneous coordinates $X_0, X_1$ and $T = X_1/X_0$. The ideal of $E$ is $uB$ on the first chart and $vB'$ on the second chart. These are generated by nonzerodivisors, so $E$ is an effective Cartier divisor. This proves part 2.

*Step 4: the conormal sheaf.* The sheaf $\mathcal{I}_E/\mathcal{I}_E^2$ is an invertible sheaf on $E$. On $\{X_0 \ne 0\}$ it is generated by the class $e_0$ of $u$, and on $\{X_1 \ne 0\}$ by the class $e_1$ of $v$. On the overlap $v = Tu$, so $e_1 = (X_1/X_0) e_0$. The sheaf $\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)$ is generated by $X_0$ on $\{X_0 \ne 0\}$ and by $X_1$ on $\{X_1 \ne 0\}$, and $X_1 = (X_1/X_0) X_0$. Thus $e_0 \mapsto X_0$ and $e_1 \mapsto X_1$ define an isomorphism $\mathcal{I}_E/\mathcal{I}_E^2 \cong \mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^1}(1)$. This proves part 3. $\square$

The proof uses only that $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is a regular local ring of dimension 2. Thus the theorem holds in every characteristic and in mixed characteristic. An example is the blowup of $\operatorname{Spec} \mathbb{Z}_p[t]$ at the closed point $(p, t)$, with $u = p$ and $v = t$. Then $E \cong \mathbb{P}^1_{\mathbb{F}_p}$.

### The self-intersection

For an invertible sheaf $\mathcal{L}$ on $\mathbb{P}^1_\kappa$ there is a unique integer $n$ with $\mathcal{L} \cong \mathcal{O}(n)$, since $\operatorname{Pic} \mathbb{P}^1_\kappa \cong \mathbb{Z}$ [Har, Section II.6]. We write $\deg \mathcal{L} = n$.

**Definition 1.1.8.** In the situation of Theorem 1.1.7, the *self-intersection* of $E$ is $E \cdot E = \deg \mathcal{O}_{X'}(E)|_E$, where $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(E) = \mathcal{I}_E^{-1}$.

**Corollary 1.1.9.** In the situation of Theorem 1.1.7, $E \cdot E = -1$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* The restriction $\mathcal{I}_E|_E = \mathcal{I}_E \otimes \mathcal{O}_E$ is $\mathcal{I}_E/\mathcal{I}_E^2$. Thus $\mathcal{O}_{X'}(E)|_E$ is the dual of $\mathcal{I}_E/\mathcal{I}_E^2$. By Theorem 1.1.7 this dual is $\mathcal{O}(-1)$, which has degree $-1$. $\square$

**Remark 1.1.10.** For a regular projective surface over an algebraically closed field, $E \cdot E$ is the intersection number of [Har, Section V.3], and the corollary is the formula $E^2 = -1$ there. On such a surface, a curve $C \cong \mathbb{P}^1$ with $C \cdot C = -1$ is an *exceptional curve of the first kind*. The theorem of Castelnuovo says that each such curve comes from the blowup of a point on another regular projective surface [Har, Section V.5]. We use neither fact.

### The blowup along a regular curve

A blowup along a closed subscheme whose ideal is already invertible does nothing. We show that this is the case for a regular curve on a regular surface. The proof works in each dimension. Thus the centers of the resolution of a surface are points, and a blowup along a regular curve changes the scheme only in dimension $3$ or more.

**Theorem 1.1.11.** Let $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ be a regular local ring of dimension $d$, and let $P$ be an ideal of $R$ such that $R/P$ is a regular local ring of dimension $d - 1$. Then there is an element $x \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$ with $P = (x)$. In particular, $P$ is a prime ideal.

*Proof.* Step 1. We show that $P \not\subseteq \mathfrak{m}^2$. The maximal ideal of $R/P$ is $\mathfrak{m}/P$. Since $R/P$ is regular of dimension $d - 1$, there are $y_1, \dots, y_{d-1} \in \mathfrak{m}$ whose images generate $\mathfrak{m}/P$. Then $\mathfrak{m} = (y_1, \dots, y_{d-1}) + P$. Assume that $P \subseteq \mathfrak{m}^2$. Then $\mathfrak{m} = (y_1, \dots, y_{d-1}) + \mathfrak{m}^2$, and the lemma of Nakayama (Volume 1, Lemma 1.1.1) gives $\mathfrak{m} = (y_1, \dots, y_{d-1})$. By Volume 1, Corollary 1.1.3, $d = \dim R \le d - 1$. This is false. So there is an element $x \in P$ with $x \notin \mathfrak{m}^2$.

Step 2. By Volume 1, Proposition 1.2.6, the ring $R/(x)$ is a regular local ring of dimension $d - 1$. By Volume 1, Theorem 1.3.3, it is a domain. Thus $(x)$ is a prime ideal, and $(x) \subseteq P$.

Step 3. We show that $P = (x)$. Assume that $y \in P$ and $y \notin (x)$. Let $D = R/(x)$ and let $\bar{y}$ be the image of $y$ in $D$. Then $\bar{y} \ne 0$, and $\bar{y}$ is in the maximal ideal of $D$. Since $D$ is a domain, its only minimal prime is $0$, and each prime of $D$ that contains $\bar{y}$ is not minimal. So a chain of primes of $D/(\bar{y})$ of length $n$ gives a chain of primes of $D$ of length $n + 1$, if we add the prime $0$ at the bottom. Thus $\dim D/(\bar{y}) \le \dim D - 1 = d - 2$. The ring $R/P$ is a quotient of $D/(\bar{y}) = R/(x, y)$, so $\dim R/P \le d - 2$. This contradicts $\dim R/P = d - 1$. Thus $P = (x)$. $\square$

The case $d = 2$ is a regular curve on a regular surface, at a closed point of the curve. In this case a stronger fact is true. A regular local ring is a UFD by the theorem of Auslander and Buchsbaum [Mat, Theorem 20.3], so each prime of height $1$ is principal. Thus each curve on a regular surface, regular or not, is an effective Cartier divisor. Theorem 1.1.11 does not use this theorem, and it gives one more fact: the generator $x$ is a part of a regular system of parameters.

**Corollary 1.1.12.** Let $R$ and $P = (x)$ be as in Theorem 1.1.11. Then the map $R \to R[P/x]$ is an isomorphism, and the blowup of $\operatorname{Spec} R$ along $V(P)$ is an isomorphism. Let $X$ be a regular Noetherian scheme and let $C \subseteq X$ be a closed subscheme such that for each $c \in C$ the ring $\mathcal{O}_{C,c}$ is regular and $\dim \mathcal{O}_{C,c} = \dim \mathcal{O}_{X,c} - 1$. Then the blowup of $X$ along $C$ is an isomorphism.

*Proof.* The ideal $P$ is generated by the single element $x$. By Lemma 1.1.3, part 1, with $r = 1$, the chart $\operatorname{Spec} R[P/x]$ covers the blowup. Each generator of $R[P/x]$ over $R$ is a fraction $a/x$ with $a \in P = (x)$. So $a = cx$ for some $c \in R$, and $a/x = c$ in $R_x$. Thus $R[P/x]$ is the image of $R$ in $R_x$. The ring $R$ is a domain and $x \neq 0$, so $R \to R_x$ is injective. Thus $R \to R[P/x]$ is an isomorphism.

For the global statement, let $\mathcal{I}$ be the ideal sheaf of $C$, and let $c \in C$. By Theorem 1.1.11, the stalk $\mathcal{I}_c$ is generated by one element $f \neq 0$ of the domain $\mathcal{O}_{X,c}$. The sheaf $\mathcal{I}$ is coherent, so there is an affine open neighborhood $V = \operatorname{Spec} A$ of $c$ with $\mathcal{I}|_V = fA$. The annihilator of $f$ is a coherent ideal of $A$ with the stalk $0$ at $c$, so after we make $V$ smaller, $f$ is a nonzerodivisor of $A$. The construction of Definition 1.1.1 is local on $X$, so $\pi^{-1}(V)$ is the blowup of $V$ along $V(f)$. The argument of the first paragraph uses only that $f$ is a nonzerodivisor and generates the ideal, so $A \to A[fA/f]$ is an isomorphism, and $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $V$. Over $X \setminus C$, $\pi$ is an isomorphism by Theorem 1.1.2, part 2. $\square$
