# Introduction

This volume treats the resolution of singularities of surfaces. The resolution of curves in each characteristic is in Volume 1, Chapter 2, so this volume begins in dimension 2. A surface here is a Noetherian scheme of dimension at most 2. We do not fix the characteristic. The base can be a field of characteristic 0, a field of characteristic $p > 0$, or a ring of mixed characteristic such as $\mathbb{Z}_p$. The main theorem is the theorem of Lipman [Lip78]: a reduced excellent Noetherian scheme of dimension at most 2 has a resolution. The resolution comes from two operations, normalization and the blowup of the singular points, which we apply in turn until the scheme is regular.

Chapter 1 gives the two operations. Section 1.1 defines the blowup and computes the blowup of a regular surface at a closed point. Section 1.2 shows that the normalization of an excellent surface is finite and that a normal excellent surface has only finitely many singular points. Chapter 2 is about the theorem of Lipman. Section 2.1 defines rational singularities, Section 2.2 states the theorem and gives the structure of its proof, and Section 2.3 computes examples in characteristic $p$. Chapter 3 states the embedded resolution of surfaces, the theorem of Cossart-Jannsen-Saito [CJS20], and gives the history from Zariski [Zar39] and Abhyankar [Abh56s].

The reader we have in mind knows Volume 1 and the first three chapters of a book on schemes, such as Hartshorne [Har] or Liu [Liu]. We use the cohomology of coherent sheaves, the Leray spectral sequence and the theorem on formal functions without proof.

We prove most results of Chapter 1. We cite three facts: the universal property of the blowup and the description of the strict transform as a blowup [Har], and the finiteness of the normalization of an excellent scheme [EGA4-7]. In Chapter 2 we prove the formal parts of the theorem of Lipman: the process is well defined, its result is a resolution when it stops, and rational singularities stay rational under a normal modification. We cite the two hard steps from [Lip78]: the bound on the length of $H^1(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y)$ over the normal modifications $Y$, and the end of the process at rational singularities. The examples of Section 2.3 are complete computations in charts. Chapter 3 states theorems without proof, and it computes one embedded resolution in charts.

The resolution of surfaces is not proved in Lean yet. Most statements of this volume have the line "Lean: none yet". The Lean names that do occur are of four kinds. Theorem 1.1.11 and Corollary 1.1.12, on the blowup along a regular curve, have Lean proofs for the local ring and the chart. Theorem 1.2.2 gives the finiteness of the normalization of an affine domain over a field from Volume 1. Theorem 1.1.7 gives the two Lean results on a chart of the blowup of a regular local ring from Section 1.10 of Volume 1. Theorem 2.2.1 gives the Lean statement `Singularity.ResolutionInDim`, which is a definition of the statement and not a proof.
