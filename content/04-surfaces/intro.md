# Introduction

This volume treats the resolution of singularities of surfaces. The resolution of curves in each characteristic is in Volume 1, Chapter 2, so this volume begins in dimension 2. A surface here is a Noetherian scheme of dimension at most 2. We do not fix the characteristic. The base can be a field of characteristic 0, a field of characteristic $p > 0$, or a ring of mixed characteristic such as $\mathbb{Z}_p$. The main theorem is the theorem of Lipman [Lip78]: a reduced excellent Noetherian scheme of dimension at most 2 has a resolution. The resolution comes from two operations, normalization and the blowup of the singular points, which we apply in turn until the scheme is regular.

Chapter 1 gives the two operations. Section 1.1 defines the blowup and computes the blowup of a regular surface at a closed point. Section 1.2 shows that the normalization of an excellent surface is finite and that a normal excellent surface has only finitely many singular points. Chapter 2 is about the theorem of Lipman. Section 2.1 defines rational singularities and studies the curves over them. Section 2.2 states the theorem, proves that the process is local and that the length of $H^1$ does not go down along it, and gives a table of the steps of the proof with what is proved and what is cited. Section 2.3 computes examples in characteristic $p$. It resolves $A_n$, $D_4$ and $E_6$ with their dual graphs and self-intersections, shows how these equations change in small characteristic, and shows that the cone over a plane cubic is not rational. Chapter 3 states the embedded resolution of surfaces, the theorem of Cossart-Jannsen-Saito [CJS20], and gives the history from Zariski [Zar39] and Abhyankar [Abh56s]. It also proves the embedded resolution of a curve on a smooth surface by blowups of points, in every characteristic.

The reader we have in mind knows Volume 1 and the first three chapters of a book on schemes, such as Hartshorne [Har] or Liu [Liu]. We use the cohomology of coherent sheaves, the Leray spectral sequence and the theorem on formal functions without proof.

We prove most results of Chapter 1, including the universal property of the blowup and the description of the strict transform as a blowup. We cite the finiteness of the normalization of an excellent scheme [EGA4-7]. In Chapter 2 we prove the formal parts of the theorem of Lipman: the process is well defined, its result is a resolution when it stops, and rational singularities stay rational under a normal modification. We cite three hard steps from [Lip78]: the bound on the length of $H^1(Y, \mathcal{O}_Y)$ over the normal modifications $Y$, the fact that the process reaches a surface with only rational singularities, and the end of the process at rational singularities. We also prove that rationality can be checked at the local rings. The examples of Section 2.3 are complete computations in charts, and two of them study the cohomology group $H^1$ of a resolution. Chapter 3 states the theorem for surfaces without proof, and it computes two embedded resolutions in charts. It proves the embedded resolution of curves on a smooth surface over an algebraically closed field in full, with the decrease of the multiplicity and of the delta invariant.

The resolution of surfaces is not proved in Lean yet. Most statements of this volume have the line "Lean: none yet". The Lean names that do occur are of six kinds. Theorem 1.1.11 and Corollary 1.1.12, on the blowup along a regular curve, have Lean proofs for the local ring and the chart. Theorem 1.2.2 gives the finiteness of the normalization of an affine domain over a field from Volume 1. Theorem 1.1.7 gives the two Lean results on a chart of the blowup of a regular local ring from Section 1.10 of Volume 1. Theorem 2.2.1 gives the Lean statement `Singularity.ResolutionInDim`, which is a definition of the statement and not a proof. The theorem `Singularity.resolutionInDim_two` for the case of dimension 2 is planned, and it has no Lean statement yet. Chapter 3 gives the Lean definitions `Singularity.IsPermissible`, `Singularity.IsSncDivisor` and `Singularity.IsEmbeddedResolution`, two small results with Lean proofs (`Singularity.isPermissible_vanishingIdeal_singleton` and `Singularity.isSncDivisor_of_codim_one`), and the Lean statement `Singularity.EmbeddedResolutionSurface`, which is not proved. The last kind are the chart equations of the examples: the blowups of $xy = z^n$ and the chain of Proposition 2.3.10, the Whitney umbrella in Examples 1.2.7 and 3.1.6, the inseparable blowup of Example 2.3.5 and the two cusps of Example 3.1.10. These are identities of polynomials. The geometric claims of the examples are not in Lean.
