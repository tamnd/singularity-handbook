## The points where the log-Jacobian order stays

This section reports the fourth research result of the milestone S7. It is the first step toward the open step of the route of the purely inseparable equations in Section 2.3, the proof that the residual order $\rho$ of the log-Jacobian ideal goes down. Volume 7, Section 3.7, gives the same result with all the details.

### The problem

Volume 7, Theorem 3.2.4, says that $\rho$ does not go up under a point blowup, in each dimension. For a resolution invariant this is half of the work. The other half is to show that $\rho$ cannot stay the same forever. In the theory of Hironaka, the first step for the order is to find the points where the order stays. They lie in the projective space of the directrix of the initial form (Volume 7, Theorem 2.2.5). The question of this section is: where are the points at which $\rho$ stays?

### The result

We use the setting of Volume 7, Section 3.2. Let $g = x^r G$ be clean with $\operatorname{ord} g \ge p$, let $d = \operatorname{ord} G$, let $\rho = \rho(g)$, and let $\sigma \in \{\rho, \rho + 1\}$ be the order of the ideal $\widetilde\Theta_0(G)$ with $\Theta_0(g) = x^r \widetilde\Theta_0(G)$. Let $v$ be the vector of a chart of the point blowup, and let $g'_c$ be the clean part of the transform.

**Theorem 3.4.1.** Assume that $\rho(g'_c) = \rho$.

1. If $\sigma = \rho$, then $v \in T(\operatorname{in}(h))$ for each $h \in \widetilde\Theta_0(G)$ of order $\rho$.
2. If $\sigma = \rho + 1$, then $v \in T(\operatorname{in}(h))$ for each $h \in \widetilde\Theta(G)$ of order $\rho$.
3. If $\rho = d$ and $p \nmid |r| + d$, then $v \in T(\operatorname{in}(G))$.
4. If $\rho = d - 1$, then $v \in T(\partial_j \operatorname{in}(G))$ for each $j \notin \Delta$ with $\partial_j \operatorname{in}(G) \ne 0$.

So the points where $\rho$ stays lie in the projective space of a linear subspace $T_\rho(g)$, and if $T_\rho(g) = 0$, then $\rho$ goes down at each point after one blowup. Part 1 is false with $\widetilde\Theta(G)$ in the place of $\widetilde\Theta_0(G)$. For $p = 3$, $g = x^3 (y^2 + x)$ and $E = V(x)$, we have $\rho = \sigma = 1$, and $\rho$ stays at the origin of the $y$-chart, but $v = (0, 1)$ is not in the directrix of the element $\partial_y G = 2 y$ of $\widetilde\Theta(G)$ (Volume 7, Example 3.7.4).

### The proof

Parts 1 and 2. Put $h_0 = h$ in part 1 and $h_0 = x_1 h$ in part 2. Then $h_0 \in \widetilde\Theta_0(G)$ has order $\sigma$, and $x_1^{-\sigma} \pi(h_0)$ is the weak transform $h'$ of $h$. The proof of Volume 7, Theorem 3.2.4, writes $\Theta'(g'_c) = x^{r'} \widetilde\Theta(G')$ with $\widetilde\Theta(G') = x^{-s} W$, where $W$ is generated by the elements $x_1^{-\sigma} \pi(k)$ with $k \in \widetilde\Theta_0(G)$. So $\rho = \rho(g'_c) \le \operatorname{ord} W \le \operatorname{ord} h' \le \rho$. The order of $h$ stays the same, and the theorem of Hironaka for $h$ gives $v \in T(\operatorname{in}(h))$.

Part 3. The element $|r| G + \sum_i x_i \partial_i G$ is in $\widetilde\Theta_0(G)$, and by the Euler identity its initial form is $(|r| + d) \operatorname{in}(G) \ne 0$. So $\sigma = \rho = d$, and part 1 applies to it.

Part 4. Here $\sigma \ge d = \rho + 1$. The element $\partial_j G$ of $\widetilde\Theta(G)$ has the initial form $\partial_j \operatorname{in}(G)$ of degree $\rho$, and part 2 applies to it.

### What the result does not give

1. It does not say that $\rho$ goes down. At the points of $\mathbb{P}(T_\rho(g))$ the invariant can stay, and a second invariant must decide what happens there. This is the open step of route R1.
2. In part 4 the subspace can be larger than $T(\operatorname{in}(G))$. A random test found 1,878 points where $\rho = d - 1$ stays, and at 732 of them $v \notin T(\operatorname{in}(G))$.
3. The test found no point where $\rho = d$ stays, $p \mid |r| + d$ and $v \notin T(\operatorname{in}(G))$. We have no proof that this cannot happen.
4. It is for point blowups only. The blowups of Section 3.1 along a regular center need a similar result for the directions of the center.
