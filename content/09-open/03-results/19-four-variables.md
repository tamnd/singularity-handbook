## Corner runs in four variables

This section reports the nineteenth research result of the milestone S7, from the milestone S7.18. It continues Section 3.18, which says that the runs in four or more variables are not treated. Here we treat the corner runs in four variables with two letters, that is, $n = 4$ and $k = 2$, with a translation vector in the two free coordinates at each step. The finiteness of the translations is false in this case. But in each example that we know, the run lies on a regular hypersurface, the corner curves have $\nu^G \ge \rho$, and Volume 7, Question 3.13.18 has a positive answer. Volume 7, Section 3.17, gives the same results with all the details.

### The problem

At $P_m$ the coordinates are $(x_m, w_m, y_m, z_m)$, with $V(y_m)$ and $V(z_m)$ in $\Delta$. Each step is the $y$-chart or the $z$-chart, followed by a translation $(a_m, b_m) \in K^2$ of the two free coordinates, and each chart occurs an infinite number of times with $\rho \ge 1$ constant. The Notes of Volume 7, Section 3.14, ask whether only a finite number of the translations can be nonzero, as in Section 3.18 for three variables. The second question is what can replace the finiteness, if it fails, to give a positive answer to Volume 7, Question 3.13.18.

### The result

We use the setting of Volume 7, Section 3.17. The results fall into three groups.

Proved without a hypothesis:

1. The closed form, the independence of the free coordinates and the criterion (★★) extend to two free coordinates (Volume 7, Lemmas 3.17.1 and 3.17.2 and Proposition 3.17.3). Each direction $\lambda$ of the translations with an infinite number of nonzero $t^\lambda_j$ gives a pseudo Cauchy sequence of transcendental type (Volume 7, Proposition 3.17.4). The property that only a finite number of the translations are nonzero does not depend on the free coordinates (Volume 7, Lemma 3.17.6).
2. For $p \ge 3$ the points $x^2 y^{p-2} z^{p-2} + F$ with each monomial of $F$ divisible by $y^p z^p$ keep $\rho = 1$ exactly for the translations $(0, b_m)$, with each $b_m \in K$ (Volume 7, Proposition 3.17.8). For $F = w y^p z^p$ and $F = x w y^p z^p$ no point of the run is a product of a point in three variables with a line (Volume 7, Lemma 3.17.11). So the answer to the question of Section 3.14 is negative (Volume 7, Corollary 3.17.12).
3. At a cylindrical point the run is a run in three variables, and if the function is a polynomial in the free coordinate, Section 3.18 gives the corner curves (Volume 7, Theorem 3.17.13 and Corollary 3.17.14). Each point $\operatorname{cl} \Phi(\gamma_1 x + \gamma_2 w, y, z)$ becomes cylindrical after a finite number of steps, and for $\Phi$ of degree $1$ no run keeps $\rho \ge 1$ (Volume 7, Theorem 3.17.15 and Corollary 3.17.17).
4. The order of $G$ along a corner surface $V(X, z)$ is at most $\max(1, \operatorname{mult}_\ell h)$, where $h$ is a binary initial form of degree less than $p$ and $\ell$ is the linear part of $X$ (Volume 7, Lemma 3.17.19). For $p \ge 5$ the point $x w (x + w) y^{p-3} z^{p-3}$ has $\rho = 2$, the run with all translations $0$ keeps it, and each corner surface has $\nu^G \le 1$ (Volume 7, Theorem 3.17.21). The point $y^{p-4} z^{p-4} (x^4 + y z w^3)$ is a second example (Volume 7, Example 3.17.22). So Conjecture L+, which asks for corner surfaces with $\nu^G \ge \rho$, is false. In both examples the corner curves have $\nu^G \ge \rho$.
5. A point of type $0$ with $1 \le \rho < p$ cannot be the point before a change of the letter (Volume 7, Theorem 3.17.27 and Corollary 3.17.28). So there is no corner run from the points of $K[[y, z]][x^p, w^p]$ with $1 \le \rho < p$, and none from the points $y^{pA} z^{pB} (y x^p + z w^p + B_0)$ (Volume 7, Theorem 3.17.29).
6. If the initial form of $G$ is a binary form $h$ of degree $d$ with $2 \le d < p$ and a nonzero translation $u$ keeps $\rho$, then $h = c \ell^d$ with $\ell(u) = 0$, and this form stays in the initial form after the steps along $\ell$ (Volume 7, Proposition 3.17.30 and Lemma 3.17.31).

Proved for $\deg_{x, w} g < p$:

1. Under the hypothesis H1 (Volume 7, Hypothesis 3.17.32), which asks that the initial form of $G_m$ is binary for $m$ large, each corner run with $\deg_{x, w} g < p$ is linear, that is, the translations of one free coordinate are $0$ from some point on, and the run lies on the strict transforms of a regular hypersurface (Volume 7, Theorem 3.17.33).
2. For a linear run with an infinite number of nonzero translations and $\deg_{x, w} g < p$, there is a free coordinate $x$ with the translations $0$ such that $\operatorname{in} G_m = c\, x_m^d$ with $2 \le d = \rho + 1 < p$, the corner curves have $\nu^G = d$ and the corner surfaces have $\nu^G \ge \rho$ for $m$ large (Volume 7, Theorem 3.18.6 and Corollary 3.18.7).
3. For a planar run, that is, a run whose late translations do not lie on one line, and for each $f$ of degree less than $p$, the value $v(f(\varphi_m, \psi_m))$ is constant for $m$ large and $f(\varphi_\infty, \psi_\infty) \ne 0$ (Volume 7, Theorem 3.18.11). This is the conjecture T of Volume 7, Remark 3.17.34, for these $f$.
4. There is no planar corner run with $1 \le \deg_{x, w} g < p$ (Volume 7, Theorem 3.18.17). So the hypothesis H1 and Conjectures L and L° hold for each corner run with $\deg_{x, w} g < p$, and Theorem 3.17.33 holds without the hypothesis (Volume 7, Corollary 3.18.18).
5. For each degree, a corner run satisfies Conjecture L if and only if it is not planar, and planarity does not depend on the free coordinates (Volume 7, Theorem 3.18.19).

Proved for $\deg_{x, w} g \ge p$:

1. For a planar run and each nonzero $f$ of any degree, $f(\varphi_\infty, \psi_\infty) \ne 0$, its value is that of $f(\varphi_m, \psi_m)$ for $m$ large, and the dominant part of $f$ at the limit is additive (Volume 7, Theorem 3.18.26 and Corollary 3.18.27). This is the conjecture T for the planar runs in each degree.
2. There is no planar corner run if $\deg_{x, w} g \le p$, if the derivatives $g^{[p^e, 0]}$ and $g^{[0, p^e]}$ with $e \ge 1$ do not depend on $x$ and $w$, or if $g \in K[[y, z]][x^p, w^p]$. So Conjecture L holds for these $g$ (Volume 7, Theorems 3.18.34 and 3.18.35 and Corollary 3.18.36).
3. For $g \in K[[y, z]][x^p, w^p]$, in particular for $g = B_1 x^p + B_2 w^p + B_0$ of each rank, Conjecture L° holds, and for the runs with an infinite number of nonzero translations $\operatorname{in} G_m = c\, x_m'^{\,d}$ with $d = \rho$ and Conjecture L+ holds (Volume 7, Theorem 3.18.37 and Corollary 3.18.38).
4. There is no planar corner run for each prime $p$ and each clean $g \in K[[y, z]][x, w]$ with $\deg_{x, w} g \ge 1$. This is Conjecture P (Volume 7, Theorem 3.19.6). So each corner run of a clean $g$ is linear, and Conjecture L holds for each clean $g$ in each free coordinate system (Volume 7, Corollary 3.19.7). The proof applies the results of item 2 again at each later point of the run and compares the two runs.
5. For $p = 2$ and $\deg_{x, w} g = 3$, the hypothesis H1 and Conjecture L° hold for the linear runs whose function has no monomial $x'^2 w'$, $w'^2$ or $w'^3$ in adapted coordinates (Volume 7, Theorem 3.19.11).
6. For each prime $p$ and each $\lambda \in K$, the clean polynomial $g = Y x^p (\lambda x + w)$, with $Y$ a monomial in $y, z$ of class $0$, has linear corner runs with $\rho = p$ and an infinite number of nonzero translations of $w$, and at each point $\operatorname{in} G_m = x_m^p (\lambda x_m + w_m)$. For $\lambda = 0$ this is not a power of a line. So the strong form of H1, $\operatorname{in} G_m = c\, x_m'^{\,d}$ for the linear runs, is false in each degree at least $p + 1$, and Volume 7, Proposition 3.17.30 does not extend to $d = p + 1$ (Volume 7, Theorem 3.19.14, Example 3.19.15 and Corollary 3.19.16).
7. For each prime $p$, the clean polynomial $g = y x^p + y^p z^{p-1} w$ has linear corner runs with $\rho = p$ and an infinite number of nonzero translations of $w$, and each corner surface has $\nu^G \le p - 1 < \rho$. For $p = 2$ the cubic $y x^2 + y^2 z w + x^3$, which lies in the class of item 5, has the same property with $\rho = 2$. So Conjecture L+ is false also for the linear runs (Volume 7, Theorem 3.19.17 and Corollary 3.19.18).
8. At a point before a nonzero translation $u$ that keeps $\rho$, the initial form of $G$ is in a short list that depends only on the degree, $p$ and $\rho$, and for $p = 2$ in degree $3$ each such point has $\rho = 2$ (Volume 7, Proposition 3.19.19 and Corollary 3.19.20).

Conjectures: Conjecture L (Volume 7, Conjecture 3.17.18) asks that each corner run in four variables lies eventually on the strict transforms of a regular hypersurface $V(x')$. Conjecture L° (Volume 7, Conjecture 3.17.24) asks in addition that the corner curves $V(x'_m, w'_m, y_m)$ and $V(x'_m, w'_m, z_m)$ have $\nu^G \ge \rho$ for $m$ large. Conjecture L° implies a positive answer to Volume 7, Question 3.13.18 for these runs. By item 4 of the last list, Conjecture L holds for each clean $g \in K[[y, z]][x, w]$. Conjecture L° and the hypothesis H1 are open for $\deg_{x, w} g \ge p$ outside $K[[y, z]][x^p, w^p]$, and the open part is in Volume 7, Conjectures 3.19.21 and 3.19.22. The form of H1 with a power of a line, the conjecture of the earlier editions, is false in degree at least $p + 1$ by item 6 of the last list. Conjecture P and Conjecture L for power series in $x, w$, and for $g \in K[[x, y, z]]$, are open, since the proof uses the finite degree.

In each known example, Volume 7, Question 3.13.18 has a positive answer: for the origin runs by Volume 7, Theorem 3.14.7, at the cylindrical points by Corollary 3.17.14, for the class of one linear form by Theorem 3.17.15, for the family of Proposition 3.17.8, for the two counterexamples to Conjecture L+, and for the families of Volume 7, Theorems 3.19.14 and 3.19.17, where the corner curves have $\nu^G \ge \rho$.

### The proof in short

The closed form is the one of Section 3.17, with one sum for each free coordinate. Both coordinates are multiplied by the same monomial at each step, so the proofs of Volume 7, Section 3.15, apply. The value group depends only on the letters, so for two letters it has the rational rank $2$ for each $n$, and the transcendental type holds one direction at a time.

For the family $x^2 Y + F$ with $Y = y^{p-2} z^{p-2}$, each term of $F$ gives terms whose exponents, after the division by $M_m^2 Y$, are at least $E_m(A', B') + (i + s - 2) e_m$, so they are divisible by $y_m z_m$. Thus $G_m = x^2 + R$ with $R \in (y_m z_m)$ and $\rho = 1$. A nonzero translation in $x$ makes $\partial_x g$ a monomial times a unit, so $\rho = 0$. To show that no point is cylindrical, take the derivation $\partial / \partial w''$ of a hypothetical coordinate system. It kills $x^2 + R$, and a divisibility argument shows that its coefficients vanish at the point. For the independence of the coordinates, a regular surface $x = \alpha$, $w = \beta$ that contains the run forces $\alpha$ to contain each term $a_i M_{i+1}$, so $\alpha$ would have an infinite number of terms below a fixed value.

For the counterexamples to Conjecture L+, a derivation $D$ with $D(y) = D(z) = 0$ lowers the order along $V(X, z)$ by at most $1$, and the order of a series with a binary initial form $q$ along $V(X, z)$ is at most $\operatorname{mult}_\ell q$. For the points of type $0$, the directrix condition of Volume 7, Theorem 3.7.1, part 1, forces the initial form to be $c z^d$ before a $y$-step and $c y^d$ before a $z$-step, and this form survives the $y$-step. For the binary forms, part 2 of the same theorem gives $D_u h = 0$ for the translation $u$, and since $d < p$ this means that $h$ is a power of the line that vanishes on $u$. Under H1, the unique factorization of the binary forms makes the line the same for all late translations.

For a linear run, the translations of one free coordinate $x$ are $0$, so the coefficient of $x^i$ in $g_m$ depends on one sequence, and the dominance lemma of Volume 7, Section 3.15, makes it a monomial times a unit. A form of this lemma that is uniform in the later translations controls the constant term, and the initial form of $G_m$ becomes $c\, x_m^d$. For a planar run, a linear combination of the two tails with coefficients of finite type has an infinite number of terms below $1 + \theta$, so it is not $0$. The Taylor expansion at $\Phi_m$ and an induction on the degree give the conjecture T for the degrees less than $p$. Then $\partial_x g_m$ and $\partial_w g_m$ become monomials times units, the point becomes cylindrical, and the class $\mu x + B_0$ in three variables has no run.

### The test data

Two independent sets of scripts tested the results over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ for $p = 2, 3, 5, 7$, with no shared code.

1. The closed form agreed with the charts in $180$ of $180$ checks in the first set, and with $0$ mismatches in $120$, $180$, $100$ and $100$ checks in the second set. A version without the cleaning gave $71$ mismatches of $150$, so the test can see errors.
2. Volume 7, Proposition 3.17.8, had no failure at $9000$ points in the first set and at $15200$ points in the second set. With $F = w y^{p-1} z^{p-1}$, which is outside the hypothesis, $\rho$ changed in many runs.
3. Random walks that keep $\rho$ in several classes of low degree found no planar run. Each deep walk with late translations had them in the kernel of one line.
4. The bound of Volume 7, Lemma 3.17.19, had no failure on $45000$ random pairs over $\mathbb{F}_9$, $\mathbb{F}_{25}$ and $\mathbb{F}_{49}$, and Volume 7, Theorem 3.17.21, was checked over five fields with free coordinates that are not linear.
5. In $42$ origin runs of the class $x^2 + c w^2$, no line over $\mathbb{F}_p$ gave corner surfaces with $\nu^G \ge \rho$. All $42$ runs pass with the lines over $\mathbb{F}_{25}$, since $x^2 + c w^2$ has a linear factor there. Walks with translations over $\mathbb{F}_{p^2}$ found no failure of Conjecture L°.

The translations of the tests are in finite fields, the words are random or periodic, and the walks have a finite depth. So the tests are not a proof.

### What the result means

The method of Sections 3.17 and 3.18, which shows that only a finite number of the translations are nonzero, does not extend to four variables: the translations in a direction that $g$ does not see can be arbitrary. What can replace the finiteness is Conjecture L, a regular hypersurface that contains the run. Conjecture L alone does not give the order of $G$ along a subvariety, and the corner surfaces in the hypersurface can have a small order, by the counterexamples to Conjecture L+. The corner curves are the right objects, as for the origin runs of Section 3.16, and Conjecture L° asks for them. For $\deg_{x, w} g < p$ Volume 7, Section 3.18, proves Conjecture L°, so Volume 7, Question 3.13.18 has a positive answer for these runs. The result does not say that $\rho$ drops. The runs with $k \ge 3$ letters and the runs in five or more variables are not treated.
