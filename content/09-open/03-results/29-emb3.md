## Embedded resolution of threefolds as the central open problem

This section reports the twenty-ninth research result, from the track FP (tamnd/singularity#320) and from the review of the whole road in tamnd/singularity#334. It continues Sections 3.24 and 3.25. It answers two questions that the review of the road asked. The first question is whether embedded resolution of 3-folds gives principalization in dimension 4. The answer is yes for the weak form $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ of Definition 3.25.2, if the embedded resolution has an isomorphism clause and a global form, and no for the strong form by this method. The second question is whether embedded local uniformization of 3-folds gives the missing case (O7) of route V. The answer is yes for every valuation without defect, in every multiplicity, and the case of defect reduces to one exact statement (D), which is open. Together the two answers make embedded resolution of 3-folds the single node that carries most of the road.

We keep the conventions of Sections 3.24 and 3.25. The field $k$ is a field of characteristic $p > 0$, and from the subsection on the purely inseparable cover on it is algebraically closed. A variety is a separated scheme of finite type over $k$, and a regular 4-fold is a regular quasi-projective variety of pure dimension 4. All schemes are of finite type over $k$, so they are excellent. A divisor $B = \sum_i B_i$ on a regular variety has *simple normal crossings*, or is *snc*, if each $B_i$ is regular, possibly not connected, and at each point $y$ the equations of the $B_i$ through $y$ are part of a regular system of parameters of $\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}$. For an ideal $I$ we write $I = \mathcal{O}_U(-D) I'$ as in Lemma 3.25.1, so that the non-invertible locus of $I$ is $V(I')$.

### Motivation

In dimension 3, Cutkosky proved principalization on regular 3-folds from order reduction for weak transforms of ideals [Cut09, Theorems 1.3 and 5.6], and the input in dimension 2 was embedded resolution of surfaces. In dimension 4 the review of the road (research note 94) drew a dependency graph with two arrows that no note had proved. The first arrow was $\mathrm{Emb}_3 \Rightarrow \mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$: an embedded resolution of a divisor that contains the generators of an ideal should make the ideal locally monomial, and a combinatorial sequence of blowups should then make it invertible. The second arrow was $\mathrm{ELU}_3 \Rightarrow \text{(O7)}$: at a point where $z^p - a$ has multiplicity less than $p$, a monomial form of $a$ along the valuation should reduce the problem to the binomial case $z^p - u x^\alpha$.

Both arrows look standard, and a student may think that they need no proof. This section shows that each of them hides a point that matters. For the first arrow the point is the isomorphism clause: route J, as Proposition 77.13 of note 77 gives it, does not control the locus where the divisor already has simple normal crossings, and without this control only a very weak principalization comes out. For the second arrow the point is the defect: the binomial case with an exponent divisible by $p$ is not an end case, since it contains every instance of the problem, and the loop that improves the approximation stops exactly when the valuation has no defect.

The section has two parts. The first part, from Definition 3.29.1 to the list of gaps, is about principalization. The second part, from Definition 3.29.13 to Lemma 3.29.28, is about the purely inseparable cover. The last part puts the results on the road map.

### Forms of embedded resolution

Let $Y$ be a regular 4-fold, $E$ an snc divisor on $Y$, and $X \subset Y$ a reduced hypersurface with no component in $E$. An *admissible sequence* for $(Y, X, E)$ is a sequence $Y_r \to \dots \to Y_0 = Y$ with $Y_{i+1} = \mathrm{Bl}_{C_i} Y_i$, where $X_i$ is the strict transform of $X$, $E^{(i)}$ is the strict transform of $E$ plus all exceptional divisors, and $C_i \subseteq X_i$ is regular and has normal crossings with $E^{(i)}$. Then each $Y_i$ is a regular 4-fold and each $E^{(i)}$ is snc. Because $C_i \subseteq X_i$, the total transform of $X \cup E$ in $Y_r$ has the support $X_r \cup E^{(r)}$.

**Definition 3.29.1 (forms of $\mathrm{Emb}_3$).**

1. $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$: each triple $(Y, X, E)$ as above has an admissible sequence with $X_r + E^{(r)}$ snc. This is the form of Proposition 77.13 of note 77, $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ with a boundary.
2. $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$: the same, and in addition each $C_i$ lies in the locus where $X_i + E^{(i)}$ is not snc. Then $Y_r \to Y$ is an isomorphism over the open set where $X + E$ is snc.
3. $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\log}$ and $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\log,\rm iso}$: the same two statements for a reduced divisor $D$ that can have components in $E$. The sequence must be admissible for $(Y, D_0, E)$, where $D_0$ is the union of the components of $D$ that are not in $E$, and at the end the total transform of $D \cup E$ must be snc.
4. $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso,irr}$: the statement $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ only for irreducible $X$.

In each form $Y$ is a global regular 4-fold, not a local ring.

Lean: `Singularity.EmbeddedResolutionThreefoldBoundary` and `Singularity.EmbeddedResolutionThreefoldBoundaryIso` (items 1 and 2, the statements only, without the clauses on blowups). `Singularity.Fidelity.embeddedResolutionThreefoldBoundary_of_iso` proves that item 2 implies item 1

**Definition 3.29.2 (two more forms of principalization).** Let $U$ be a regular 4-fold and $I$ a nonzero coherent ideal on $U$.

1. $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm bl}$: there is a composite $\sigma \colon W \to U$ of blowups in regular centers, each of which lies over $V(I')$, with $W$ regular and $I \mathcal{O}_W$ invertible.
2. $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm vw}$: there is a composite $\sigma \colon W \to U$ of blowups in regular centers, with $W$ regular and $I \mathcal{O}_W$ invertible. There is no condition on the locus where $\sigma$ is an isomorphism.

Lean: `Singularity.VeryWeakPrincipalizationFour` (item 2, the statement only, without the clauses on blowups) and `Singularity.WeakPrincipalizationFour` ($\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$). `Singularity.Fidelity.veryWeakPrincipalizationFour_of_weakPrincipalizationFour` proves that $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ implies $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm vw}$

So $\mathrm{Princ}_4 \Rightarrow \mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm bl} \Rightarrow \mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w} \Rightarrow \mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm vw}$. For the middle arrow, a composite of blowups whose centers lie over $V(I')$ is projective, birational, and an isomorphism over $U \setminus V(I')$.

**Lemma 3.29.3.**

1. $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ implies $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\log}$, and $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ implies $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\log,\rm iso}$.
2. $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso,irr}$ implies $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* (1) Let $D$ be reduced and $D_0$ the union of its components that are not in $E$. As sets $D \cup E = D_0 \cup E$, and $D_0$ has no component in $E$. So the reduced divisors $D \cup E$ and $D_0 + E$ are equal, and their non-snc loci are equal. An admissible sequence for $(Y, D_0, E)$ that ends with $(D_0)_r + E^{(r)}$ snc is what $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\log}$ asks for, and the isomorphism clause is the same on both sides.

(2) Let $X = H_1 + \dots + H_s$ with $H_j$ irreducible, and put $B_0 = E$. Suppose that after $j - 1$ steps we have $Y^{(j-1)} \to Y$ and an snc divisor $B_{j-1}$, the sum of the strict transforms of $H_1, \dots, H_{j-1}$ and of $E$ and of all exceptional divisors. The strict transform $\tilde H_j$ is irreducible and reduced. It is not a component of $B_{j-1}$, because an exceptional divisor maps into a center of codimension at least 2, and $H_j$ is not a component of $E$ or of $H_1 + \dots + H_{j-1}$. Apply $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso,irr}$ to $(Y^{(j-1)}, \tilde H_j, B_{j-1})$. Its centers lie in $\tilde H_j \subseteq X_i$ and have normal crossings with the current transform of $B_{j-1}$, which contains the strict transform of $E$ and the exceptional divisors. So the composite sequence is admissible for $(Y, X, E)$. At the end of step $j$ the divisor $\tilde H_j + B_{j-1}$ is snc, and its total transform stays snc under the later blowups, since their centers are regular and have normal crossings with it. After step $s$, $X_r + E^{(r)} = \tilde H_s + B_{s-1}$ is snc.

For the isomorphism clause, let $y \in Y$ be a point where $X + E$ is snc, and assume that steps $1, \dots, j - 1$ are isomorphisms over a neighbourhood $N$ of $y$. Over $N$ the divisor $\tilde H_j + B_{j-1}$ is $H_1 + \dots + H_j + E$, a sum of components of the snc divisor $X + E$, so it is snc. The centers of step $j$ lie in the non-snc locus, so they do not meet the preimage of $N$. By induction the whole sequence is an isomorphism over $N$. $\square$

**Remark 3.29.4 (route J does not give the isomorphism clause).** Proposition 77.13 of note 77 gets $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ from $\mathrm{MD}_3$ and $\mathrm{SNC}_3$, applied to all of $X$ at the same time, and neither step respects the snc locus. Let $Y = \mathbb{A}^4$ with coordinates $(x, y, z, w)$, $X = \{xy = 0\}$ and $E = 0$. Then $X$ is snc, and $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ does nothing. But the maximal multiplicity of $X$ is 2, and $\mathrm{MD}_3$ must blow up centers in the locus $\{x = y = 0\}$ of multiplicity 2 until the multiplicity drops. So the output of Proposition 77.13 is not an isomorphism over the snc locus. Also $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ asks only for $C_i \subseteq X_i$, so its centers can meet the snc locus.

The repair has two parts. First, add to $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ the clause that $C_i$ lies in the non-snc locus of $X_i + E^{(i)}$. Second, use $\mathrm{MD}_3$ only for irreducible $X$, and then use Lemma 3.29.3(2). For irreducible $X$ the points of multiplicity at least 2 are singular points of $X$, so they are in the non-snc locus, and $\mathrm{MD}_3$ is compatible with the isomorphism clause. With the two parts, the proof of Proposition 77.13 gives $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso,irr}$, and so $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$. The test case Problem 77.P3 of note 77 and Example 77.15 satisfy the first part, because there each center lies in the non-snc locus.

### From an ideal to a locally monomial ideal

Let $U$ be a regular 4-fold and $I$ a nonzero coherent ideal on $U$. By Lemma 3.25.1 a sequence of blowups principalizes $I$ if and only if it principalizes $I'$. So in this subsection we replace $I$ by $I'$. Then $V(I)$ has codimension at least 2, and $X := V(I)_{\rm red}$ has dimension at most 2.

**Lemma 3.29.5 (snc cosupport).** There is a composite $\rho \colon U_1 \to U$ of blowups in regular centers over $X$ such that $E := \rho^{-1}(X)_{\rm red}$ is an snc divisor and $\rho$ is an isomorphism over $U \setminus X$. Let $I_1 = I \mathcal{O}_{U_1}$. Then there is an effective divisor $R$ supported on $E$ with $\mathcal{O}_{U_1}(-R) \subseteq I_1$, and $I_1 = \mathcal{O}_{U_1}$ off $E$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* The first part is the embedded resolution of Cossart, Jannsen and Saito for reduced subschemes of dimension at most 2 [CJS20, Corollary 0.4], as in the proof of Proposition 3.25.5. Off $E$, $I_1 = \mathcal{O}_{U_1}$, so $V(I_1) \subseteq E$ and $\sqrt{I_1} \supseteq \mathcal{O}_{U_1}(-E)$. Since $U_1$ is noetherian, $\mathcal{O}_{U_1}(-E)^m = \mathcal{O}_{U_1}(-mE) \subseteq I_1$ for some $m \ge 1$. Put $R = mE$. $\square$

This is possible only after the replacement of $I$ by $I'$. For the original ideal the divisorial part $\rho^* D$ need not be supported on $E$.

**Lemma 3.29.6 (generators, with Bertini).** Assume that $k$ is infinite and that $U_1 \setminus E$ is smooth over $k$. Then there are a line bundle $M$ on $U_1$ and global sections $t_1, \dots, t_r$ of $I_1 \otimes M$ that generate $I_1 \otimes M$, with the following property. Let $H_j$ be the union of the components of $\operatorname{div}(t_j)$ that are not in $E$. Then $H_1, \dots, H_r$ have no common components, and $H_1 + \dots + H_r$ is reduced and snc on $U_1 \setminus E$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* The variety $U_1$ is quasi-projective because $\rho$ is projective. Let $L$ be a very ample line bundle on $U_1$, and choose $N_1$ such that $I_1 \otimes L^{N_1}$ and $L^{N_1}(-R)$ are generated by global sections. This is possible for coherent sheaves on a quasi-projective variety: extend the sheaf to a projective closure and apply the theorem of Serre [Har, Theorem II.5.17]. Put $M = L^{N_1 + 1}$. Let $g_1, \dots, g_s$ generate $I_1 \otimes L^{N_1}$, and multiply them by sections of $L$ with no common zero. This gives sections $g'_1, \dots, g'_{s'}$ that generate $I_1 \otimes M$. Let $u_1, \dots, u_a$ generate $L^{N_1}(-R)$, and let $f_0, \dots, f_b$ be a basis of a subspace of $H^0(L)$ that gives an immersion. By Lemma 3.29.5 the products $u_\alpha f_\beta$ are sections of $\mathcal{O}(-R) \otimes M \subseteq I_1 \otimes M$. Let $V \subseteq H^0(U_1, I_1 \otimes M)$ be the span of the $g'_i$ and of the $u_\alpha f_\beta$.

On $U_1 \setminus E$ we have $I_1 \otimes M = M$ and $\mathcal{O}(-R) = \mathcal{O}$. The $u_\alpha$ have no common zero there, and the $f_\beta$ give an immersion. So the map that the $u_\alpha f_\beta$ give is the composite of the map $(\phi_u, \phi_f)$ to $\mathbb{P}^{a-1} \times \mathbb{P}^b$ and the Segre embedding. It is an immersion, because $\phi_f$ is an immersion and the graph of $\phi_u$ is a closed immersion. This immersion is the map of $V$ followed by a linear projection, so the map of $V$ is also an immersion on $U_1 \setminus E$: if $g \circ f$ is an immersion and $g$ is separated, then $f$ is an immersion.

Now apply the theorem of Bertini to the linear system $V$, which has no base points on the smooth variety $U_1 \setminus E$ and gives an immersion. For a general $t \in V$ the divisor $\operatorname{div}(t) \setminus E$ is smooth. Apply this again to the restriction of $V$ to each stratum of the divisors already chosen. Each stratum is smooth, and the restricted map is still an immersion. By induction, for $(t_1, \dots, t_r)$ in a dense open subset of $V^r$, the divisors $\operatorname{div}(t_j) \setminus E$ are smooth and meet transversally, so their sum is reduced and snc on $U_1 \setminus E$. This is the theorem of Bertini over an infinite field, in the form of Jouanolou for immersions, and [Har, Theorem II.8.18] in the projective case. Take $r = \dim V$. A general $(t_1, \dots, t_r)$ is a basis of $V$, so the $t_j$ generate the same subsheaf as $V$. This is $I_1 \otimes M$, because $V$ contains the $g'_i$. $\square$

Without the isomorphism clause Lemma 3.29.6 is not needed: any generators of $I \otimes M$ will do, over any field (Theorem 3.29.12).

**Lemma 3.29.7 (the ideal becomes locally monomial).** Assume $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$, and let $t_j$ and $H_j$ be as in Lemma 3.29.6. Put $H = H_1 + \dots + H_r$. Apply $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ to $(U_1, H, E)$, and let $\pi \colon Y' \to U_1$ be the result and $B' = \pi^{-1}(H \cup E)$ its total transform, an snc divisor. Then:

1. $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $U_1 \setminus E$, so each center of $\pi$ lies over $X$;
2. with $\mathcal{J} = I_1 \mathcal{O}_{Y'}$, $D_j = \operatorname{div}(\pi^* t_j)$ and $R_0 = \pi^* R$,
$$\mathcal{J} = \mathcal{O}_{Y'}(-R_0) + \mathcal{O}_{Y'}(-D_1) + \dots + \mathcal{O}_{Y'}(-D_r),$$
and $R_0, D_1, \dots, D_r$ are supported in $B'$. So $\mathcal{J}$ is *locally monomial*: at each point $y \in Y'$, if $x_1, \dots, x_e$ are the equations of the components of $B'$ through $y$, then $\mathcal{J}_y$ is generated by monomials in $x_1, \dots, x_e$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* (1) By Lemma 3.29.6 the divisor $H + E$ is snc near each point of $U_1 \setminus E$, so its non-snc locus lies in $E$. The centers of $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ lie in the non-snc locus of the current divisor, and the argument at the end of the proof of Lemma 3.29.3(2) shows that $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $U_1 \setminus E$.

(2) The $t_j$ generate $I_1 \otimes M$, so the $\pi^* t_j$ generate $\mathcal{J} \otimes \pi^* M$, and this is the statement $\mathcal{J} = \sum_j \mathcal{O}(-D_j)$. The term $\mathcal{O}(-R_0)$ is in $\mathcal{J}$ by Lemma 3.29.5, so it changes nothing. On $U_1$, $\operatorname{div}(t_j) = H_j + V_j$ with $V_j$ supported in $E$. So $D_j = \pi^* H_j + \pi^* V_j$ has support in $\pi^{-1}(H \cup E) = B'$, and the same holds for $R_0$. The ring $\mathcal{O}_{Y',y}$ is factorial and the prime divisors through $y$ in $B'$ are the $(x_i)$, so $\mathcal{O}(-D_j)_y = (x^a)$ for an exponent vector $a$. $\square$

**Example 3.29.8 (one hypersurface does not suffice).** The divisor that Lemma 3.29.7 resolves is the reduced divisor of the product $t_1 \cdots t_r$ plus $E$. One element of $I$ is not enough. Let $U = \mathbb{A}^4$ and $I = (x, y)$, and let $f = x + y^2$, which is a general element of $I$ of degree 2. The hypersurface $V(f)$ is already smooth, so an embedded resolution of $V(f)$ with $E = 0$ does nothing, and $I$ is not invertible. The resolution of one hypersurface makes $f \mathcal{O}$ monomial, but $I \mathcal{O} \supseteq f \mathcal{O}$ need not be monomial. Here one blowup of the plane $\{x = y = 0\}$ makes $I$ invertible, and the divisor of $xy$ is the divisor that Lemma 3.29.7 would resolve with the generators $x, y$. On the other side, order reduction for ideals in the sense of Hironaka is not needed for the weak form. Lemma 3.29.7 uses only embedded resolution of divisors, and the next step is combinatorial.

### Principalization of locally monomial ideals

Let $Y$ be a regular variety with an snc divisor $B = \sum_{i \in \Lambda} B_i$, with $B_i$ regular and possibly not connected. Write a divisor supported on $B$ as $D = \sum_i d_i B_i$. When we blow up a regular center $C$ that has normal crossings with $B$, the new snc divisor is the sum of the strict transforms of the $B_i$ and of the exceptional divisor $F$, which gets a new label. The method is that of Goward [Gow05]. We give a complete proof of the case that we need, the case of two divisors.

**Lemma 3.29.9 (two divisors).** Let $D$ and $D'$ be effective divisors supported on $B$, and put $\mathcal{K} = \mathcal{O}_Y(-D) + \mathcal{O}_Y(-D')$. There is a finite sequence of blowups $Y_n \to \dots \to Y_0 = Y$ such that:

1. each center is $B^{(l)}_i \cap B^{(l)}_j$ for two labels $i \ne j$ of the current snc divisor $B^{(l)}$, so it is regular and has normal crossings with $B^{(l)}$;
2. each center lies in the non-invertible locus of $\mathcal{K} \mathcal{O}_{Y_l}$, and in $\operatorname{Supp} D^{(l)} \cap \operatorname{Supp} D'^{(l)}$, where $D^{(l)}$ and $D'^{(l)}$ are the pullbacks;
3. $\mathcal{K} \mathcal{O}_{Y_n}$ is invertible.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Put $G = \min(D, D')$, coefficient by coefficient, $A = D - G = \sum_i \alpha_i B_i$ and $A' = D' - G = \sum_i \beta_i B_i$. Then $\alpha_i \beta_i = 0$ for each $i$, and $\mathcal{K} = \mathcal{O}(-G) \bigl(\mathcal{O}(-A) + \mathcal{O}(-A')\bigr)$.

*The non-invertible locus.* Let $y \in Y$, and let $x_i$ be the equations of the $B_i$ through $y$. Then $\mathcal{K}_y = x^g (x^\alpha, x^\beta)$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are the exponent vectors on the $B_i$ through $y$, and their supports are disjoint. A monomial ideal $(x^\alpha, x^\beta)$ in a regular local ring is principal if and only if one monomial divides the other. Indeed, by Nakayama a principal ideal with two generators is generated by one of them, and $x^\alpha \in (x^\beta)$ means $\beta \le \alpha$, since the $x_i$ are distinct primes of the factorial ring $\mathcal{O}_{Y,y}$. With disjoint supports this means $\alpha = 0$ or $\beta = 0$ at $y$. So the non-invertible locus of $\mathcal{K}$ is the union of the sets $B_i \cap B_j$ over the *bad pairs*, the pairs $(i, j)$ with $\alpha_i > 0$, $\beta_j > 0$ and $B_i \cap B_j \ne \emptyset$. The *weight* of a bad pair is $\alpha_i + \beta_j$.

*The step.* If there is no bad pair, $\mathcal{K}$ is invertible. If there is one, choose a bad pair $(i, j)$ of maximal weight $S$ and blow up $C = B_i \cap B_j$. It is regular of codimension 2 and a stratum of $B$, so it has normal crossings with $B$. It lies in the non-invertible locus and in $\operatorname{Supp} A \cap \operatorname{Supp} A' \subseteq \operatorname{Supp} D \cap \operatorname{Supp} D'$. This gives (1) and (2). Let $F$ be the exceptional divisor. For $l \ne i, j$, no component of $C$ lies in $B_l$, because $B_i \cap B_j \cap B_l$ has codimension 3. So $\pi^* B_i = \tilde B_i + F$, $\pi^* B_j = \tilde B_j + F$ and $\pi^* B_l = \tilde B_l$ for $l \ne i, j$. Since $\alpha_j = \beta_i = 0$, the coefficients of $F$ in $\pi^* A$ and $\pi^* A'$ are $\alpha_i$ and $\beta_j$. After we subtract the new common part, the new divisors $A_1$ and $A'_1$ have the old coefficients on the $\tilde B_l$. On $F$ the coefficient is $\alpha_i - \beta_j$ in $A_1$ and $0$ in $A'_1$ if $\alpha_i \ge \beta_j$, and $0$ in $A_1$ and $\beta_j - \alpha_i$ in $A'_1$ if $\beta_j > \alpha_i$.

*The new bad pairs.* First, $\tilde B_i \cap \tilde B_j = \emptyset$, because the blowup of the intersection of two transversal regular divisors separates their strict transforms. Second, for a pair $(l, m)$ of old labels other than $(i, j)$, $\tilde B_l \cap \tilde B_m \subseteq \pi^{-1}(B_l \cap B_m)$, so the pair is bad only if $(l, m)$ was bad, and then its weight is the same. Third, the pairs with $F$. Suppose $\alpha_i > \beta_j$. The other case is symmetric, and if $\alpha_i = \beta_j$ then $F$ is in no bad pair. Then $F$ is on the side of $A$, and a bad pair $(F, m)$ needs $\beta_m > 0$ and $F \cap \tilde B_m \ne \emptyset$. The second condition implies $C \cap B_m \ne \emptyset$, so $B_i \cap B_m \ne \emptyset$ and $(i, m)$ was a bad pair, of weight $\alpha_i + \beta_m \le S = \alpha_i + \beta_j$. So $\beta_m \le \beta_j$, and the weight of $(F, m)$ is
$$\alpha_i - \beta_j + \beta_m \le \alpha_i \le S - \beta_j < S.$$

*Termination.* Let $\mu$ be the finite multiset of the weights of the bad pairs. One step removes one element $S$, the pair $(i, j)$, keeps or removes the other elements, and adds a finite number of elements less than $S$. So $\mu$ decreases strictly in the multiset order on $\mathbb{N}$, which is well founded by the theorem of Dershowitz and Manna. So the process stops, and at the end there is no bad pair, which is (3). $\square$

**Corollary 3.29.10 (merging, with control of the centers).** In the situation of Lemma 3.29.7 there is a composite $\psi \colon W \to Y'$ of blowups in regular centers such that $\mathcal{J} \mathcal{O}_W$ is invertible, each center lies over $\operatorname{Supp} R_0 \subseteq \pi^{-1}(E)$, and $W$ is a regular 4-fold.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Put $G_0 = R_0$. Suppose that we have $\psi_{k-1} \colon W_{k-1} \to Y'$ such that $\mathcal{O}(-R_0) + \mathcal{O}(-D_1) + \dots + \mathcal{O}(-D_{k-1})$ pulls back to an invertible ideal $\mathcal{O}(-G_{k-1})$, with $G_{k-1}$ supported on the current snc divisor, the transform of $B'$, and with $G_{k-1} \le \psi_{k-1}^* R_0$. The inequality holds because $\mathcal{O}(-\psi_{k-1}^* R_0) \subseteq \mathcal{O}(-G_{k-1})$. Apply Lemma 3.29.9 to $G_{k-1}$ and $\psi_{k-1}^* D_k$. Its centers lie in $\operatorname{Supp} G_{k-1} \subseteq \operatorname{Supp} \psi_{k-1}^* R_0$. After it, $\mathcal{O}(-G_{k-1}) + \mathcal{O}(-D_k)$ pulls back to an invertible ideal $\mathcal{O}(-G_k)$ with the same properties. After the step $k = r$ the pullback of $\mathcal{J} = \mathcal{O}(-R_0) + \sum_j \mathcal{O}(-D_j)$ is invertible. The centers are strata of snc divisors, so they are regular, and the blowup of a regular variety in a regular center is regular. Blowups keep quasi-projectivity. $\square$

The order of the merges matters. If we merge $D_k$ and $D_l$ directly, Lemma 3.29.9 blows up components of $\tilde H_k \cap \tilde H_l$, and these lie over $U_1 \setminus E$, where $I_1$ is already invertible. If we merge each $D_k$ into the running divisor $G_{k-1} \le \psi^* R_0$, all centers stay over $E$.

### The two theorems on principalization

**Theorem 3.29.11 ($\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ gives $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm bl}$).** Assume that $k$ is infinite and that each regular 4-fold over $k$ is smooth over $k$. This holds, for example, if $k$ is perfect and infinite. Assume $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ over $k$, or only $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso,irr}$. ### The four steps in dimension 4 and the gaps

The review of the road asked us to check four steps. In dimension 3, Cutkosky does not use this argument for principalization: he gets it from order reduction for weak transforms of ideals [Cut09, Theorems 1.3 and 5.6]. The route "embedded resolution of a divisor, then a locally monomial ideal, then combinatorial blowups" is the standard way to get weak principalization from embedded resolution of divisors. Here is the status of each step in dimension 4 and characteristic $p$.

1. *Resolve a divisor that contains the generators.* This is Lemma 3.29.7. The divisor must be the divisor of the product of a set of generators (Example 3.29.8). For the isomorphism clause, Lemma 3.29.5 and Lemma 3.29.6 make the divisor snc off the cosupport, and Lemma 3.29.6 needs the theorem of Bertini on $U_1 \setminus E$.
2. *Reduce to a locally monomial ideal.* This is Lemma 3.29.7(2). It uses only that $B'$ is snc and that the local rings of $Y'$ are factorial. Normal crossings that are not simple would also do locally, but Lemma 3.29.9 uses regular components so that its centers $B_i \cap B_j$ are regular. If an embedded resolution gives only normal crossings, the blowups of the strata of self-intersection, which lie in the non-snc locus, make them simple.
3. *Principalize by combinatorial blowups.* This is Lemma 3.29.9 and Corollary 3.29.10, in each characteristic. The centers are strata of codimension 2 of an snc divisor. Nothing depends on the characteristic, because the whole computation is with exponent vectors.
4. *Keep the global structure.* Each step is global. Lemma 3.29.5 is global by the theorem of Cossart, Jannsen and Saito, Lemma 3.29.7 is global because $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ is assumed in its global form, and Lemma 3.29.9 is defined by global divisors and a global choice of a bad pair: for a bad pair of maximal weight the whole set $B_i \cap B_j$ is the center. So no patching is needed.

The following list gives the gaps between these steps and the strong statement.

- (G1) *The isomorphism clause in $\mathrm{Emb}_3$.* Route J gives $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ and not $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ (Remark 3.29.4). With $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ alone the result is $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm vw}$ (Theorem 3.29.12). The repair is to add to $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ that its centers lie in the non-snc locus, and to use Lemma 3.29.3(2). The project expects that the method of Cossart, Jannsen and Saito for $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ in Problem 77.P3 of note 77 respects this, because their centers lie in the non-snc locus of the boundary, but this is not checked.
- (G2) *A global, not a local, $\mathrm{Emb}_3$.* Theorem 3.29.11 needs $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ for the global triple $(U_1, H, E)$. A local or valuative form, such as embedded local uniformization, is not enough: one would have to glue the local principalizations, and this is item 2 of the open list of Section 3.25. Given the global form, the points that are not closed cause no problem, because Lemma 3.29.9 treats all points at the same time.
- (G3) *Bertini.* Lemma 3.29.6 needs $k$ infinite and $U \setminus V(I')$ smooth. For a finite field the theorem of Poonen gives smooth hypersurface sections of large degree, and a version with the extra condition that the $t_j$ generate $I_1 \otimes M$ probably holds, but it is not checked. For an imperfect field, where regular does not imply smooth, no theorem of Bertini for regularity that gives Lemma 3.29.6 is known to us. Without the isomorphism clause none of this matters (Theorem 3.29.12). Over the algebraically closed field of Section 3.24 the gap does not occur.
- (G4) *The strong form does not come out.* The centers of Lemma 3.29.5 lie over $V(I')$, but they need not lie in the non-invertible locus of the transform of $I$. The centers of Lemma 3.29.7 lie in the non-snc locus of $H + E$, which is in general larger than $V(I_1)$. The centers of Corollary 3.29.10 lie in the non-invertible locus of the ideal $\mathcal{O}(-G_{k-1}) + \mathcal{O}(-D_k)$, which can be larger than that of $\mathcal{J}$. Lemma 3.24.11 needs the strong form, because its proof follows the centers one at a time. The natural tool for the strong form is order reduction for weak transforms of ideals, as in the proof of Cutkosky in dimension 3. Its analogue in dimension 4 contains $\mathrm{MD}_3$ for principal ideals, so the strong form is at least as hard as $\mathrm{MD}_3$.

The case of components of the boundary in the divisor, which the review of the road listed as a possible gap, is not a gap, by Lemma 3.29.3(1).

### The purely inseparable cover

From now on $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $p > 0$. All rings are noetherian, and *essentially of finite type* means a localization of a finitely generated $k$-algebra. For a regular local ring $A$ with a regular system of parameters $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ and $\alpha \in \mathbb{Z}^n$ we write $x^\alpha = \prod_i x_i^{\alpha_i}$, and we write $\alpha \equiv 0$ if $p$ divides each $\alpha_i$.

**Definition 3.29.13.**

1. An *instance* of $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ (Conjecture 3.24.2) is a triple $(A, a, \nu)$: a regular local ring $A$, essentially of finite type over $k$, with fraction field $L$ of transcendence degree 4, an element $a \in A$ with $b = a^{1/p} \notin L$, and a valuation $\nu$ of $L(b)/k$ that dominates $A$. A *solution* is a regular local ring $A' \subset L(b)$, essentially of finite type, with fraction field $L(b)$, dominated by $\nu$, with $A[b] \subseteq A'$.
2. (O7) is $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ for the instances where the multiplicity of $A[z]/(z^p - a)$ at its closed point is less than $p$. This is problem (O7) of note 82, the missing case of route V.
3. $\mathrm{ELU}_3$, embedded local uniformization of 3-folds, says: for each regular local ring $A$ of dimension 4, essentially of finite type over $k$, with fraction field $L$, each valuation $\mu$ of $L/k$ that dominates $A$, and each $f \in A \setminus \{0\}$, there are a regular local ring $A_1 \supseteq A$, essentially of finite type, with fraction field $L$, dominated by $\mu$, and a regular system of parameters $x$ of $A_1$ with $f = \text{unit} \cdot x^\alpha$.

The closed point of $A[b] = A[z]/(z^p - a)$ lies over $z = c$, where $c \in k$ and $c^p$ is the residue of $a$. The multiplicity there is $\min(p, \operatorname{ord}_A(a - \tilde c^{\,p}))$ for each lift $\tilde c \in A$ of $c$. Indeed, put $z' = z - \tilde c$. Then $z^p - a = z'^p - (a - \tilde c^{\,p})$, and the order of this element of $A[z']_{(\mathfrak{m}, z')}$ is $\min(p, \operatorname{ord}(a - \tilde c^{\,p}))$. A change of the lift changes $a - \tilde c^{\,p}$ by an element of $\mathfrak{m}^p$, so the minimum does not change.

**Lemma 3.29.14 (moves).** Let $(A, a, \nu)$ be an instance.

1. Let $A_1 \supseteq A$ be a regular local ring, essentially of finite type, with fraction field $L$ and dominated by $\nu$. Then a solution of $(A_1, a, \nu)$ is a solution of $(A, a, \nu)$.
2. For $d \in A$, a solution of $(A, a - d^p, \nu)$ is a solution of $(A, a, \nu)$.
3. For $c \in A$ with $c^p \mid a$, a solution of $(A, a / c^p, \nu)$ is a solution of $(A, a, \nu)$.
4. To solve $(A, a, \nu)$ we can assume that $\nu$ is zero dimensional, that is, that its residue field is $k$.

*Proof.* (1) $A[b] \subseteq A_1[b] \subseteq A'$. (2) $(a - d^p)^{1/p} = b - d$ and $A[b - d] = A[b]$. (3) $A[b] \subseteq A[b / c]$. In (2) and (3) the new $p$-th root is not in $L$.

### The defectless case

Fix an instance $(A, a, \nu)$ with $\nu$ zero dimensional. The extension $L(b)/L$ is purely inseparable, so $\nu|_L$ has only one extension, and $p = e f d$, where $e$ is the ramification index, $f$ the residue degree and $d$ the defect (Ostrowski; see Volume 3, Chapter 3). Put
$$\Delta = \{\nu(b - c) : c \in L\},$$
the set of the values of the distance of $b$ to $L$.

*Proof.* (1) $p \nu(b - c) = \nu(a - c^p) \in \Gamma_L$. So if $\nu(b - c) \notin \Gamma_L$, then $[\Gamma_{L(b)} : \Gamma_L] \ge p$, and $e = p$. ### The binomial case and the missing statement

By $\mathrm{ELU}_3$ each instance becomes binomial: $a = u x^\alpha$ in some $A_2$. There are two cases.

*The case $\alpha \not\equiv 0$.* Lemma 3.29.16 solves it for each zero dimensional $\nu$. It does not use $\mathrm{ELU}_3$, and it has no condition on the rank, the rational rank or the defect.

*The case $\alpha \equiv 0$.* This case contains every instance. Indeed $a = x^{p\beta} u$, and by Lemma 3.29.14(3) the instance is equivalent to $(A_2, u, \nu)$. With $c_0$ as in the proof of Theorem 3.29.19, Lemma 3.29.14(2) makes it $(A_2, u - c_0^p, \nu)$, and $u - c_0^p$ is an arbitrary element of $\mathfrak{m}_2$. Conversely, for each instance $(A, a, \nu)$ with $a \in \mathfrak{m}$, the binomial instance $(A, 1 + a, \nu)$ has $(1 + a)^{1/p} = 1 + b$ and the same solutions. So "$z^p - u$ with $u$ a unit that is not a $p$-th power" is the general problem, and not an end case.

So the binomial case with $\alpha \equiv 0$ forces a change of the approximation $c$ of $b$. This gives a loop. Start with $c_0 = 0$ and $A^{(0)} = A$. Given $c_n$ and $A^{(n)}$, apply $\mathrm{ELU}_3$ to $a - c_n^p$, and get $a - c_n^p = u x^\alpha$ in a ring $A^{(n)}_+$. If $\alpha \not\equiv 0$, stop and apply Lemma 3.29.16. If $\alpha = p\beta$, put $c_{n+1} = c_n + c'_0 x^\beta$ with $c'^{\,p}_0 \equiv u$, and $A^{(n+1)} = A^{(n)}_+$. Then $\nu(b - c_{n+1}) > \nu(b - c_n)$, by the computation in the proof of Theorem 3.29.19. So the values $\nu(b - c_n)$ increase strictly in $\Delta$. Theorem 3.29.19 avoids the question whether the loop stops in the defectless case, because it goes directly to $c^*$. In the case of defect there is no $c^*$, and the loop stops if and only if at some step the exponent is not divisible by $p$. This is the following statement.

**Definition 3.29.21 (the statement (D)).** An instance $(A, a, \nu)$ *satisfies (D)* if there are $c \in L$ with $\nu(c) \ge 0$, a regular local ring $A_1 \supseteq A[c]$, essentially of finite type, with fraction field $L$ and dominated by $\nu$, and a regular system of parameters $x$ of $A_1$, such that $a - c^p = u x^\alpha$ with $u \in A_1^\times$ and $\alpha \not\equiv 0$.

**Proposition 3.29.22.**

1. If $(A, a, \nu)$ satisfies (D), it has a solution. This does not use $\mathrm{ELU}_3$.
2. Assume $\mathrm{ELU}_3$. Then each defectless instance satisfies (D).
3. If (D) fails, then for each $c$ and each $A_1$ on which $a - c^p$ is monomial, the exponent is divisible by $p$, and the loop strictly improves $c$. So the loop never stops, for any choices.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* (1) Lemma 3.29.14(1) and (2) with $d = c$, and then Lemma 3.29.16 in $A_1$. (2) This is the proof of Theorem 3.29.19, which finds $c = c^*$ and $A_1 = A_2$. (3) This is a restatement of the definition and of the loop. $\square$

**Problem 3.29.23.** Does $\mathrm{ELU}_3$ imply (D) for each instance of (O7), or of $\mathrm{PILU}_4$, with defect? Does $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ for an instance imply (D) for it?

Status: open. No argument in either direction is known. A solution $A'$ of $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ is a regular local ring of $L(b)$, while (D) asks for a regular local ring of $L$ over which $L(b)$ is a toric cover of Kummer type. The second statement is the purely inseparable analogue of local monomialization of $A_1 \to A'$, and local monomialization can fail in the separable case with defect (see the subsection on hard valuations below). So the reduction of (O7) to $\mathrm{ELU}_3$ fails exactly at the valuations with defect that do not satisfy (D). No instance where (D) fails is known, and (D) is not proved. Section 3.31 shows that on a fixed model (D) is the end case $\rho = 0$ of Volume 7, Section 3.5, for a boundary chosen on the model (Theorem 3.31.2 and Corollary 3.31.5), and it proves (D) when $\nu|_L$ is discrete of rank one, with or without defect (Proposition 3.31.19). So the first question is open only for the value groups other than $\mathbb{Z}$ (Problem 3.31.21). For the second question, Section 3.31 proves only the case where $A[b]$ is regular (Proposition 3.31.18).

**Example 3.29.24 (defect without an obstruction).** Let $A = k[x, y, u, w]_{(x, y, u, w)}$ and $a = x$. Then $A[b] = A[z]/(z^p - x)$ is regular, with the regular system of parameters $z, y, u, w$. So $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ holds for each $\nu$, and (D) holds with $c = 0$. Choose $z(t), u(t), w(t) \in t k[[t]]$ algebraically independent over $k(t)$. This is possible because $k((t))$ has infinite transcendence degree over $k(t)$. The map $k[z, y, u, w] \to k[[t]]$ with $z \mapsto z(t)$, $y \mapsto t$, $u \mapsto u(t)$ and $w \mapsto w(t)$ is injective, and $\nu = \operatorname{ord}_t$ is a valuation of $L(b) = k(z, y, u, w)$ that dominates $A$. Then $\Gamma_{L(b)} = \Gamma_L = \mathbb{Z}$, because $\nu(y) = 1$, and both residue fields are $k$. So $e = f = 1$ and $d = p$. Defect alone is not an obstruction to $\mathrm{PILU}_4$, nor to (D).

### Worked example: the cusp for p at least 5

This is Example 82.4 of note 82, the counterexample to the step "multiplicity less than $p$ gives a regular normalization" of route V. We show that it is not a counterexample to $\mathrm{PILU}_4$.

**Example 3.29.25.** Let $p \ge 5$, $A = k[x, y, u, w]_{(x, y, u, w)}$ and $a = x^2 + y^3$. Note 82 shows that $A[b]$ is normal, of multiplicity 2, and singular. We show that $(A, a, \nu)$ has a solution for each $\nu$, with no hypothesis.

Blow up the plane $k^2_{x,y}$ three times, as in the standard embedded resolution of the cusp, and take the product with the plane $k^2_{u,w}$. The centers are regular: the plane $\{x = y = 0\}$ and then two planes over it. In the charts that contain the points over the origin:

1. $E_1$: $x = x_1 y$ and $a = y^2 (x_1^2 + y)$, where $y = 0$ is $E_1$.
2. $E_2$: $y = y_2 x_1$ and $a = x_1^3 y_2^2 (x_1 + y_2)$, where $x_1 = 0$ is $E_2$ and $y_2 = 0$ is the strict transform of $E_1$.
3. $E_3$: $x_1 = x_3 y_2$ and $a = x_3^3 y_2^6 (x_3 + 1)$, where $y_2 = 0$ is $E_3$, $x_3 = 0$ is the strict transform of $E_2$, and $x_3 = -1$ is the strict transform $C$ of the curve.

So $E_1$, $E_2$ and $E_3$ have the multiplicities 2, 3 and 6 in the total transform of $a$, which is snc. Let $Y \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ be the composite of the three blowups, and let $A_1$ be the local ring of the center of $\nu|_L$ on $Y$. It exists because $Y \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ is proper. Since $\nu(x) > 0$ and $\nu(y) > 0$, the center lies over $\{x = y = 0\}$, so on $E_1 \cup E_2 \cup E_3$. In $A_1$, $a = \text{unit} \cdot x^\alpha$ for a regular system of parameters, and the exponents are the multiplicities of the components through the center. They are among 1, 2, 3 and 6, and at least one of them is 2, 3 or 6. For $p \ge 5$ none of 2, 3 and 6 is divisible by $p$. So (D) holds with $c = 0$, and Proposition 3.29.22(1) gives a solution.

So the failure in Example 82.4 is a failure of the method of Proposition 77.12 of note 77, and not of $\mathrm{PILU}_4$. For $p = 2$ the multiplicities 2 and 6 are divisible by $p$, and for $p = 3$ the multiplicities 3 and 6 are, so there the argument needs more (Exercise 7). A second proof uses the next proposition, since $a$ comes from a ring of dimension 3.

**Proposition 3.29.26 (instances from dimension 3).** Let $A_3$ be a regular local ring of dimension 3, essentially of finite type over $k$, with fraction field $L_3$, and let $A$ be a localization of $A_3[w]$, with $w$ an indeterminate, at a prime over $\mathfrak{m}_3$. Let $a \in A_3$ with $a^{1/p} \notin L_3(w)$. Then each instance $(A, a, \nu)$ of $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ has a solution.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* The ring $A_3[b]$ is a localization of a finitely generated domain, so it is a local ring of a 3-fold $X$. Let $X' \to X$ be a resolution, proper and birational with $X'$ regular. It exists by the theorem of Cossart and Piltant [CP08, CP09]. Since $\nu_3 = \nu|_{L_3(b)}$ is nonnegative on $A_3[b]$, it has a center on $X$ and, by properness, on $X'$. Its local ring $A'_3$ is regular and contains $A_3[b]$. Now $B = A'_3[w]$ is regular [Mat, Theorem 19.5], lies in $L_3(b)(w) = L(b)$, and $\nu \ge 0$ on $B$. Put $A' = B_{\mathfrak{m}_\nu \cap B}$. An element of $A_3[w]$ outside $\mathfrak{m}_\nu$ is outside $\mathfrak{m}_A$, so it is a unit in $A'$, and $A \subseteq A'$. So $A'$ is a solution. $\square$

### Worked example: an exponent divisible by p below multiplicity p

The hypothesis of (O7) is that the multiplicity is less than $p$. One may hope that this hypothesis keeps all exponents prime to $p$. The next example shows that it does not.

**Example 3.29.27.** Let $p \ge 3$ and $a = x^2 + y^{2p+1}$, of order $2 < p$. Blow up with $x = x_j y$ for $j = 1, \dots, p$. Then $a = y^{2j} (x_j^2 + y^{2p+1-2j})$, so the exceptional divisor $E_j$ has multiplicity $2j$, and after $p$ steps $a = y^{2p} (x_p^2 + y)$. Two more blowups separate the strict transform from $E_p$. In the chart $y = y' x_p$ we get $a = x_p^{2p+1} y'^{2p} (x_p + y')$, so $E_{p+1}$ has multiplicity $2p + 1$. In the chart $x_p = x'' y'$ we get $a = x''^{2p+1} y'^{4p+2} (x'' + 1)$, so $E_{p+2}$ has multiplicity $4p + 2$. The only multiplicity that is divisible by $p$ is $2p$, at $E_p$.

Let $\nu$ have its center at a point of $E_p$ with $y = 0$ and $x_p = \gamma$, $\gamma \ne 0$. No other component of the total transform passes through this point. There $a = y^{2p} u$ with the unit $u = x_p^2 + y$, so $\alpha \equiv 0$, and (D) fails with $c = 0$. Take $c_0 \in k$ with $c_0^p = \gamma^2$ and $c = c_0 y^2 \in A$. Then $a - c^p = y^{2p} (u - \gamma^2)$. With $x' = x_p - \gamma$,
$$u - \gamma^2 = 2\gamma x' + x'^2 + y,$$
and this is a regular parameter together with $y$, because $2\gamma \ne 0$. So $a - c^p = y^{2p} t$ with $(y, t)$ part of a regular system of parameters, and (D) holds with the exponent 1 on $t$. At the other points of the exceptional locus the multiplicities of the components through the center include one of $2, 4, \dots, 2p - 2$, $2p + 1$ and $4p + 2$, and all of these are prime to $p$ for $p \ge 3$. So the instance has a solution for each $\nu$.

In (O7) the exponents of the total transform can be divisible by $p$ at the center of $\nu$, even though the multiplicity is 2. One improvement step is needed here, and it works for each $\nu$. The information "multiplicity $m < p$" survives the first blowup as the exponent $m$ of $E_1$, but the center of $\nu$ can leave $E_1$, and then the information is lost. No way is known to use the hypothesis of (O7) after this. So the proof of (O7) cannot rest on the multiplicity, and the node of the road map can be stated for all of $\mathrm{PILU}_4$.

### Which valuations are hard

Let $\nu$ be zero dimensional, and let $r$ be the rational rank of $\nu|_L$.

- *Abhyankar valuations, $r = 4$.* Then $\nu$ has no defect, by the generalized stability theorem [Kuh10], and Theorem 3.29.19 applies, given $\mathrm{ELU}_3$. In fact no hypothesis is needed: Knaf and Kuhlmann prove local uniformization of Abhyankar places with a separable residue field extension [KK05], and applied to $L(b)$ and to a finite set of generators of $A$ together with $b$, this gives a solution. The form of their statement with a finite set is not checked here.
- *The rank.* Lemmas 3.29.15 to 3.29.17 and Theorem 3.29.19 use only the valuative criterion and the order of the value group, so they hold for each rank. Novacoski and Spivakovsky reduce local uniformization to rank one in suitable categories [NS14]. Whether their reduction applies to the relative statement $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ is not checked.
- *Without defect, $r \le 3$.* Solved given $\mathrm{ELU}_3$ (Theorem 3.29.19).
- *Defect $d = p$.* Then $\Delta \subseteq \Gamma_L$ has no maximum, and this needs $r \le 3$. The instance is solved if (D) holds (Proposition 3.29.22), and (D) can hold (Example 3.29.24). This is the only open case. Kuhlmann classifies the Artin-Schreier defect extensions as dependent or independent, by whether they are connected with purely inseparable defect extensions [Kuh10b]. In $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ the defect extension is itself purely inseparable. Whether a finer invariant of $\Delta$ decides (D), for example whether its supremum has the form $H^-$ for a convex subgroup $H$, as in the treatment of independent defect, is open. Section 3.31 shows that the approximations of $b$ do not decide (D) on a given model (Corollary 3.31.6), and that the open instances are those where $\nu|_L$ is not discrete of rank one (Proposition 3.31.19).

*The counterexample of Cutkosky.* Cutkosky gives a defect extension of function fields of dimension 2 that has no local monomialization, also no weak one [Cut15]. The construction is $K^* = k(x, y)$ over $K = k(u, v)$ with $u = x^p (1 + y)$ and $v = y^p + x$. The Jacobian determinant of $(u, v)$ is not zero, so the extension is separable. Its degree is $p^2 + 1$, and its defect is $p^2$. So it is neither purely inseparable nor of degree $p$, and it does not give an instance where (D) fails. It does not refute (D): (D) is a statement about one purely inseparable extension of degree $p$ with $A_1$ a ring of the base field, and the counterexample lives in a separable extension of a different degree. It shows only that the analogue of (D) for separable defect extensions can fail, which is the reason why Problem 3.29.23 is a real question. Whether a purely inseparable analogue of the counterexample exists is part of Problem 3.29.23.

*Inseparable local uniformization.* Temkin proves local uniformization after a finite purely inseparable extension of the function field that is not controlled [Tem13]. This does not give $\mathrm{PILU}_4$, because a regular model of a larger field does not give a regular model of $L(b)$.

### The link between the two parts

**Lemma 3.29.28 ($\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ gives $\mathrm{ELU}_3$).** Assume $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ for regular 4-folds over $k$. Then $\mathrm{ELU}_3$ holds.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Let $A$, $\mu$ and $f$ be as in $\mathrm{ELU}_3$. Choose a regular affine 4-fold $Y$ with a point $y$ such that $A = \mathcal{O}_{Y,y}$ and $f \in \mathcal{O}(Y)$. For this, shrink a model of $A$ to its regular locus, which is open because $Y$ is excellent. Let $X$ be the reduced divisor of $f$. Apply $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ to $(Y, X, 0)$, and get $\pi \colon Y_r \to Y$, a composite of blowups in regular centers, with $X_r + E^{(r)}$ snc. Since $\pi$ is proper and $\mu \ge 0$ on $A$, $\mu$ has a center $y_r$ on $Y_r$. Put $A_1 = \mathcal{O}_{Y_r, y_r}$. The divisor of $\pi^* f$ is supported on $X_r + E^{(r)}$, which is snc at $y_r$, and $A_1$ is factorial, so $f = \text{unit} \cdot x^\alpha$ for a regular system of parameters $x$ of $A_1$. $\square$

The isomorphism clause and the global form are not needed here. So the gaps (G1) and (G2) do not affect route V: route V needs only $\mathrm{ELU}_3$, which is local, and route J as it stands gives it by Lemma 3.29.28. One node, $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$, gives $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm vw}$ over each field (Theorem 3.29.12) and $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ for each valuation without defect (Theorem 3.29.19 with Lemma 3.29.28). With the isomorphism clause it also gives $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ (Theorem 3.29.11).

### The new critical path

The review of the road, research note 94, drew the dependency graph of the open items and found the critical path. With the results of this section the graph changes in four places.

1. The arrow $\mathrm{Emb}_3 \Rightarrow \mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ is now proved (Theorem 3.29.11), with two corrections. The source must be $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ in global form, and not $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ in the form of Proposition 77.13. The field must be infinite with regular equal to smooth, which holds over the algebraically closed field of Section 3.24, or one accepts (G3). So $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ is no longer a separate node: it follows from the same node $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ that route J needs, in a slightly stronger form, with the centers of $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ in the non-snc locus.
2. Without the isomorphism clause, route J as it stands gives $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm vw}$ over each field (Theorem 3.29.12). This covers Steps 2 and 3 of the proof of Theorem 3.24.13. Only Step 5 needs the isomorphism clause.
3. The arrow $\mathrm{ELU}_3 \Rightarrow \text{(O7)}$ is replaced by $\mathrm{ELU}_3 \Rightarrow \mathrm{PILU}_4$ for valuations without defect (Theorem 3.29.19), and by (D) $\Rightarrow \mathrm{PILU}_4$ for all valuations (Proposition 3.29.22). The new open node is (D) for valuations with defect, Problem 3.29.23. The multiplicity condition of (O7) plays no role. The new route to $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ does not use $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$, and Proposition 77.12 of note 77 is not repaired.
4. The strong $\mathrm{Princ}_4$ is needed only for Lemma 3.24.11, that is, only on the route that proves (A$^{\rm w}$) for the components with $d = 2$ by the argument of that lemma. It stays open and does not follow from $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ by this method (G4).

So, for $k$ algebraically closed and $p > 24$, the critical path is
$$\mathrm{MD}_3 \wedge \mathrm{SNC}_3^{\rm iso} \ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso} \ \Rightarrow\ \bigl(\mathrm{LU}_4^- \text{ and } \mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}\bigr), \qquad \mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w} \wedge \text{patching with } \mathrm{RSF}_3 \ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{Fund}_4,$$
and then Theorem 3.24.4. Here $\mathrm{SNC}_3^{\rm iso}$ is $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ with centers in the non-snc locus. For each $p$, the local half can also come from $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd} \Rightarrow \mathrm{ELU}_3$ and Theorem 3.29.19, together with (D) for the valuations with defect, and Theorem 3.24.5. The patching with $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ is not affected by this section, and it stays the second critical item. The most load-bearing open statement is embedded resolution of 3-folds in characteristic $p$, now in the form $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$. It is itself an open problem in the literature: Cossart and Piltant resolve 3-folds [CP08, CP09], but not in the embedded form.

The literature of 2026 does not change this picture. We list two items, with what is checked about them.

- Abramovich and Temkin, arXiv:2602.15612 [AT26], classify pinch points in characteristic 2 and show that weighted blowups cannot give a resolution that keeps normal crossings for them. Checked at the level of the abstract. The statement is about normal crossings and not about Conjecture 2.5.5 or $\mathrm{Emb}_3$, and its relevance for the track NW, that weighted centers lose their main advantage in characteristic $p$, is not verified.
- Martínez-Núñez, arXiv:2611.18258 [MN26], proves equivariant resolution in characteristic $p$, over an algebraically closed field, for varieties with a torus action of complexity at most 3, that is, whose general orbits have codimension at most 3. Checked at the level of the abstract. For a 4-fold this covers each nontrivial torus action, also the small tori of Examples 3.22.14 and 3.22.17. The proof uses divisorial fans and log resolution in dimension at most 3. The exact form of the input in dimension 3 is not verified, so it is not known whether the paper needs a form of $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ or only the theorem of Cossart and Piltant.

### What is open

1. $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ with centers in the non-snc locus, beginning with Problem 77.P3 of note 77. With $\mathrm{MD}_3$ it gives $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ (Remark 3.29.4), and so $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ (Theorem 3.29.11).
2. $\mathrm{MD}_3$, and so $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ itself. This is embedded resolution of 3-folds in characteristic $p$, and it is the central open node of the road.
3. A theorem of Bertini that gives Lemma 3.29.6 over finite fields, with the extra condition that the sections generate, and over imperfect fields (G3).
4. The strong $\mathrm{Princ}_4$, or a proof of (A$^{\rm w}$) that avoids Lemma 3.24.11 (G4).
5. Problem 3.29.23: (D) for valuations with defect, from $\mathrm{ELU}_3$ or by a direct argument. By Section 3.31 this is the end case $\rho = 0$ along the valuation, the approximations of $b$ do not decide it on a given model, and it is open only when the value group of $\nu|_L$ is not $\mathbb{Z}$ (Problem 3.31.21). So key polynomials of the extension are not the natural tool, and the problem is to make $\rho$ drop on some model.
6. A purely inseparable analogue of the counterexample of Cutkosky [Cut15], that is, an instance where (D) fails.
7. A use of the hypothesis "multiplicity less than $p$" of (O7) after the center of the valuation leaves $E_1$ (Example 3.29.27).
8. The input in dimension 3 of [MN26], and whether that paper settles the part of the track FO that is about torus actions.

Poonen, Bertini theorems over finite fields, Ann. of Math. 160 (2004). The data of the main sources are as follows.

- [CJS20] V. Cossart, U. Jannsen and S. Saito. The arXiv preprint 0905.2191 has the title "Canonical embedded and non-embedded resolution of singularities for excellent two-dimensional schemes". The book version is Desingularization: invariants and strategy, Application to dimension 2, Lecture Notes in Math. 2270, Springer, 2020. Research note 95 gives the book title with the arXiv number; the number and the title above are the correct pairs. Lemma 3.29.5 uses Corollary 0.4.
- [Kuh10b] F.-V. Kuhlmann, A classification of Artin-Schreier defect extensions and characterizations of defectless fields, Illinois J. Math. 54 (2010), 397-448, arXiv 1003.5639. Research note 76 gives arXiv 1004.2135 for this paper; that number is the survey of Kuhlmann "The defect", in Commutative Algebra, Springer, 2011, 277-318.
- [Cut09] S. D. Cutkosky, Resolution of singularities for 3-folds in positive characteristic, Amer. J. Math. 131 (2009), 59-127, arXiv math/0606530. Theorems 1.3 and 5.6.
- [Cut15] S. D. Cutkosky, Counterexamples to local monomialization in positive characteristic, Math. Ann. 362 (2015), 321-334, arXiv 1404.7459. Theorem 1.4 and the construction at the start of Section 3 were read for note 96.
- [CM17] S. D. Cutkosky and H. Mourtada, Defect and local uniformization, arXiv 1711.02726. Theorem 7.1.
- [CP08, CP09] V. Cossart and O. Piltant, Resolution of singularities of threefolds in positive characteristic I and II, J. Algebra 320 (2008), 1051-1082, and 321 (2009), 1836-1976. They give non-embedded resolution of 3-folds, not $\mathrm{Emb}_3$.
- [Tem13] M. Temkin, Inseparable local uniformization, J. Algebra 373 (2013), 65-119, arXiv 0804.1554.
- [KK05] H. Knaf and F.-V. Kuhlmann, Abhyankar places admit local uniformization in any characteristic, Ann. Sci. ENS (4) 38 (2005), 833-846.
- [Kuh10] F.-V. Kuhlmann, Elimination of ramification I: the generalized stability theorem, Trans. AMS 362 (2010), 5697-5727, arXiv 1003.5678.
- [Kap42] I. Kaplansky, Maximal fields with valuations, Duke Math. J. 9 (1942), 303-321. The exact numbering for Lemma 3.29.18(3) is not checked.
- [NS14] J. Novacoski and M. Spivakovsky, arXiv 1204.4751 and 1509.03122, and the survey arXiv 1509.06350, with the related papers on key polynomials.
- [KKMS] G. Kempf, F. Knudsen, D. Mumford and B. Saint-Donat, Toroidal embeddings I, Lecture Notes in Math. 339, Springer, 1973. The exact numbering for Step 1 of Lemma 3.29.15 is not checked.
- [Mat] H. Matsumura, Commutative ring theory, Theorems 19.5, 23.1, 23.7 and 25.2.
- [AT26] D. Abramovich and M. Temkin, arXiv:2602.15612 (2026). Checked at the level of the abstract.
- [MN26] Martínez-Núñez, arXiv:2611.18258 (November 2026). Checked at the level of the abstract; the input in dimension 3 is not verified.
