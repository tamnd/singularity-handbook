## The end case of the log-Jacobian route

This section reports the second research result of the milestone S7. It is about the equations $z^p + g = 0$ of Conjecture 2.3.7, as in Section 3.1. Volume 7, Section 3.5, gives the same result with all the details. Here we state the result, we give the proof in a short form, and we explain what the result does not give.

### The problem

The route of the purely inseparable equations in Section 2.3 uses the residual order $\rho$ of the log-Jacobian ideal as its main invariant. Section 3.1 shows that $\rho$ does not go up under the blowup of a point or of a center with (H1) and (H2). If a resolution algorithm also makes $\rho$ go down, it ends at points with $\rho = 0$. The question of this section is: which singularities can occur at such a point, and can we resolve them?

### The result

We use the notation of Section 3.1. Let $g \ne 0$ be clean, with $g = x^r G$, where $x^r$ is the largest monomial in the variables of $\Delta$ that divides $g$.

**Theorem 3.2.1.** The residual order $\rho(g)$ is $0$ if and only if one of these holds:

1. (type 0) $G(0) \ne 0$, and $p \nmid r_i$ for some $i \in \Delta$;
2. (type 1) $G(0) = 0$, and $\partial_j G(0) \ne 0$ for some $j \notin \Delta$.

If $\rho(g) = 0$, then there is a change of the coordinates of $K[[x]]$ that keeps each component of $E$, after which $g = x^r$ in type 0, and $g = x^r x_j$ with $j \notin \Delta$ in type 1.

So, at a point with $\rho = 0$, the hypersurface is $z^p + x^r = 0$ or $z^p + x^r x_j = 0$ in suitable formal coordinates. These equations are binomial, and binomial varieties have a resolution in each characteristic (Theorem 2.4.3).

### The proof

The ideal $\widetilde\Theta(G)$ is generated by the elements $r_i G + x_i \partial_i G$ for $i \in \Delta$ and $\partial_j G$ for $j \notin \Delta$. It has an element of order $0$ if and only if a generator has a constant term that is not $0$. The constant terms are $r_i G(0)$ and $\partial_j G(0)$. If $G(0) \ne 0$, then the monomial $x^r$ has a coefficient that is not $0$ in $g$, and since $g$ is clean, some $r_i$ is prime to $p$. This gives the two types.

In type 0, the unit $G$ has an $r_i$-th root $v$ in $K[[x]]$, by Hensel's lemma, because $r_i$ is not $0$ in $K$. The new coordinate $x_i v$ gives $g = x^r$. In type 1, the element $G$ has a linear term in $x_j$, so $G$ can replace $x_j$ as a coordinate, and $g = x^r x_j$. Volume 7, Proposition 3.5.1 and Theorem 3.5.3 give the details.

### The Lean proof

The Lean library proves the first part of Theorem 3.2.1 for polynomials over a field of characteristic $p$, with the standard axioms only, in `Singularity/Invariant/LogJacobianEndCase.lean`. The theorem `Singularity.logJacobianOrder_eq_zero_iff_exists` says, for each $g$, that $\rho = 0$ if and only if a generator of $\widetilde\Theta(G)$ has a constant term that is not $0$. Only the direction to type 0 uses that $g$ is clean. The library does not prove the second part, the change of the coordinates in $K[[x]]$.

### What the result does not give

1. It does not say that $\rho$ goes down. This is the main open step of the route, and Section 3.1 gives only that $\rho$ does not go up.
2. The normal forms are local and formal. A resolution algorithm must glue them. In type 1 the divisor $V(x_j)$ of the new coordinate is not a component of $E$, so the toroidal structure near the point is not the one that $E$ gives.
3. The second part has no Lean proof.
4. The proofs of Theorem 2.4.3 use their own invariants, which are built from the monomials. The result does not say how these invariants fit with $\rho$ in one algorithm.
