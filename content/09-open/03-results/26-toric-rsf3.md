## Toric relative strong factorization in dimension three

This section reports the twenty-sixth research result, from the track FP (tamnd/singularity#320), the patching of models. It continues Section 3.25 and works on three of its open questions, all in the toric case. The first question is item 8 of the list at the end of Section 3.25: does the condition (A$^{\rm w}$) of Proposition 3.24.21 hold on all of the model $V_2'$ of Proposition 3.25.11? The second question is item 3 of that list: toric relative strong factorization $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ for all regular cones in the octant. The third question is part (N4) of item 7: an invariant that goes down along the alternating blowups of patching along curves.

The answers are as follows. If Step 3 of the proof of Theorem 3.24.13 is read in the weak form of Lemma 3.25.3(2), then (A$^{\rm w}$) holds for every toric pair and every toric $V_2$, for a simple reason (Proposition 3.26.5). So Theorem 3.24.20(2) is a statement about the strong form of Step 3, in which $W_1$ is a composite of blowups along regular centers. For this strong form, Lemma 3.26.8 gives an exact criterion for a failure of (A$^{\rm w}$), and Proposition 3.26.9 gives a sufficient condition. The criterion recovers the failure of Theorem 3.24.20(2) by a short combinatorial argument, and it finds no failure on $V_2'$. Whether (A$^{\rm w}$) holds on $V_2'$ in the strong form is reduced to a problem on the octant, which is open. For toric $\mathrm{RSF}_3$, Lemmas 3.26.12 and 3.26.13 decide by a finite recursion whether one regular cone is reached from another by smooth star subdivisions. With them, each regular cone with entries at most 4 has a certificate, which includes the 11 classes that Theorem 3.25.12 left open. With entries at most 5 there are 5496 classes, and 5494 of them have a certificate. The other 2 are open. On 801 toric pairs, one round of a repair with certificates removes each obstruction of Lemma 3.26.8 of dimension 3. But the number $\mu$ of bad cones of Definition 3.26.22 does not always go down under such a repair (Counterexample 3.26.24), and Proposition 3.26.25 gives a condition under which it does.

We keep the conventions of Sections 3.24 and 3.25. The field $k$ is algebraically closed of characteristic $p > 0$. The triple $(V_0, V_1, N)$, the models $V_2$ and $V_3$, the maps $\alpha \colon V_2 \to V_1$ and $\gamma \colon V_3 \dashrightarrow V_2$, and the sets $L$ and $\Phi$ are as in the proof of Theorem 3.24.13. We write $U_i = \mathrm{Reg}\, V_i$. For toric varieties we use [Ful93] and the book of Cox, Little and Schenck, which we cite by the numbers of its results. All the computations of this section are exact computations with integer vectors, from research note 88.

### Motivation

Section 3.24 showed that the proof of Cutkosky for 3-folds does not extend to 4-folds as it is. Its Claim A fails for the toric pair of Construction 3.24.19, and the weaker condition (A$^{\rm w}$) of Proposition 3.24.21 fails too, at the point $z$ of Theorem 3.24.20(2). Section 3.25 gave two tools against this failure. The first tool is the freedom in the choice of $V_2$ (Lemma 3.25.7): one may replace $V_2$ by a blowup $V_2'$ that keeps the centers of the valuations of $L$ regular. The second tool is relative strong factorization (Definition 3.25.8): at the generic point of a bad curve, the two local rings $R$ and $S$ have a common blowup that is reached from each of them along regular centers. Proposition 3.25.11 used both tools on Construction 3.24.19 and removed the failure near the bad curve $\Gamma$.

This leaves three questions. Does the repair work on all of $V_2'$, and not only near $\Gamma$? Does $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ hold for every pair of toric rings, and not only for $\sigma_0$? And if each repair removes one bad curve, does a finite number of repairs remove all of them? In the toric case all three questions are questions about fans, and this section treats them as such. A reader who knows the basic dictionary between fans and toric varieties can follow all the proofs. The main point of the section is that the questions become combinatorial games on cones, and that a large part of these games can be decided exactly.

### Toric preliminaries

Let $N = \mathbb{Z}^4$ with the coordinates $(t, x, y, z)$. As in Construction 3.24.19, $\Sigma_1$ is the fan with the rays $f = (2,1,1,1)$, $e_x$, $e_y$, $e_z$ and $v_5 = (-1,-1,-1,-1)$. Its only singular cone is $C = \langle f, e_x, e_y, e_z \rangle$, and $U_1 = V_1 \setminus \{p_1\}$. Let $\tau_1 = \langle e_x, e_y, e_z \rangle$. Let $\Sigma_2$ be a fan that refines $\Sigma_1$, with $V_2 = X(\Sigma_2)$ and $\alpha$ the toric morphism. A cone $\varphi$ of $\Sigma_2$ lies *over $U_1$* if its relative interior is not contained in the interior of $C$. Then the orbit $O(\varphi)$ maps into $U_1$. A face of a cone over $U_1$ is again over $U_1$, so these cones form a subfan. A cone $\varphi$ in the boundary $\partial C$ with $\varphi \ne C$ lies over $U_1$.

Let $\mathrm{L}(\Sigma_2)$ be the set of the cones $\psi$ of $\Sigma_2$ such that the relative interior of $\psi$ is in the interior of $C$, and such that the cone of $\Sigma_0$ whose relative interior contains the relative interior of $\psi$ is regular.

**Lemma 3.26.1 (the set $L$ in toric terms).** Let $V_0 = X(\Sigma_0)$ and $V_2 = X(\Sigma_2)$ be toric, with $\Sigma_2$ a refinement of $\Sigma_0$ and of $\Sigma_1$. Then $L$ is contained in the union of the orbits $O(\psi)$ with $\psi \in \mathrm{L}(\Sigma_2)$, and for each $\psi \in \mathrm{L}(\Sigma_2)$ the set $L$ contains the generic point of $O(\psi)$.

*Proof.* Let $v$ be a valuation of the function field, trivial on $k$, and let $w_v \in N_{\mathbb{R}}$ be the vector of its values on the characters, $v(\chi^m) = \langle m, w_v \rangle$. On a toric variety $X(\Sigma)$ the center of $v$ lies in the orbit $O(\kappa)$ of the cone $\kappa$ of $\Sigma$ whose relative interior contains $w_v$. So the center of $v$ on $V_1$ is $p_1$ if and only if $w_v$ is in the interior of $C$. The regularity of a toric variety is constant along an orbit, and $O(\kappa)$ consists of regular points if and only if $\kappa$ is regular (Cox, Little and Schenck, Theorem 3.1.19). So if $v \in N$ has a singular center on $V_1$, then its center on $V_0$ is regular, and $w_v$ is in the relative interior of a cone $\psi \in \mathrm{L}(\Sigma_2)$. Conversely, for $\psi \in \mathrm{L}(\Sigma_2)$ let $v$ be the monomial valuation with a weight in the relative interior of $\psi$. Its center on $V_2$ is the generic point of $O(\psi)$, its center on $V_1$ is $p_1$, and its center on $V_0$ is regular. So $v \in N$, and the generic point of $O(\psi)$ is in $L$. $\square$

In particular $L \subseteq U_2$ if and only if each cone of $\mathrm{L}(\Sigma_2)$ is regular. Lemma 3.25.7 asks for this condition.

Now let $W_1 \to U_1$ be toric, with the fan $W$ over $U_1$. Over $\alpha^{-1}(U_1)$ the map $\gamma^{-1} \colon V_2 \dashrightarrow V_3$ is the toric rational map $X(\Sigma_2) \dashrightarrow X(W)$ given by the identity of $N$.

**Lemma 3.26.2 (the fundamental locus in toric terms).** The set $\Phi \cap \alpha^{-1}(U_1)$ is the union of the orbits $O(\varphi)$ for the cones $\varphi$ of $\Sigma_2$ over $U_1$ that are contained in no cone of $W$. If $W$ refines $\Sigma_2$ over $U_1$, then a cone $\varphi$ of $\Sigma_2$ over $U_1$ is contained in a cone of $W$ if and only if $\varphi$ is a cone of $W$.

*Proof.* The domain of definition of the toric rational map is an open set that the torus fixes, so it is a union of orbits. It contains the chart $U_\varphi$ if and only if $\varphi$ is contained in a cone of $W$ (Cox, Little and Schenck, Theorem 3.3.4). This gives the first statement. For the second, let $\varphi \subseteq \kappa$ with $\kappa \in W$, and let $\psi \in \Sigma_2$ be a cone that contains $\kappa$. Then $\varphi \subseteq \psi$, and $\varphi$ and $\psi$ are cones of the same fan, so $\varphi = \psi \cap H$ for a supporting hyperplane $H$ of $\psi$. The hyperplane $H$ also supports $\kappa \subseteq \psi$, and $\varphi \subseteq \kappa \cap H \subseteq \psi \cap H = \varphi$. So $\varphi$ is a face of $\kappa$, and a cone of $W$. $\square$

**Definition 3.26.3 (stars and direct reachability).** Let $F$ be a regular fan and $\kappa$ a cone of $F$, with $\rho_\kappa$ the sum of the generators of $\kappa$. The *smooth star* of $F$ at $\kappa$ replaces each cone $k \supseteq \kappa$ by the cones spanned by $\rho_\kappa$ and the generators of $k$ except one generator $u$ of $\kappa$, for each such $u$. Its toric variety is the blowup of $X(F)$ along the regular orbit closure $V(\kappa)$ (Cox, Little and Schenck, Proposition 3.3.15). A *star sequence* of a regular cone $c$ is a fan obtained from the fan $\{c\}$ of the faces of $c$ by finitely many smooth stars. A regular cone $\delta \subseteq c$ is *directly reachable in $c$* if it is a cone of some star sequence of $c$.

A smooth star at a ray changes nothing. So in dimension 3 the stars that matter are the stars at 2-dimensional cones, which blow up regular curves, and the stars at 3-dimensional cones, which blow up points. For a regular 3-cone $\sigma$ in the octant $\tau$, with the local ring $S_\sigma$ over $R = F[x,y,z]_{(x,y,z)}$, the cone $\sigma$ is directly reachable in $\tau$ if and only if $S_\sigma$ is reached from $R$ by blowups along torus-invariant regular centers.

**Lemma 3.26.4 (restriction to a face).** Let $F$ be a regular fan, $c$ a cone of $F$, and $F'$ the smooth star of $F$ at a cone $\kappa$. Write $F|_c$ for the fan of the cones of $F$ contained in $c$. If $\kappa \subseteq c$, then $F'|_c$ is the smooth star of $F|_c$ at $\kappa$. If $\kappa \not\subseteq c$, then $F'|_c = F|_c$. So for each star sequence $F$ of a regular fan and each cone $c$ of the first fan, $F|_c$ is a star sequence of $c$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Each new cone contains $\rho_\kappa$, which is in the relative interior of $\kappa$. If $\kappa \not\subseteq c$, then $\kappa \cap c$ is a proper face of $\kappa$, so $\rho_\kappa \notin c$. No new cone lies in $c$, and the old cones in $c$ do not contain $\kappa$, so they stay. If $\kappa \subseteq c$, the cones of $F'$ in $c$ are the old cones in $c$ that do not contain $\kappa$, together with the new cones made from a cone $k \supseteq \kappa$ with $k \subseteq c$. A new cone made from a cone $k \not\subseteq c$ has a generator outside $c$. So the cones of $F'$ in $c$ form the star of $F|_c$ at $\kappa$. The last statement follows by induction on the number of stars. $\square$

### The condition (A$^{\rm w}$) in the weak model

Lemma 3.25.3(2) says that Step 3 of the proof of Theorem 3.24.13 needs only a regular $W_1$, projective and birational over $U_1$, that dominates $Z = \alpha^{-1}(U_1)$. We call this the *weak model* of Step 3. In the weak model the toric case is easy.

**Proposition 3.26.5 (the weak model).** Let $(V_0, V_1)$ be a toric pair, let $V_2$ be toric with a toric morphism $\alpha \colon V_2 \to V_1$, and assume $L \subseteq U_2$. Let $W_1$ be a toric resolution of $\alpha^{-1}(U_1)$ by star subdivisions that keeps each regular cone of $\Sigma_2$ over $U_1$. Then $W_1$ is a valid choice in Step 3 in the sense of Lemma 3.25.3(2), and (A$^{\rm w}$) holds with $\tilde U_2 = U_2$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Such a resolution exists (Cox, Little and Schenck, Theorem 11.1.9). It is regular, it is projective over $U_1$ because star subdivisions keep projectivity, and its fan refines the fan of $\alpha^{-1}(U_1)$, so it dominates $Z$. By Lemma 3.25.3(2) this is all that Step 3 needs. Let $\varphi$ be a regular cone of $\Sigma_2$ over $U_1$. Then $\varphi$ is a cone of $W$, so $O(\varphi) \cap \Phi = \emptyset$ by Lemma 3.26.2. So $\Phi \cap \alpha^{-1}(U_1)$ is contained in $\mathrm{Sing}\, V_2$, and $\Phi \cap U_2$ is contained in $\alpha^{-1}(\mathrm{Sing}\, V_1) = \alpha^{-1}(p_1)$. This set is closed, so the closure of $\Phi \cap U_2$ does not meet $\alpha^{-1}(U_1)$. Also $L \subseteq U_2$, and $U_2$ is open. $\square$

**Remark 3.26.6.** Proposition 3.26.5 applies to $V_2 = V_0$ of Construction 3.24.19. There $\sigma_0$ is a regular cone over $U_1$, so it is a cone of $W$, and the generic point $\eta_2$ of the bad curve $\Gamma$ is not in $\Phi$. So parts (1) and (2) of Theorem 3.24.20 do not hold for the weak choice of $W_1$ that Lemma 3.25.3(2) allows. They hold for $W_1$ a composite of blowups of $U_1$ along regular centers, which is the reading of Step 3 in Theorem 3.24.13: the proof of Theorem 3.24.20(1) uses that $\mathcal{O}_{W_1,q}$ is an iterated monoidal transform of $R$.

The weak model therefore tells nothing about the general route. Outside the toric case, a regular $W_1$ that keeps the regular locus of $Z$ is a resolution of the 4-fold $Z$ that is an isomorphism over $\mathrm{Reg}\, Z$, and this is the problem itself. Section 3.25, in the discussion after Lemma 3.25.7, makes the same remark about the choice of $V_2$. The computations of note 88 agree with Proposition 3.26.5. For the model $W_1'$ of Proposition 3.25.11, the fundamental locus $\Phi'$ has 25 irreducible components over $U_1$, none of them meets $L'$, and the condition (A$^{\rm w}$) holds. The test is the forced closure from the proof of Proposition 3.24.21: start with the set $L$, call a component of $\Phi$ forced if its closure meets the current set, and add its part over $U_1$ to the set. Then (A$^{\rm w}$) holds if and only if the final set lies in the regular locus. For $V_2 = V_0$ and $W$ a toric resolution of $\Sigma_0$ there are 9 components over $U_1$, and none of them meets $L$.

### The condition (A$^{\rm w}$) in the strong model

**Definition 3.26.7 (the strong toric model).** In the *strong toric model* of Step 3, the fan $W$ is a star sequence of $\Sigma_1$ over $U_1$, so that $W_1 \to U_1$ is a composite of blowups along regular torus-invariant centers, and $W$ refines $\Sigma_2$ over $U_1$. We assume $L \subseteq U_2$. Let $\Lambda(\Sigma_2)$ be the set of the cones $\varphi$ of $\Sigma_2$ with $\varphi \subseteq \partial C$ that are faces of a cone of $\mathrm{L}(\Sigma_2)$. They are regular, since a face of a regular cone is regular.

By Lemma 3.26.4, for each proper face $c$ of $C$ the fan $W|_c$ is a star sequence of $c$. This is the constraint that the strong model adds. A cone of $\Sigma_2$ in $c$ can be a cone of $W$ only if it is directly reachable in $c$.

**Lemma 3.26.8 (an obstruction in the strong model).** Let $W$ be as in Definition 3.26.7, let $\varphi \in \Lambda(\Sigma_2)$, and let $c$ be the smallest face of $C$ that contains $\varphi$. If $\varphi$ is not directly reachable in $c$, then $O(\varphi) \subseteq \Phi$. If, in addition, some cone $\psi \supseteq \varphi$ of $\Sigma_2$ over $U_1$ is not regular, then (A$^{\rm w}$) fails for $W$: for each open set $\tilde U_2$ with $L \subseteq \tilde U_2 \subseteq U_2$, the closure of $\Phi \cap \tilde U_2$ contains the orbit $O(\psi)$, which lies in $\alpha^{-1}(U_1) \setminus U_2$.

*Proof.* The cone $\varphi$ lies in $\partial C$ and $\varphi \ne C$, so it lies over $U_1$. If $\varphi$ were a cone of $W$, it would be a cone of $W|_c$, which is a star sequence of $c$ by Lemma 3.26.4. This is against the hypothesis. So $O(\varphi) \subseteq \Phi$ by Lemma 3.26.2. Now let $\varphi \subseteq \chi$ with $\chi \in \mathrm{L}(\Sigma_2)$. By Lemma 3.26.1, $L$ contains the generic point of $O(\chi)$. So each open set $\tilde U_2 \supseteq L$ contains it, and it also contains each generization of it, in particular the generic point $\eta_\varphi$ of $O(\varphi)$. So $\eta_\varphi \in \Phi \cap \tilde U_2$, and the closure of $\Phi \cap \tilde U_2$ contains $V(\varphi) \supseteq O(\psi)$. The orbit $O(\psi)$ lies over $U_1$, because $\psi$ does, and it consists of singular points of $V_2$, while $\tilde U_2 \subseteq U_2$. $\square$

Let $\Phi_U$ be the set of the cones $\varphi$ of $\Sigma_2$ over $U_1$ with $O(\varphi) \subseteq \Phi$. Then (A$^{\rm w}$) holds with

The hypothesis of Proposition 3.26.9 is a condition on $\partial C$ only. Away from $\partial C$ one still needs a star sequence of $\Sigma_1$ over $U_1$ that refines $\Sigma_2$ over $U_1$. Without the constraint on $\partial C$, such sequences exist by toric principalization with regular invariant centers [Gow05]. ### Toric relative strong factorization


Let $\tau = \langle e_1, e_2, e_3 \rangle$ be the octant in $\mathbb{Z}^3$. Each facet of $C$ is a regular 3-dimensional cone, and in the basis of its rays it is the octant. For $\tau_1$ the basis is $(e_x, e_y, e_z)$, and for the facet $\langle f, e_y, e_z \rangle$ it is $(f, e_y, e_z)$, because $f = (2,1,0,0) + e_y + e_z$ and $(2,1,0,0)$, $e_y$, $e_z$ generate the lattice points of its span. So the questions of direct reachability on $\partial C$ are questions about cones in the octant. The first tool decides them for one cone.

**Lemma 3.26.12 (the single cone game).** Let $\delta \subseteq \kappa$ be regular 3-dimensional cones, let $(u_1, u_2, u_3)$ be the rays of $\kappa$, and let $M$ be the nonnegative integer matrix whose rows are the rays of $\delta$ in this basis. Consider the two kinds of moves:

1. (2-face move) for $i \ne l$ with $M_{ri} \ge M_{rl}$ for each row $r$, subtract column $l$ from column $i$, and replace $u_l$ by $u_i + u_l$;
2. (3-cone move) for $l$ with $M_{rc} \ge M_{rl}$ for each row $r$ and each column $c$, subtract column $l$ from the two other columns, and replace $u_l$ by $u_1 + u_2 + u_3$.

Then $\delta$ is directly reachable in $\kappa$ if and only if $M$ is a permutation matrix, or some move gives a matrix $M'$ for which $\delta$ is directly reachable in the new cone. Each move lowers the sum of the entries of $M$, so this recursion decides direct reachability in finitely many steps.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* The star at the 2-face $\langle u_i, u_l \rangle$ replaces $\kappa$ by $\kappa_1 = \langle u_i, u_i + u_l, u_j \rangle$ and $\kappa_2 = \langle u_i + u_l, u_l, u_j \rangle$, where $j$ is the third index. A vector $a u_i + b u_l + c u_j$ with $a \ge b$ equals $(a - b) u_i + b (u_i + u_l) + c u_j$, so it lies in $\kappa_1$, and its coordinates there come from a subtraction of column $l$ from column $i$. If $a \le b$, it lies in $\kappa_2$ in the same way. So $\delta$ lies in one cone of the star if and only if a 2-face move at $\{i, l\}$ applies in one of the two orders, and then $M'$ is the matrix of $\delta$ in that cone. For the star at $\kappa$, with $\rho = u_1 + u_2 + u_3$, a vector $\sum_c r_c u_c$ equals $r_l \rho + \sum_{c \ne l} (r_c - r_l) u_c$. It lies in the new cone without $u_l$ if and only if $r_l \le r_c$ for each $c$, and this gives the 3-cone move.

Suppose that a move gives $M'$ with $\delta$ directly reachable in the new cone $\kappa'$, as a cone of a star sequence $G$ of $\kappa'$. Make the star of the move on $\{\kappa\}$, then the stars of $G$ in order. By Lemma 3.26.4, at each stage the cones of the current fan in $\kappa'$ are the cones of the corresponding stage of $G$, so each center is a cone of the current fan, and at the end $\delta$ is a cone. Conversely, let $\delta$ be a cone of $F_m$, where $\{\kappa\} = F_0, F_1, \dots, F_m$ is a star sequence. If $m = 0$, then $\delta = \kappa$ and $M$ is a permutation matrix. A star at a ray changes nothing, so we may assume that the first star is at a 2-face or at $\kappa$. The fan $F_m$ refines $F_1$, so $\delta$ lies in a cone $\kappa'$ of $F_1$, which is a new cone of the first star. So a move applies and gives the matrix of $\delta$ in $\kappa'$. By Lemma 3.26.4, $F_m|_{\kappa'}$ is a star sequence of $\kappa'$, and $\delta$ is one of its cones. For termination, note that $M$ is invertible, so no column of $M$ is zero, and each move lowers the sum of the entries by at least 1. $\square$

They share the 2-face $\langle (3,0,2), (2,5,5) \rangle$. The cone $u_1$ has 115 certificates of depth at most 4: 1 of depth 2, 10 of depth 3 and 104 of depth 4. For each of them the certificate repair gives $\mu = w \in \{3, 4, 5\}$. So the repair of the bad cone with the least entries raises $\mu$ and $w$ for each certificate of depth at most 4. The cone $u_2$ has no certificate of depth at most 4, but its certificates of depth 5 and 6 give $\mu = w = 0$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* This is an exact computation from research note 89, which lists all the certificates of $u_1$ of depth at most 4, makes each repair in the order of the certificate, checks that the new stars give $\Lambda$ in $\tau_1$, and computes $\mu$ and $w$ again. $\square$

A second case behaves in the same way. For the seed 31814 the start has $w = 2$, all 695 certificates of $u_1$ of depth at most 4 give $w \ge 2$, and the 2 certificates of $u_2$ of depth 4 give $w = 0$. In a random test with seeds 0 to 40000, 2212 pairs have $\mu > 0$ at the start. After the first certificate repair of the bad cone with the least entries, $\mu$ goes down in 1990 cases, stays the same in 7 cases and goes up in 1 case. In the other 214 cases that cone has no certificate of depth at most 4, so the test does not apply. The weaker statement, that for some bad cone and some certificate of it the repair lowers $\mu$, holds in all these data, but it is open. Even if it holds, it does not by itself give termination, since the depth of the certificate that is needed is not bounded.

The counterexample shows where a proof must be careful. A star of $\Lambda$ at a 2-dimensional face of $u$ also cuts the neighbouring cone that has this face. If the neighbour is bad or not regular, the pieces can be new bad cones. If not, nothing goes wrong.

**Proposition 3.26.25 (a condition for the drop of $\mu$).** Let $u$ be a bad 3-dimensional cone of $\Sigma_2$ in a facet $c$ of $C$, and let $(\Lambda, F)$ be a certificate for $u$ in the basis of $c$. Let $N(u)$ be the set of the 3-dimensional cones $k \ne u$ of $\Sigma_2$ in $\partial C$ that share a 2-dimensional face with $u$ and that are not regular or not directly reachable in their facet. Assume that no star of $\Lambda$ is at a cone contained in $u \cap k$ for some $k \in N(u)$. Then the certificate repair of $u$ lowers $\mu$ by at least 1.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Let $\Sigma_2^+$ be the fan after the repair. Each star of $\Lambda$ is at a cone $\epsilon \subseteq u$ of the current fan, and it changes only the cones that contain $\epsilon$. Stars at rays change nothing. The support of $\partial C$ is the boundary of a 4-dimensional cone, so each 2-dimensional cone of the current fan in $\partial C$ lies in exactly two 3-dimensional cones of it in $\partial C$. So a star at a 2-dimensional cone $\epsilon$ in a 2-face $g$ of $u$ changes, in $\partial C$, only cones in $u$ and cones in the 3-dimensional cone $k$ of $\Sigma_2$ in $\partial C$ with $u \cap k = g$. A star at a cone whose relative interior is in the interior of $u$ changes only cones in $u$. Now compare the 3-dimensional cones of $\Sigma_2^+$ in $\partial C$ with those of $\Sigma_2$.

(a) The cones in $u$ are the maximal cones of $\Lambda$, by Lemma 3.26.4. They are cones of the star sequence $F$ of $c$, so they are directly reachable in $c$, and none of them is bad.

(b) Let $k$ share the 2-face $g$ with $u$, with $k \notin N(u)$. Then $k$ is regular and is a cone of a star sequence $G$ of its facet $c_k$. By Lemma 3.26.4, the cones of $\Sigma_2^+$ in $k$ are the cones of the star sequence $H$ of $k$ that consists of the stars of $\Lambda$ at cones in $g$. As in the proof of Lemma 3.26.16, $G$ followed by $H$ is a star sequence of $c_k$. So each cone of $\Sigma_2^+$ in $k$ is directly reachable in $c_k$, and none of them is bad.

(c) Each other 3-dimensional cone of $\Sigma_2$ in $\partial C$ is a cone of $\Sigma_2^+$, with the same status. For $k \in N(u)$ this is the hypothesis.

So the bad 3-dimensional cones of $\Sigma_2^+$ are among the bad 3-dimensional cones of $\Sigma_2$ other than $u$. $\square$

In Counterexample 3.26.24 the cone $u_2$ is in $N(u_1)$, so Proposition 3.26.25 does not apply to $u_1$. Proposition 3.26.25 says nothing about $\mu_2$, and Theorem 3.26.23(2) shows that bad 2-dimensional cones can remain.

### What is open

1. Toric $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ for all regular cones in the octant. The two classes $\langle (0,1,3), (1,2,5), (5,2,0) \rangle$ and $\langle (0,2,3), (1,4,5), (5,3,0) \rangle$ of Theorem 3.26.19(3) now have certificates of depth 12, and every regular cone with entries at most 6 has a certificate (Theorem 3.27.20). The question for all cones is open (Section 3.27). Section 3.28 shows that the multi-pivot theorem 3.27.17 does not reduce it: its bounded form is a bound on the number of pivots of a certificate, and this bound is open for each $k \ge 2$.
2. A uniform certificate for the family of the three cones of Theorem 3.26.19(4), which by Theorem 3.27.22 cannot be of pivot type, and a proof of toric $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ that uses that only one cone is prescribed (Remark 3.26.21).
3. The weak form of the drop of $\mu$: for each fan $\Sigma_2$ with $\mu > 0$, some bad cone has a certificate whose repair lowers $\mu$. Then an invariant that also controls $\mu_2$ and gives termination of the repairs. This is part (N4) of item 7 in Section 3.25, in the toric case. Proposition 3.27.24 and Corollary 3.27.25 give data and a partial result for a different choice of the bad cone.
4. Problem $(\mathrm{P}_{\tau_1})$ for $V_2'$ and for $V_2''$, and so (A$^{\rm w}$) on $V_2'$ in the strong model.
5. A star sequence of $\Sigma_1$ over $U_1$ that refines $\Sigma_2$ over $U_1$ and has prescribed stars on $\partial C$, as needed after Proposition 3.26.9.
6. Outside the toric case, every question of the list at the end of Section 3.25 stays open, in particular $\mathrm{Princ}_4$ at closed points in characteristic $p$.

### Status

Note 89 checked each proof of Lemmas 3.26.1 to 3.26.4, Propositions 3.26.5 and 3.26.9, Lemma 3.26.8 and Lemmas 3.26.12, 3.26.16 and 3.26.17, and it found them correct. The map has nodes for Theorem 3.24.20, Proposition 3.24.21, Lemma 3.25.7, Definition 3.25.8, Theorem 3.25.12 and Proposition 3.25.13, which this section uses, and no nodes yet for its new statements. None of the statements has a Lean proof. An outside expert has not yet read the result.
