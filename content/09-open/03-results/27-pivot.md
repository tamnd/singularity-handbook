## The Pivot Theorem for toric relative strong factorization

This section reports the twenty-seventh research result, from the track FP (tamnd/singularity#320), the patching of models. It continues Section 3.26 on toric relative strong factorization $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ in the octant. It has four parts. The first part describes direct reachability as membership in a monoid of matrices. The second part proves the Pivot Theorem: if all cones of the subdivision of $\sigma$ can share one ray, then a certificate of a fixed shape exists. A multi-pivot form with several rays and its converse follow. The third part gives the computations: every regular 3-dimensional cone with entries at most 6 has a certificate, the depth grows in two families, and the computations show which classes satisfy the pivot condition. The fourth part treats the route of repairs and the literature on the strong factorization conjecture of Oda.

The results come from research note 90 and from research note 91, which is a referee report on note 90. Where the two notes differ, this section follows note 91. We keep the notation of Section 3.26. The octant is $\tau = \langle e_1, e_2, e_3 \rangle$ in $N = \mathbb{Z}^3$, and $\sigma = \langle a_1, a_2, a_3 \rangle \subseteq \tau$ is a regular 3-dimensional cone. We write $M$ for the matrix with the rows $a_1, a_2, a_3$. It has nonnegative integer entries and determinant $\pm 1$. A star sequence, direct reachability, a certificate $(\Lambda, F)$ and its depth are as in Definitions 3.26.3 and 3.26.15. All fans in this section are regular, and we say "the cones of $\Lambda$" for the 3-dimensional cones of $\Lambda$ when no confusion is possible.

### Motivation

Section 3.26 left two classes with entries at most 5 without a certificate, and it gave certificates only cone by cone, from a computer search. A search can settle finitely many cones. It cannot prove $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ for an infinite set of cones, and it does not explain why a certificate exists. So the questions of this section are: is there a condition on $\sigma$ that gives a certificate with a proof, and how much of the problem does such a condition cover?

The answer comes from one observation. Suppose that all the cones of $\Lambda$ contain one ray $b$. Then near $b$ both fans $\Lambda$ and $F$ look like a 2-dimensional fan, the link of $b$, and in dimension 2 every problem of this kind is solved by the classical theory of continued fractions. The Pivot Theorem makes this exact. It covers all the classes of small depth, and it gives the first infinite family of cones that are not directly reachable but have a certificate with a proof. The cones of large depth do not satisfy it, but the certificates that the search finds for them have several rays of this kind. Theorem 3.27.17 is the corresponding statement for several rays.

A reader needs Section 3.26 up to Definition 3.26.15 and Lemma 3.26.16. For the 2-dimensional facts we use [Ful93, Sections 2.6 and 3.1].

### The monoid of direct reachability

Lemma 3.26.12 decides direct reachability by moves on the columns of $M$. We first show that the 3-cone moves are not needed, and then we describe the result in terms of matrices.

**Definition 3.27.1 (elementary matrices and the monoid $S$).** For $i \ne j$ in $\{1, 2, 3\}$ let $E_{ij} = I + e_{ij}$, where $e_{ij}$ is the matrix with the entry 1 at the place $(i, j)$ and 0 elsewhere. Let $S$ be the monoid of $3 \times 3$ matrices generated by the permutation matrices and the matrices $E_{ij}$. A *2-face move* on a nonnegative matrix $M$ is the map $M \mapsto M E_{ij}^{-1}$ when the result is nonnegative. It subtracts column $i$ from column $j$, and it is allowed exactly when column $j$ is at least column $i$ in each row.

The 2-face move of Definition 3.27.1 is the move of Lemma 3.26.12(1), and every element of $S$ has nonnegative entries and determinant $\pm 1$.

**Lemma 3.27.2 (a 3-cone move is two 2-face moves).** Let $\kappa = \langle \kappa_1, \kappa_2, \kappa_3 \rangle$ be a regular 3-dimensional cone and let $\{i, j, l\} = \{1, 2, 3\}$. The 3-cone move of Lemma 3.26.12(2) that omits $\kappa_i$ is allowed if and only if the two 2-face moves that subtract column $i$ from column $j$ and then column $i$ from column $l$ are allowed, and the two ways give the same matrix. So direct reachability is decided by 2-face moves alone.

*Proof.* Let $x = x_i \kappa_i + x_j \kappa_j + x_l \kappa_l$ be a row of the matrix. Make the star at $\langle \kappa_i, \kappa_j \rangle$, with the new ray $u = \kappa_i + \kappa_j$. If $x_j \ge x_i$, then $x = x_i u + (x_j - x_i) \kappa_j + x_l \kappa_l$ lies in the piece $\langle u, \kappa_j, \kappa_l \rangle$. Now make the star of this piece at its 2-face $\langle u, \kappa_l \rangle$, with the new ray $s = u + \kappa_l = \kappa_1 + \kappa_2 + \kappa_3$. If $x_l \ge x_i$, then $x = x_i s + (x_j - x_i) \kappa_j + (x_l - x_i) \kappa_l$ lies in the piece $\langle s, \kappa_j, \kappa_l \rangle$. These are the coordinates of $x$ in the piece of the 3-cone star that omits $\kappa_i$, and the two conditions $x_j \ge x_i$ and $x_l \ge x_i$ for all rows are the condition of the 3-cone move. The two 2-face stars give a different fan than the 3-cone star, but the recursion of Lemma 3.26.12 depends only on the matrix. So each 3-cone move in a successful recursion can be replaced by two 2-face moves. $\square$

**Corollary 3.27.3 (direct reachability is membership in $S$).** The cone $\sigma$ is directly reachable in $\tau$ if and only if $M \in S$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* By Lemma 3.26.12 and Lemma 3.27.2, $\sigma$ is directly reachable if and only if a sequence of 2-face moves takes $M$ to a permutation matrix $P$. If $M E_1^{-1} \cdots E_k^{-1} = P$ for elementary matrices $E_1, \dots, E_k$, then $M = P E_k \cdots E_1 \in S$. Conversely let $M \in S$. For a permutation $q$ of $\{1,2,3\}$ let $Q$ be the matrix with $Q e_{ij} Q^{-1} = e_{q(i) q(j)}$ for all $i, j$. Then $Q E_{ij} Q^{-1} = E_{q(i) q(j)}$, so $E_{ij} Q = Q E_{q^{-1}(i) q^{-1}(j)}$. With this relation we move all permutation matrices of a word for $M$ to the left, and we get $M = P E_k \cdots E_1$ with $P$ a permutation matrix. Each partial product $P E_k \cdots E_r$ is a product of nonnegative matrices, so it is nonnegative. So the moves $M \mapsto M E_1^{-1} \mapsto M E_1^{-1} E_2^{-1} \mapsto \cdots$ are allowed, and they end at $P$. $\square$

So the directly reachable cones form one orbit of a monoid, and the cones that are not directly reachable are the nonnegative unimodular matrices outside $S$. The side of $\sigma$ of a certificate is described by the rows.

**Lemma 3.27.4 (stars of $\sigma$ and the action of $S$).**

1. A star at the 2-face $\langle x, y \rangle$ of a cone with the rows $x, y, z$ gives the two cones with the rows $x + y, y, z$ and $x, x + y, z$. A star at the cone gives three cones, each with one row replaced by $x + y + z$. So the matrix of each cone of a star sequence of $\sigma$ is $X M$, where $X$ is a product of permutation matrices and matrices $E_{ij}$.
2. If $\kappa$ is directly reachable in $\tau$, then each cone of a star sequence of $\kappa$ is directly reachable in $\tau$.
3. If $\sigma$ has a certificate and $K \in S$, then the cone with the rows of $M K$ has a certificate of the same depth.

*Proof.* (1) The replacement of the row $x$ by $x + y$ is the left multiplication by an elementary matrix, and the replacement of $x$ by $x + y + z$ is the product of two of them. (2) Let $G$ be a star sequence of $\tau$ with $\kappa$ as a cone. The stars of the star sequence of $\kappa$ are at faces of cones of the current fan, also after $G$, by Lemma 3.26.4. So they continue $G$. (3) Let $\varphi$ be the linear map with $\varphi(e_c) = K_c$, the row $c$ of $K$, so that $\varphi(v) = v K$ for a row vector $v$. Since $\det K = \pm 1$, $\varphi$ is an automorphism of $N$. It maps $\tau$ onto the cone $\tau'$ with the rows of $K$, it maps $\sigma$ onto the cone with the rows of $M K$, and it maps star sequences to star sequences. By Corollary 3.27.3, $\tau'$ is directly reachable in $\tau$. So $(\varphi \Lambda, \varphi F)$ is a certificate in $\tau'$, and Lemma 3.26.16 gives a certificate in $\tau$ with the same $\Lambda$. $\square$

**Definition 3.27.5 (stuck cones).** The cone $\sigma$ is *stuck at $\tau$* if $M$ is not a permutation matrix and no 2-face move applies to $M$, that is, no column of $M$ is at least another column in each row.

**Lemma 3.27.6 (stuck cones).** Let $\sigma$ be stuck at $\tau$. Then $\sigma$ is not directly reachable in $\tau$, and the first star of every star sequence of $\tau$ (at a 2-face or at $\tau$) cuts $\sigma$: no cone of the new fan contains $\sigma$. So every certificate of $\sigma$ has depth at least 1, and Lemma 3.26.16 cannot replace $\tau$ by a smaller cone.

**Example 3.27.7 (stuck cones).** (1) The cone $O_1 = \langle (0,1,3), (1,2,5), (5,2,0) \rangle$ of Theorem 3.26.19(3) has the columns $(0,1,5)$, $(1,2,2)$ and $(3,5,0)$. No column is at least another one, so $O_1$ is stuck. The same check shows that $O_2 = \langle (0,2,3), (1,4,5), (5,3,0) \rangle$, the three classes $A$, $B$, $C$ of Theorem 3.26.19(4), the class $D_{10} = \langle (0,1,4), (4,2,5), (5,1,0) \rangle$ and the cone $\sigma_0$ of Example 3.26.14(1) are stuck.

(2) For $k \ge 1$ let $P_k = \langle (1,1,1), (0,1,k), (k+1,k,0) \rangle$. Its determinant is $-k^2 + k(k+1) - (k+1) = -1$, so $P_k$ is regular. Its columns are $(1,0,k+1)$, $(1,1,k)$ and $(1,k,0)$. For $k \ge 2$ they are pairwise incomparable, so $P_k$ is stuck and not directly reachable. For $k = 1$ the second column is at least the third one, and Exercise 2 shows that $P_1$ is directly reachable.

### The Pivot Theorem

**Definition 3.27.8 (links and the pivot condition).** Let $G$ be a regular fan and $b$ a ray of $G$. Write $\mathrm{St}_G(b)$ for the set of the 3-dimensional cones of $G$ that contain $b$, and $|\mathrm{St}_G(b)|$ for their union. Let $\pi \colon N \to \bar N = N / \mathbb{Z} b$ be the quotient map. For a cone $\langle b, w, w' \rangle$ of $\mathrm{St}_G(b)$ the vectors $b, w, w'$ form a basis of $N$, so $\pi(w), \pi(w')$ form a basis of $\bar N \cong \mathbb{Z}^2$. The cones $\pi \langle w, w' \rangle$ for $\langle b, w, w' \rangle \in \mathrm{St}_G(b)$ and their faces form a regular 2-dimensional fan $L_G(b)$, the *link* of $b$ in $G$ [Ful93, Section 3.1]. Its support is $\pi(|\mathrm{St}_G(b)|)$, and its rays correspond to the rays $w$ of $G$ such that $\langle b, w \rangle$ is a cone of $G$. Let
$$B(\sigma) = \{a_1, a_2, a_3, a_1 + a_2, a_1 + a_3, a_2 + a_3, a_1 + a_2 + a_3\}.$$
These are the vectors that are rays of $\sigma$ or rays of the first star of $\sigma$. The cone $\sigma$ is a *pivot cone* with *pivot* $b$ if $b \in B(\sigma)$ and there is a star sequence $F_0$ of $\tau$ such that $b$ is a ray of $F_0$ and $\sigma \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_{F_0}(b)|$.

The Pivot Theorem works in the link, and there it needs one fact from dimension 2. We prove it, since the proof is short.

**Lemma 3.27.9 (regular subdivisions of a regular 2-cone).** Let $\delta = \langle u, v \rangle$ be a regular 2-dimensional cone in a lattice of rank 2, and let $D$ be a fan of regular cones with support $\delta$. Then $D$ is obtained from the fan of faces of $\delta$ by a finite sequence of stars at 2-dimensional cones.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* We use induction on the number $n$ of 2-dimensional cones of $D$. If $n = 1$, then $D$ is the fan of faces of $\delta$. Let $n \ge 2$. In the basis $(u, v)$ each ray of $D$ has a primitive generator with nonnegative integer coordinates. The vector $u + v = (1,1)$ lies in a 2-dimensional cone $\langle r, r' \rangle$ of $D$, and since this cone is regular, $u + v = \alpha r + \beta r'$ with integers $\alpha, \beta \ge 0$. If $\alpha \ge 1$ and $\beta \ge 1$, then $r + r' \le (1,1)$ in each coordinate, so $\{r, r'\} = \{(1,0), (0,1)\}$ and $\langle r, r' \rangle = \delta$. Then $n = 1$, which is false. So $u + v$ is a multiple of $r$ or of $r'$, and since $(1,1)$ is primitive, $u + v$ is a ray of $D$. The cones of $D$ in $\langle u, u + v \rangle$ and in $\langle u + v, v \rangle$ form two fans with these supports, each with fewer than $n$ cones of dimension 2, and both cones are regular. Make the star of $\delta$ at $\delta$, and then the stars that the induction gives for each half. A star inside one half does not change the other half. $\square$

**Theorem 3.27.10 (the Pivot Theorem).** Let $\sigma \subseteq \tau$ be a pivot cone with pivot $b$, and let $F_0$ be as in Definition 3.27.8. Then $\sigma$ has a certificate $(\Lambda, F)$ in which $b$ is a ray of every cone of $\Lambda$. Explicitly, $\Lambda$ is the star of $\sigma$ that creates $b$ (no star if $b$ is a ray of $\sigma$), followed by stars at 2-faces $\langle x, x' \rangle$ with $\langle b, x, x' \rangle \in \Lambda$. The fan $F$ is $F_0$, followed by stars at 2-faces $\langle w, w' \rangle$ with $\langle b, w, w' \rangle \in F$, followed by stars at 2-faces $\langle b, w \rangle$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* *Step 0 (the start).* Let $\Lambda_0$ be the fan of faces of $\sigma$ if $b$ is a ray of $\sigma$, the star of $\sigma$ at $\langle a_i, a_j \rangle$ if $b = a_i + a_j$, and the star of $\sigma$ at $\sigma$ if $b = a_1 + a_2 + a_3$. In each case every 3-dimensional cone of $\Lambda_0$ contains $b$, and $|\Lambda_0| = \sigma$. The image $\pi(\sigma)$ is a regular 2-dimensional cone, a half-plane or the plane, and it is the support of $L_{\Lambda_0}(b)$. By the hypothesis $\sigma \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_{F_0}(b)|$, so $\pi(\sigma) \subseteq |L_{F_0}(b)|$. During the proof we write $L_F$ and $L_\Lambda$ for the links of $b$ in the current fans $F$ and $\Lambda$. Each star is at a face of a cone of the current fan, so all fans stay regular.

*Step 1 (lifting of link stars).* Let $\langle b, w, w' \rangle$ be a cone of $F$. The star of $F$ at $\langle w, w' \rangle$ changes only the cones that contain $\langle w, w' \rangle$. These are $\langle b, w, w' \rangle$ and at most one cone $\langle w, w', z \rangle$ with $z \ne b$. The first one becomes $\langle b, w, w + w' \rangle$ and $\langle b, w + w', w' \rangle$, and the pieces of the second one do not contain $b$. So $|\mathrm{St}_F(b)|$ does not change, and $L_F$ changes by the 2-dimensional star at $\pi \langle w, w' \rangle$, with the new ray $\pi(w) + \pi(w')$. Each star of $L_F$ at a 2-dimensional cone is of this form. The same holds for $\Lambda$: there every 3-dimensional cone contains $b$, so $\langle b, x, x' \rangle$ is the only cone of $\Lambda$ that contains $\langle x, x' \rangle$, and $|\mathrm{St}_\Lambda(b)| = \sigma$ stays the same.

*Step 2 (a common refinement in the link).* Let $R$ be the fan made from $L_F$ as follows. In each 2-dimensional cone of $L_F$, insert the rays of $L_\Lambda$ that lie in it, and replace each 2-dimensional cone of the result that is not regular by a regular subdivision [Ful93, Section 2.6]. A ray of $L_\Lambda$ on the boundary of a cone of $L_F$ is a ray of that cone, so $R$ is a regular fan that refines $L_F$, and each ray of $L_\Lambda$ is a ray of $R$. Let $R_\Lambda$ be the set of the cones of $R$ in $\pi(\sigma)$. The boundary of $\pi(\sigma)$ consists of rays of $L_\Lambda$, and a 2-dimensional cone of $R$ has no ray of $L_\Lambda$ in its interior. So each 2-dimensional cone of $R$ whose interior meets $\pi(\sigma)$ lies in one cone of $L_\Lambda$, and $R_\Lambda$ is a regular fan with support $\pi(\sigma)$ that refines $L_\Lambda$. By Lemma 3.27.9, applied to each 2-dimensional cone of $L_F$ and of $L_\Lambda$, the fan $R$ is obtained from $L_F$ by stars at 2-dimensional cones, and $R_\Lambda$ is obtained from $L_\Lambda$ in the same way. Lift these stars to $F$ and to $\Lambda$ by Step 1. After this, the cones of $L_F$ in $\pi(\sigma)$ are the cones of $L_\Lambda = R_\Lambda$, and $\sigma \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_F(b)|$ still holds.

*Step 3 (raising).* For each ray $\rho$ of $R_\Lambda$ let $x_\rho$ be the ray of $\Lambda$ and $w_\rho$ the ray of $F$ with $\pi(x_\rho)$ and $\pi(w_\rho)$ on $\rho$, such that $\langle b, x_\rho \rangle \in \Lambda$ and $\langle b, w_\rho \rangle \in F$. Both $\pi(x_\rho)$ and $\pi(w_\rho)$ are the primitive generator of $\rho$, so $x_\rho = w_\rho + \gamma_\rho b$ with $\gamma_\rho \in \mathbb{Z}$. We show that $\gamma_\rho \ge 0$. The point $x_\rho$ lies in $\sigma \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_F(b)|$, so it lies in a cone $\langle b, w, w' \rangle$ of $F$. Then $\pi(x_\rho) \in \pi \langle w, w' \rangle$. The ray $\rho$ is a ray of the fan $L_F$ that lies in the cone $\pi \langle w, w' \rangle$, so it is a ray of this cone, say $\rho = \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0} \pi(w)$. Then $w = w_\rho$, and $x_\rho = \lambda b + \mu w_\rho + \nu w'$ with $\lambda, \mu, \nu \ge 0$ and $\mu \pi(w_\rho) + \nu \pi(w') = \pi(w_\rho)$. So $\mu = 1$, $\nu = 0$ and $\gamma_\rho = \lambda \ge 0$.

For each 2-dimensional cone $\langle \rho, \rho' \rangle$ of $R_\Lambda$ the cone $\langle b, x_\rho, x_{\rho'} \rangle$ is a cone of $\Lambda$, and $\langle b, w_\rho, w_{\rho'} \rangle$ is a cone of $F$. Since $x_\rho \in \langle b, w_\rho \rangle$ and $x_{\rho'} \in \langle b, w_{\rho'} \rangle$, the first cone lies in the second one. Now let $\gamma_\rho \ge 1$ and make the star of $F$ at $\langle b, w_\rho \rangle$. It replaces each cone $\langle b, w_\rho, z \rangle$ by $\langle b, w_\rho + b, z \rangle$ and $\langle w_\rho + b, w_\rho, z \rangle$. So in $\mathrm{St}_F(b)$ the ray $w_\rho$ is replaced by $w_\rho + b$, the link $L_F$ does not change, $\gamma_\rho$ goes down by 1, and the other numbers $\gamma$ do not change. Since $\gamma_\rho \ge 0$ after the star, the inclusions of the cones of $\Lambda$ in cones of $F$ with the ray $b$ stay true, and so $\sigma \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_F(b)|$ stays true. Make these stars until all $\gamma_\rho$ are 0. Then $\langle b, x_\rho, x_{\rho'} \rangle = \langle b, w_\rho, w_{\rho'} \rangle$ for each 2-dimensional cone of $R_\Lambda$. These are all the 3-dimensional cones of $\Lambda$, so each cone of $\Lambda$ is a cone of $F$. $\square$

The converse explains the name: a pivot cone is a cone with a certificate whose cones turn around one ray.

**Proposition 3.27.11 (the converse).** The cone $\sigma$ is a pivot cone if and only if $\sigma$ has a certificate $(\Lambda, F)$ such that all the 3-dimensional cones of $\Lambda$ have a common ray.

*Proof.* One direction is Theorem 3.27.10. Conversely, let $b$ be a common ray of the cones of $\Lambda$. Since they are cones of $F$, they lie in $\mathrm{St}_F(b)$, so $\sigma = |\Lambda| \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_F(b)|$, and $b$ is a ray of $F$. It remains to show that $b \in B(\sigma)$. If $b$ is a ray of $\sigma$, this is clear. Otherwise $b$ is the new ray of a star at a face $f$, made on the fan $\Lambda'$ of some stage of $\Lambda$. The vector $b$ is in the relative interior of $f$. A cone of $\Lambda'$ that does not contain $f$ meets $f$ in a proper face of $f$, so it does not contain $b$, and no cone of $\Lambda$ inside it contains $b$. Each cone of $\Lambda'$ contains a cone of $\Lambda$, since $\Lambda$ refines $\Lambda'$ with the same support. So all the 3-dimensional cones of $\Lambda'$ contain $f$. Three distinct 3-dimensional cones of a fan in $\mathbb{R}^3$ cannot share a 2-dimensional face, and only one cone contains a 3-dimensional $f$. So $\Lambda'$ has one 3-dimensional cone, or two with the common 2-face $f$. In the first case $\Lambda'$ is the fan of faces of $\sigma$ and $f$ is a face of $\sigma$, so $b = \sum f \in B(\sigma)$. In the second case $\Lambda'$ comes from $\sigma$ by one star at a 2-face $\langle a_i, a_k \rangle$, since a star at a 2-face adds one cone and a star at a 3-dimensional cone adds two. Its two cones $\langle a_i + a_k, a_k, a_l \rangle$ and $\langle a_i, a_i + a_k, a_l \rangle$ have the only common 2-face $f = \langle a_i + a_k, a_l \rangle$, so $b = a_1 + a_2 + a_3 \in B(\sigma)$. $\square$

The faces of $\Lambda_1$ and $\Lambda_2$ are faces of cones of $F$, so $\Lambda$ is contained in $F$. $\square$

The pivot condition asks for a star sequence of $\tau$ with a property. The next proposition reduces it to a search over small configurations of cones.

**Proposition 3.27.16 (a state search for the pivot condition).** Call a *state* a set $X$ of 3-dimensional cones of some star sequence of $\tau$ such that $X$ is one cone that contains $\sigma$, or two cones with a common 2-face whose union contains $\sigma$. A *move* from $X$ is a star at a face of a cone of $X$, followed by the choice of one piece, or of two pieces with a common 2-face, whose union contains $\sigma$. A state $X$ is a *hit* if some face $f$ of a cone of $X$ (of dimension 1, 2 or 3) with $\sum f \in B(\sigma)$ is contained in all cones of $X$, where $\sum f$ is the sum of the rays of $f$. Then $\sigma$ is a pivot cone if and only if a hit is reached from $\{\tau\}$ by a finite number of moves. In particular, if the set of states reached from $\{\tau\}$ is finite and contains no hit, then $\sigma$ is not a pivot cone.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* First let $X$ be a hit, reached by moves. By induction on the number of moves, the cones of $X$ are cones of an actual star sequence $G$ of $\tau$: a star at a face of a cone of $X$ is a star of $G$, and its pieces are cones of the new fan. Let $f$ be the face of the hit and $b = \sum f$. If $f$ is a ray, then $b$ is a ray of $G$, and all cones of $X$ contain $b$, so $\sigma \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_G(b)|$. Otherwise make the star of $G$ at $f$. Each cone of $X$ contains $f$, so its pieces that contain $b$ cover it, and again $\sigma \subseteq |\mathrm{St}(b)|$. So $\sigma$ is a pivot cone.

Conversely let $\sigma$ be a pivot cone with pivot $b$ and the star sequence $F_0$. If $b$ is a ray of $\tau$, then $\{\tau\}$ is a hit with $f = \langle b \rangle$. Otherwise $b$ is the new ray of a star at a face $f$ of some stage $G$ of $F_0$, and $b = \sum f$. A later star changes a cone that contains $b$ into pieces, and the pieces that contain $b$ lie in the old cone. So $|\mathrm{St}(b)|$ does not grow along $F_0$, and $\sigma \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_{G'}(b)|$ for the fan $G'$ just after the star at $f$. The cones of $G'$ that contain $b$ are the pieces of the cones of $G$ that contain $f$. So the set $X$ of the cones of $G$ that contain $f$ has its union $\supseteq \sigma$. If $f$ is a 3-dimensional cone, $X = \{f\}$. If $f$ is a 2-dimensional cone, $X$ has one or two cones. So $X$ is a hit. It remains to show that $X$ is reached by moves. For each earlier stage $G_r$, let $X_r$ be the set of the cones of $G_r$ that contain a cone of $X$. Since $G$ refines $G_r$, each cone of $X$ lies in one cone of $G_r$, so $X_r$ has one or two cones. If it has two, they meet in a common face that contains $f$, and two distinct 3-dimensional cones meet in at most a 2-dimensional face, so they have a common 2-face. Their union contains $\sigma$. So each $X_r$ is a state, $X_0 = \{\tau\}$, and $X_{r+1}$ is $X_r$ or is reached from $X_r$ by one move. $\square$

Research note 91 points out that the test $\sum f \in B(\sigma)$ belongs in the definition of a hit; note 90 stated the search without it. The test whether the union of two cones with a common 2-face contains $\sigma$ is exact: it cuts $\sigma$ by the plane of the common 2-face and tests each part. The set of states reached from $\{\tau\}$ can be infinite, and then a search with a limit decides nothing. Theorem 3.27.22 shows that this happens.

### Several pivots

The proof of Theorem 3.27.10 needs only two things near $b$: every cone that is not yet matched contains $b$, and the star of $b$ in $\Lambda$ lies in the star of $b$ in $F$. The next theorem uses several such rays at the same time, and it allows cones that are already matched. It is from research note 91.

**Theorem 3.27.17 (the multi-pivot theorem).** Let $\Lambda_0$ be a star sequence of $\sigma$, let $F_0$ be a star sequence of $\tau$, and let $b_1, \dots, b_m$ ($m \ge 0$) be rays of both $\Lambda_0$ and $F_0$. Assume:

- (a) every 3-dimensional cone of $\Lambda_0$ that is not a cone of $F_0$ contains one of the $b_i$;
- (b) $|\mathrm{St}_{\Lambda_0}(b_i)| \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_{F_0}(b_i)|$ for each $i$;
- (c) no cone of $F_0$ contains two of the $b_i$.

Then $\sigma$ has a certificate $(\Lambda, F)$. Here $\Lambda$ is $\Lambda_0$ followed by stars at 2-faces $\langle x, x' \rangle$ with $\langle b_i, x, x' \rangle \in \Lambda$ for some $i$, and $F$ is $F_0$ followed by stars at 2-faces $\langle w, w' \rangle$ with $\langle b_i, w, w' \rangle \in F$ for some $i$ and at 2-faces $\langle b_i, w \rangle$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* We use two facts on simplicial fans. (F1) A ray of a fan that lies in a cone of the fan is a ray of that cone. (F2) If two distinct 3-dimensional cones of a fan have a common 2-face, they lie on the two sides of its plane. Call a 3-dimensional cone *matched* if it is a cone of both $\Lambda$ and $F$.

*Step 0.* No cone of $\Lambda_0$ contains two of the $b_i$. Indeed, let a cone of $\Lambda_0$ contain $b_i$ and $b_j$. Then $b_j \in |\mathrm{St}_{\Lambda_0}(b_i)| \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_{F_0}(b_i)|$, so $b_j$ lies in a cone of $F_0$ with the ray $b_i$, and by (F1) it is a ray of that cone. This is against (c). So by (a) each 3-dimensional cone of $\Lambda_0$ contains exactly one $b_i$ or is matched. During the construction the conditions (a), (b) and (c) stay true for the current pair $(\Lambda, F)$, and so does the conclusion of Step 0, by the same argument. We check the conditions at the end.

*Processing $b_i$.* Process $i = 1, \dots, m$ in turn. To process $i$, do the Steps 1 to 3 of the proof of Theorem 3.27.10 with $b = b_i$, with the quotient map $\pi_i \colon N \to N / \mathbb{Z} b_i$, with $L_F$ the link of $b_i$ in $F$ and with $L_\Lambda$ the link of $b_i$ in $\Lambda$. By (b), $|L_\Lambda| \subseteq |L_F|$, and the boundary of $|L_\Lambda|$ consists of rays of $L_\Lambda$, so Step 2 works as before. In Step 2 take for $R$ the fan that inserts the rays of $L_\Lambda$ into the cones of $L_F$ and subdivides only the cones of $L_F$ that change. Then each cone of $L_F$ that is also a cone of $L_\Lambda$ is a cone of $R$ and of $R_\Lambda$. In dimension 2 a star at a cone removes this cone for ever. So the stars of Lemma 3.27.9 from $L_F$ to $R$ and from $L_\Lambda$ to $R_\Lambda$ are never at a cone that is common to $L_F$ and $L_\Lambda$. We call this fact (R1).

*Key lemma.* Let $K$ be a matched cone with $b_i \notin K$, let $\langle y, y' \rangle$ be a 2-face of $K$, and let $Q = \langle b_i, y, y' \rangle$. If $Q$ is a cone of $\Lambda$ or of $F$, then $Q$ is a cone of both.

First let $Q \in \Lambda$. Then $Q$ and $K$ are on the two sides of $\langle y, y' \rangle$ by (F2). Since $Q \subseteq \tau = |F|$ and $\langle y, y' \rangle$ is a face of the cone $K$ of $F$, there is a 3-dimensional cone $K'$ of $F$ on the side of $Q$ that contains $\langle y, y' \rangle$. Take a point $p$ in the interior of $Q$, so near an interior point of $\langle y, y' \rangle$ that $p$ is in the interior of $K'$. By (b), $p$ lies in a cone of $F$ with the ray $b_i$. A point in the interior of a 3-dimensional cone of a fan lies in no other 3-dimensional cone of the fan, so this cone is $K'$. By (F1), $b_i$ is a ray of $K'$, so $K' = \langle b_i, y, y' \rangle = Q$, and $Q \in F$.

Next let $Q \in F$. Then $Q \ne K$, since $b_i \notin K$. By (F2), $b_i$ and $K$ lie strictly on the two sides of the plane $H$ of $\langle y, y' \rangle$. Both lie in $\sigma$, so $H$ does not support $\sigma$, and there is a 3-dimensional cone $K'$ of $\Lambda$ on the side of $b_i$ that contains $\langle y, y' \rangle$. Take $p$ in the interior of $K'$ and of $Q$, as before. If $K'$ contains no $b_k$, then $K'$ is matched by (a), so $K'$ and $Q$ are cones of $F$ with the common interior point $p$, and $K' = Q \ni b_i$, a contradiction. So $K'$ contains some $b_k$. Then $p \in |\mathrm{St}_\Lambda(b_k)| \subseteq |\mathrm{St}_F(b_k)|$ by (b), and since $p$ is in the interior of $Q$, the cone $Q$ contains $b_k$. By (c), $k = i$. So $K'$ contains $b_i$, $y$ and $y'$, and $K' = Q$. This proves the key lemma.

*Consequence.* While $i$ is processed, no matched cone $K$ with $b_i \notin K$ is changed, in $\Lambda$ or in $F$. Indeed:

- A link star of $F$ at $\langle w, w' \rangle$, with $\langle b_i, w, w' \rangle \in F$, changes only the two cones of $F$ that contain $\langle w, w' \rangle$. If one of them is a matched cone $K$ with $b_i \notin K$, then by the key lemma $\langle b_i, w, w' \rangle \in \Lambda$, so $\pi_i \langle w, w' \rangle$ is common to $L_F$ and $L_\Lambda$, and by (R1) it is not starred.
- A link star of $\Lambda$ at $\langle x, x' \rangle$, with $\langle b_i, x, x' \rangle \in \Lambda$: the same argument with $\Lambda$ and $F$ exchanged.
- A raising star of $F$ at $\langle b_i, w \rangle$ changes only cones that contain $b_i$.

A raising star at $\langle b_i, w_\rho \rangle$ is made only when $\gamma_\rho \ge 1$, that is, when $w_\rho \ne x_\rho$. Then no cone of $\Lambda$ contains both $b_i$ and $w_\rho$, so the raising stars do not change matched cones that contain $b_i$ either.

*The invariants.* (c): each new cone lies in an old cone, and by (F1) its rays among the old rays are rays of the old cone. The new rays are not among the $b_k$. So (c) stays true. (b) for $k \ne i$: a link star at $\langle x, x' \rangle$, on either side, changes a cone with the ray $b_k$ only if this cone is $\langle b_k, x, x' \rangle$, because $b_k \notin \{x, x'\}$ by (c) and Step 0. Both pieces contain $b_k$, so $|\mathrm{St}(b_k)|$ does not change. A raising star at $\langle b_i, w \rangle$ does not change cones with the ray $b_k$, since they do not contain $b_i$. For $k = i$, (b) stays true by Steps 1 to 3 of the proof of Theorem 3.27.10. (a): a link star of $\Lambda$ at $\langle x, x' \rangle$ cuts $\langle b_i, x, x' \rangle$ into pieces with the ray $b_i$. The cone of $\Lambda$ across $\langle x, x' \rangle$, if there is one, is $\langle b_k, x, x' \rangle$ for some $k$, with pieces that contain $b_k$, or a cone with no $b_k$. Such a cone is matched by (a), and by the consequence it is not cut. So each new 3-dimensional cone of $\Lambda$ contains some $b_k$, and the cones with no $b_k$ do not change and stay matched.

*The end.* At the end of the processing of $i$, each cone of $\mathrm{St}_\Lambda(b_i)$ is matched, by Step 3 of the proof of Theorem 3.27.10. Later steps $j > i$ do not change these cones, by the consequence, since they do not contain $b_j$. After the step $m$, each 3-dimensional cone of $\Lambda$ is matched: the cones with some $b_i$ by the step $i$, and the others by (a). So $\Lambda$ is contained in $F$. $\square$

For $m = 1$, with $\Lambda_0$ as in Step 0 of Theorem 3.27.10, Theorem 3.27.17 is Theorem 3.27.10. For $m = 0$ the hypothesis says that $(\Lambda_0, F_0)$ is already a certificate. The new case is $m \ge 2$ with no matched cones: then the hypothesis asks only for inclusions of stars, not for equal cones. Research note 90 expected that the raising steps for two pivots would conflict on a common boundary. The proof shows that they do not: a star of $F$ at $\langle b_1, w \rangle$ changes only cones that contain $b_1$, and the key lemma removes the conflict at a common wall.

**Proposition 3.27.18 (the converse of the multi-pivot theorem).** The following are equivalent.

1. There are $\Lambda_0$, $F_0$ and rays $b_1, \dots, b_m$ that satisfy (b), (c) and (a$'$): every 3-dimensional cone of $\Lambda_0$ contains one of the $b_i$.
2. The cone $\sigma$ has a certificate $(\Lambda, F)$ and a set $T$ of $m$ rays of $\Lambda$ such that no two rays of $T$ span a 2-face of $\Lambda$ and every 3-dimensional cone of $\Lambda$ contains a ray of $T$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* (1) implies (2): Theorem 3.27.17 gives $(\Lambda, F)$, and we take $T = \{b_1, \dots, b_m\}$. Every 3-dimensional cone of the final $\Lambda$ contains some $b_i$, by the invariant (a) of the proof, since at the start no cone is without a $b_i$. Two of the $b_i$ do not span a 2-face of $\Lambda$, since $\Lambda$ is contained in $F$ and (c) holds for the final $F$. (2) implies (1): take $\Lambda_0 = \Lambda$, $F_0 = F$ and the rays of $T$. Then (a$'$) holds, and (b) holds because the cones of $\Lambda$ are cones of $F$. For (c), let a cone of $F$ contain $b_i, b_j \in T$. Then $e = \langle b_i, b_j \rangle$ is a cone of $F$, and $e \subseteq \sigma$. Take $p$ in the relative interior of $e$ and a cone $D$ of $\Lambda$ with $p \in D$. Since $D \in F$, the set $e \cap D$ is a face of $e$ and of $D$. It contains $p$, so it is $e$. So $e$ is a face of $D$, a 2-face of $\Lambda$, against the choice of $T$. $\square$

For $m = 1$, condition (2) says that all cones of $\Lambda$ share a ray. So by Proposition 3.27.11 condition (1) with $m = 1$ is the pivot condition, and the conditions with $m = 1, 2, 3, \dots$ form a hierarchy that starts with it. We call a certificate with a set $T$ as in (2) a certificate of *multi-pivot type with $m$ pivots*.

**Remark 3.27.19 (depth 2 and the shape of $\Lambda$).** The shape of $\Lambda$ alone does not give a common ray, already at depth 2. Let $c$ be the new ray of the star of $\sigma$ at $\sigma$, and $d = c + a_1$ the new ray of the star at $\langle c, a_1 \rangle$. The result has the five cones $\langle d, a_1, a_2 \rangle$, $\langle c, d, a_2 \rangle$, $\langle d, a_1, a_3 \rangle$, $\langle c, d, a_3 \rangle$ and $\langle c, a_2, a_3 \rangle$. They have no common ray, and $\{c, a_1\}$ is a set $T$ as in Proposition 3.27.18(2). In the same way, the star at $\langle a_1, a_2 \rangle$ with the new ray $s$, followed by the star at the cone $\langle s, a_2, a_3 \rangle$ with the new ray $w$, gives four cones with no common ray and the set $T = \{w, a_1\}$. Each shape of depth 2 has a set $T$ with at most 2 rays. So a proof that every cone of depth 2 is a pivot cone must use $F$ as well as the shape of $\Lambda$.

### The route with the strategy "best"

The route of Section 3.26 repairs the bad cones of Definition 3.26.22 on the facets of $C$ by lifted certificates. Counterexample 3.26.24 shows that $\mu = \mu_3$ can go up under the repair of the bad cone with the least entries. Research note 90 tested a different choice, the strategy "best". In each round it tries every bad 3-dimensional cone, takes a certificate from the guided search with a time limit of 20 seconds, makes the certificate repair in the order of the certificate, and keeps the repair with the least pair $(\mu_3, \mu_2)$ in the lexicographic order. When $\mu_3 = 0$ it stars the bad 2-dimensional cone with the least entries at the sum of its rays. It stops after 15 rounds.

**Proposition 3.27.24 (the route data).** Take the random pairs of Theorem 3.26.23 and Counterexample 3.26.24 with the seeds 0 to 9999. Of the 575 pairs with $\mu_3 > 0$ at the start (530 with $\mu_3 = 1$, 42 with $\mu_3 = 2$ and 3 with $\mu_3 = 3$):

1. in 9 pairs the search finds no certificate in the time limit for some bad 3-dimensional cone, and the run stops;
2. in 565 of the other 566 pairs, $\mu_3$ goes down in every round, and it reaches 0 after 1 round (532 pairs), 2 rounds (32 pairs) or 3 rounds (1 pair);
3. for the seed 5332, in the run of note 90, with timeouts for some bad cones, $\mu_3$ went up for 15 rounds. In two later runs of note 91 with the same programs and time limits of 20 and 60 seconds per cone, the pair $(\mu_3, \mu_2)$ goes $(2,5)$, $(2,4)$, $(4,6)$, $(5,6)$, $(5,6)$, $(5,6)$, $(4,4)$, $(2,3)$, $(0,2)$, with certificates of depths 5, 3, 7, 7, 7, 7, 1 and 1. So $\mu_3$ reaches 0 after 8 rounds;
4. after $\mu_3 = 0$, the rule for 2-dimensional cones ends with $\mu = 0$ in 457 of the 565 pairs, and in the other 108 pairs it does not end in 15 rounds, and $\mu_2$ can grow (up to 17);
5. for the seeds 30023 and 31814 of Section 3.26, the strategy "best" ends in 3 and 1 rounds.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Exact computations from research notes 90 and 91. In the runs of note 91 for the seed 5332, $\mu_3$ is computed again with an independent test of direct reachability in each round, and each certificate used is replayed. $\square$

The runs for the seed 5332 agree for three rounds and then differ, because the guided search returns different certificates within the time limit. In the runs of note 91 it found no certificate in time for the bad cones $\langle (6,4,3), (5,2,0), (2,5,8) \rangle$, $\langle (14,13,14), (5,2,0), (23,15,11) \rangle$ and $\langle (14,13,14), (23,15,11), (33,28,28) \rangle$. These are timeouts, not proofs that no certificate exists. So the run of note 90 for this seed is not reproducible, and it is no evidence that the strategy "best" does not end. The entries of the bad cones still grow, to 159 at round 6. A bad 2-dimensional cone is a regular 2-dimensional cone in a facet of $C$ that is not directly reachable in the octant of the facet. This is a problem in dimension 3, so the 2-dimensional theory of Lemma 3.27.9 does not settle it.

For a bad cone of pivot type with a pivot that is one of its rays, the condition of Proposition 3.26.25 becomes a condition on one neighbour only.

**Corollary 3.27.25 (the repair of a bad pivot cone).** Let $u$ be a bad 3-dimensional cone of $\Sigma_2$ in a facet $c$ of $C$, seen in the octant by the basis of $c$. Assume that $u$ is a pivot cone with a pivot $b$ that is a ray of $u$, and let $g$ be the 2-face of $u$ opposite to $b$. Let $(\Lambda, F)$ be the certificate of Theorem 3.27.10. Then every star of $\Lambda$ is at a 2-dimensional cone contained in $g$. So if the 3-dimensional cone $k$ of $\Sigma_2$ in $\partial C$ with $u \cap k = g$ is not in the set $N(u)$ of Proposition 3.26.25, then the certificate repair of $u$ with $(\Lambda, F)$ lowers $\mu_3$ by at least 1.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Since $b$ is a ray of $u$, there is no first star, and $\Lambda_0 = \{u\}$. The stars of $\Lambda$ are at 2-faces $\langle x, x' \rangle$ with $\langle b, x, x' \rangle \in \Lambda$. At the start, $x$ and $x'$ are the two rays of $u$ other than $b$, which span $g$. A star at $\langle x, x' \rangle$ with $x, x' \in g$ adds the ray $x + x' \in g$, and the new cones that contain $b$ have their two other rays in $g$. So by induction all stars of $\Lambda$ are at 2-dimensional cones contained in $g$. A 2-face of $u$ other than $g$ meets $g$ in a ray, so it contains no 2-dimensional cone in $g$. So the only neighbour $k'$ of $u$ for which a star of $\Lambda$ can be at a cone in $u \cap k'$ is $k$. If $k \notin N(u)$, the hypothesis of Proposition 3.26.25 holds, and $\mu_3$ goes down by at least 1. $\square$

Research note 90 stated this only for the 2-faces that lie on the boundary of $u$. Note 91 observed that all of them lie in $g$.

### Oda's strong factorization conjecture

Toric $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ is a special case of a well known open problem.

**Proposition 3.27.26 (Oda's conjecture implies toric $\mathrm{RSF}_3$).** Assume the strong factorization conjecture of Oda in dimension 3: any two regular fans with the same support have a common refinement that is obtained from each of them by a finite sequence of smooth stars. Then every regular 3-dimensional cone $\sigma \subseteq \tau$ has a certificate.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Let $\Delta'$ be a regular fan with support $\tau$ that has $\sigma$ as a cone, for example the fan $\Delta$ in the proof of Proposition 3.25.13. By the conjecture there is a fan $F$ that is a star sequence of the fan $\{\tau\}$ and a star sequence of $\Delta'$. By Lemma 3.26.4, applied to the cone $\sigma$ of $\Delta'$, the fan $\Lambda = F|_\sigma$ is a star sequence of $\sigma$. Its cones are cones of $F$, so $(\Lambda, F)$ is a certificate. $\square$

So a proof of toric $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ in general would prove a case of an open conjecture, and a regular cone without a certificate would disprove the conjecture of Oda in dimension 3. Since the weighted form of the conjecture holds (see below), an obstruction must use that the centers are regular.

The status of the literature is as follows. Research note 91 checked the sources of note 90 on publisher pages, reviews and abstracts, and corrected several citations. We give the corrected forms.

- *The conjecture.* Oda states the strong factorization problem for toric varieties in his book [Oda88]. In dimension 2 it holds, and Lemma 3.27.9 is its local form. In dimension at least 3 it is open in all cases. The introduction of [DSK11] says so, and no later paper that proves it is known to us.
- *Weak factorization.* Weak factorization of toric birational maps, with blowups and blowdowns in any order, is proved by Włodarczyk [Wło97] and by Morelli [Mor96], with the completion of the argument of Morelli by Abramovich, Matsuki and Rashid [AMR99]. In dimension 3 it is earlier, by Danilov [Dan83] and Ewald [Ewa87]. In characteristic 0 weak factorization holds for all varieties [AKMW02], [Wło03].
- *Morelli's strong factorization.* Morelli also claimed strong factorization. The argument has a gap, which K. Karu pointed out, and the gap is recorded in the correction of Matsuki [Mat00]. Research note 90 attributed this remark to [AKMW02] and [AMR99]. Whether [AKMW02] itself states the gap is not checked.
- *Partial results.* Da Silva and Karu [DSK11] give an algorithm that would give strong factorization if it ends, and they prove that it ends in special cases. Machacek [Mac21] proves strong factorization for fans that are refined by the braid arrangement fan.
- *The weighted form.* Adiprasito and Pak [AP26] prove the form of the conjecture with weighted stars at rationally smooth centers, together with the conjecture of Alexander on common stellar subdivisions of triangulations. The smooth form, which toric $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ needs, stays open.
- *Local strong factorization.* Karu proves local strong factorization of toric birational maps in all dimensions [Kar03]. For 3-folds in characteristic 0, local strong factorization is due to Cutkosky [Cut97]. Research note 90 cited the Astérisque volume 260 of Cutkosky for this, but that volume treats the local monomialization of morphisms.
- *Toric principalization.* Principalization of monomial ideals by regular torus-invariant centers is [Gow05].

### What is open

1. Toric $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ for all regular cones in the octant. By Proposition 3.27.26 it is a case of the conjecture of Oda in dimension 3. The first test case is $\sigma_7 = \langle (0,1,6), (1,1,5), (7,1,0) \rangle$, with entries at most 7, for which no certificate is known.
2. The least depths of $O_1$ and $O_2$ (between 7 and 12) and of $D_{10}$ (between 7 and 10), and a bound on the depth in terms of the entries.
3. A complete finite search for the multi-pivot condition of Proposition 3.27.18 with $m \ge 2$, or a class with no certificate of multi-pivot type. For $m = 1$ the state search of Proposition 3.27.16 is complete but need not end, and for $m \ge 2$ the fan $\Lambda_0$ is not determined by the pivots.
4. Is every cone of depth 2 a pivot cone (Remark 3.27.19)?
5. The pivot condition for the 127 classes with entries at most 5 that the state search does not decide (Theorem 3.27.22).
6. A uniform certificate for the classes $A$, $B$, $C$ or for the families $\sigma_n$ and $F_k$. By Theorem 3.27.22 it cannot be of pivot type.
7. Termination of the strategy "best" for bad 3-dimensional cones, a rule for bad 2-dimensional cones, and termination of the route (part (N4) of item 7 in Section 3.25, in the toric case). The seed 5332 is not a counterexample in the reproducible runs.
