## Purely inseparable hypersurfaces

The hypersurfaces $z^p + g(x_1, \dots, x_n) = 0$ in characteristic $p$ are the first test of each method of resolution in positive characteristic. Hauser explains in his survey [Hau10] how the known invariants of characteristic 0 fail on this family, and the cycles of Hauser and Perlega [HP19a] are in the larger family $z^q + g$ with $q = p^e$. The project calls the resolution of this family the *core problem P1*. This section proves that P1 is a special case of the main statement. Thus a resolution of all fourfolds in characteristic $p$ gives a resolution of these hypersurfaces, and a counterexample in the family would refute the main statement.

### The statement

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p > 0$, let $n \ge 0$, and let $g \in k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$. Let
$$A_g = k[z, x_1, \dots, x_n]/(z^p + g), \qquad X_g = \operatorname{Spec} A_g.$$
The projection $(z, x) \mapsto x$ makes $X_g$ a scheme over $\mathbb{A}^n_k$. It is a homeomorphism, and the field extension is purely inseparable when $X_g$ is integral: the $p$-th power of $z$ is $-g$, which is a function on $\mathbb{A}^n_k$.

**Conjecture 1.5.1 (P1).** Let $p$ be a prime and let $n \ge 0$. For each field $k$ of characteristic $p$ and each $g \in k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ such that $X_g$ is reduced, $X_g$ has a resolution.

The hypothesis that $X_g$ is reduced is necessary. Let $k = \mathbb{F}_p$ and $g = x_1^p$. Then $z^p + x_1^p = (z + x_1)^p$, and $X_g$ is not reduced. Over an imperfect field the hypersurface can be reduced, and even a field, when $g$ is a constant. For $k = \mathbb{F}_p(t)$, $n = 0$ and $g = t$, the ring $A_g = k[z]/(z^p + t)$ is the field $K$ of Example 1.1.7, because $-t$ is not a $p$-th power in $k$. So P1 contains the example that shows that regular and smooth are different.

### P1 is a special case

**Lemma 1.5.2.** Let $p \ne 0$. The polynomial $f = z^p + g$ is not zero in $k[z, x_1, \dots, x_n]$.

Lean: `Singularity.p1_equation_ne_zero`

*Proof.* The polynomial $g$ does not contain the variable $z$, so the coefficient of the monomial $z^p$ in $g$ is $0$. Since $p \ne 0$, the monomial $z^p$ is not the constant monomial, and its coefficient in $f$ is $1$. Thus $f \ne 0$. $\square$

The condition $p \ne 0$ is necessary: for $p = 0$ we have $z^0 = 1$, and $f = 1 + g$ can be $0$.

**Lemma 1.5.3.** Let $A$ be a ring and let $f \in A$ be a nonzerodivisor. Then $\dim A/(f) + 1 \le \dim A$.

Lean: `ringKrullDim_quotient_succ_le_of_nonZeroDivisor` (in Mathlib)

*Proof.* Let $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_d$ be a chain of prime ideals of $A$ that contain $f$. It comes from a chain of length $d$ in $A/(f)$. Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a minimal prime ideal of $A$ in $\mathfrak{p}_0$. By Lemma 1.2.3, $f \notin \mathfrak{p}$, so $\mathfrak{p} \ne \mathfrak{p}_0$. Thus $\mathfrak{p} \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_d$ is a chain of length $d + 1$ in $A$. $\square$

**Lemma 1.5.4.** Let $p \ne 0$. Then $\dim X_g \le n$.

Lean: `Singularity.topologicalKrullDim_p1Hypersurface_le`

*Proof.* The polynomial ring $k[z, x_1, \dots, x_n]$ is a domain of dimension $n + 1$ [AM69, Chapter 11]. By Lemma 1.5.2, $f$ is a nonzero element, so it is a nonzerodivisor. By Lemma 1.5.3, $\dim A_g + 1 \le n + 1$. The dimension of $X_g$ is the dimension of $A_g$. $\square$

In fact $\dim X_g = n$, by Krull's principal ideal theorem, but we need only the upper bound.

**Theorem 1.5.5 (P1 is a special case).** Let $p \ne 0$ and $n \ge 0$. If $\mathrm{Res}(p, n)$ holds, then Conjecture 1.5.1 holds for $p$ and $n$. In particular, if $\mathrm{Res}(p, 4)$ holds for each prime $p$, then Conjecture 1.5.1 holds for $n = 4$.

*Proof.* Let $k$ and $g$ be as in Conjecture 1.5.1, with $X_g$ reduced. The scheme $X_g$ is affine, so it is separated over $k$. The ring $A_g$ is a quotient of a polynomial ring in finitely many variables, so $X_g$ is of finite type over $k$. It is reduced by hypothesis. Thus $X_g$ is a variety over $k$, and by Lemma 1.5.4 its dimension is at most $n$. By $\mathrm{Res}(p, n)$ it has a resolution. $\square$

The theorem goes in one direction only. A proof of P1 does not give $\mathrm{Res}(p, 4)$. It is a necessary test for each proposed method: a method that fails on P1 cannot prove the main statement. For $n \le 3$, P1 follows from the theorem of Cossart and Piltant on threefolds (Theorem 2.1.4) [CP08, CP09, CP19], which is not yet in Lean. For $n = 4$ it is open.

### An example

**Example 1.5.6.** Let $g = x_1$. Then $A_g = k[z, x_1, \dots, x_n]/(z^p + x_1) \cong k[z, x_2, \dots, x_n]$, because the relation solves for $x_1$. Thus $X_g \cong \mathbb{A}^n_k$ is regular, and the identity is a resolution. The projection to $\mathbb{A}^n_k$ is the Frobenius map in the first coordinate, a homeomorphism that is not an isomorphism. When $k$ is perfect, the singular points of $X_g$ are the points where all the partial derivatives $\partial g/\partial x_i$ vanish. This follows from the Jacobian criterion, because $\partial f/\partial z = p z^{p-1} = 0$. The Jacobian criterion is in Volume 1.

### The Lean proof

The Lean proof is in the file `Singularity/Fidelity/Core.lean`. The statement is in `Singularity/Statements/Core.lean`. There the variable $z$ is `MvPolynomial.X none` and the variable $x_i$ is `MvPolynomial.X (some i)`, so the polynomial ring has the variables `Option (Fin n)`.

```lean
lemma p1_equation_ne_zero (hp : p ≠ 0) (g : MvPolynomial (Fin n) k) :
    (X none : MvPolynomial (Option (Fin n)) k) ^ p + rename some g ≠ 0

lemma ringKrullDim_p1Ring_le (hp : p ≠ 0) (g : MvPolynomial (Fin n) k) :
    ringKrullDim (P1Ring k p n g) ≤ n

lemma isVariety_p1Hypersurface (g : MvPolynomial (Fin n) k)
    (hred : IsReduced (P1Hypersurface k p n g)) :
    IsVariety (P1Hypersurface k p n g) (p1Structure k p n g)

theorem purelyInseparableHypersurfaceResolution_of_resolutionInDim (hp : p ≠ 0)
    (h : ResolutionInDim.{u} p n) : PurelyInseparableHypersurfaceResolution.{u} p n
```

The first lemma is Lemma 1.5.2. It compares the coefficients of the monomial $z^p$, written `Finsupp.single none p`. The lemma `coeff_rename_eq_zero` says that the coefficient of $z^p$ in `rename some g` is $0$, because no monomial of `rename some g` contains the variable `none`.

The second lemma is Lemma 1.5.4. Lemma 1.5.3 is the Mathlib lemma `ringKrullDim_quotient_succ_le_of_nonZeroDivisor`, and the dimension of the polynomial ring is `MvPolynomial.ringKrullDim_of_isNoetherianRing_of_finite`. The dimension in Mathlib takes values in $\mathbb{N} \cup \{\pm\infty\}$, so the proof needs a small lemma, `le_of_add_one_le_add_one`, to cancel the $+1$ in that type.

The third lemma is the first part of the proof of Theorem 1.5.5. Finite type comes from the fact that the algebra map from $k$ to a quotient of a polynomial ring is of finite type. Quasi-compactness and separatedness of an affine scheme over an affine scheme are instances of Mathlib.

The theorem combines the lemmas. A second theorem, `purelyInseparableHypersurfaceResolution_four`, gives the case $n = 4$ from the open statement `ResolutionDimFourCharP`.

### Notes and references

The family $z^p + g$ and its role in the problem are explained in [Hau10]. Hauser and Perlega [HP19a] give hypersurfaces in the larger family $z^q + g$ with $q = p^e$ for which a sequence of natural blowups comes back to a singularity of the same type, so that a naive induction on an invariant does not end. For the dimension of a polynomial ring see [AM69, Chapter 11]. Lemma 1.5.3 holds for every ring. For the principal ideal theorem see [Mat, Theorem 13.5].
