## Integral varieties in characteristic 0

Hironaka proved in 1964 that each variety over a field of characteristic 0 has a resolution [Hir64]. The project does not prove this theorem again. The Lean library resolutionorg/hironaka [HirLean], which we call *the base*, has a Lean proof of Hironaka's Main Theorem I for integral varieties. The base uses its own definitions, and its theorem does not have the form of our Definition 2.1.5 of Volume 0. This section proves that the morphism that the base gives is a resolution in our sense. Most of the work is a lemma about invertible ideal sheaves, Lemma 1.2.4.

### Blowups

We recall the facts about blowups that we need. Let $X$ be a Noetherian scheme and let $\mathcal{I} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ be a coherent ideal sheaf. Let $Z = V(\mathcal{I})$ be the closed subscheme that $\mathcal{I}$ defines, so that the support of $\mathcal{I}$ in the sense of this section is the closed set $Z$. The *blowup* of $X$ along $\mathcal{I}$ is
$$\pi : \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal{I}} X = \operatorname{Proj} \bigoplus_{d \ge 0} \mathcal{I}^d \longrightarrow X.$$

**Proposition 1.2.1 (properties of the blowup).** Let $X$, $\mathcal{I}$ and $\pi$ be as above.

Lean: `Scheme.IdealSheafData.blowUp.isProper_π`, `blowUp.isIso_π_restrict_compl_support` and `blowUp.isInvertible_comap_π` (in the base)

1. $\pi$ is proper.
2. Let $U = X \setminus Z$. Then $\pi^{-1}(U) \to U$ is an isomorphism.
3. The inverse image ideal sheaf $\mathcal{E} = \pi^{-1}\mathcal{I} \cdot \mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Bl}}$ is invertible. That is, each point of the blowup has an affine open neighbourhood $\operatorname{Spec} A$ on which $\mathcal{E}$ is generated by one element $g \in A$ that is a nonzerodivisor.

For a proof see [Har, II.7.13] for parts 2 and 3, and [Har, Section II.7] for part 1: the blowup is projective over $X$, and a projective morphism to a Noetherian scheme is proper. The base proves all three parts in Lean. The closed set $V(\mathcal{E}) = \pi^{-1}(Z)$ is the *exceptional divisor*.

### Main Theorem I

**Theorem 1.2.2 (Hironaka, Main Theorem I, integral form).** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic 0 and let $X$ be an integral variety over $k$. Then there is a coherent ideal sheaf $\mathcal{I}$ on $X$ such that the support of $\mathcal{I}$ is the singular locus $\operatorname{Sing}(X) = X \setminus \operatorname{Reg}(X)$ and the blowup $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal{I}} X$ is regular.

In the base the theorem is `AlgebraicGeometry.exists_support_eq_singularLocus_isRegular_blowUp`. The project plans the name above for the same theorem in its own vocabulary.

Hironaka's own Main Theorem I gives a finite sequence of blowups along regular centres. The base states the theorem in the form above, with one ideal sheaf whose support is the singular locus. We use it as a black box. The proof is the subject of Volume 2, which follows Kollár's book [Kol07]. For the meaning of a proof in the base, and for the checks that the project runs on it, see Volume 0, Chapter 1.

The theorem gives a morphism $\pi : Y = \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal{I}} X \to X$ with $Y$ regular. We must still show the conditions 2, 3 and 4 of a resolution. Conditions 2 and 3 follow from Proposition 1.2.1. Condition 4 needs a lemma.

### The complement of an exceptional divisor is dense

**Lemma 1.2.3.** Let $A$ be a ring, let $g \in A$ be a nonzerodivisor, and let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a minimal prime ideal of $A$. Then $g \notin \mathfrak{p}$. Consequently the basic open set $D(g)$ is dense in $\operatorname{Spec} A$.

Lean: `Singularity.Base.dense_basicOpen_of_mem_nonZeroDivisors`

*Proof.* Assume that $g \in \mathfrak{p}$. The ring $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ has only one prime ideal, $\mathfrak{p} A_{\mathfrak{p}}$, because $\mathfrak{p}$ is minimal (use [AM69, Corollary 3.13]). Thus $\mathfrak{p} A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is the nilradical of $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$, and the image of $g$ in $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$ is nilpotent. So there are $s \notin \mathfrak{p}$ and $n \ge 1$ with $g^n s = 0$ in $A$. Choose the least such $n$. Then $g^{n-1}s \ne 0$: for $n = 1$ this is $s \ne 0$, which holds because $s \notin \mathfrak{p}$. But $g \cdot g^{n-1}s = 0$, so $g$ is a zerodivisor. This is a contradiction.

For the second statement, let $W \subseteq \operatorname{Spec} A$ be a nonempty open set and let $\mathfrak{q} \in W$. Each prime ideal contains a minimal prime ideal, so there is a minimal prime $\mathfrak{p} \subseteq \mathfrak{q}$. Then $\mathfrak{q}$ is in the closure of the point $\mathfrak{p}$. An open set that contains a point of the closure of $\{\mathfrak{p}\}$ contains $\mathfrak{p}$, so $\mathfrak{p} \in W$. By the first part, $g \notin \mathfrak{p}$, so $\mathfrak{p} \in W \cap D(g)$. Thus $D(g)$ meets each nonempty open set. $\square$

For a Noetherian ring the first part is the familiar fact that the minimal primes are associated primes, so they consist of zerodivisors [Mat, Theorem 6.1 and Theorem 6.5]. The proof above does not need the Noetherian hypothesis.

**Lemma 1.2.4.** Let $Y$ be a scheme and let $\mathcal{E}$ be an ideal sheaf on $Y$ that is locally generated by one nonzerodivisor. Then $Y \setminus V(\mathcal{E})$ is dense in $Y$.

Lean: `Singularity.Base.dense_compl_support_of_locally_nonZeroDivisor`

*Proof.* Let $V \subseteq Y$ be a nonempty open set and let $y \in V$. Choose an affine open set $\operatorname{Spec} A \ni y$ on which $\mathcal{E}$ is generated by a nonzerodivisor $g \in A$. On $\operatorname{Spec} A$ the closed set $V(\mathcal{E})$ is $V(g)$, so its complement is $D(g)$. The set $W = V \cap \operatorname{Spec} A$ is open in $\operatorname{Spec} A$ and contains $y$. By Lemma 1.2.3 it meets $D(g)$. Thus $V$ meets $Y \setminus V(\mathcal{E})$. $\square$

### The theorem

**Theorem 1.2.5 (integral varieties in characteristic 0).** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic 0 and let $X$ be an integral variety over $k$. Then $X$ has a resolution. More exactly, let $\mathcal{I}$ be as in Theorem 1.2.2. Then the blowup $\pi : \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal{I}} X \to X$ is a resolution of $X$.

Node: SG0000001Z · Lean: `Singularity.Base.integralResolutionInDim_zero`

*Proof.* Let $Y = \operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal{I}} X$ and let $Z$ be the support of $\mathcal{I}$. A scheme of finite type over a field is Noetherian, so Proposition 1.2.1 applies. We examine the four conditions.

1. $Y$ is regular by Theorem 1.2.2.
2. $\pi$ is proper by Proposition 1.2.1.
3. By Theorem 1.2.2, $Z = \operatorname{Sing}(X)$, so $\operatorname{Reg}(X) = X \setminus Z$. This set is open, because $Z$ is closed. By Proposition 1.2.1, $\pi$ is an isomorphism over it.
4. The inverse image of $\operatorname{Reg}(X)$ is $Y \setminus \pi^{-1}(Z) = Y \setminus V(\mathcal{E})$, where $\mathcal{E}$ is the inverse image ideal sheaf. By Proposition 1.2.1 the sheaf $\mathcal{E}$ is invertible, and by Lemma 1.2.4 the set $Y \setminus V(\mathcal{E})$ is dense. $\square$

The theorem gives the integral case of $\mathrm{Res}(0, n)$ for each $n$. To get $\mathrm{Res}(0, n)$ itself we must pass from integral varieties to reduced varieties. This is Theorem 1.3.1.

**Theorem 1.2.6 (characteristic 0).** $\mathrm{Res}(0, n)$ holds for each $n$.

*Proof.* Theorem 1.2.5 is the hypothesis of Theorem 1.3.1 for $p = 0$. $\square$

In Lean, `Singularity.resolutionInDim_zero` takes `ReductionToIntegral` as an argument, and `Singularity.resolutionInDim_charZero` applies it to `Singularity.Reduction.reductionToIntegral`.

### An example

**Example 1.2.7 (the node).** Let $k$ be a field of characteristic 0 and let $X \subset \mathbb{A}^2_k$ be the curve $y^2 = x^2(x + 1)$. It is integral, and its only singular point is the origin $O$. Let $\mathcal{I} = (x, y)$, the ideal of $O$. The blowup has two affine charts. In the chart with $y = tx$ the total transform is $x^2(t^2 - x - 1) = 0$. The exceptional divisor is $x = 0$, and the blowup of $X$ is the curve $t^2 = x + 1$, a smooth parabola. It meets the exceptional divisor in the two points $t = \pm 1$, which lie over the two branches of the node. In the other chart, with $x = sy$, the blowup is $1 = s^2(sy + 1)$. It meets the exceptional divisor $y = 0$ in the points $s = \pm 1$. These are the same two points, because $s = 1/t$, and the curve is smooth there. Thus $Y$ is regular, $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $X \setminus \{O\}$, and the inverse image of $X \setminus \{O\}$ is $Y$ minus two points, which is dense. Here the ideal sheaf of Theorem 1.2.2 can be the ideal of the singular point. For a general variety the theorem chooses a more complicated ideal sheaf with the same support.

### The Lean proof

The Lean proof is in the file `base/SingularityBase/CharZero.lean` of `tamnd/singularity-lean`. It is in a separate Lake package, `SingularityBase`, because it depends on the base, and the base is a large build. The main package `Singularity` does not depend on the base. Thus a reader can check the statements and the other proofs without the base.

```lean
lemma dense_basicOpen_of_mem_nonZeroDivisors {A : Type*} [CommRing A] {g : A}
    (hg : g ∈ nonZeroDivisors A) :
    Dense (PrimeSpectrum.basicOpen g : Set (PrimeSpectrum A))

lemma dense_compl_support_of_locally_nonZeroDivisor {Y : Scheme.{u}} (J : Y.IdealSheafData)
    (hJ : ∀ y : Y, ∃ U : Y.affineOpens, y ∈ U.1 ∧
      ∃ g ∈ nonZeroDivisors Γ(Y, U), J.ideal U = Ideal.span {g}) :
    Dense ((J.support : Set Y)ᶜ)

theorem exists_isResolution_of_isIntegral {k : Type u} [Field k] [CharZero k] (X : Scheme.{u})
    (f : X ⟶ Spec (.of k)) [hX : IsVariety X f] [IsIntegral X] :
    ∃ (Y : Scheme.{u}) (π : Y ⟶ X), IsResolution π

theorem integralResolutionInDim_zero (n : ℕ) : IntegralResolutionInDim.{u} 0 n
```

The first lemma is Lemma 1.2.3. For the first part it uses the Mathlib lemma `Ideal.disjoint_nonZeroDivisors_of_mem_minimalPrimes`, which is the statement that a minimal prime contains no nonzerodivisor. The second part is the argument with specialization above.

The second lemma is Lemma 1.2.4. The hypothesis `hJ` is the form in which the base states that the exceptional divisor is invertible (`blowUp.isInvertible_comap_π`). The proof moves a point of $\operatorname{Spec} A$ to $Y$ with the map `IsAffineOpen.fromSpec`, and it uses the fact of Mathlib that the inverse image of the support of $\mathcal{E}$ in $\operatorname{Spec} A$ is the zero locus of the ideal.

The third declaration is Theorem 1.2.5. It first turns our `IsVariety` into the object `AlgScheme k` of the base, then calls Main Theorem I, and then fills in the four fields of `IsResolution`. The fields `proper` and `isIso_over` use the base lemmas `blowUp.isProper_π` and `blowUp.isIso_π_restrict_compl_support`. The field `dense` uses the second lemma. One more short lemma, `regularLocus_eq_compl_singularLocus`, identifies our regular locus with the complement of the singular locus of the base. Both are defined by the same condition on the local rings, so the proof is `simp`.

The last theorem changes the hypothesis `CharP k 0` of our statement into the hypothesis `CharZero k` of the base.

### Notes and references

Main Theorem I is in Hironaka's paper [Hir64]. The modern proofs, which give a resolution that is functorial for smooth morphisms, are in [Kol07]. The base formalizes Hironaka's original proof with the invariants of the paper. Example 1.2.7 is a standard example of a blowup, see [Har, Example I.4.9.1] for the same curve. Lemma 1.2.4 is the reason why condition 4 of a resolution holds for each blowup of a Noetherian scheme: the exceptional divisor of a blowup never contains a component of the blowup.
