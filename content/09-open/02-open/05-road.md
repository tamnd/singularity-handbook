## The road to dimension four

This section is a guide for a student who wants to work on resolution of singularities of fourfolds in characteristic $p$. It says how a proof is expected to go, which parts of it are proved, which parts are false, and which parts are open. It does not give a proof of $\mathrm{Res}(p, 4)$, and none is known. The rest of this chapter and Chapter 3 contain the details. Here we put them in one order, and we give a map of the routes.

Each claim in this section has one of four marks. A claim that is *proved in the handbook* has a statement with a number and a node of the map, and we give both. A claim that is *cited* is proved in a published source or in a research note of the project, and it is not yet a statement of the handbook. We give the source, and for a research note we say if a referee report has checked it. A claim that is *false* has a counterexample, and we give it. A claim that is *open* has no proof and no counterexample. Some open claims are conjectures that the project expects to be true, and some are questions with no expected answer. We say which.

The project keeps its research notes in the repository of the map. A reference such as "note 45" is to the research note with this number. A result of a note is a cited result in the sense above, and it is not part of the handbook until a section of the handbook proves it.

### The statement

We recall the definitions of Section 1. A *variety* over a field $k$ is a reduced scheme that is separated and of finite type over $k$. A *resolution* of a scheme $X$ is a proper morphism $\pi \colon Y \to X$ with $Y$ regular, such that the regular locus $\operatorname{Reg}(X)$ is open, $\pi$ is an isomorphism over it, and $\pi^{-1}(\operatorname{Reg}(X))$ is dense in $Y$.

**Definition 2.5.1.** Let $p$ be a prime and let $n \ge 0$.

1. $\mathrm{Res}(p, n)$ is the statement: each variety of dimension at most $n$ over a field of characteristic $p$ has a resolution. When $p$ is fixed we write $\mathrm{Res}_n$.
2. $\mathrm{Emb}_m$ is the statement: each reduced hypersurface $D$ in a regular variety $W$ of dimension $m + 1$ over a field of characteristic $p$ has an embedded resolution. That is, there is a finite sequence of blowups $W_r \to \dots \to W_0 = W$ in regular centers such that the total transform of $D$ in $W_r$ is a divisor with simple normal crossings.
3. $\mathrm{LU}_n$ is local uniformization in dimension $n$: for each function field $K$ of transcendence degree $n$ over $k$, each valuation $v$ of $K$ trivial on $k$ and each finite set $S$ in the valuation ring $\mathcal{O}_v$, there is a regular local ring $A \subset K$, essentially of finite type over $k$, with fraction field $K$, dominated by $\mathcal{O}_v$ and with $S \subset A$. The form $\mathrm{LU}_4^-$ without the finite set $S$ is Definition 3.24.1 (node SG000000BS).
4. $\mathrm{Patch}_n$ is the statement: $\mathrm{LU}_n$ implies $\mathrm{Res}_n$. This is the patching problem of Zariski [Zar44].

The problem of this volume is $\mathrm{Res}(p, 4)$. It is Conjecture 2.1.1, with the node SG00000007 and the Lean statement `Singularity.ResolutionDimFourCharP`. Status: open for each prime $p$. Section 3.24 works with a form of it over an algebraically closed field $k$: $\mathrm{Res}_4^{\rm pr}$ says that each variety $X$ of dimension 4 has a projective birational morphism $X' \to X$ with $X'$ regular. This form does not ask that $X' \to X$ is an isomorphism over $\operatorname{Reg}(X)$.

The known cases are these. $\mathrm{Res}(p, n)$ holds for $n \le 3$ and each $p$, by the work of Cossart and Piltant [CP08, CP09] (cited; Theorems 2.1.4 and 2.1.5, nodes SG0000001V and SG0000001F, which the handbook states and does not prove). Cossart and Piltant also resolve each quasi-excellent scheme of dimension 3 [CP19]. Cossart, Jannsen and Saito give a canonical embedded resolution of each reduced closed subscheme of dimension at most 2 of a regular excellent scheme [CJS20] (cited; Theorem 2.1.3, node SG0000001C). Note 02 uses their Corollary 0.4 for $\mathrm{Emb}_2$. $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ is open for each $p$, and Section 3.29 shows that it is the central open node of the road. $\mathrm{LU}_4$ is Conjecture 2.3.1 (node SG0000001H), and it is open. A form of $\mathrm{Patch}_4$ is Conjecture 2.3.2 (node SG0000001J), and it is open. In characteristic 0 the statement $\mathrm{Patch}_n$ is true, because $\mathrm{Res}_n$ is true there, but no proof of it by the method of patching is known for $n \ge 4$.

### Why dimension four in characteristic p is hard

The proofs of characteristic 0 use an induction on the dimension. At a point of maximal order $d$ of a hypersurface they choose a regular hypersurface $V$ of *maximal contact*: $V$ contains all the nearby points of order $d$, and it keeps this property after each permissible blowup. The problem then goes down to $V$. Section 2.2 explains why each step of this method fails in characteristic $p$. Three failures matter most in dimension 4.

*No maximal contact.* Narasimhan gave a hypersurface in $\mathbb{A}^4$ in characteristic 2 whose points of order 2 near the origin lie on no regular hypersurface germ, also not on a formal one [Nar83]. This is Example 2.2.1 (proved in the handbook, node SG00000018, with a Lean proof `Singularity.Examples.narasimhan`). The curve of points of order 2 in that example has embedding dimension 4, so no induction on a hypersurface sees all of it. Section 3.21 gives a related question for germs of order less than $p$ (item 5 of its list of open problems).

*The residual order goes up.* For an equation $f = z^q + F(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ with $q = p^e$, Volume 7, Section 1.3, defines the residual order $d$: write the clean part of $F$ as $x^r G$ with a monomial $x^r$ in the components of the divisor, and put $d = \operatorname{ord} G$. In characteristic 0 the analogue of $d$ does not go up at points where the order stays. In characteristic $p$ it can go up. Moh's bound, Volume 7, Theorem 1.4.1 (proved in the handbook for point blowups, node SG00000015), says that $d' \le d + p^{e-1}$ after a point blowup [Moh87]. Volume 7, Example 1.4.2 (node SG00000016) is a point blowup with $p = 2$ and $q = 4$ where $d$ goes from 5 to 7, so the bound is sharp there. A point after a blowup where the order stays and the residual order goes up is a *kangaroo point*, in the terms of Hauser [Hau10]. For $q = p$, Volume 7, Corollary 3.2.5 gives $d' \le d + 1$, and Volume 7, Section 3.2, describes the kangaroo points with the log-Jacobian ideal.

*Unbounded growth.* Hauser and Perlega found an equation $z^{p^3} + F$ in four variables for $p \ge 3$, and in five variables for $p = 2$, with an infinite sequence of point blowups along which the order stays $p^3$ and the residual order tends to infinity [HP19a]. This is Volume 7, Theorem 1.4.3 (cited, node SG00000017). Volume 7, Example 1.4.5 (node SG00000039) certifies one cycle of it in Lean. The examples do not refute resolution, because larger centers are permissible at some steps (Volume 7, Remark 1.4.4). But they show that point blowups and the pair (order, residual order) are not enough. Volume 7, Theorem 3.20.18, makes this precise; see the track HS below.

Two more failures are in Section 2.2: over an imperfect field a regular variety need not be smooth (Example 2.2.2, node SG0000000C), and a valuation can have defect (Example 2.2.3). The second one is the subject of the track VD below. The first one comes back in the global step: in Section 3.24 the local rings at the generic point of a curve are regular local rings of dimension 3 over a field that is not perfect.

### The two local cores

A proof of $\mathrm{Res}_4$ by a known route meets one of two local problems. Both are about purely inseparable equations.

**Definition 2.5.2.** Let $e \ge 1$. The statement $\mathrm{Core}_3(e)$ says: for each regular local ring $R$ of dimension 3, essentially of finite type over a perfect field $k$ of characteristic $p$, each divisor $E$ with simple normal crossings on $\operatorname{Spec} R$, and each $g \in R$, the hypersurface $z^{p^e} + g = 0$ in $\operatorname{Spec} R[z]$ has an embedded resolution by blowups in regular centers that is compatible with $E$.

The first core is $\mathrm{Emb}_3$, and inside it the statements $\mathrm{Core}_3(e)$. At a point where the multiplicity is prime to $p$, the Tschirnhausen transformation gives a hypersurface of maximal contact, as in characteristic 0. At a point where the multiplicity is $p^e$, the local equation can be put in the form $z^{p^e} + g$, and this is $\mathrm{Core}_3(e)$. At a point where the multiplicity is $p^e m_0$ with $e \ge 1$ and $p \nmid m_0 > 1$, the two cases mix. Note 77, Proposition 77.13, proves that $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ follows from two statements: $\mathrm{MD}_3$, which says that blowups in regular centers in the locus of maximal multiplicity make the maximal multiplicity of a reduced hypersurface of a regular 4-fold drop, with a boundary divisor; and $\mathrm{SNC}_3$, which says that a regular hypersurface becomes a divisor with simple normal crossings together with the boundary (cited; note 82, the referee report on note 77, checks it). Note 77, Example 77.15, shows that $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ needs centers of dimension 2, and Proposition 77.16 proves $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ at the generic points of surfaces (cited, checked in note 82). So the hard part of $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ is $\mathrm{MD}_3$, and $\mathrm{Core}_3(e)$ and the mixed case are its local parts. Status: each $\mathrm{Core}_3(e)$ is open, and so are $\mathrm{MD}_3$ and $\mathrm{SNC}_3$.

The second core is a theorem of Cossart, one dimension higher. Let $Y$ be a regular variety of dimension $n$ over a perfect field $k$ of characteristic $p$, with a divisor $E$ with simple normal crossings, and let $g \in \mathcal{O}_Y$. Locally, with coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_n$ and $E = V(\prod_{i \in \Delta} x_i)$, Volume 7, Definition 3.1.1 (node SG0000003E) defines the log-Jacobian ideal $\Theta(g)$, the ideal generated by $x_i \partial_i g$ for $i \in \Delta$ and by $\partial_j g$ for $j \notin \Delta$. It does not change when we add a $p$-th power to $g$. For a clean $g = x^r G$ it is $x^r \widetilde\Theta(G)$, and Volume 7, Definition 3.1.3 (node SG0000003F) defines $\rho = \operatorname{ord} \widetilde\Theta(G)$. Volume 7, Section 3.4, gives two conditions (H1) and (H2) on a regular center $C$: (H1) says that $\widetilde\Theta(G)$ is equimultiple along $C$, and (H2) says the same for a second ideal $\widetilde\Theta_C(G)$ in one case.

**Conjecture 2.5.3 (Cossart's theorem on a base of dimension 4).** Let $Y$ be a regular variety of dimension 4 over a perfect field of characteristic $p$, let $E$ be a divisor on $Y$ with simple normal crossings, and let $g \in \mathcal{O}_Y$ be locally not a $p$-th power. Then there is a finite sequence of blowups of $Y$ in regular centers that satisfy (H1) and (H2), with $E$ replaced at each step by its total transform, such that at the end $\rho = 0$ at each point of the locus where $z^p + g$ has multiplicity $p$.

Status: open. For $\dim Y = 3$ the statement is the theorem of Cossart's thesis [Cos87], and Posva gives a global form of it [Pos24] (cited). This is the problem that Volume 7, Chapter 3, works on. The following parts of the proof in dimension 3 hold in each dimension, and so in dimension 4 (proved in the handbook):

1. the dichotomy $d - 1 \le \rho \le d$, Volume 7, Theorem 3.1.4 (node SG0000003F);
2. the law of transformation under a point blowup, Volume 7, Theorem 3.2.3 (node SG0000003M), and the fact that $\rho$ does not go up, Theorem 3.2.4 (node SG0000003N);
3. the same for a regular center with (H1) and (H2), Volume 7, Theorem 3.4.5 (node SG0000004Q), and Example 3.4.6 (node SG0000004R) shows that (H2) is necessary;
4. the end case: at a point with $\rho = 0$ the equation is binomial, $z^p + x^r$ or $z^p + x^r x_j$, Volume 7, Theorem 3.5.3 (node SG0000004S);
5. the equality of $\rho$ with the invariant $\omega$ of Cossart and Piltant, Volume 7, Proposition 3.6.1 (node SG0000004T).

*Remark.* The end case is also the open part of route V$'$ below. Let $(A, a, \nu)$ be an instance of $\mathrm{PILU}_4$. The statement (D) of Definition 3.29.21 holds if and only if some model along $\nu$ has a regular system of parameters and a boundary with $\rho = 0$ for $a$ (Theorem 3.31.2 and Corollary 3.31.5). So (D) is the end case of Conjecture 2.5.3 along one valuation, with arbitrary models and no conditions (H1) and (H2) on the centers, and the strong form of $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$ gives (D). Section 3.31 proves (D) when $\nu$ is discrete of rank one on $L$ (Proposition 3.31.19).

What does not carry over is the case analysis of Cossart in dimension 3. It uses a secondary invariant with a finite number of values and treats each value by hand. In dimension 4 the runs of blowups where $\rho$ stays are much richer, and Volume 7, Sections 3.7 to 3.19, studies them. For the route V below only a form along one valuation is necessary. Note 77 calls it $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$: for a regular local ring $A$ of dimension 4, an element $g \in A$ that is not a $p$-th power in the fraction field, and a valuation $\mu$ that dominates $A$, there is a sequence of local blowups along $\mu$ in regular centers with (H1) and (H2) after which $\rho = 0$ at the center of $\mu$, if the multiplicity of $z^p + g$ is still $p$ there. Conjecture 2.5.3 implies $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$. Status of $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$: open.

### The routes to Res(p, 4)

The global part of the problem is now one statement. Section 3.24 proves the following, over an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic $p$ (proved in the handbook).

1. Theorem 3.24.4 (node SG000000BS): $\mathrm{LU}_4^-$ and $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ imply $\mathrm{Res}_4^{\rm pr}$, and conversely $\mathrm{Res}_4^{\rm pr}$ implies $\mathrm{LU}_4$ and $\mathrm{Fund}_4$. Here $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ is Zariski's fundamental theorem in dimension 4, Conjecture 3.24.3 (node SG000000BQ): two normal projective models of a function field of dimension 4 are dominated by a third normal projective model on which each valuation with a regular center on one of the two has a regular center.
2. Theorem 3.24.5 (node SG000000BT): $\mathrm{Res}_4^{\rm pr}$ holds if and only if $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ and $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ hold. Here $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ is Conjecture 3.24.2 (node SG000000BR), local uniformization for one purely inseparable cover $L(a^{1/p})$ of the fraction field $L$ of a regular local ring $A$ of dimension 4, with $A[a^{1/p}]$ in the regular local ring. The proof uses the theorem of Temkin on inseparable local uniformization [Tem13, Theorem 1.3.2] and the descent of note 02, Theorem 2.6.
3. Corollary 3.24.6: $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ implies Conjecture 3.21.8, the patching statement of the track TT.

These results are equivalences, so they are a reformulation and not a reduction. Their content is that the step from local uniformization to resolution of 4-folds is exactly $\mathrm{Fund}_4$. Note 77 identifies $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ as the weakest link of the whole plan: each route below needs it, and only the track FP works on it. Status of $\mathrm{Fund}_4$: open, and no reduction of it to simpler statements is known.

There are three routes to the local half and one route to $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ that the project has tested. We give each with its status.

*Route V, for each $p$.* Note 77, Proposition 77.12, claims that $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$ implies $\mathrm{PILU}_4$. With Theorem 3.24.5 this gives $\mathrm{Res}_4^{\rm pr}$ from $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$ and $\mathrm{Fund}_4$. Note 82, the referee report on note 77, finds a gap. The proof uses the step "if the multiplicity of $z^p - a$ is less than $p$, then the normalization of $A[a^{1/p}]$ is regular", and this step is false. The counterexample is Example 82.4 of note 82: let $p \ge 5$, $A = k[x, y, u, w]_{(x, y, u, w)}$ and $a = x^2 + y^3$. Then $A[a^{1/p}] = A[z]/(z^p - x^2 - y^3)$ has multiplicity $2 < p$ at the origin, it is normal, and it is singular at the origin. Status of route V: a conditional statement (cited, note 82). It holds with a stronger form of $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$ that asks for $\rho = 0$, or for a regular normalization, at each multiplicity. The missing case, $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ at the points where the multiplicity of $z^p - a$ is less than $p$, is open. It is problem (O7) of note 82. Section 3.29 changes this picture. Given $\mathrm{ELU}_3$, embedded local uniformization of 3-folds, which follows from $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ (Lemma 3.29.28), $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ holds for each valuation without defect, in each multiplicity (Theorem 3.29.19). For valuations with defect it holds if the statement (D) of Definition 3.29.21 holds, and (D) is open (Problem 3.29.23). The multiplicity condition of (O7) plays no role, and the new route does not use $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$. Example 82.4 itself is solved: three blowups make $a$ monomial with the exponents 2, 3 and 6, which are prime to $p$ for $p \ge 5$ (Example 3.29.25). Section 3.31 shows that (D) is the end case $\rho = 0$ on some model along the valuation (Corollary 3.31.5), so the strong form of $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$ also gives (D). It proves (D) when the valuation is discrete of rank one on $L$, with or without defect (Proposition 3.31.19). So (D) is open only for the value groups other than $\mathbb{Z}$ (Problem 3.31.21).

*Route J, for $p > 24$.* Proposition 77.13 of note 77 gives $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ from $\mathrm{MD}_3$ and $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ (cited, checked in note 82). $\mathrm{Emb}_3$ gives $\mathrm{Emb}(\mathbb{P}^4)$ of Definition 3.21.1 (node SG0000007W). Theorem 3.21.7 (proved in the handbook with three cited hypotheses, node SG00000082) gives $\mathrm{LU}_4^-$ from $\mathrm{Emb}(\mathbb{P}^4)$ for $p > 24$. Theorem 3.24.4 then gives $\mathrm{Res}_4^{\rm pr}$ with $\mathrm{Fund}_4$. So $\mathrm{MD}_3$, $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ and $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ give $\mathrm{Res}_4^{\rm pr}$ for $p > 24$. The bound comes from the projection to $\mathbb{P}^4$: each function field of dimension 4 has a normal projective model whose points have multiplicity at most $4! = 24$ [Cut09, Theorem 1.4], and the method needs this multiplicity to be less than $p$ (note 82, Section 5.1). Status: the implications are proved or cited; $\mathrm{MD}_3$, $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ and $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ are open. Proposition 77.13 gives $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ of Definition 3.29.1, without the isomorphism clause over the snc locus (Remark 3.29.4). If the centers of $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ lie in the non-snc locus, the same proof gives $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$, and this also gives $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ (Theorem 3.29.11).

*Route A, through a regular cover.* Let $\mathrm{AO}_n$ be the conjecture of Abramovich and Oort: for each variety $X$ of dimension $n$ there are a regular variety $Y$ and a proper surjective morphism $Y \to X$ with $k(Y)/k(X)$ finite and purely inseparable. Let $\mathrm{FQ}_n$ be the statement: for each regular variety $Y$ of dimension $n$ and each saturated $p$-closed foliation $\mathcal{F}$ on $Y$ (see the track FO below), the quotient $Y/\mathcal{F}$ has a resolution. Let $\mathrm{PIRes}_n$ be the statement: the normalization of each regular variety $Y$ of dimension $n$ in $k(Y)(b^{1/p})$ has a resolution. Note 02, Theorem 2.9, proves that $\mathrm{AO}_n$ and $\mathrm{FQ}_n$ give $\mathrm{Res}_n$ (cited). Note 02, Corollary 2.10, claims that $\mathrm{AO}_n$ and $\mathrm{PIRes}_n$ give $\mathrm{Res}_n$. Note 58 found a gap in its proof: for a foliation of rank one on an $n$-fold the quotient is a cover of degree $p^{n-1}$ of the Frobenius twist, not of degree $p$. Note 73, the referee report on note 58, proves that $\mathrm{PIRes}_n$ implies $\mathrm{FQ}_n$ by a different argument that uses only steps over regular bases (its Proposition 5.1). So Corollary 2.10 is true (cited, note 73). The local equation of $\mathrm{PIRes}_4$ is $z^p + g$ over a regular 4-fold, which is the equation of Conjecture 2.5.3. Status: $\mathrm{AO}_4$, $\mathrm{FQ}_4$ and $\mathrm{PIRes}_4$ are open. This route does not pass through $\mathrm{Fund}_4$, but $\mathrm{AO}_4$ is a global statement of the same depth.

*The route of Cutkosky to $\mathrm{Fund}_4$.* Theorem 3.24.13 (node SG000000C1) proves that principalization of ideals on regular 4-folds, $\mathrm{Princ}_4$ (Conjecture 3.24.7, node SG000000BV), and a factorization statement $\mathrm{Fac}_3^L$ for the regular local rings of dimension 3 at the generic points of curves (Definition 3.24.12, node SG000000BZ) give the conclusion of $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ for a pair of models. This is the proof of Cutkosky for 3-folds [Cut09], one dimension higher. Two steps of this route are false.

1. $\mathrm{Fac}_3$ is false (Corollary 3.24.18). The counterexample is toric. Let $\sigma_0$ be the cone spanned by $(0,1,3)$, $(1,1,1)$ and $(2,1,0)$, and let $S_0$ be the regular local ring of dimension 3 that it gives over $R = F[x, y, z]_{(x, y, z)}$. Theorem 3.24.17 (node SG000000C3) proves that $S_0$ is not an iterated monoidal transform of $R$ for any choice of regular centers, and that $\mathfrak{m}_R S_0$ is not principal. The ideal $K = (x^3 y^3 z^2, x^4 y z^3, y^9, x^5 z^3)$ on $\mathbb{A}^4$ then has no principalization of the kind that $\mathrm{Fac}_3$ asks for.
2. $\mathrm{Fac}_3^L$ is false (Theorem 3.24.20, node SG000000C5). Construction 3.24.19 is a toric pair of 4-folds on which the proof of Theorem 3.24.13 breaks at the step of Abhyankar's theorem, its Claim A, for each choice of the principalization. The weaker condition (A$^{\rm w}$) of Proposition 3.24.21 (node SG000000C6) also fails. But $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ itself holds for this pair, so the pair refutes the method and not the statement.

Proposition 3.24.22 (node SG000000C7) says why: in dimension 3 the rings at the generic point of a curve have dimension 2, and Abhyankar's theorem applies; in dimension 4 they have dimension 3 over a field that is not perfect, and both forms of the step fail. The step of Cutkosky fails for $S_0$, and the step of Cossart and Piltant fails already for one monoidal transform along a curve. What can replace the step is open: regular reachability of toric rings (Conjecture 3.24.24, node SG000000C8) and patching along curves (Conjecture 3.24.25, node SG000000C9). Section 3.25 shows that the local input of patching along curves is relative strong factorization $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ (Definition 3.25.8, node SG000000CG), which is stronger than local strong factorization. It proves $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ for $S_0$ by 14 toric blowups (Theorem 3.25.10, node SG000000CH), and with it repairs Construction 3.24.19 near its bad curve (Proposition 3.25.11, node SG000000CJ). Karu proves the toric case of local strong factorization (Theorem 3.24.23), and Proposition 3.25.14 (node SG000000CN) proves local strong factorization in each characteristic for valuations of rank 1 and rational rank 3. Section 3.25 also shows that the route needs only a weak form $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ of principalization except in one step (Lemma 3.25.3), and that $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ already contains resolution of the 4-folds with a projective birational morphism to a regular 4-fold (Proposition 3.25.6). Note that $\mathrm{Princ}_4$ is not known to follow from $\mathrm{Res}_4^{\rm pr}$, because it is a statement of embedded type. Section 3.29 proves that $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ gives $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ over an infinite field on which regular 4-folds are smooth (Theorem 3.29.11), and that $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ alone gives a very weak form $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm vw}$ over each field (Theorem 3.29.12), which is enough for Steps 2 and 3 of the proof of Theorem 3.24.13. So $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ is no longer a separate open node. The strong $\mathrm{Princ}_4$ is needed only in Lemma 3.24.11, and it does not follow by this method.

### The tracks

The project works on eight tracks at the same time. Each one attacks one piece of the problem. The names are the names of the issues of the project. For each track we give the main statement, what is proved, what is false, and what is open.

#### Track C4: corner runs in four variables

The track C4 (tamnd/singularity#286) works on Conjecture 2.5.3 through the runs of point blowups where $\rho$ stays. The worst runs are at the corners of the divisor.

**Definition 2.5.4.** Let $R = K[[x, y, z, w]]$ with the divisor $E = V(yz)$, and let $g \in R$ be clean. A *corner run in four variables* is an infinite sequence of point blowups $P_N, P_{N+1}, \dots$ such that each $P_{m+1}$ lies in the $y$-chart or in the $z$-chart at the corner $y_{m+1} = z_{m+1} = 0$, each of the two charts occurs an infinite number of times, and $\rho(P_m) \ge 1$ is the same for all $m$. The point $P_{m+1}$ is given by a translation vector $(a_m, b_m) \in K^2$ in the free coordinates $(x, w)$. The run is *linear* if the vectors $(a_m, b_m)$ for $m$ large lie on one line through the origin, and *planar* if not.

This is the setting of Volume 7, Section 3.17, and the linear runs are those of Volume 7, Definition 3.17.5. Conjecture L (Volume 7, Conjecture 3.17.18, node SG0000006Z) asks for a free coordinate whose translations are 0 from some point on. Conjecture L° (Volume 7, Conjecture 3.17.24, node SG00000075) asks in addition that the corner curves have the order of $G$ at least $\rho$, so that a resolution can blow them up. Conjecture L+ (Volume 7, Conjecture 3.17.20, node SG00000071) asks the same for the corner surfaces. Hypothesis H1 is Volume 7, Hypothesis 3.17.32 (node SG0000007D): the initial form of $G_m$ is binary for $m$ large. By Volume 7, Theorem 3.18.19 (node SG0000008N), a corner run is planar if and only if Conjecture L fails for it. Section 3.19 of this volume reports the track.

*What is proved in the handbook.*

1. Volume 7, Corollary 3.18.18 (node SG0000008M): for $\deg_{x, w} g < p$, each corner run is linear, and H1, L and L° hold.
2. Volume 7, Corollary 3.18.36 (node SG00000093) and Theorem 3.18.37 (node SG00000094): Conjecture L holds if $\deg_{x, w} g \le p$, or if $g$ satisfies a condition (C) on its derivatives of order $p^e$, or if $g \in K[[y, z]][x^p, w^p]$, and Conjecture L° holds for $g \in K[[y, z]][x^p, w^p]$. Corollary 3.18.38 (node SG00000095) is the case $g = B_1 x^p + B_2 w^p + B_0$.
3. Conjecture P is proved for each prime $p$: no clean $g \in K[[y, z]][x, w]$ with $\deg_{x, w} g \ge 1$ has a planar corner run in four variables (Volume 7, Theorem 3.19.6, node SG00000096). Theorem 3.19.5 is the first case, $p = 2$ and $\deg_{x, w} g \le 3$. So each corner run of a clean $g \in K[[y, z]][x, w]$ is linear, and Conjecture L holds in each free coordinate system (Volume 7, Corollary 3.19.7).
4. For $p = 2$ and $\deg_{x, w} g = 3$, H1 and L° hold for the linear runs whose function has no monomial $x'^2 w'$, $w'^2$ or $w'^3$ in adapted coordinates (Volume 7, Theorem 3.19.11).
5. At a point before a nonzero translation that keeps $\rho$, the initial form of $G$ is in a short list that depends only on the degree, $p$ and $\rho$ (Volume 7, Proposition 3.19.19, node SG000000BM, and Corollary 3.19.20).

*What is false.*

1. The strong form of H1, $\operatorname{in} G_m \in K[x'_m]$ for the linear runs, is false in each degree at least $p + 1$. The counterexample is Volume 7, Theorem 3.19.14 (node SG000000BJ): for $g = Y x^p (\lambda x + w)$ with a monomial $Y$ in $y, z$ of class 0, there are linear corner runs with an infinite number of nonzero translations of $w$, and $G_m = x_m^p (\lambda x_m + w_m)$, $d = p + 1$ and $\rho = p$ at each point. For $\lambda = 0$ the initial form is not a power of a line. Corollary 3.19.16 gives examples in each degree $D \ge p + 1$. In particular, Conjecture 4.4 of note 69, the strong form for $p = 2$ and degree 3, is false, and so is its form in earlier editions of Volume 7, where it was Conjecture 3.19.14: Example 3.19.15 is the case $g = x^2 w$. Volume 7, Proposition 3.17.30, which assumes $d < p$, does not extend to $d = p + 1$ (Corollary 3.19.16, part 2).
2. Conjecture L+ is false. Volume 7, Theorem 3.17.21, is a counterexample for $p \ge 5$, the point $x w (x + w) y^{p-3} z^{p-3}$, with a run whose translations are all 0. Volume 7, Theorem 3.19.17 (node SG000000BK), shows that L+ is false also for the linear runs with an infinite number of nonzero translations: for $g = y x^p + y^p z^{p-1} w$ there are such runs with $\rho = p$, and each corner surface has $\nu^G \le p - 1 < \rho$. For $p = 2$ the cubic $y x^2 + y^2 z w + x^3$ has the same property (Corollary 3.19.18), and it lies in the class of Theorem 3.19.11.
3. Two older warnings: Volume 7, Hypothesis 3.13.12 (H4', node SG0000005K), is false by Example 3.13.15 (node SG0000005M), and Volume 7, Conjecture 3.11.3 (node SG0000005D) on kangaroo paths is false by Example 3.11.6 (node SG0000005G). Question 3.13.18 of Volume 7 is the corrected question.

*What is open.* The corrected form of H1 and L° for the linear runs with an infinite number of nonzero translations is Volume 7, Conjecture 3.19.22 (node SG000000BP): for $m$ large, $\operatorname{in} G_m$ is one of the forms of Proposition 3.19.19, and the corner curves have $\nu^G \ge \rho$. Its first case is Volume 7, Conjecture 3.19.21 (node SG000000BN), for $p = 2$ and degree 3. The question that replaces L+ is whether the corner surfaces have $\nu^G \ge \rho$ when $\operatorname{in} G_m$ is not a $p$-th power, as in Theorem 3.19.14. Also open are Conjecture P for power series in $x$ and $w$, the runs that leave a component of the divisor an infinite number of times, the conjecture V of Volume 7, Section 3.15, the case $g \in K[[x, y, z]]$ with more than two divisor components at the corner, and a rule for the centers. Note 79 is the source of the results of Volume 7, Section 3.19, after Conjecture P, and note 84 is its referee report.

#### Track M2: mixed multiplicities

The track M2 (tamnd/singularity#287) works on the mixed case of $\mathrm{Emb}_3$, Problem 10.2 of note 10, for the multiplicity $m = p^e m_0$ with $e \ge 1$, $p \nmid m_0$ and $m_0 > 1$. Write the equation as $f = z^m + a_1 z^{m-1} + \dots + a_m$ over a regular local ring $R$.

*The rigid layer.* Volume 7, Theorem 4.1.3 (nodes SG0000003J and SG0000007G): a translation $z \mapsto z + c$ does not change $a_1$, and if $a_1 = \dots = a_{i-1} = 0$ with $i < p^e$, it does not change $a_i$. Volume 7, Proposition 4.1.4 (nodes SG0000003J and SG0000007F): the coefficient $a_{p^e}$ changes by $m_0 c^{p^e}$ plus terms in the lower coefficients. Both have Lean proofs. For the purely inseparable case $m = p$, Volume 7, Theorem 4.3.6 (node SG0000007N) proves that a blowup of the base along a regular center lifts to a permissible blowup if and only if the hypersurface has multiplicity $p$ at the generic point of the center (proved in the handbook).

*The first case.* Let $p$ be odd, $m = 2p$, and $R = k[[x_0, x_1, x_2]]$ with a divisor $E$ given by a set of the coordinates. Volume 7, Definition 4.4.1, defines the slopes $\delta$ and $\mu_k$ and the residual slope
$$\omega = \delta - \sum_{k \in E} \mu_k ,$$
and Definition 4.4.5 gives Rule 61.9', which chooses the center at each point. The main statement of the track is Volume 7, Conjecture 4.4.6: each sequence of blowups of Rule 61.9' through points of multiplicity $2p$ is finite. Status: open.

*What is proved in the handbook.*

1. Volume 7, Theorem 4.4.13 (node SG0000009T): the rule terminates at $\omega = 0$. Then $\omega$ stays 0, and $\delta$ drops by a fixed amount at each step.
2. Volume 7, Theorem 4.5.9 (node SG000000BE): in the toric model, $\omega$ never goes up and each run on which $\omega$ is constant is finite. So each toric branch of Rule 61.9' is finite. Corollary 4.5.10 gives a lexicographic invariant $(\omega, \ell)$ on the toric branches, and Proposition 4.5.11 computes a hidden quantity of the toric model. Theorem 4.5.21 (node SG000000CR) extends the theorem to chart origins with any boundary, and Theorem 4.5.28 (node SG000000CV) to the generic steps at points that are not chart origins. For germs with $a_p = 0$ or $a_{2p} = 0$, $\omega$ never goes up (Corollary 4.5.18, node SG000000CQ), and each branch through rational points on which no vertex cancels is finite (Corollary 4.5.29, node SG000000CW).

*What is false.*

1. $\omega$ does not decrease under the rule: Volume 7, Theorems 4.4.7 and 4.4.8, show that it can go up, by $1/p$ in the second one.
2. No correction by the low flag is monotone (Volume 7, Proposition 4.4.12, with Lemma 4.4.10 and Example 4.4.11).
3. No lexicographic pair that starts with $\omega$ decreases at each step of the rule outside the toric model (Volume 7, Proposition 4.5.14). Example 4.5.15 is a step on germ 57 that keeps $\omega = 2/15$ at a point that is not a chart origin. It is a generic step, and the extended model covers it (Example 4.5.25).

*What is open.* Conjecture 4.4.6 for $\omega > 0$ outside the extended model of Volume 7, Definition 4.5.26, that is, at steps where a vertex cancels or where $z$ must be prepared again. By Proposition 4.5.30 a rise of $\omega$ needs a new preparation, and Example 4.5.32 is a step on germ 63 where no quantity of Section 4.5 drops. Proposition 4.4.14 and Example 4.4.15 describe a mechanism of period 5 that any proof must control. The computations of notes 81 and 86 found that all 81 subtrees of the search of note 72 end, also exactly at the germ level, so no cycle is known (cited). Section 3.30 shows that the quantity $h$ of Volume 7, Lemma 4.5.6, is not defined at the rises of Theorems 4.4.7 and 4.4.8 (Proposition 3.30.17), and it reports the computed rises: in all trees computed so far each branch has at most one rise, and each rise is at a solvable vertex for the weight $(1,1,1)$ (Propositions 3.30.19 and 3.30.20). The open question is a bound on the number of such rises along a branch (Remark 3.30.21).

#### Track NW: Newton polyhedra and Frobenius

The track NW (tamnd/singularity#296) asks if a change of coordinates and weighted blowups can make $z^p + g$ Newton nondegenerate. Volume 7, Section 3.3, defines the Newton polyhedron of $f$ and its initial forms. The equation is *Newton nondegenerate* if no initial form for a compact face has a critical point in the torus.

*What is proved in the handbook.* Volume 7, Theorem 3.3.13 (node SG0000008P, with a Lean proof `Singularity.newtonNondegenerate_purelyInseparable_iff`): over a perfect field, $z^p + g$ is Newton nondegenerate if and only if no initial form $\operatorname{in}_w g$ with $w \in \mathbb{Z}^n_{>0}$ has a critical point in the torus. Example 3.3.14 (node SG0000008Q) shows that the field must be perfect. Theorems 3.3.16 and 3.3.17 (nodes SG0000008R and SG0000008S) give an obstruction. Let $C$ be the projective critical set of the lowest form $h$ of $g$, of degree $D$. If $C$ is not in the union of $n$ hyperplanes with independent equations, then no change $x \mapsto \varphi(x)$, $z \mapsto z + q$ makes the equation Newton nondegenerate, and for $D > p$ no change of all the coordinates does. Proposition 3.3.20 (node SG0000008T) decides when a change that keeps one coordinate removes the torus critical points, and Theorem 3.3.22 (node SG0000008Y) gives the obstruction of the Fano plane for $p = 2$. Conjecture 3.3.24 (node SG0000008Z) is the case $D = p$. Theorem 3.3.26 (node SG0000008W) resolves a Newton nondegenerate germ by a toric morphism, Proposition 3.3.27 (node SG0000008V) finds the singular points of the strict transform, and Theorem 3.3.29 (node SG0000008X) treats a binary form $h$ with $p \mid D$ by one weighted blowup.

*What is cited.* The map has the nodes SG00000097 to SG0000009E from notes 68 and 70, and the handbook does not yet prove them. SG00000097 and SG00000099 extend the obstruction to $D = p$ under conditions. SG0000009A is a form of degree 5 in three variables for $p = 5$ that no change of coordinates prepares. SG0000009D says that one weighted blowup with the weights $(1, \dots, 1, \nu/p)$ lowers the residual order $\nu$ when $p \mid \nu$, outside some exceptional points. Here $\nu$ is the order of the part of $g$ without monomials that are $p$-th powers.

**Conjecture 2.5.5.** Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p > 0$, let $n \ge 3$, and let $f = z^p + g$ with $g \in k[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$ and residual order $\nu \ge 2$. Then there is a finite sequence of weighted blowups such that at each step the largest residual order at the points of the strict transform over the origin drops. In particular, after a finite number of steps the strict transform is smooth at each point over the origin.

Node: SG0000009E · Lean: none yet

Status: open, and the project does not know if it is true. The first open cases are the case $p \nmid \nu$, the exceptional points of SG0000009D, and the termination. The decision of notes 55 and 68 was negative for the original aim: the three test cases of the track C4 in note 55 resist every change of all the coordinates (note 68, Corollary 3.2, checked by the referee report in note 70). So Newton nondegeneracy alone does not give Conjecture 2.5.3. Abramovich and Temkin [AT26], arXiv:2602.15612, show that in characteristic 2 weighted blowups cannot give a resolution of pinch points that keeps normal crossings. This was checked at the level of the abstract only. Its relevance for Conjecture 2.5.5 is not verified.

Here $\mathrm{Emb}(\mathbb{P}^4)$ is Definition 3.21.1 (node SG0000007W), embedded resolution of reduced divisors in $\mathbb{P}^4$, which follows from $\mathrm{Emb}_3$. #### Track HS: Hasse-Schmidt ideals for e at least 2

The track HS (tamnd/singularity#288) works on $\mathrm{Core}_3(e)$ for $e \ge 2$, where the examples of Hauser and Perlega live. For $q = p^e$ the derivations of order one kill all $p$-th powers, but the cleaning only removes $q$-th powers. So the log-Jacobian ideal does not see enough of $g$, and a finer object is needed.

**Definition 2.5.6.** Let $R = K[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$, let $E = V(\prod_{i \in \Delta} x_i)$, and let $F$ be the complement of $\Delta$. For $g \in R$ and $1 \le m < q$, let $\Theta^{\le m}_E(g)$ be the ideal of $R$ generated by the coefficients of the monomials $s^\alpha$ with $1 \le |\alpha| \le m$ in the expansion of $g\bigl(x_i (1 + s_i) \ (i \in \Delta), \ x_j + s_j \ (j \in F)\bigr)$ in new variables $s_1, \dots, s_n$. Let $\rho_m$ be the order of the residual factor of $\Theta^{\le m}_E(g)$ after the monomial factor is removed.

For $m = 1$ the ideal $\Theta^{\le 1}_E(g)$ is the log-Jacobian ideal $\Theta(g)$ of Volume 7, Section 3.1. Volume 7, Section 3.20, studies these ideals. Its Definition 3.20.1 is the form of the handbook.

*What is proved in the handbook.* With $P = p^{e-1}$:

1. The ideals do not depend on the adapted coordinates, and they do not change when we add a $q$-th power to $g$ (Volume 7, Propositions 3.20.3 and 3.20.4).
2. The dichotomy $\rho_m \le d \le \rho_m + m$ for $P \le m < q$ (Volume 7, Theorem 3.20.6).
3. The graded law of transformation under a point blowup, with an imbalance $\delta$ (Volume 7, Theorem 3.20.8). As a consequence $\rho_1$ never goes up (Corollary 3.20.10), and the levels $m \ge 2$ can go up (Proposition 3.20.15). Lemma 3.20.16 gives upper semicontinuity.
4. The no-go theorem, Volume 7, Theorem 3.20.18 (node SG0000009Z). Let $e = 3$, and let $n \ge 5$ for $p = 2$ and $n \ge 4$ for $p \ge 3$. Assume the theorem of Hauser and Perlega on their cycles. Then no invariant of the germ, with values in a well-ordered set, both does not go up under the blowup of each closed point of order $q$ and bounds the residual order $d$. Corollary 3.20.19 shows that each invariant that bounds $d$, in particular $\rho_P$, goes up at an infinite number of steps of the cycles. Corollary 3.20.20 shows that $\rho_1$ does not go up and does not bound $d$. Remark 3.20.21 extends the theorem to $e \ge 4$ for germs that are not reduced.
5. For each $e$, an invariant with the two properties exists if and only if a uniform bound $D^*$ of Moh type is finite on each germ (Volume 7, Proposition 3.20.22).

*What the no-go does not say.* The germs of the theorem have $n \ge 4$ variables, so the hypersurfaces have dimension at least 4. They are not threefolds, and the theorem says nothing for $n \le 3$, that is, for $\mathrm{Core}_3(e)$ itself. It also says nothing about a rule that chooses the centers, about centers of positive dimension, or about invariants that remember the history of the resolution. So it excludes one design: a germ-local invariant that controls point blowups for $e = 3$ in dimension 4. The results are from notes 45 and 75, with the referee reports in notes 48 and 78.

*What is open.* The three open questions of Volume 7, Section 3.20: the exponent $e = 2$, that is, whether $D^*$ is finite on each germ; threefolds with $e = 3$, that is, $n = 3$; and reduced germs with $e \ge 4$. A rule for centers of positive dimension, with a law like Theorem 3.20.8 for them, is also open.

#### Track VD: valuations and defect

The track VD (tamnd/singularity#298) works on $\mathrm{LU}_4$ directly, through the defect of Section 2.2. Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic $p$, let $F$ be a function field of transcendence degree 4 over $k$, and let $\nu$ be a valuation of $F$, trivial on $k$, with residue field $k$.

**Definition 2.5.7.** A *projection* of $F$ is a subfield $K = k(y_1, \dots, y_4)$ with $F/K$ finite and separable. It has *zero defect* if the defect of $(F/K, \nu)$ in the sense of Section 2.2 is 1. The valuation $\nu$ is of *KK type*, for Knaf and Kuhlmann, if $F$ lies in the completion of a subfield $F_0$ on which $\nu$ is an Abhyankar valuation (Definition 3.23.1).

**Conjecture 2.5.8 (V7).** Let $\nu$ be as above, of rank one. If $F$ has a projection with zero defect for $\nu$, then $\nu$ has local uniformization.

This is Problem V7 of note 06. Status: open in general. For a valuation of KK type it is a theorem of Knaf and Kuhlmann [KK09, Theorem 1.5] (Theorem 3.23.2, cited). Note 57 corrects a count of dimensions: the theorem of Cutkosky and Mourtada on zero defect [CM17, Theorem 7.1], for a function field of dimension 4, needs embedded local uniformization in dimension 4, not in dimension 3. So Conjecture 2.5.8 is not a consequence of the work in dimension 3. Section 3.23 treats the value group $\mathbb{Z}^2$ (proved in the handbook, nodes SG000000AS to SG000000BD).

1. Rank 2. Write $\nu = v_1 \circ \bar v$, with $\kappa$ the residue field of $v_1$ and $d$ its transcendence degree. If $\kappa$ is finitely generated over $k$, then $\nu$ has local uniformization (Theorems 3.23.3 and 3.23.5). If $d \ge 2$, then $\nu$ is not of KK type (Theorem 3.23.4). Counterexample 3.23.7 shows that the hypothesis on $\kappa$ cannot simply be dropped from the argument.
2. Rank 1. The problem reduces to a rational projection (Lemma 3.23.10 and Theorem 3.23.11), and it is solved for each projection whose tower has the ball property at each step (Theorem 3.23.15, node SG000000B3, and Corollary 3.23.16). Example 3.23.19 (node SG000000B6) is an explicit valuation of this kind that is not of KK type. So local uniformization holds beyond the class of Knaf and Kuhlmann.
3. Defect. Proposition 3.23.20 and Example 3.23.21 give defect extensions with value group exactly $\mathbb{Z}^2$, and Example 3.23.23 with Proposition 3.23.24 is the test family T.

The results are from note 76, with the referee report in note 80.

*What is open.* Conjecture 3.23.8, local uniformization of the discrete valuation $v_1$ when $d \in \{1, 2\}$ and $\kappa$ is not finitely generated, which is the only open part of Conjecture 2.5.8 in rank 2. Conjecture 3.23.17, the existence of a good projection, which implies Conjecture 2.5.8 in rank 1 with value group $\mathbb{Z}^2$. Conjecture 3.23.25 (Question T1) for the family T. The conjecture V4 of note 06 on towers of independent defect is open.

#### Track FO: foliations and their quotients

The track FO (tamnd/singularity#299) works on $\mathrm{FQ}_4$ of route A. Let $Y$ be a regular variety of dimension $n$ over a perfect field $k$ of characteristic $p$, with tangent sheaf $T_Y$.

**Definition 2.5.9.** A *$p$-closed foliation* on $Y$ is a saturated subsheaf $\mathcal{F} \subseteq T_Y$ that is closed under the Lie bracket and under $D \mapsto D^p$. Its rank is its rank as a sheaf, and its corank is $n$ minus the rank. Its *quotient* $Y/\mathcal{F}$ is the normal variety with the structure sheaf of the local sections $f$ of $\mathcal{O}_Y$ with $D(f) = 0$ for all local sections $D$ of $\mathcal{F}$. A foliation of rank one is *multiplicative* at a point if near the point it is generated by $\sum_i c_i x_i \partial_i$ with $c_i \in \mathbb{F}_p$ in some coordinates.

By the correspondence of Jacobson and Ekedahl, the quotients $Y/\mathcal{F}$ are the normal varieties $Z$ with $k(Y)^p \subseteq k(Z) \subseteq k(Y)$. Posva proves for 1-foliations on surfaces, and in corank one on threefolds, that blowups make the singularities multiplicative [Pos24] (cited).

**Conjecture 2.5.10 (multiplicative reduction in dimension 4).** Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a $p$-closed foliation of rank one on a regular variety of dimension 4. Then there is a proper birational morphism $Y' \to Y$, a composite of blowups, such that the transform of $\mathcal{F}$ on $Y'$ has only multiplicative singularities.

This is Problem 4.9 of note 04. Status: open. Section 3.22 treats the foliations that a torus fixes (proved in the handbook, nodes SG000000A7 to SG000000AR).

1. For the maximal torus $\mathbb{G}_m^4$, the invariant $p$-closed foliations of each rank are classified by the subspaces of $\mathbb{F}_p^4$ (Theorem 3.22.6). A finite sequence of weighted blowups with weights less than $p$ makes each of them regular on a regular tame Deligne-Mumford stack, and on a regular variety for $p = 2$ (Theorem 3.22.10). The quotients are toric (Corollary 3.22.11).
2. For tori of smaller rank the toric method fails: Example 3.22.14 (rank one) and Example 3.22.17 (rank three) have no toric resolution. It is not known if the foliation of Example 3.22.14 has a resolution.
3. For the torus $\mathbb{G}_m$, Proposition 3.22.18 reduces the problem near the exceptional divisor to a twisted problem on a threefold, and Theorem 3.22.23 to Proposition 3.22.27 treat the additive case.

The results are from note 58, with the referee report in note 73.

*What is open.* Conjecture 3.22.16 (one weighted blowup at a multiplicative point), Conjecture 3.22.20 (the twisted pair problem), Conjecture 3.22.21 (equivariant gluing), Problem 4.10 of note 04, and $\mathrm{AO}_4$. Note also that Conjecture 2.5.10 is about rank one, while the local equation $z^p = g$ of $\mathrm{PIRes}_4$ belongs to corank one. In dimension 4 these are different problems. Note 73 shows that route A does not need the link between them, because $\mathrm{PIRes}_4$ implies $\mathrm{FQ}_4$ directly. Martínez-Núñez [MN26], arXiv:2611.18258, proves equivariant resolution in characteristic $p$ for varieties with a torus action of complexity at most 3. For a 4-fold this covers each nontrivial torus action, also the tori of Examples 3.22.14 and 3.22.17. This was checked at the level of the abstract only. The input in dimension 3 of the proof is not verified, so it is not known whether the paper uses a form of $\mathrm{Emb}_3$.

Its results are in the section on the routes above: Theorems 3.24.4 and 3.24.5 (proved), the route of Cutkosky, Theorem 3.24.13 (proved), and its failure, Corollary 3.24.18 and Theorem 3.24.20 (proved counterexamples). Section 3.26 shows that (A$^{\rm w}$) holds for toric pairs in the weak form of Step 3, gives an exact obstruction in the strong form, proves toric $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ for all regular cones with entries at most 4 and for all but two classes with entries at most 5 (Theorem 3.26.19), and refutes the drop of the number of bad cones under a certificate repair (Counterexample 3.26.24). Section 3.27 proves the Pivot Theorem 3.27.10 and its form with several rays (Theorem 3.27.17), and proves toric $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ for all regular cones with entries at most 6 (Theorem 3.27.20). Section 3.28 shows that every cone with a certificate has a certificate with a set of pivots (Lemma 3.28.12), so the multi-pivot theorem is not a reduction, and that its form with at most $k$ pivots is a bound on the number of pivots of a certificate (Proposition 3.28.15). Section 3.29 proves that $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$ gives $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ (Theorem 3.29.11), that $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ gives $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm vw}$ over each field (Theorem 3.29.12), and that $\mathrm{ELU}_3$ gives $\mathrm{PILU}_4$ for each valuation without defect (Theorem 3.29.19). Section 3.31 proves that (D) is the end case $\rho = 0$ of Volume 7, Section 3.5, on some model (Theorem 3.31.2 and Corollary 3.31.5), and that (D) holds when the valuation is discrete of rank one on $L$ (Proposition 3.31.19). The results are from notes 77, 83, 85, 88, 90, 92, 95, 96, 97 and 98, with the referee reports in notes 82, 87, 89, 91, 93 and 99, the check in note 98a, and the review of the road in note 94.

*What is open.* The lists at the end of Sections 3.24 to 3.31: $\mathrm{Fund}_4$; $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$, which gives $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ (Theorem 3.29.11); the strong $\mathrm{Princ}_4$, for Lemma 3.24.11 only; regular reachability of toric rings (Conjecture 3.24.24, with 96 open cones for entries at most 4); patching along curves (Conjecture 3.24.25) and its parts (N1) to (N5); $\mathrm{RSF}_3$, also in the toric case, where it is known for entries at most 6 and no certificate is known for the cone $\sigma_7$ of Proposition 3.27.21; the best lower bound on the number of pivots of certificates, where the bound $\mathrm{C}(k)$ of Definition 3.28.14 is false for every $k$ (Corollary 3.30.12) and the proved lower bound for $\sigma_n$ is $\frac{n-1}4$ (Corollary 3.30.11); local strong factorization of regular local rings of dimension 3 over imperfect fields, beyond Theorem 3.24.23 and Proposition 3.25.14; and $\mathrm{PILU}_4$, where only the valuations with defect and a value group other than $\mathbb{Z}$ are open given $\mathrm{ELU}_3$ (Problems 3.29.23 and 3.31.21). For Construction 3.24.19, $\mathrm{Fund}_4$ holds by toric resolution, but it is not known whether it holds in the form of Conjecture 3.24.25.

### The critical path

The review of the road in note 94 asked which open statement carries the most arrows. Section 3.29 answers this. For $k$ algebraically closed and $p > 24$ the critical path is: $\mathrm{MD}_3$ and $\mathrm{SNC}_3$ with centers in the non-snc locus give $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd,iso}$; this gives $\mathrm{LU}_4^-$ through $\mathrm{Emb}(\mathbb{P}^4)$ and Theorem 3.21.7, and $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ through Theorem 3.29.11; $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ and the patching with $\mathrm{RSF}_3$ give $\mathrm{Fund}_4$; and Theorem 3.24.4 gives $\mathrm{Res}_4^{\rm pr}$. For each $p$ the local half can also come from $\mathrm{Emb}_3^{\rm bd}$ through $\mathrm{ELU}_3$ and Theorem 3.29.19 for the valuations without defect, with (D) for the valuations with defect, and then Theorem 3.24.5. This route does not use $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$, and the multiplicity condition of (O7) plays no role. The strong $\mathrm{Princ}_4$ is needed only in Lemma 3.24.11. So the open nodes on the critical path are three: $\mathrm{Emb}_3$, which is the central node, the patching with $\mathrm{RSF}_3$, and (D). Section 3.31 shows that (D) is the end case $\rho = 0$ along the valuation, and that it is open only for the value groups other than $\mathbb{Z}$ (Problem 3.31.21). $\mathrm{Princ}_4^{\rm w}$ is no longer a separate node.

### Exercises

The exercises go from the proved results to the open ones. The last part of each exercise points to an open problem.

1. Let $q = p^e$ and $1 \le m < q$, and let $p^j$ be the least power of $p$ with $p^j > m$. Prove that $\Theta^{\le m}_E(g + h^{p^j}) = \Theta^{\le m}_E(g)$ for each $h \in R$. Show by the example $g = 0$, $h = x_1$ with $1 \notin \Delta$ and $m = p$ that $\Theta^{\le p}_E(h^p)$ can be the unit ideal. Then read the proof of Volume 7, Theorem 3.20.18, and say which step uses that the cycles of Hauser and Perlega have $n \ge 4$ variables. This is the reason why the case $n = 3$ is open.
2. Let $m = 2p$ with $p$ odd. Use the theorem of Lucas to prove that $\binom{2p}{i} \equiv 0 \pmod p$ for $0 < i < p$ and $\binom{2p}{p} \equiv 2 \pmod p$. Deduce Volume 7, Proposition 4.1.4, for $e = 1$ and $m_0 = 2$ in the case $a_1 = \dots = a_{p-1} = 0$: then $a'_p = a_p + 2 c^p$. Then let $f = z^{2p} + x_0^{a} x_1^{b}$ with $a + b \ge 2p$, with $a$ and $b$ not both divisible by $2p$, and with $E = \{1\}$. Use Lemma 61.2 of note 61, which says that the supremum in the definition of $\delta(w)$ may be taken over the translations $z \mapsto z + c$, to prove that $\delta = (a + b)/(2p)$ and $\mu_1 = b/(2p)$. So $\omega = a/(2p)$, and $\omega = 0$ if and only if $a = 0$. The case $\omega = 0$ is the case of Volume 7, Theorem 4.4.13, and the case $\omega > 0$ outside the toric model is the open part of Volume 7, Conjecture 4.4.6.
3. Let $(x, w) \mapsto (x, w) A$ be a change of the free coordinates by a matrix $A \in \mathrm{GL}_2(K)$ at the point $P_N$ of a corner run in four variables. Show that it commutes with the corner charts, and that it maps the translation vectors $(a_m, b_m)$ by the same matrix. Deduce that being linear in the sense of Definition 2.5.4 does not depend on linear free coordinates. Volume 7, Theorem 3.18.19, proves this for all free coordinates. Then let $p = 2$ and $g = x^2 w$, and check with Volume 7, Theorem 3.19.14, that $G_m = x_m^2 w_m$ at each point of the run, so that $\operatorname{in} G_m$ is not a power of a line. Explain why this refutes Conjecture 4.4 of note 69 and why Volume 7, Conjecture 3.19.21, allows the form $x_m'^{\,2} (c\, x'_m + c' w'_m)$.
4. Let $h \in k[x_1, \dots, x_n]$ be a form of degree $D$. Prove the identity of Euler, $\sum_i x_i \partial_i h = D h$, in each characteristic. Deduce that if $p \nmid D$, each critical point of $h$ lies on $h = 0$. For $p = 2$, show that $h = x^3 y + x^2 y^2 + x y^3$ has the critical point $(1, 1)$ with $h(1, 1) \ne 0$, and that after the cleaning, which removes the square $x^2 y^2$, this critical point lies on the zero set. Then explain why the weights $(1, \dots, 1, \nu/p)$ of the node SG0000009D are not available when $p \nmid \nu$. This is one reason why the case $p \nmid \nu$ of Conjecture 2.5.5 is open.
5. Let $X = V(F) \subset \mathbb{P}^5$ with $F = \sum_{i=0}^{5} x_i^{q+1}$ and $q = p^m$, and let $P \notin X$. Show that the projection from $P$ has local degree at least $q$ at each point of $X \cap V(\sum_i P_i x_i^q)$. This is part of Example 3.21.10. Explain why this does not contradict Theorem 3.21.7, and why it shows that a global statement, Conjecture 3.21.8 or $\mathrm{Fund}_4$, and not one projection, carries the global part of the method of Jung.
6. Let $\nu$ be a valuation of rank one with value group $\mathbb{Z}^2$, and let $x_1, x_2$ be elements whose values are a basis of it, such that $F$ lies in the completion of $k(x_1, x_2)$. So $\nu$ is of KK type. Let $F/K$ be a projection with $K \supseteq k(x_1, x_2)$. Show that $F$ lies in the henselization $K^h$ of $K$, and deduce that $F/K$ has zero defect. Use that for a valuation of rank one the henselization is separably closed in the completion. Then explain why this argument says nothing about the valuation of Example 3.23.19, and which theorem of Section 3.23 replaces it there.
7. Let $Y$ be a regular variety of dimension $n$ and $\mathcal{F}$ a $p$-closed foliation of rank $r$ on $Y$, with $Z = Y/\mathcal{F}$. Prove that $[k(Y) : k(Z)] = p^r$ and $[k(Z) : k(Y)^p] = p^{n-r}$. Deduce that $Z$ is locally a cover $z^p = g$ of the Frobenius twist of $Y$ only when $r = n - 1$. This is the gap that note 58 found in the proof of Corollary 2.10 of note 02. Then read Proposition 5.1 of note 73 and explain why its proof does not need corank one.
8. Let $V \subseteq \mathbb{F}_3^2$ be the line spanned by $(1, 2)$, and let $\mathcal{F}_V$ be the foliation on $\mathbb{A}^2$ generated by $x_1 \partial_1 + 2 x_2 \partial_2$. Find the weighted blowup with weights in $\{1, 2\}$ that makes it regular, as in Theorem 3.22.10, and describe the quotient as a toric surface. Then read Example 3.22.14 and explain why the same method fails for a torus of rank 1 in dimension 4, which leads to Conjecture 3.22.20.
9. Let $p \ge 5$, $A = k[x, y, u, w]_{(x, y, u, w)}$ and $a = x^2 + y^3$, as in Example 82.4 of note 82. Show that $B = A[z]/(z^p - a)$ has multiplicity 2 at the origin, that its singular locus is $V(x, y, z)$, and that $B$ is normal by the criterion of Serre. Deduce that $B$ is not regular, so that the multiplicity clause of $\mathrm{C4}^{\rm val}$ does not give $\mathrm{PILU}_4$. Then find a regular local ring $A' \supseteq B$ dominated by a given valuation. Use a resolution of the surface $z^p = x^2 + y^3$ in the coordinates $x, y, z$, times the plane of $u, w$. This case is easy, because $a$ does not depend on $u$ and $w$. Compare with Example 3.29.25 and Proposition 3.29.26. Problem (O7) of note 82 is the general case of multiplicity less than $p$, and Section 3.29 reduces it to the statement (D) for valuations with defect.
10. Let $\sigma_0$ be the cone spanned by $g_1 = (0,1,3)$, $g_2 = (1,1,1)$ and $g_3 = (2,1,0)$. Prove that $\det(g_1, g_2, g_3) = -1$. For the ideal $(x, y, z)$, show that no generator $x^u$ has $\langle g_i, u \rangle \le \langle g_i, u' \rangle$ for each $i$ and each other generator $x^{u'}$, and deduce that $\mathfrak{m}_R S_0$ is not principal. Then explain with Proposition 3.24.22 why this blocks the step of Abhyankar's theorem in the proof of Theorem 3.24.13, and why Conjecture 3.24.25 would bypass it.
