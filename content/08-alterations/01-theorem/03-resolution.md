## Alterations and resolution

This section compares the theorem of de Jong with the problem of this handbook. The problem asks for a resolution of each variety of dimension 4 in characteristic $p$, in the sense of Volume 0, Definition 2.1.5. Theorem 1.2.1 gives a regular alteration $\varphi \colon X_1 \to X$. The alteration fails to be a resolution in two ways. Its degree $d$ can be greater than 1, and even when $d = 1$ the morphism need not be an isomorphism over the regular locus. We study the degree. When $d$ is prime to $p$ and $\varphi$ is finite, the structure sheaf of a normal $X$ is a direct summand of the direct image of the structure sheaf of $X_1$. When $p$ divides $d$, this fails in general, and the theorems of Gabber and Temkin in Chapter 3 show that the powers of $p$ are the only obstruction left by these methods.

### The trace

Let $A$ be a domain with fraction field $K$, and let $L$ be a finite extension of $K$ of degree $d$. For $b \in L$, the trace $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(b)$ is the trace of the $K$-linear map $L \to L$, $x \mapsto bx$. It is $K$-linear, and $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(a) = d a$ for $a \in K$.

**Lemma 1.3.1.** Let $A$ be a normal Noetherian domain with fraction field $K$, let $L/K$ be a finite extension, and let $b \in L$ be integral over $A$. Then $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(b) \in A$.

*Proof.* Let $f \in K[T]$ be the minimal polynomial of $b$ over $K$, of degree $m$. In a splitting field of $f$, each root of $f$ is a conjugate of $b$, so it satisfies the same integral equation as $b$ and is integral over $A$. The coefficients of $f$ are polynomials in the roots, so they are integral over $A$, and they lie in $K$. Since $A$ is normal, the coefficients lie in $A$. The matrix of multiplication by $b$ on $K(b)$ in the basis $1, b, \dots, b^{m-1}$ is the companion matrix of $f$, so $\operatorname{Tr}_{K(b)/K}(b) = -c_{m-1}$, where $c_{m-1}$ is the coefficient of $T^{m-1}$. Choose a basis of $L$ over $K(b)$. In the basis of products, multiplication by $b$ is block diagonal with $[L : K(b)]$ copies of the companion matrix. So $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(b) = -[L : K(b)] \, c_{m-1} \in A$. $\square$

The Lean statement does not need the hypothesis that $A$ is Noetherian. It needs only that $A$ is an integrally closed domain.

**Proposition 1.3.2.** Let $\varphi \colon X' \to X$ be a finite alteration of degree $d$, with $X$ normal and Noetherian, and assume that $d$ is invertible on $X$. Then the map $\mathcal{O}_X \to \varphi_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}$ has an $\mathcal{O}_X$-linear left inverse.

Lean: `Singularity.exists_leftInverse_algebraMap_of_isUnit_finrank`, `Singularity.traceToBase_algebraMap` (the affine case)

*Proof.* Let $K = k(X)$ and $L = k(X')$, so $[L : K] = d$. For an affine open subset $\operatorname{Spec} A \subseteq X$, the ring $B = \Gamma(\varphi^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec} A), \mathcal{O}_{X'})$ is a domain finite over $A$ with fraction field $L$. Each element of $B$ is integral over $A$, so by Lemma 1.3.1 the map $s_A = \frac{1}{d} \operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}$ sends $B$ to $A$. It is $A$-linear, and $s_A(a) = a$ for $a \in A$. The maps $s_A$ are restrictions of the one map $\frac{1}{d} \operatorname{Tr}_{L/K} \colon L \to K$, so they agree on the overlaps and glue to a map $\varphi_* \mathcal{O}_{X'} \to \mathcal{O}_X$. $\square$

**Remark 1.3.3.** Two remarks show the limits of the proposition. First, if $p = \operatorname{char} K$ divides $d$, the proof fails, and if $L/K$ is inseparable it fails completely, because then $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K} = 0$. Second, even when $d$ is prime to $p$, the splitting does not make $X$ regular. In Example 1.1.9, the ring $k[s^2, st, t^2]$ is a direct summand of $k[s, t]$ in each characteristic, by the map that keeps the monomials of even degree, but the cone is singular. The splitting passes some properties of $X'$ to $X$, but regularity is not one of them.

### The degree and the open problem

Theorem 1.2.1 gives no control of the degree. The proof of Chapter 2 makes alterations at many steps: normalizations in Galois extensions to make the components of $Z$ into sections, the finite étale covers of level structures, and the alterations of the induction hypothesis. Each of them multiplies the degree. If $k$ is perfect, each step is generically étale, but the degrees of these extensions can be divisible by $p$.

**Proposition 1.3.4.** Let $\varphi \colon X_1 \to X$ be an alteration of degree $d$ of an integral variety $X$ over a field $k$, with $X_1$ regular. If $d = 1$ and $\varphi$ is an isomorphism over $\operatorname{Reg}(X)$, then $\varphi$ is a resolution of $X$. If $d > 1$, then no nonempty open subset of $X_1$ maps isomorphically onto an open subset of $X$.

Lean: `Singularity.bijective_algebraMap_of_finrank_eq_one`, `Singularity.finrank_eq_one_of_algHom`, `Singularity.finrank_comp_alteration` (the degree facts). The conditions on schemes are not in Lean yet.

*Proof.* If $d = 1$ and $\varphi$ is an isomorphism over $\operatorname{Reg}(X)$, then $\varphi$ is proper, $X_1$ is regular, and $\varphi^{-1}(\operatorname{Reg}(X))$ is a nonempty open subset of the irreducible space $X_1$, so it is dense. These are the conditions of Volume 0, Definition 2.1.5. If an open subset of $X_1$ maps isomorphically onto an open subset of $X$, then the two function fields are isomorphic over $k(X)$, so $d = 1$. $\square$

The problem is therefore to remove the degree. There are two cases. If the extension $k(X_1)/k(X)$ is Galois with group $G$, then $X$ is birational to the quotient $X_1/G$ (Section 3.1), and one must resolve the quotient. If $|G|$ is prime to $p$, the action is tame, and the quotient can be resolved by a modification. The modification theorem of Gabber [ILO14, Exposé VIII, Theorem 1.1] applies to a tame and generically free action of a finite group on a Noetherian, quasi-excellent, separated, log regular scheme. A regular scheme with the trivial log structure is log regular, and $G$ acts generically freely on $X_1$, because it acts faithfully on $k(X_1)$. The theorem gives an equivariant projective modification $X_1' \to X_1$ on which the action is very tame. Then $X_1'/G$ is log regular, and log regular schemes have resolutions by modifications [ILO14, Exposé VIII, Section 3]. The proof uses canonical desingularization in characteristic 0 [ILO14, Exposé VIII, Section 2]. The result is a regular modification of $X_1/G$, so a regular alteration of $X$ that is generically purely inseparable. It is still not a resolution of $X$ in the sense of Volume 0 (Section 3.2). If $p$ divides $|G|$, the action can be wild, and no general method is known. Temkin's theorem (Section 3.3) makes the degree a power of $p$. So after the theorems of this volume, the part of the degree that no known method removes is a power of $p$, and for a Galois alteration it comes from a $p$-group, whose action is wild. This is route R6 of the map, and Volume 9, Section 2.3 records that nobody knows how to do this descent in dimension 4.

**Example 1.3.5.** In characteristic $p$, a purely inseparable alteration carries no information. Let $X$ be an integral variety over a perfect field $k$ of characteristic $p$, and let $F \colon X \to X$ be the absolute Frobenius, which is the identity on points and the $p$-th power on functions. It lies over the Frobenius of $\operatorname{Spec} k$. Let $X'$ be the scheme $X$ with the structure morphism composed with the Frobenius of $\operatorname{Spec} k$, which is an isomorphism because $k$ is perfect. Then $F$ is a $k$-morphism $X' \to X$. On function fields it is the inclusion $K^p \subseteq K$ with $K = k(X)$, and $[K : K^p] = p^{\dim X}$ for a function field over a perfect field. So $F$ is a finite alteration of degree $p^{\dim X}$, purely inseparable, and a homeomorphism. Its source is $X$ itself, so it is regular only when $X$ is regular. This shows that the purely inseparable part of an alteration does not help. In Theorem 1.2.1 over a perfect field, de Jong can choose $\varphi_1$ generically étale, so the degree is the degree of a separable extension.

### What alterations give for cohomology

A resolution $\tilde X \to X$ is an isomorphism over a dense open subset. An alteration is not, but it still carries information on the cohomology of $X$, and how much depends on the degree. The basic mechanism is the trace of Proposition 1.3.2. We first state it for coherent cohomology, then give an example where the degree is a power of $p$ and the mechanism fails, and then state it for étale cohomology.

**Proposition 1.3.6.** Let $\varphi \colon X' \to X$ be a finite alteration of degree $d$, with $X$ normal and Noetherian, and assume that $d$ is invertible on $X$. Let $\mathcal{F}$ be a quasi-coherent $\mathcal{O}_X$-module. Then for each $i \ge 0$ the pullback map $H^i(X, \mathcal{F}) \to H^i(X', \varphi^* \mathcal{F})$ is injective, and it has a left inverse.

Lean: `Singularity.exists_leftInverse_tensorOneMap`, `Singularity.exists_leftInverse_tensorOneMap_of_isUnit_finrank`, `Singularity.isSplitMono_map_of_leftInverse` (the affine splitting). The statement on cohomology is not in Lean yet.

*Proof.* The morphism $\varphi$ is affine. So $H^i(X', \varphi^* \mathcal{F}) = H^i(X, \varphi_* \varphi^* \mathcal{F})$ [Stacks], and for separated $X$ this is [Har, Exercise III.4.1]. Also $\varphi_* \varphi^* \mathcal{F} \cong \mathcal{F} \otimes_{\mathcal{O}_X} \varphi_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}$, because over an affine open $\operatorname{Spec} A$ with inverse image $\operatorname{Spec} B$ both sides correspond to the $A$-module $M \otimes_A B$. Under these isomorphisms the pullback map is induced by the map $\mathcal{F} \to \mathcal{F} \otimes \varphi_* \mathcal{O}_{X'}$, $m \mapsto m \otimes 1$. Let $s \colon \varphi_* \mathcal{O}_{X'} \to \mathcal{O}_X$ be the left inverse of Proposition 1.3.2. Then $\operatorname{id} \otimes s$ is a left inverse of $m \mapsto m \otimes 1$. Cohomology is an additive functor, so it maps a split injection to a split injection. $\square$

The hypothesis that $d$ is invertible is necessary. The next example shows an alteration of degree $p$ in characteristic $p$ whose pullback kills a nonzero class.

**Example 1.3.7 (a supersingular elliptic curve).** Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$ with $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, and let $E \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ be the projective closure of the affine curve $y^2 = x^3 - x$. The polynomial $f(x) = x^3 - x$ has the three distinct roots $0$, $1$ and $-1$, so $E$ is a smooth curve of genus 1, and $H^1(E, \mathcal{O}_E)$ is a $k$-vector space of dimension 1. Let $F \colon E \to E$ be the absolute Frobenius. As in Example 1.3.5, it is an alteration of degree $p$, purely inseparable. Its pullback on $H^1(E, \mathcal{O}_E)$ is a $p$-linear map. By [Har, Chapter IV, Section 4], for a curve $y^2 = f(x)$ with $f$ a cubic with distinct roots and $p$ odd, this map is zero if and only if the coefficient of $x^{p-1}$ in $f(x)^m$ is zero, where $m = (p - 1)/2$. Here
$$f(x)^m = (x^3 - x)^m = \sum_{j=0}^{m} \binom{m}{j} (-1)^{m-j} x^{m + 2j}.$$
The exponent $m + 2j$ equals $p - 1 = 2m$ only if $2j = m$. Since $p \equiv 3 \pmod 4$, the integer $m$ is odd, so there is no such $j$, and the coefficient is zero. So $F^*$ is zero on $H^1(E, \mathcal{O}_E)$, which is not zero. The curve $E$ is regular, so the example is not about singularities. It shows that an alteration of degree $p$ in characteristic $p$ can lose the coherent cohomology of its target. So Proposition 1.3.6 says nothing for an alteration whose degree is a power of $p$, and these are the alterations of Section 3.3.

For étale cohomology there is the same mechanism. Let $\Lambda$ be a ring and let $\varphi \colon X' \to X$ be a finite locally free morphism of constant degree $d$. There is a trace map $\operatorname{Tr} \colon \varphi_* \Lambda \to \Lambda$ of étale sheaves on $X$ such that the composite of $\Lambda \to \varphi_* \Lambda$ and $\operatorname{Tr}$ is multiplication by $d$ [Stacks]. For a finite morphism the functor $\varphi_*$ on étale sheaves is exact, so $H^i(X'_{\mathrm{et}}, \Lambda) = H^i(X_{\mathrm{et}}, \varphi_* \Lambda)$ [Stacks].

**Proposition 1.3.8.** Let $\varphi \colon X' \to X$ be a finite locally free morphism of constant degree $d$, and let $\Lambda$ be a ring in which $d$ is invertible. Then for each $i \ge 0$ the pullback map $H^i(X_{\mathrm{et}}, \Lambda) \to H^i(X'_{\mathrm{et}}, \Lambda)$ is injective, and it has a left inverse.

Lean: `Singularity.trace_leftInverse_of_isUnit`, `Singularity.exists_leftInverse_algebraMap_of_free` (the trace splitting of a free algebra). Étale cohomology is not in Lean yet.

*Proof.* Under the identification $H^i(X'_{\mathrm{et}}, \Lambda) = H^i(X_{\mathrm{et}}, \varphi_* \Lambda)$, the pullback map is induced by the map of sheaves $\Lambda \to \varphi_* \Lambda$. The map $d^{-1} \operatorname{Tr}$ is a left inverse of this map of sheaves, and cohomology maps it to a left inverse of the pullback. $\square$

So for $\Lambda = \mathbb{Z}/\ell^n$ with $\ell$ prime to $d$, or for $\Lambda = \mathbb{Q}_\ell$-coefficients in the usual limit sense, the cohomology of $X$ is a direct summand of the cohomology of $X'$. Flatness cannot be dropped, even in degree 1, as the next example shows.

**Example 1.3.9 (the nodal cubic).** Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, let $X \subseteq \mathbb{P}^2_k$ be a plane cubic with one node, for example $y^2 z = x^2 (x + z)$ when $\operatorname{char} k \ne 2$, and let $\nu \colon \mathbb{P}^1_k \to X$ be its normalization. Then $\nu$ is finite of degree 1, and it is not flat at the node, because the fibre there has two points while the generic fibre has one. Let $n \ge 2$ be an integer. Let $Y$ be the curve obtained from $n$ copies $P_0, \dots, P_{n-1}$ of $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ by gluing the point over one branch of the node in $P_i$ to the point over the other branch in $P_{i+1}$, with indices modulo $n$. So $Y$ is a cycle of $n$ projective lines. The cyclic shift $P_i \mapsto P_{i+1}$ generates a free action of $\mathbb{Z}/n$ on $Y$, and $Y/(\mathbb{Z}/n) = X$. At each point of $Y$, also at the nodes, the map $Y \to X$ induces an isomorphism of complete local rings, so $Y \to X$ is finite étale, and it is a $\mathbb{Z}/n$-torsor. It is not trivial, because $Y$ is connected. The nontrivial $\mathbb{Z}/n$-torsors are nonzero classes in $H^1(X_{\mathrm{et}}, \mathbb{Z}/n)$ [Stacks], so this group is not zero. The pullback of $Y$ to $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ is the disjoint union of the $n$ copies $P_i$, which is the trivial torsor. In fact $H^1(\mathbb{P}^1_{k,\mathrm{et}}, \mathbb{Z}/n) = 0$, because by the Hurwitz formula $\mathbb{P}^1_k$ has no connected finite étale cover of degree greater than 1 [Har, Section IV.2]. So $\nu^*$ is not injective on $H^1$, although its degree is 1. This happens for each $n$, also for $n$ divisible by $\operatorname{char} k$. The point of the example is that the target $X$ is not normal, and $\nu$ is not flat.

### Cohomology with $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ coefficients

De Jong explains in the introduction of [dJ96] how Theorem 1.2.1 is used in $\ell$-adic cohomology. Let $X$ be a variety over a perfect field $k$, and let $\ell$ be a prime different from the characteristic. One applies Theorem 1.2.1 to $X$, then to the fibre products of the alteration with itself, and so on, by the standard construction of a proper hypercovering. This gives a simplicial scheme $X_\bullet$, projective and smooth over $k$, a strict normal crossings divisor $D_\bullet$ in $X_\bullet$, and a proper hypercovering $U_\bullet = X_\bullet \setminus D_\bullet \to X$. De Jong states the isomorphism
$$H^i(X \otimes_k \bar k, \mathbb{Q}_\ell) \cong H^i(U_\bullet \otimes_k \bar k, \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$$
[dJ96, Introduction]. For $k$ finite he deduces that the eigenvalues of Frobenius on $H^i(X \otimes_k \bar k, \mathbb{Q}_\ell)$ are Weil numbers. This gives a new proof of a result of Deligne's second paper on the Weil conjectures, from the result of the first one for smooth projective varieties. Here $X$ need not be proper: one applies the theorem to a compactification $\bar X$ of $X$, with the closed subset $\bar X \setminus X$. In this use the degree of the alteration plays no role, because cohomological descent for a proper hypercovering does not use a direct summand.

The degree matters when one wants a direct summand, as in Propositions 1.3.6 and 1.3.8. With coefficients $\mathbb{Q}_\ell$ the trace can be divided by each degree. With coefficients $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$ or $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ the trace argument needs a degree prime to $\ell$. Temkin writes that the theorem of Gabber extends the applications of alterations to cohomology with coefficients in which $\ell$ is not inverted, such as $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$ and $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$ [Tem17, 1.1.2], and it is how Gabber's theorems are used for étale cohomology of quasi-excellent schemes in [ILO14] and for motives with coefficients in which $p$ is invertible in [Kel17]. For coefficients in which $p$ is not invertible, such as coherent cohomology in characteristic $p$, the degree must be prime to $p$. Temkin's theorem in Section 3.3 gives the opposite: a degree that is a power of $p$. Example 1.3.7 shows that such an alteration can lose information. So the theorems of this volume give the $\ell$-adic cohomology of $X$ for $\ell \ne p$, but they give little for $p$-adic or coherent questions. A resolution would give both. This is one more way to see that alterations do not replace resolution in characteristic $p$.

### Notes and references

The introduction of [Tem17] explains that alterations of degree prime to $\ell$ extend the applications of de Jong's theorem to étale cohomology with coefficients in $\mathbb{Z}/\ell$ and $\mathbb{Z}_\ell$. A trace map as in Proposition 1.3.2 is the basic tool there. Nothing of this section is in Lean.
