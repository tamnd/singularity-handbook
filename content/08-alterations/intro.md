# Introduction

This volume treats alterations. An alteration of an integral variety $X$ is a proper, dominant and generically finite morphism $X' \to X$ from an integral variety of the same dimension. A resolution of $X$ is an alteration of degree 1 that is an isomorphism over the regular locus. In 1996 de Jong proved that each variety over a field has an alteration $X' \to X$ with $X'$ regular, and that one can also make a given closed subset into a divisor with simple normal crossings [dJ96]. The theorem holds in each dimension and in each characteristic. It does not give a resolution, because the degree of the alteration can be greater than 1, and in characteristic $p$ it can be divisible by $p$.

Gabber and Temkin later controlled the degree. For a prime $\ell$ different from the characteristic, Gabber proved that the degree can be made prime to $\ell$ [ILO14, Exposé X]. Temkin proved that in characteristic $p$ the degree can be made a power of $p$ [Tem17]. In characteristic 0 the theorem of Temkin gives a degree equal to 1. In characteristic $p$ the two results together say that the only obstruction to a resolution by these methods is a power of $p$. This is the same obstruction that Volume 9 meets in dimension 4: the action of a group of order $p$ in characteristic $p$ is wild, and nobody knows how to resolve its quotients in general.

Chapter 1 gives the definitions and states the theorem of de Jong. Section 1.1 defines alterations, their degree, and the strict semistable pairs of de Jong, and it proves the elementary properties. Section 1.2 states the theorem over a field and over a trait, and it proves the case of curves. Section 1.3 explains what the theorem gives for the problem of this handbook and what it does not give. Chapter 2 explains the proof of de Jong. Section 2.1 studies semistable curves and proves the local computations of blowups that make the total space regular. Section 2.2 states the theorems of Deligne-Mumford and Knudsen on stable curves and their moduli, and it shows how the moduli give the extension of a family of curves after an alteration of the base. Sections 2.3 and 2.4 give the induction on the dimension step by step. Chapter 3 treats the refinements. Section 3.1 is about Galois alterations and quotients, Section 3.2 about the theorem of Gabber, and Section 3.3 about the theorem of Temkin. Chapter 4 is short. It gives the nodes of the map and a plan for the Lean statement of the theorem of de Jong with the moduli of stable curves as a named hypothesis, as issue Q-08 of the project asks.

The reader we have in mind knows Volume 1, the blowups of Volume 4, Chapter 1, and the basic theory of schemes and curves in [Har] and [Liu]. We cite [Mat] for commutative algebra and [Stacks] for facts on schemes.

We prove the elementary steps. These are the properties of alterations, the curve case, the trace splitting for a degree prime to $p$, the local computations of the blowups of Section 2.1 and Section 2.4, the extension of a family of curves from the moduli as a named hypothesis, the reductions of the induction, the descent through a Galois group, the Sylow step of Gabber, and the separable distillation of field extensions. We cite the theorems of de Jong, Deligne-Mumford, Knudsen, Gabber and Temkin with precise references, and we say at each place what we prove and what we cite.

None of the theorems of this volume is proved in Lean. The map has the nodes SG0000001K (de Jong), SG0000001M (Gabber) and SG0000001N (Temkin), all of route R6, and none of them has a Lean statement yet. The Lean names that occur in this volume are the definitions of a regular scheme, a resolution and a divisor with simple normal crossings, which are in the library.
