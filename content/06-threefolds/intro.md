# Introduction

This volume treats the resolution of singularities of threefolds in characteristic $p > 0$. Volume 4 resolves surfaces in each characteristic, and Volume 9 studies the open case of dimension 4. Dimension 3 is the largest dimension in which resolution is known in characteristic $p$. Abhyankar proved it for projective threefolds over an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p > 5$ [Abh66], Cutkosky gave a shorter proof of this case [Cut09], and Cossart and Piltant proved it for each $p$ and for quasi-excellent schemes of dimension 3, also in mixed characteristic [CP08, CP09, CP19].

Chapter 1 states the theorems and gives the plan of the proofs. Section 1.1 states the theorems in the order of their proofs and recalls the method of Zariski. Section 1.2 explains the role of maximal contact when the multiplicity is small, and it gives the plan of Cossart and Piltant and their main local theorem. Chapter 2 reduces local uniformization to equations of degree $p$. Section 2.1 proves the field theory of extensions of degree $p$, Section 2.2 gives the plan of the reduction and proves its purely inseparable step for function fields, and Section 2.3 studies the equations $z^p + g(x, y, w)$. Chapter 3 is about the characteristic polyhedron of Hironaka [Hir67] and the invariants of Cossart and Piltant. Section 3.1 defines the polyhedron and proves the vertex preparation, Section 3.2 defines the invariants, proves that cleaning gives the minimal polyhedron of a purely inseparable equation, and explains the induction on the invariant, and Section 3.3 computes examples by hand. Chapter 4 explains where the proof uses dimension 3, and what is missing in dimension 4. Section 4.3 reports the experiment E4 of the lab of the project, which computes the invariants of Chapter 3 on random point blowups in dimensions 3 and 4.

The reader we have in mind knows Volume 1, the valuations of Volume 3, and the residual order of Volume 7, Chapter 1. We use the basic theory of regular local rings and of complete local rings [Mat].

The theorems of Abhyankar and of Cossart and Piltant are long, and we do not prove them. We state them with precise references, and we say at each place what we prove and what we cite. We prove the field theory of Chapter 2, the purely inseparable step of the reduction for function fields, the lemmas on the equations $z^p + g$, the vertex preparation lemma, the basic properties of $\delta$, the description of the solvable vertices of $X^p + f$, the description of the minimal polyhedron of $X^p + f$ over a perfect field by cleaning, the first step of Theorem 3.2.7 in the case $\epsilon = 0$, and the formal induction on $\iota$ that gives Theorem 1.2.6 from the cited theorems. The description by cleaning shows that the invariant $\epsilon$ of Cossart and Piltant is the residual order of Volume 7. We cite the theorem of Hironaka on the existence of a minimal polyhedron, the theorem of Cossart and Piltant that their invariant does not increase, and the Projection Theorem. Each example is computed by hand, except for the parts that we mark as cited, and we checked the computations with a short program.

The resolution of threefolds is not proved in Lean. Most statements of this volume have the line "Lean: none yet". The Lean names that occur are of three kinds. Some are statements of the map, such as `Singularity.localUniformization_three`, which are definitions of the statements and not proofs. Some are Lean proofs of results of Chapter 2: the field theory of Section 2.1, the choice of the model in Lemma 2.2.2, the induction on the degree in Steps 3 and 4 of Section 2.2 (with the step of degree $p$ as an explicit hypothesis), and the lemmas on the equations $z^p + g$ and the example of Narasimhan in Section 2.3. The others are from Volume 7: the definitions of the characteristic polyhedron and of the residual order, and Moh's bound for point blowups of polynomials. The Lean work on the characteristic polyhedron and on Moh's bound is described in Volume 7.
