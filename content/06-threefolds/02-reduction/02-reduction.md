## The reduction to equations of degree p

In this section we explain how Cossart and Piltant deduce Theorem 1.1.3 from the local Theorem 1.2.6 [CP19, Proposition 4.10]. We give the plan of the proof and its references. We prove one step completely: the purely inseparable steps can be written with an equation $X^p - x$ whose constant term lies in a regular local ring.

### The statement

**Proposition 2.2.1 (Cossart-Piltant).** Theorem 1.2.6 implies Theorem 1.1.3.

This is [CP19, Proposition 4.10]. Together with Theorem 1.2.6, it proves Theorem 1.1.3 and therefore Theorem 1.1.2 and Theorem 1.1.6. We give the steps of the proof. Each step is a theorem of [CP19] or of [CP08], and we do not reproduce the proofs.

*Plan of the proof.* Step 1 (patching). By [CP19, Propositions 4.6 and 4.8], it suffices to prove local uniformization for each complete local domain $(A, \mathfrak{m}_A)$ of dimension 3 and each valuation of its fraction field with center $\mathfrak{m}_A$, and one may assume that the valuation has rank 1. Here local uniformization means that a finite sequence of local blowups along the valuation makes the center regular. The patching follows the method of Zariski [Zar44], as Volume 3, Chapter 4 explains for function fields. It uses the principalization of ideals on regular excellent schemes of dimension 3 [CP19, Proposition 4.4] and the embedded resolution of surfaces. In the equal characteristic 0 case the theorem is known, so one may assume that the residue field of the valuation has characteristic $p > 0$.

Step 2 (a regular subring). By the Noether normalization theorem for complete local rings [Mat, Theorem 29.4], there is a complete regular local ring $S \subseteq A$ of dimension 3 such that $A$ is a finite $S$-module. Let $F$ and $K$ be the fraction fields of $S$ and $A$. Then $K/F$ is a finite extension. The center of the valuation on $S$ is regular, so local uniformization holds for $F$. The task is to carry it up along $K/F$.

Step 3 (the purely inseparable part). Let $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ be the separable closure of $F$ in $K$. By Lemma 2.1.2, $K/K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ is a tower of steps $K_i = K_{i-1}(c_i)$ with $c_i^p \in K_{i-1}$ and $c_i \notin K_{i-1}$. Assume local uniformization for $K_{i-1}$, and let $S_{i-1}$ be a regular local ring at the center. One can choose $x_i = c_i^p$ in $S_{i-1}$, after a change of $c_i$. Cossart and Piltant prove this with the principalization of the ideal generated by a numerator and a denominator of $x_i$ [CP19, Proposition 4.4]. Lemma 2.2.2 below proves the same fact for function fields. Then $h = X^p - x_i \in S_{i-1}[X]$ satisfies condition 1 of Theorem 1.2.6, and Theorem 1.2.6 gives local uniformization for $K_i$.

Step 4 (the separable part). Now let $K/F$ be separable. Let $\bar K$ be a Galois closure of $K/F$ and let $\bar v$ be an extension of the valuation to $\bar K$. The ramification theory of valuations [ZS2, Chapter VI] gives the inertia fields $F^i \subseteq K^i$ and the ramification fields $F^r \subseteq K^r$ of $\bar K/F$ and of $\bar K/K$ for $\bar v$. They form a diagram
$$F \subseteq F^i \subseteq F^r, \qquad K \subseteq K^i \subseteq K^r, \qquad F \subseteq K, \quad F^i \subseteq K^i, \quad F^r \subseteq K^r.$$
These extensions behave as follows [CP19, Section 4.2]. The extensions $F^i/F$ and $K^i/K$ are unramified. The extensions $F^r/F^i$ and $K^r/K^i$ are totally ramified and abelian of degree prime to $p$. The extension $K^r/F^r$ is a tower of totally ramified Galois extensions of degree $p$. For the unramified and the tame parts, local uniformization passes up and down with classical methods: [CP19] refers to the proof of [CP08, Theorem 8.1] and proves an approximation of the ramification groups of the valuation by those of local rings [CP19, Proposition 4.13]. For the ramification theory of local rings, [CP19] refers to the book of Abhyankar [Abh59]. For each Galois step of degree $p$, Theorem 1.2.6 with condition 2 gives local uniformization. In equal characteristic $p$ these steps are Artin-Schreier extensions by Theorem 2.1.3. $\square$

The proof needs Theorem 1.2.6 only for the Galois and inseparable steps of degree $p$ that are immediate for the valuation, that is, the steps with defect $p$ (Section 2.1). For the steps that are not immediate there is a much simpler proof [CP19, Remark 4.11].

### The purely inseparable step

We prove the part of Step 3 that is elementary, in the language of function fields of Volume 3. Let $K/k$ be a function field over a field $k$ of characteristic $p$, and let $v$ be a valuation of $K/k$.

**Lemma 2.2.2.** Assume that $v$ has local uniformization, and let $c \in K \setminus K^p$. Then there are an element $x \in \mathcal{O}_v$ with $x \notin K^p$ and $K(x^{1/p}) = K(c^{1/p})$, and an affine model $A$ of $K/k$ with $x \in A \subseteq \mathcal{O}_v$ such that $A_P$ is regular, where $P = \mathfrak{m}_v \cap A$. For such $A$ and $x$, the polynomial $h = X^p - x \in A_P[X]$ is irreducible over $K$, and the valuation $v$ has a unique extension $w$ to $L = K(x^{1/p})$. The center of $w$ on $\operatorname{Spec} A_P[X]/(h)$ lies over $P$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Since $\mathcal{O}_v$ is a valuation ring, $c \in \mathcal{O}_v$ or $c^{-1} \in \mathcal{O}_v$. Let $x = c$ in the first case and $x = c^{-1}$ in the second case. We have $x \notin K^p$, since $c^{-1} = b^p$ would give $c = (b^{-1})^p$. Also $(c^{-1})^{1/p} = (c^{1/p})^{-1}$, so $K(x^{1/p}) = K(c^{1/p})$. By the affine form of local uniformization, Proposition 4.1.6 of Volume 3, applied to the finite set $\{x\} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_v$, there is an affine model $A$ with $x \in A \subseteq \mathcal{O}_v$ and $A_P$ regular. By Lemma 2.1.1, $h = X^p - x$ is irreducible over $K$, since $x \notin K^p$. Let $\theta = x^{1/p} \in L$. Each $b \in L$ satisfies $b^p \in K$, since $L = K(\theta)$ and the Frobenius map is a ring homomorphism. Let $w$ be an extension of $v$ to $L$. For $b \in L$ we have $p\, w(b) = v(b^p)$, and $p\, w(b) \ge 0$ if and only if $w(b) \ge 0$, since an ordered group is torsion free. So $\mathcal{O}_w = \{ b \in L : b^p \in \mathcal{O}_v \}$. This ring does not depend on $w$, so the extension is unique. Finally $\theta^p = x \in \mathcal{O}_v$, so $\theta \in \mathcal{O}_w$. Thus $A_P[\theta] \subseteq \mathcal{O}_w$, and $\mathfrak{m}_w \cap A_P = P A_P$. The ring $A_P[X]/(h)$ is $A_P[\theta]$, and the center of $w$ on it is $\mathfrak{m}_w \cap A_P[\theta]$, which lies over $P A_P$. $\square$

If $P$ is the maximal ideal of a closed point and $\operatorname{trdeg}_k K = 3$, then $S = A_P$ is an excellent regular local ring of dimension 3, and $(S, h)$ is in case 1 of Theorem 1.2.6. If the center $P$ has smaller dimension, the local ring $A_P$ has dimension at most 2, and the theorems for surfaces apply. To finish Step 3 one must also show that the new model can be chosen to contain a given finite subset of $\mathcal{O}_w$. This is part of [CP19, Section 4.2], and we do not prove it here.

Compare the theorem of Temkin, Theorem 4.2.2 of Volume 3 and [Tem13]. In each dimension, it gives local uniformization after a finite purely inseparable extension of the function field, and the theorem chooses this extension. In Step 3 the extension is given, so the theorem of Temkin does not apply there.

### Condition (E)

The proof of Theorem 1.2.6 uses one more hypothesis, which the reduction can provide. Let $S$ be an excellent regular local ring with a regular system of parameters $u_1, \dots, u_n$, and let $E = \operatorname{div}(u_1 \cdots u_e)$ for some $e \le n$. Let $h$ be as in Theorem 1.2.6, let $X = \operatorname{Spec} S[X]/(h)$, and let $\eta \colon X \to \operatorname{Spec} S$ be the projection.

**Definition 2.2.3 (condition (E)).** The triple $(S, h, E)$ satisfies *condition (E)* if the residue field of $S$ has characteristic $p$ and one of these holds.

1. In case 1 of Theorem 1.2.6, the image under $\eta$ of the locus $\operatorname{Sing}_p X$ of the points of multiplicity $p$ of $X$ is contained in $E$.
2. In case 2 of Theorem 1.2.6, the discriminant locus of $\eta$ is contained in $E$. If $S$ has characteristic 0, then also each component of $E$ lies in the locus $p = 0$, that is $E \subseteq \operatorname{div}(p)_{\mathrm{red}}$.

This is [CP19, Definition 2.32]. In dimension 3 one can achieve condition (E) by preparatory blowups, with the known resolution theorems for schemes of dimension 2 [CP19, Corollary 4.19]. Section 2.3 gives a simple test for condition (E) in case 1, and Section 3.3 uses it in the examples. The divisor $E$ is the place where the earlier blowups leave their trace, as in characteristic 0, and the invariants of Chapter 3 depend on $E$.

### Notes and references

Section 4 of [CP19] gives the full reduction for quasi-excellent schemes, also in mixed characteristic. For varieties over fields $k$ with $[k : k^p] < \infty$, the reduction is in [CP08], whose title describes it: a reduction to local uniformization on Artin-Schreier and purely inseparable coverings. The defect of the steps of degree $p$ is studied by Kuhlmann [Kuh-book].
