## The reduction to equations of degree p

In this section we explain how Cossart and Piltant deduce Theorem 1.1.3 from the local Theorem 1.2.6 [CP19, Proposition 4.10]. We give the plan of the proof and its references. We prove one step completely: the purely inseparable steps can be written with an equation $X^p - x$ whose constant term lies in a regular local ring.

*Motivation.* Theorem 1.1.3 is a global statement about schemes, and Theorem 1.2.6 is a local statement about one equation of degree $p$ and one valuation. The reduction connects them in three moves. First, the method of Zariski reduces resolution to local uniformization, one valuation at a time. Second, a finite projection to a regular scheme reduces local uniformization of a local domain to a finite field extension $K/F$, where local uniformization for $F$ is trivial. Third, Section 2.1 cuts $K/F$ into steps, and each step that is not classical is one equation of degree $p$. Each move uses a theorem that is known in dimension 3. We give the moves in order and say for each one which theorem it uses.

### The statement

**Proposition 2.2.1 (Cossart-Piltant).** Theorem 1.2.6 implies Theorem 1.1.3.

*Plan of the proof.* Step 1 (patching). By [CP19, Propositions 4.6 and 4.8], it suffices to prove local uniformization for each complete local domain $(A, \mathfrak{m}_A)$ of dimension 3 and each valuation of its fraction field with center $\mathfrak{m}_A$, and one may assume that the valuation has rank 1. Here local uniformization is the property (LU) of [CP19, Section 4.1]: for each valuation $v$ with $\mathfrak{m}_v \cap A = \mathfrak{m}_A$ whose residue field is algebraic over the residue field of $A$, there is a finitely generated $A$-algebra $T$ with $A \subseteq T \subseteq \mathcal{O}_v$ such that $T_P$ is regular, where $P = \mathfrak{m}_v \cap T$. The patching follows the method of Zariski [Zar44], as Volume 3, Chapter 4 explains for function fields. It uses the principalization of ideals on regular excellent schemes of dimension 3 [CP19, Proposition 4.4] and the embedded resolution of surfaces. In the equal characteristic 0 case the theorem is known, so one may assume that the residue field of the valuation has characteristic $p > 0$.

Step 2 (a regular subring). By the Noether normalization theorem for complete local rings [Mat, Theorem 29.4], there is a complete regular local ring $S \subseteq A$ of dimension 3 such that $A$ is a finite $S$-module. Let $F$ and $K$ be the fraction fields of $S$ and $A$. Then $K/F$ is a finite extension. The center of the valuation on $S$ is regular, so local uniformization holds for $F$. The task is to carry it up along $K/F$.

Step 3 (the purely inseparable part). Let $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ be the separable closure of $F$ in $K$. Local uniformization for $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ comes from Step 4, since $K^{\mathrm{sep}}/F$ is separable. By Lemma 2.1.2, $K/K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ is a tower of steps $K_i = K_{i-1}(c_i)$ with $c_i^p \in K_{i-1}$ and $c_i \notin K_{i-1}$. Assume local uniformization for $K_{i-1}$, and let $S_{i-1}$ be a regular local ring at the center. One can choose $x_i = c_i^p$ in $S_{i-1}$, after a change of $c_i$. Cossart and Piltant prove this with the principalization of the ideal generated by a numerator and a denominator of $x_i$ [CP19, Proposition 4.4]. Lemma 2.2.2 below proves the same fact for function fields. Then $h = X^p - x_i \in S_{i-1}[X]$ satisfies condition 1 of Theorem 1.2.6, and Theorem 1.2.6 gives local uniformization for $K_i$. The induction along this tower is in Lean, with the step of degree $p$ as a hypothesis; see the subsection on the induction in Lean below.

Step 4 (the separable part). Now let $K/F$ be separable. Let $\bar K$ be a Galois closure of $K/F$ and let $\bar v$ be an extension of the valuation to $\bar K$. The ramification theory of valuations [ZS2, Chapter VI] gives the inertia fields $F^i \subseteq K^i$ and the ramification fields $F^r \subseteq K^r$ of $\bar K/F$ and of $\bar K/K$ for $\bar v$. They form a diagram
$$F \subseteq F^i \subseteq F^r, \qquad K \subseteq K^i \subseteq K^r, \qquad F \subseteq K, \quad F^i \subseteq K^i, \quad F^r \subseteq K^r.$$
These extensions behave as follows [CP19, Section 4.2]. The extensions $F^i/F$ and $K^i/K$ are unramified. The extensions $F^r/F^i$ and $K^r/K^i$ are totally ramified and abelian of degree prime to $p$. The extension $K^r/F^r$ is a tower of Galois extensions of degree $p$. For the unramified and the tame parts, local uniformization passes up and down with classical methods: [CP19] refers to the proof of [CP08, Theorem 8.1] and proves an approximation of the ramification groups of the valuation by those of local rings [CP19, Proposition 4.13]. For the ramification theory of local rings, [CP19] refers to the book of Abhyankar [Abh59]. For each Galois step of degree $p$, Theorem 1.2.6 with condition 2 gives local uniformization. In equal characteristic $p$ these steps are Artin-Schreier extensions by Theorem 2.1.3. The induction along a Galois extension of degree $p^r$ is in Lean, with the step of degree $p$ as a hypothesis. The passage from $K$ to the decomposition field and from the decomposition field to the inertia field are in Lean with no hypothesis. The tame part is not in Lean. $\square$

For the Galois steps of degree $p$ of Step 4, the proof needs Theorem 1.2.6 only for the steps that are immediate for the valuation, that is, the steps with defect $p$ (Section 2.1). For the Galois steps that are not immediate there is a much simpler proof [CP19, Remark 4.11]. Step 3 uses Theorem 1.2.6 for each purely inseparable step.

### The induction on the degree in Lean

The Lean library proves the induction on the degree in Steps 3 and 4. It takes the passage through one step of degree $p$ as an explicit hypothesis, and it does not prove this hypothesis. So it does not prove Proposition 2.2.1. The setting is that of Volume 3: $k$ is a field of characteristic $p$, the fields are extensions of $k$, and local uniformization of a valuation ring $W$ over $k$ is the affine form of Volume 3, Proposition 4.1.6 (`Singularity.HasLocalUniformization`). This is not the setting of complete local domains of Step 1. Fix $n \ge 0$. The two hypotheses are as follows.

- The inseparable step hypothesis (`Singularity.LocalUniformizationInseparableStep`). Let $E$ be an extension of $k$ with $\operatorname{trdeg}_k E = n$, let $E'/E$ be a purely inseparable extension of degree $p$, and let $W$ be a valuation ring of $E'$. If $W \cap E$ has local uniformization over $k$, then $W$ has local uniformization over $k$.
- The Galois step hypothesis (`Singularity.LocalUniformizationGaloisStep`). The same statement for each Galois extension $E'/E$ of degree $p$.

For $n = 3$ these hypotheses follow from Theorem 1.2.6, case 1 for the inseparable step and case 2 for the Galois step [CP19, Theorem 1.5], together with the patching of [CP19, Section 4]. Lemma 2.2.2 below is the elementary part of the inseparable step. For $n = 4$ the hypotheses are open. With these hypotheses the library proves three results. Let $K$ be an extension of $k$ with $\operatorname{trdeg}_k K = n$, let $L/K$ be a finite extension, and let $W$ be a valuation ring of $L$ such that $W \cap K$ has local uniformization over $k$.

1. If $L/K$ is purely inseparable, then $W$ has local uniformization over $k$. This uses the inseparable step hypothesis (`Singularity.hasLocalUniformization_of_isPurelyInseparable`).
2. Step 3. Let $F \subseteq K$ be a subfield that contains $k$, with $\operatorname{trdeg}_k F = n$ and $[K : F]$ finite, and let $V$ be a valuation ring of $K$. If $V \cap K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ has local uniformization over $k$, where $K^{\mathrm{sep}}$ is the separable closure of $F$ in $K$, then $V$ has local uniformization over $k$. This uses the inseparable step hypothesis (`Singularity.hasLocalUniformization_of_separableClosure`).
3. The last part of Step 4. If $L/K$ is Galois of degree $p^r$, then $W$ has local uniformization over $k$. This uses the Galois step hypothesis (`Singularity.hasLocalUniformization_of_isGalois`).

The library also proves one part of the unramified part of Step 4, with no hypothesis. Let $L/K$ be a finite Galois extension with group $G$, and let $W$ be a valuation ring of $L$. The inertia group $I \subseteq G$ is the set of $\sigma \in G$ that map $W$ into $W$ and act trivially on the residue field of $W$ (`Singularity.inertiaGroup`). Let $K^T$ be the fixed field of $I$ (`Singularity.inertiaField`), and let $K^Z$ be the fixed field of the decomposition group, that is, of the $\sigma \in G$ with $\sigma(W) = W$.

4. Assume that each $\sigma \in G$ maps $W$ into $W$. Then the residue extension of $W \cap K^T$ over $W \cap K$ is separable, and its degree is $[K^T : K]$ (`Singularity.isSeparable_and_finrank_residueField_inertiaField`). The proof uses the bound of the residue degree by the degree of the extension (`Singularity.finiteDimensional_residueField_and_finrank_le`). So the second part of Volume 5, Lemma 1.3.3 gives an étale generator, Volume 5, Theorem 1.3.4 applies, and local uniformization of $W \cap K$ over $k$ gives local uniformization of $W \cap K^T$ over $k$ (`Singularity.hasLocalUniformization_inertiaField`). Over the decomposition field the assumption holds, so local uniformization of $W \cap K^Z$ gives local uniformization of $W \cap K^T$ (`Singularity.hasLocalUniformization_inertiaField_decompositionField`).

5. Local uniformization of $W \cap K$ over $k$ gives local uniformization of $W \cap K^Z$ over $k$ (`Singularity.hasLocalUniformization_decompositionField`). With result 4 it also gives local uniformization of $W \cap K^T$ over $k$ (`Singularity.hasLocalUniformization_inertiaField_of_base`).

The usual proof of result 5 uses the henselization of $W \cap K$. The Lean proof does not use it. It finds an étale generator of $K^Z$ over $K$ directly. Let $A = W \cap K$ and let $D$ be the decomposition group. For each $\tau \in G$ with $\tau(W) \neq W$, the rings $W$ and $\tau(W)$ are two different extensions of $A$ to $L$, so there is an element $a_\tau$ that is integral over $A$, such that $a_\tau - 1$ is in the maximal ideal of $W$ and $a_\tau$ is in the maximal ideal of $\tau(W)$ (`Singularity.exists_isIntegral_sub_one_of_ne`). Let $y$ be the product of the elements $a_\tau$ and let $z = \prod_{\delta \in D} \delta(y)$. Then $z$ is in $K^Z$ and is integral over $A$, $z - 1$ is in the maximal ideal of $W$, and $\sigma(z)$ is in the maximal ideal of $W$ for each $\sigma \notin D$ (`Singularity.exists_mem_fixedField_decompositionSubgroup`). Thus $z$ is a unit of $W$ and $\sigma(z) \neq z$ for $\sigma \notin D$, so $K(z) = K^Z$. The derivative of the minimal polynomial of $z$ at $z$ is the product of the elements $z - \sigma(z)$ over the cosets of $D$ different from $D$, and each factor is a unit of $W$. So $z$ is an étale generator (`Singularity.isEtaleGenerator_decompositionField`), and Volume 5, Theorem 1.3.4 applies.

Results 4 and 5 do not need the hypotheses on the step of degree $p$. These parts are not in the library: Steps 1 and 2, the tame part of Step 4 [CP08, Theorem 8.1] [CP19, Proposition 4.13], and the descent from the Galois closure to $K$.

The Lean proofs are inductions on the degree. A finite extension does not change the transcendence degree (`Singularity.trdeg_eq_of_finiteDimensional`), so each intermediate field again has transcendence degree $n$ over $k$, and the hypotheses apply to it. In the purely inseparable case the induction is on $[L : K]$. If $[L : K] = 1$, the map $K \to L$ is an isomorphism of $k$-algebras, and the models move along it (`Singularity.HasLocalUniformization.of_algEquiv`). Otherwise Lemma 2.1.5 gives the first step $K \subset E_1$ of a tower, of degree $p$ (`Singularity.exists_purelyInseparable_tower_from`). The hypothesis gives local uniformization of $W \cap E_1$, and the induction for $L/E_1$, of smaller degree, gives local uniformization of $W$. Result 2 is result 1 for $K/K^{\mathrm{sep}}$. In the Galois case the induction is on $r$. For $r \ge 1$ the Galois group has order $p^r$. The theorem of Sylow gives a subgroup of order $p^{r-1}$, which is normal because its index $p$ is the smallest prime factor of the order. Its fixed field $M$ is Galois over $K$ of degree $p$, and $L/M$ is Galois of degree $p^{r-1}$ (`Singularity.exists_isGalois_finrank_eq_prime`). This is the first step of the tower of Proposition 2.1.4. The hypothesis gives local uniformization of $W \cap M$, and the induction for $L/M$ gives the rest.

### The purely inseparable step

We prove the part of Step 3 that is elementary, in the language of function fields of Volume 3. Let $K/k$ be a function field over a field $k$ of characteristic $p$, and let $v$ be a valuation of $K/k$.

**Lemma 2.2.2.** Assume that $v$ has local uniformization, and let $c \in K \setminus K^p$. Then there are an element $x \in \mathcal{O}_v$ with $x \notin K^p$ and $K(x^{1/p}) = K(c^{1/p})$, and an affine model $A$ of $K/k$ with $x \in A \subseteq \mathcal{O}_v$ such that $A_P$ is regular, where $P = \mathfrak{m}_v \cap A$. For such $A$ and $x$, the polynomial $h = X^p - x \in A_P[X]$ is irreducible over $K$, and the valuation $v$ has a unique extension $w$ to $L = K(x^{1/p})$. The center of $w$ on $\operatorname{Spec} A_P[X]/(h)$ lies over $P$.

*Proof.* Since $\mathcal{O}_v$ is a valuation ring, $c \in \mathcal{O}_v$ or $c^{-1} \in \mathcal{O}_v$. Let $x = c$ in the first case and $x = c^{-1}$ in the second case. We have $x \notin K^p$, since $c^{-1} = b^p$ would give $c = (b^{-1})^p$. Also $(c^{-1})^{1/p} = (c^{1/p})^{-1}$, so $K(x^{1/p}) = K(c^{1/p})$. By the affine form of local uniformization, Proposition 4.1.6 of Volume 3, applied to the finite set $\{x\} \subseteq \mathcal{O}_v$, there is an affine model $A$ with $x \in A \subseteq \mathcal{O}_v$ and $A_P$ regular. By Lemma 2.1.1, $h = X^p - x$ is irreducible over $K$, since $x \notin K^p$. Let $\theta = x^{1/p} \in L$. Each $b \in L$ satisfies $b^p \in K$, since $L = K(\theta)$ and the Frobenius map is a ring homomorphism. Let $w$ be an extension of $v$ to $L$. For $b \in L$ we have $p\, w(b) = v(b^p)$, and $p\, w(b) \ge 0$ if and only if $w(b) \ge 0$, since an ordered group is torsion free. So $\mathcal{O}_w = \{ b \in L : b^p \in \mathcal{O}_v \}$. This ring does not depend on $w$, so the extension is unique. Finally $\theta^p = x \in \mathcal{O}_v$, so $\theta \in \mathcal{O}_w$. Thus $A_P[\theta] \subseteq \mathcal{O}_w$, and $\mathfrak{m}_w \cap A_P = P A_P$. The ring $A_P[X]/(h)$ is $A_P[\theta]$, and the center of $w$ on it is $\mathfrak{m}_w \cap A_P[\theta]$, which lies over $P A_P$. $\square$

If $P$ is the maximal ideal of a closed point and $\operatorname{trdeg}_k K = 3$, then $S = A_P$ is an excellent regular local ring of dimension 3, and $(S, h)$ is in case 1 of Theorem 1.2.6. If $P$ is not a maximal ideal, the local ring $A_P$ has dimension at most 2, and the theorems for surfaces apply. To finish Step 3 one must also show that the new model can be chosen to contain a given finite subset of $\mathcal{O}_w$. This is part of [CP19, Section 4.2], and we do not prove it here.

*Remark (when the step is easy).* Let $k$ be algebraically closed, and let $A$, $P$ and $x$ be as in Lemma 2.2.2, with $P$ a maximal ideal. We give a test for the regularity of the model $\operatorname{Spec} A_P[X]/(h)$ at the center of $w$. Since $k$ is algebraically closed, there is $a \in A_P$ with $x - a^p \in P A_P$. The center of $w$ is the maximal ideal $\mathfrak{n} = (P, X - a)$ of $A_P[X]$, and in the variable $T = X - a$ we have $h = T^p - (x - a^p)$. The ring $A_P[X]_{\mathfrak{n}}$ is regular of dimension $\dim A_P + 1$, so the quotient by $h$ is regular if and only if $h \notin \mathfrak{n}^2$. Since $p \ge 2$, this holds if and only if $x - a^p \notin P^2 A_P$. Another choice $a' = a + b$ with $b \in P A_P$ changes $x - a^p$ by $b^p \in P^p A_P \subseteq P^2 A_P$. So the test does not depend on $a$, and the model is regular at the center if and only if
$$x \notin P^2 A_P + A_P^p.$$
If this fails, the singularity of $h$ is a case of Theorem 1.2.6, after one changes $A$ and $x$ as far as one can.

*Example.* Let $k$ be algebraically closed of characteristic $p$, let $K = k(x_1, y, w)$, and let $v$ be the monomial valuation with $v(x_1) = 1$, $v(y) = \sqrt{2}$ and $v(w) = \sqrt{3}$. Its value group $\mathbb{Z} + \mathbb{Z}\sqrt{2} + \mathbb{Z}\sqrt{3}$ has rational rank 3. Let $c = x_1/y$. Then $v(c) = 1 - \sqrt{2} < 0$, so the proof of Lemma 2.2.2 takes $x = c^{-1} = y/x_1$, with $v(x) = \sqrt{2} - 1 > 0$. The element $x$ is not a $p$-th power in $K$, since $y/x_1$ has degree 1 in $y$. Let $A = k[x_1, y_1, w]$ with $y_1 = y/x_1$. All three generators have positive value, so the center of $v$ on $A$ is $P = (x_1, y_1, w)$, and $A_P$ is regular. Here $x = y_1$ lies in $P \setminus P^2$, so the test above shows that $h = X^p - y_1$ defines a regular model at once. The choice of $x$ and of the model can make the step trivial. In general one cannot find a choice with $x \notin P^2 A_P + A_P^p$, and then the work of Chapter 3 begins.

Compare the theorem of Temkin, Theorem 4.2.2 of Volume 3 and [Tem13]. In each dimension, it gives local uniformization after a finite purely inseparable extension of the function field, and the theorem chooses this extension. In Step 3 the extension is given, so the theorem of Temkin does not apply there.

### Condition (E)

The proof of Theorem 1.2.6 uses one more hypothesis, which the reduction can provide. Let $S$ be an excellent regular local ring with a regular system of parameters $u_1, \dots, u_n$, and let $E = \operatorname{div}(u_1 \cdots u_e)$ for some $e \le n$. Let $h$ be as in Theorem 1.2.6, let $X = \operatorname{Spec} S[X]/(h)$, and let $\eta \colon X \to \operatorname{Spec} S$ be the projection.

**Definition 2.2.3 (condition (E)).** The triple $(S, h, E)$ satisfies *condition (E)* if the residue field of $S$ has characteristic $p$ and one of these holds.

1. In case 1 of Theorem 1.2.6, the image under $\eta$ of the locus $\operatorname{Sing}_p X$ of the points of multiplicity $p$ of $X$ is contained in $E$.
2. In case 2 of Theorem 1.2.6, the discriminant locus of $\eta$ is contained in $E$. If $S$ has characteristic 0, then also each component of $E$ lies in the locus $p = 0$, that is $E \subseteq \operatorname{div}(p)_{\mathrm{red}}$.

This is [CP19, Definition 2.32]. In dimension 3 one can achieve condition (E) by preparatory blowups, with the known theorems on regular schemes of dimension 2 and 3: the embedded resolution of surfaces and the principalization of ideals [CP19, Propositions 4.2 to 4.4 and Corollary 4.19]. Section 4.1 lists these theorems. Section 2.3 gives a simple test for condition (E) in case 1, and Section 3.3 uses it in the examples. The divisor $E$ is the place where the earlier blowups leave their trace, as in characteristic 0, and the invariants of Chapter 3 depend on $E$.

*Remark (why condition (E) is needed).* The invariants $\epsilon$ and $\omega$ of Chapter 3 are computed in coordinates in which the components of $E$ are coordinate hyperplanes. They measure the part of $h$ that the divisor does not explain, as the residual order does in Volume 7. A blowup adds a component to $E$, and the invariants after the blowup are computed with the new divisor. Cossart and Piltant state Theorems 3.2.6 and 3.2.11 under condition (E), and they prove that it is kept by the blowups that they use [CP19, Propositions 2.28 and 2.34]. So the reduction must provide (E) at the start. Condition (E) asks that the points of multiplicity $p$, or the discriminant, lie over $E$. Then each point where the work is needed lies on the divisor, and the divisor gives the coordinates $u_1, \dots, u_e$ in which the invariants are read. We do not know an example in which Theorem 3.2.6 fails without condition (E), and we do not claim that the condition is necessary for it.

*Remark (the uses of dimension 3 in the reduction).* Each move of the reduction uses a theorem that is known only in small dimension. The patching of Step 1 uses the principalization of ideals and the embedded resolution of surfaces in regular schemes of dimension 3. Step 3 uses principalization again, to put $x_i$ in a regular local ring. Condition (E) comes from the same theorems. For a scheme of dimension 4 one would need the embedded resolution of threefolds in regular schemes of dimension 4, which is open in characteristic $p$. The field theory of Section 2.1 and the inductions in Lean hold in each dimension. Even if these theorems were known in dimension 4, one would still need the analogue of Theorem 1.2.6, and we do not claim that the rest of the reduction would go through without change. Section 4.1 lists the uses of dimension 3 in the whole proof, and Volume 5, Chapter 5, describes the programs that aim at dimension 4.

### Notes and references

Section 4 of [CP19] gives the full reduction for quasi-excellent schemes, also in mixed characteristic. For varieties over fields $k$ with $[k : k^p] < \infty$, the reduction is in [CP08], whose title describes it: a reduction to local uniformization on Artin-Schreier and purely inseparable coverings. The defect of the steps of degree $p$ is studied by Kuhlmann [Kuh-book].
