## Extensions of degree p

The reduction of Chapter 2 cuts a finite field extension into steps of degree $p$. In this section we prove the facts from field theory that it uses. There are two kinds of steps: purely inseparable steps $K(c)/K$ with $c^p \in K$, and Galois steps of degree $p$. In characteristic $p$ the Galois steps are the Artin-Schreier extensions. In this section $K$ is a field of characteristic $p > 0$, except in the last paragraph.

### Purely inseparable steps

An algebraic extension $L/K$ is *purely inseparable* if for each $c \in L$ there is $e \ge 0$ with $c^{p^e} \in K$.

**Lemma 2.1.1.** Let $a \in K$. The polynomial $X^p - a$ is irreducible in $K[X]$ if and only if $a \notin K^p$. If $a = b^p$ with $b \in K$, then $X^p - a = (X - b)^p$.

Lean: `Singularity.irreducible_X_pow_sub_C_iff`

*Proof.* If $a = b^p$, then $(X - b)^p = X^p - b^p = X^p - a$, since the Frobenius map is additive in characteristic $p$. Now let $a \notin K^p$. Let $c$ be a root of $X^p - a$ in an extension field. Then $X^p - a = X^p - c^p = (X - c)^p$. A monic factor of $X^p - a$ in $K[X]$ of degree $d$ with $0 < d < p$ is then $(X - c)^d$, by unique factorization in the larger polynomial ring. Its coefficient of $X^{d-1}$ is $-dc$, and it lies in $K$. Since $0 < d < p$, $d$ is a unit in $K$, so $c \in K$ and $a = c^p \in K^p$. This is a contradiction. So $X^p - a$ has no factor of degree between $0$ and $p$, and it is irreducible. $\square$

**Lemma 2.1.2.** Let $L/K$ be a finite purely inseparable extension. Then there is a tower $K = K_0 \subset K_1 \subset \dots \subset K_r = L$ with $K_i = K_{i-1}(c_i)$, $c_i^p \in K_{i-1}$ and $[K_i : K_{i-1}] = p$ for each $i$. In particular $[L : K] = p^r$.

Lean: `Singularity.exists_purelyInseparable_tower` (with the valuation of Lemma 2.1.5)

*Proof.* We argue by induction on $[L : K]$. If $L = K$, take $r = 0$. Otherwise let $c \in L \setminus K$, and let $e \ge 1$ be the smallest integer with $c^{p^e} \in K$. Put $c_1 = c^{p^{e-1}}$. Then $c_1 \notin K$ and $c_1^p \in K$. So $c_1^p \notin K^p$: if $c_1^p = b^p$ with $b \in K$, then $(c_1 - b)^p = 0$ and $c_1 = b \in K$. By Lemma 2.1.1, $[K(c_1) : K] = p$. The extension $L/K(c_1)$ is purely inseparable and has smaller degree, so the induction hypothesis gives the rest of the tower. $\square$

Each finite extension $L/K$ is a separable extension $K_s/K$ followed by a purely inseparable extension $L/K_s$, where $K_s$ is the separable closure of $K$ in $L$ [Lan02, Chapter V]. So Lemma 2.1.2 reduces the purely inseparable part to steps $X^p - a$.

### Artin-Schreier steps

**Theorem 2.1.3 (Artin-Schreier).** Let $a \in K$ and $P(X) = X^p - X - a$.

1. Either $P$ has a root in $K$, and then $P$ is a product of $p$ distinct linear factors in $K[X]$, or $P$ is irreducible. If $P$ is irreducible and $\theta$ is a root of $P$, then $K(\theta)/K$ is a Galois extension of degree $p$, and its Galois group is generated by the automorphism $\sigma$ with $\sigma(\theta) = \theta + 1$.
2. Conversely, let $L/K$ be a Galois extension of degree $p$. Then $L = K(\theta)$ for some $\theta$ with $\theta^p - \theta \in K$.

Lean: `Singularity.exists_artinSchreier_generator` (Part 2, with $\sigma(\theta) = \theta + 1$ for some $\sigma$ in the Galois group)

*Proof.* Part 1. Let $\theta$ be a root of $P$ in an extension field. For $i \in \mathbb{F}_p$ we have $i^p = i$, so
$$(\theta + i)^p - (\theta + i) - a = \theta^p - \theta - a + (i^p - i) = 0.$$
So the $p$ distinct elements $\theta + i$, $i \in \mathbb{F}_p$, are roots of $P$, and $P = \prod_{i \in \mathbb{F}_p} (X - \theta - i)$. If $\theta \in K$, this is a factorization into distinct linear factors over $K$. Let $\theta \notin K$, and let $Q \in K[X]$ be a monic factor of $P$ of degree $d$ with $0 < d < p$. Then $Q = \prod_{i \in I} (X - \theta - i)$ for a subset $I \subseteq \mathbb{F}_p$ with $d$ elements. The coefficient of $X^{d-1}$ in $Q$ is $-(d\theta + \sum_{i \in I} i)$, and it lies in $K$. Since $d$ is a unit, $\theta \in K$. This is a contradiction, so $P$ is irreducible. Then $K(\theta)$ contains all the roots of $P$, which are distinct. So $K(\theta)$ is the splitting field of a separable polynomial, and $K(\theta)/K$ is Galois of degree $p$. Since $\theta + 1$ is a root of the irreducible polynomial $P$, there is a $K$-automorphism $\sigma$ of $K(\theta)$ with $\sigma(\theta) = \theta + 1$. It is not the identity, and the Galois group has prime order $p$, so $\sigma$ generates it.

Part 2. Let $\sigma$ generate the Galois group, which is cyclic of order $p$. Let $N = \sigma - 1$, a $K$-linear map of $L$. Since $\sigma$ and $1$ commute and the binomial coefficients $\binom{p}{i}$ with $0 < i < p$ vanish in $K$, we have $N^p = \sigma^p - 1 = 0$. Since $\sigma \ne 1$, we have $N \ne 0$. Let $j \ge 1$ be the largest integer with $N^j \ne 0$, and choose $w \in L$ with $N^j w \ne 0$. Put $z = N^{j-1} w$ and $c = N z$. Then $c \ne 0$ and $N c = N^{j+1} w = 0$. So $\sigma(c) = c$, and $c \in K$ by Galois theory. Put $\theta = z/c$. Then
$$\sigma(\theta) = \frac{\sigma(z)}{c} = \frac{z + c}{c} = \theta + 1.$$
So $\sigma(\theta^p - \theta) = (\theta + 1)^p - (\theta + 1) = \theta^p - \theta$, and $\theta^p - \theta \in K$. Also $\theta \notin K$, since $\sigma(\theta) \ne \theta$. Since $[L : K] = p$ is prime, $L = K(\theta)$. $\square$

An extension of the form of Theorem 2.1.3 is an *Artin-Schreier extension*. In geometry one needs an equation with coefficients in a ring. Let $S$ be a domain with fraction field $K$, and let $\theta^p - \theta = a$ with $a \in K$. Choose $g \in S$, $g \ne 0$, with $g^p a \in S$. For example, if $a = b/s$ with $b, s \in S$, take $g = s$. Then $y = g \theta$ satisfies
$$y^p - g^{p-1} y - g^p a = 0,$$
because $y^p - g^{p-1} y = g^p (\theta^p - \theta)$. In Lean this identity is `Singularity.artinSchreier_integral`. This is an equation $h = Y^p - G Y + F$ in $S[Y]$ with $G = g^{p-1}$ a $(p-1)$-th power. Chapter 3 meets this form again in the initial forms $Z^p - G^{p-1} Z + F_{p, Z}$ of Cossart and Piltant.

### Towers of Galois steps

**Proposition 2.1.4.** Let $L/K$ be a finite Galois extension whose Galois group $P$ has order $p^r$. Then there is a tower $K = L_0 \subset L_1 \subset \dots \subset L_r = L$ in which each $L_i/L_{i-1}$ is an Artin-Schreier extension of degree $p$.

Lean: `Singularity.exists_artinSchreier_tower`, `Singularity.exists_isGalois_finrank_eq_prime`

*Proof.* We first show that $P$ has a chain of subgroups $1 = P_r \subset P_{r-1} \subset \dots \subset P_0 = P$ with $P_i$ normal in $P_{i-1}$ and $[P_{i-1} : P_i] = p$. We argue by induction on $r$. For $r = 0$ there is nothing to prove. For $r \ge 1$, the center $C$ of $P$ is nontrivial by the class equation [Lan02, Chapter I]. Choose $g \in C$ of order $p$; it exists, since a power of each element of $C$ different from 1 has order $p$. The subgroup $\langle g \rangle$ is normal in $P$, because $g$ is central. By induction, $P/\langle g \rangle$ has such a chain, and its preimage in $P$, followed by $\langle g \rangle \supset 1$, is a chain for $P$. Now let $L_i = L^{P_i}$. Then $L_i \subset L_{i+1}$. The extension $L/L_i$ is Galois with group $P_i$, and $P_{i+1}$ is normal in $P_i$, so by Galois theory $L_{i+1}/L_i$ is Galois with group $P_i/P_{i+1}$ of order $p$. Theorem 2.1.3, Part 2, applied to $L_{i+1}/L_i$, shows that it is an Artin-Schreier extension. Since $P_0 = P$ and $P_r = 1$, we have $L_0 = K$ and $L_r = L$. $\square$

The Lean proof builds the tower from the bottom, by induction on $r$. For $r \ge 1$, the theorem of Sylow gives a subgroup $H$ of $P$ of order $p^{r-1}$. Its index $p$ is the smallest prime factor of $|P|$, so $H$ is normal in $P$. The fixed field $L_1 = L^H$ is then Galois over $K$ of degree $p$, and $L/L_1$ is Galois of degree $p^{r-1}$. This is `Singularity.exists_isGalois_finrank_eq_prime`. Theorem 2.1.3, Part 2, gives the Artin-Schreier generator of $L_1/K$, and the induction for $L/L_1$ gives the other steps.

### Valuations on steps of degree p

Let $(K, v)$ be a henselian valued field whose residue field has characteristic $p$, let $L/K$ be an extension of degree $p$, and let $w$ be the extension of $v$ to $L$. By the theorem of Ostrowski (Theorem 3.1.4 of Volume 3), $p = e f d$, where $e$ is the ramification index, $f$ the residue degree, and $d$ the defect. Since $p$ is prime, exactly one of $e$, $f$, $d$ equals $p$. If $e = p$ or $f = p$, the extension is *not immediate*. If $d = p$, then $e = f = 1$, and the extension is *immediate*: it has the same value group and the same residue field. Proposition 3.2.5 of Volume 3 gives an immediate Artin-Schreier extension.

For the Galois steps of degree $p$, Cossart and Piltant need their deep Theorem 1.2.6 only for the immediate steps. For the Galois steps that are not immediate there is a much simpler proof [CP19, Remark 4.11], given in the case of equal characteristic in [CP08]. Kuhlmann studies the immediate extensions of degree $p$, the defect extensions, in [Kuh-book].

In mixed characteristic, $K$ has characteristic 0 and its residue field has characteristic $p$. Then a Galois extension of degree $p$ need not be an Artin-Schreier extension of $K$, but it is still a Galois extension with group $\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}$. This is why Theorem 1.2.6 states its second case with the Galois group and not with an equation.

### The purely inseparable tower and the valuation

In the reduction, the steps of Lemma 2.1.2 must also be compatible with a valuation. The next lemma gives this. Here $K$ has characteristic $p$ again, and a valuation ring of $K$ is a subring $V$ such that $b \in V$ or $b^{-1} \in V$ for each $b \in K^\times$.

**Lemma 2.1.5.** Let $L/K$ be a finite purely inseparable extension, and let $V$ be a valuation ring of $K$. Then the set
$$W = \{ b \in L : b^{p^n} \in V \text{ for some } n \ge 0 \}$$
is the unique valuation ring of $L$ with $W \cap K = V$. There is also a tower $K = E_0 \subset E_1 \subset \dots \subset E_r = L$ with $E_i = E_{i-1}(\theta_i)$, $\theta_i \notin E_{i-1}$, $\theta_i^p \in E_{i-1}$, $[E_i : E_{i-1}] = p$ and $\theta_i \in W$ for each $i$. So $E_i = E_{i-1}[X]/(X^p - x_i)$ with $x_i = \theta_i^p$ in the valuation ring $W \cap E_{i-1}$ of $E_{i-1}$.

Lean: `Singularity.existsUnique_valuationSubring_comap_eq`, `Singularity.exists_purelyInseparable_tower`

*Proof.* We first prove a fact about an arbitrary valuation ring $U$ of a field $M$: for $b \in M$ and $n \ge 1$, we have $b^n \in U$ if and only if $b \in U$. If $b \in U$, then $b^n \in U$. If $b \notin U$, then $b \ne 0$ and $b^{-1} \in U$ is not a unit of $U$, so $b^{-n} = (b^{-1})^n$ lies in the maximal ideal of $U$. Then $b^n \notin U$, since otherwise $1 = b^n b^{-n}$ lies in the maximal ideal.

Uniqueness. Let $W'$ be a valuation ring of $L$ with $W' \cap K = V$, and let $b \in L$. Since $L/K$ is purely inseparable, there is $n$ with $b^{p^n} \in K$. By the fact above, $b \in W'$ if and only if $b^{p^n} \in W'$, that is, if and only if $b^{p^n} \in V$. Also $b \in W$ if and only if $b^{p^n} \in V$. Indeed, let $b^{p^m} \in V$. If $m \le n$, then $b^{p^n} = (b^{p^m})^{p^{n-m}} \in V$. If $m > n$, then $b^{p^m} = (b^{p^n})^{p^{m-n}}$ with $b^{p^n} \in K$, and the fact above for $V$ gives $b^{p^n} \in V$. So $W' = W$.

Existence. If $b^{p^n} \in V$ and $c^{p^m} \in V$, put $l = \max(n, m)$. Then $b^{p^l}, c^{p^l} \in V$, and since the Frobenius map is a ring homomorphism, $(b + c)^{p^l} = b^{p^l} + c^{p^l}$, $(bc)^{p^l} = b^{p^l} c^{p^l}$ and $(-b)^{p^l} = (-1)^{p^l} b^{p^l}$ lie in $V$. So $W$ is a subring of $L$. Let $b \in L^\times$ and choose $n$ with $b^{p^n} \in K$. Then $b^{p^n} \in V$ or $(b^{-1})^{p^n} = (b^{p^n})^{-1} \in V$. So $b \in W$ or $b^{-1} \in W$, and $W$ is a valuation ring. For $b \in K$, the fact above applied to $V$ gives $b \in W$ if and only if $b \in V$. So $W \cap K = V$.

The tower. We argue as in the proof of Lemma 2.1.2, by induction on $[L : E]$, from an intermediate field $E$ to $L$. If $E = L$, there is nothing to do. Otherwise choose $c \in L \setminus E$ and the least $e \ge 1$ with $c^{p^e} \in E$, and put $c_1 = c^{p^{e-1}}$. Then $c_1 \notin E$ and $c_1^p \in E$. Since $W$ is a valuation ring, $c_1 \in W$ or $c_1^{-1} \in W$. Let $\theta = c_1$ in the first case and $\theta = c_1^{-1}$ in the second case. Then $\theta \notin E$, $\theta^p \in E$ and $\theta \in W$. As in the proof of Lemma 2.1.2, $\theta^p \notin E^p$, so $X^p - \theta^p$ is irreducible over $E$ by Lemma 2.1.1, and $[E(\theta) : E] = p$. The induction hypothesis applies to $E(\theta)$. Finally $[E_i : K] = p^i$ by induction on $i$, since degrees multiply in towers. $\square$

The first part of the lemma does not need that $L/K$ is finite. Lemma 2.2.2 proves the first part again for one step $K(x^{1/p})/K$ of a function field. Lemma 2.1.5 gives the purely inseparable part of Step 3 of the reduction in Section 2.2 as a statement about fields and valuation rings. It does not give the regular local rings of Step 3, and it does not prove the reduction.

### Notes and references
