# Introduction

This volume treats the resolution of singularities over a field of characteristic 0. Hironaka proved in 1964 that each variety over such a field has a resolution, and that the resolution can be reached by a sequence of blowups with smooth centers [Hir64]. The proof was long and hard to read. Thirty years of work by Villamayor [Vil89], Bierstone-Milman [BM97], Encinas-Villamayor [EV98], Encinas-Hauser [EH02], Włodarczyk [Wlo05] and others made it constructive and functorial, and much shorter. We follow the version of Kollár, which is Chapter 3 of his book [Kol07]. The survey of Hauser [Hau03] and the book of Cutkosky [Cut04] are good companions.

The project of which this handbook is a part is about characteristic $p > 0$, where resolution is open in dimension 4 and higher. Characteristic 0 is the model. Each method in characteristic $p$ is measured against it, and each failure in characteristic $p$ is a failure of one step of the proof of this volume. Thus we take care to say exactly where the proof uses the characteristic. In short, it uses it in three places. A polynomial of degree $d$ is recovered from its derivatives by the Euler identity, which divides by $d$. The Taylor coefficients of a function along a hypersurface are its derivatives divided by $i!$. And the residue fields are separable over the base field, so derivatives are defined by local coordinates. These three facts give the hypersurfaces of maximal contact, which make an induction on the dimension possible.

Chapter 1 states the theorems of Hironaka: resolution, embedded resolution, principalization of ideal sheaves, and the functorial form. It proves that principalization implies resolution, and it works out three examples by hand: curves, the cusp and the Whitney umbrella. Chapter 2 is about maximal contact. It defines the order of an ideal, the derivative ideals and the birational transforms of marked ideals, proves the theorem on maximal contact, and shows with the example of Narasimhan [Nar83] that maximal contact fails in characteristic 2. Chapter 3 gives the induction on the dimension. It proves that the points of high order of an ideal are seen by the coefficient ideal on a hypersurface of maximal contact, also after blowups, and it assembles the proof of principalization in the form of [Kol07, Chapter 3]. Chapter 4 explains how the Lean library of the project uses the theorem of Hironaka, and what it checks.

The reader we have in mind knows the basic theory of schemes [Har] and of regular local rings [Mat], and Volume 1 of this handbook. We use blowups along smooth centers in smooth varieties and their charts, which Section 2.2 recalls. We prove the lemmas on derivatives, on birational transforms, on maximal contact, on restriction to a hypersurface, on tuning, and the order reduction for monomial ideals. We do not prove the functoriality of the full algorithm in detail, and we do not prove the descent from local to global centers in the general form. There we give the plan and cite [Kol07, Chapter 3]. We cite [Kol07] by its chapter and by the titles of its sections, and not by the numbers of its statements.

The theorem of Hironaka is not proved in this project. The Lean library uses a Lean proof of Hironaka's Main Theorem I in the library resolutionorg/hironaka [HirLean], which we call the base, as a black box. Volume 9, Section 1.2, proves that the morphism of the base is a resolution in our sense. No statement of Chapters 2 and 3 is in the Lean library. The result lines of these chapters say so.
