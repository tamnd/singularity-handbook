## Blowups and marked ideals

The algorithm blows up smooth centers inside the locus of high order, and it replaces the ideal by its birational transform. This section fixes the definitions and proves the main technical fact of the chapter: the birational transform of the derivative ideal is contained in the derivative ideal of the birational transform.

### Divisors and centers

**Definition 2.2.1 (divisors with ordered index set).** A *divisor with simple normal crossings and ordered index set* on $X$ is a finite sequence $E = (E^1, \dots, E^s)$ of smooth divisors, possibly reducible or empty, such that $E^1 + \dots + E^s$ is reduced and has simple normal crossings (Definition 1.1.2). A smooth closed subvariety $Z \subseteq X$ *has simple normal crossings with $E$* if near each closed point $x \in Z$ there are local coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_n$ such that each $E^i$ through $x$ is a coordinate hyperplane $(x_{l} = 0)$ and $Z$ is a coordinate subspace $(x_{l_1} = \dots = x_{l_c} = 0)$.

The order of the index set is used only in Section 3.3. Kollár allows each $E^i$ to be reducible, so that a disjoint union of local data is again of the same type [Kol07, Chapter 3].

Let $Z \subseteq X$ be a smooth closed subvariety and $\pi \colon X' = B_Z X \to X$ the blowup of $Z$, with exceptional divisor $F = \pi^{-1}(Z)$. If $Z$ has codimension 1, then $\pi$ is the identity and $F = Z$; we still record $Z$ as the center. The *transform* of $E$ is $E' = (\pi^{-1}_* E^1, \dots, \pi^{-1}_* E^s, F)$, where $\pi^{-1}_* E^i$ is the birational transform of $E^i$, and the new divisor $F$ is put last. If $Z$ has simple normal crossings with $E$, then $E'$ again has simple normal crossings; this follows from the charts of the next lemma.

**Lemma 2.2.2 (charts).** Let $x \in Z$ be a closed point and let $x_1, \dots, x_n$ be local coordinates on $U \ni x$ with $Z \cap U = (x_1 = \dots = x_c = 0)$, where $c \ge 1$. Then $\pi^{-1}(U)$ is covered by $c$ open sets $U_1, \dots, U_c$. On $U_j$ the functions
$$y_i = x_i / x_j \ (i \le c, \ i \ne j), \qquad y_j = x_j, \qquad y_i = x_i \ (i > c)$$
are regular, they are local coordinates at each point of $U_j$, and $F \cap U_j = (y_j = 0)$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* The functions $x_i$ define an étale morphism $U \to \mathbb{A}^n$, and $Z \cap U$ is the inverse image of the linear subspace $L = (x_1 = \dots = x_c = 0)$. Blowing up commutes with flat base change, so $B_{Z \cap U} U = U \times_{\mathbb{A}^n} B_L \mathbb{A}^n$. For $B_L \mathbb{A}^n$ the statement is the usual description of the charts [Har, Section II.7], with the $y_i$ as coordinates of $\mathbb{A}^n$. The base change of an étale morphism is étale, so the pullbacks of the $y_i$ are local coordinates. $\square$

In the chart $U_j$ we have $x_i = y_i y_j$ for $i \le c$, $i \ne j$, and $x_j = y_j$. If $E^l = (x_l = 0)$ with $l > c$, then $\pi^{-1}_* E^l = (y_l = 0)$. If $l \le c$ and $l \ne j$, then $\pi^{-1}_* E^l = (y_l = 0)$, and $\pi^{-1}_* E^j$ does not meet $U_j$. This is the chart check for the transform of $E$.

### Marked ideals

**Definition 2.2.3 (marked ideals and their transforms).** A *marked ideal* on $X$ is a pair $(I, m)$ of an ideal sheaf $I$ and an integer $m \ge 0$. Let $(X, I, E)$ be a triple with $E$ as in Definition 2.2.1.

1. A blowup $\pi \colon X' \to X$ with center $Z$ has *order at least $m$* for $(I, m)$ if $Z$ is smooth, $Z \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$, and $Z$ has simple normal crossings with $E$. If also $m = \operatorname{max\text{-}ord} I$, we say that it has *order $m$*.
2. For such a blowup, $I \subseteq I_Z^m$ by Proposition 2.1.7, so $\pi^* I \subseteq \pi^* I_Z^m = \mathcal{O}_{X'}(-mF)$. The *birational transform* of $(I, m)$ is
$$\pi^{-1}_*(I, m) = \big( \mathcal{O}_{X'}(mF) \cdot \pi^* I, \; m \big).$$
3. A *blowup sequence of order at least $m$* is a sequence $(X_r, I_r, E_r) \to \dots \to (X_0, I_0, E_0) = (X, I, E)$ in which $X_{i+1} \to X_i$ is a blowup of order at least $m$ for $(I_i, m)$, with center $Z_i$, and $(I_{i+1}, m) = \pi_i^{-1}{}_*(I_i, m)$ and $E_{i+1} = E_i'$.

*Order reduction* for $(X, I, m, E)$ is a blowup sequence of order at least $m$ with $\operatorname{cosupp}(I_r, m) = \emptyset$.

Lean: none yet

Kollár writes the transform as $\pi^{-1}_*(I, m)$, and we keep his notation [Kol07, Chapter 3]. If $f$ is a local generator of $I$ and $F = (y_j = 0)$ in a chart of Lemma 2.2.2, then the transform is generated by the functions $y_j^{-m} \pi^* f$. The transform is $I$ itself outside $F$. In the language of Volume 7, the transform with $m = \operatorname{ord}_x f$ at a point blowup is the weak transform.

**Lemma 2.2.4 (formal properties).** Let $\pi$ be a blowup with smooth center $Z$.

1. If $(A, a)$ and $(B, b)$ are marked ideals with $Z$ in both cosupports, then the transform of $(AB, a + b)$ is the product of the transforms, and the transform of $(A^e, ae)$ is the $e$th power of the transform of $(A, a)$.
2. If $A$ and $B$ have order at least $a$ along $Z$, then the transform of $(A + B, a)$ is the sum of the transforms, and $A \subseteq B$ implies that the transform of $(A, a)$ is contained in that of $(B, a)$.
3. If $Z \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(A, a)$ and $a' \le a$, then the transform of $(A, a')$ is $\mathcal{O}(-(a - a')F)$ times the transform of $(A, a)$, so it is contained in it.
4. If $Z \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$, then $\operatorname{cosupp}(I', m) \subseteq \pi^{-1}(\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m))$, where $I'$ is the transform.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Parts 1 to 3 follow from $\pi^*(AB) = \pi^*A \cdot \pi^*B$, $\pi^*(A + B) = \pi^*A + \pi^*B$ and $\mathcal{O}(aF) \mathcal{O}(bF) = \mathcal{O}((a + b)F)$, since $\mathcal{O}(F)$ is invertible. For part 4, $\pi$ is an isomorphism over $X \setminus Z$, and there $I' = \pi^* I$, so a point of $X' \setminus F$ in $\operatorname{cosupp}(I', m)$ lies over $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$, and the points of $F$ lie over $Z \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$. $\square$

**Example 2.2.5.** Let $I = (y^2 - x^3)$ on $\mathbb{A}^2$, the cusp of Example 1.3.2, and blow up the origin. In the chart $y = y_1 x$ the pullback is $x^2 (y_1^2 - x)$. The transform of $(I, 2)$ is $(y_1^2 - x)$, of order 1 at the point $p_1$. The transform of $(I, 1)$ is $x (y_1^2 - x)$, of order 2 at $p_1$. With the mark 1, the exceptional divisor stays in the transform, as it does in the computation of Example 1.3.2.

*Example (a mark that is too large).* The condition $Z \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$ in Definition 2.2.3 is what makes the transform an ideal. Let $I = (y^2 - x^3)$ on $\mathbb{A}^2$ and let $Z$ be the origin, where $I$ has order 2. For the mark 3 the origin is not in $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, 3)$, and the formula of part 2 would give, in the chart $y = y_1 x$,
$$x^{-3} \cdot x^2 (y_1^2 - x) = x^{-1} (y_1^2 - x),$$
which is not a regular function near the point $x = y_1 = 0$. In general $\mathcal{O}(mF) \cdot \pi^* I$ is an ideal sheaf if and only if $\pi^* I \subseteq \mathcal{O}(-mF)$, that is, if $I$ has order at least $m$ at the generic point of each component of $Z$; by Proposition 2.1.7 this is the condition $Z \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$.

*Example (a center without simple normal crossings).* The condition that $Z$ has simple normal crossings with $E$ keeps the divisor smooth after the blowup. Let $E = (z = 0)$ in $\mathbb{A}^3$ and let $Z = (y = 0, \; z = x^2)$, a smooth curve that meets $E$ only at the origin, where it is tangent to $E$: the intersection $Z \cap E$ is defined by $y = z = x^2 = 0$, a double point. So $Z$ does not have simple normal crossings with $E$. Put $w = z - x^2$, so that $x, y, w$ are coordinates and $Z = (y = w = 0)$. In the chart of the blowup with $w = w_1 y$ and the coordinates $x, y, w_1$, the pullback of $z$ is
$$z = w + x^2 = x^2 + y w_1.$$
It is not divisible by $y$, the equation of the exceptional divisor, so the birational transform of $E$ is the surface $(x^2 + y w_1 = 0)$. Its partial derivatives $2x$, $w_1$ and $y$ vanish at the origin of the chart. So the transform of $E$ is a quadric cone, singular at the origin, and $E'$ does not have simple normal crossings. The induction of Chapter 3 restricts the problem to the divisors of $E$, and a singular divisor would stop it.

*Remark (the order of the divisors).* The new divisor $F$ is put last in $E'$. The rule of Theorem 3.3.8 uses this order: among the intersections of divisors with the largest value of the invariant it blows up the one with the lexicographically smallest set of indices. So the old divisors are treated before the new ones. Example 3.3.9 shows that a rule that always blows up the newest intersection does not end. The convention that $F$ is put last is the convention of [Kol07, Chapter 3].

*Remark (characteristic).* Definition 2.2.3 and Lemma 2.2.4 make sense in every characteristic, with the theorem of Zariski-Nagata in place of Proposition 2.1.7. What fails in characteristic $p$ is the control of the order of the transforms through the derivative ideals, Theorem 2.2.7 together with Lemma 2.1.4; see Section 2.4.

### Derivatives and blowups

**Lemma 2.2.6 (derivatives in a chart).** In the chart $U_c$ of Lemma 2.2.2, let $f \in I_Z^m$ on $U$ with $m \ge 1$, and let $g = y_c^{-m} \pi^* f$. For each $i$ the function $\partial_i f$ lies in $I_Z^{m-1}$, and
$$y_c^{-(m-1)} \pi^*(\partial_i f) = \begin{cases} \partial_{y_i} g & \text{if } i < c, \\ m g + y_c \partial_{y_c} g - \sum_{l < c} y_l \partial_{y_l} g & \text{if } i = c, \\ y_c \partial_{y_i} g & \text{if } i > c. \end{cases}$$
Also $y_c^{-(m-1)} \pi^* f = y_c g$. Here $\partial_{y_i}$ are the derivations dual to the coordinates $y_i$.

Lean: `Singularity.pderiv_mem_centerIdeal_pow`, `Singularity.chartSubst_pderiv_of_mem_erase`, `Singularity.chartSubst_pderiv_center`, `Singularity.chartSubst_pderiv_of_not_mem` (the chart of affine space, with the chain rule `Singularity.pderiv_aeval_eq_sum`). The local coordinates on a smooth variety are not in Lean yet.

*Proof.* The function $\partial_i f$ lies in $I_Z^{m-1}$ because a derivation maps $I_Z^m$ into $I_Z^{m-1}$. Write $h = \pi^* f = y_c^m g$. On $U_c$ we have $x_i = y_i y_c$ for $i < c$, $x_c = y_c$ and $x_i = y_i$ for $i > c$. By the chain rule,
$$\partial_{y_i} h = y_c \, \pi^*(\partial_i f) \ (i < c), \qquad \partial_{y_i} h = \pi^*(\partial_i f) \ (i > c), \qquad \partial_{y_c} h = \pi^*(\partial_c f) + \sum_{l < c} y_l \, \pi^*(\partial_l f).$$
For $i < c$ we get $y_c^{-(m-1)} \pi^*(\partial_i f) = y_c^{-m} \partial_{y_i}(y_c^m g) = \partial_{y_i} g$. For $i > c$ we get $y_c^{-(m-1)} \partial_{y_i}(y_c^m g) = y_c \partial_{y_i} g$. For $i = c$,
$$y_c^{-(m-1)} \pi^*(\partial_c f) = y_c^{-(m-1)} \Big( \partial_{y_c}(y_c^m g) - \sum_{l < c} y_l \, y_c^{m-1} \partial_{y_l} g \Big) = m g + y_c \partial_{y_c} g - \sum_{l < c} y_l \partial_{y_l} g. \qquad \square$$

The right-hand sides lie in the ideal generated by $g$ and its derivatives. The charts $U_j$ with $j < c$ are the same after we exchange $x_j$ and $x_c$. So Lemma 2.2.6 gives: if $Z \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$ with $m \ge 1$, then $Z \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(D(I), m - 1)$ by Lemma 2.1.4, and
$$\pi^{-1}_*(D(I), m - 1) \subseteq D\big(\pi^{-1}_*(I, m)\big). \tag{2.2.1}$$
Indeed, $D(I)$ is generated near $Z$ by the generators $f$ of $I$ and their derivatives $\partial_i f$, and Lemma 2.2.4 reduces the claim to these generators. Outside $F$ both sides are $D(I)$.

**Theorem 2.2.7 (derivatives and transforms).** Let $(X_r, I_r, E_r) \to \dots \to (X_0, I_0, E_0)$ be a blowup sequence of order at least $m$ for $(I, m)$. For $0 \le j \le m$ let $J^{(j)}_0 = D^j(I)$, with the mark $m - j$, and let $J^{(j)}_{i+1}$ be the transform of $(J^{(j)}_i, m - j)$ under $\pi_i$. Then for all $i$ and $j$ the transform is defined, that is $Z_i \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(J^{(j)}_i, m - j)$, and
$$J^{(j)}_i \subseteq D^j(I_i).$$

Lean: `Singularity.chartTransformIdeal_derivIdeal_iterate_le` (for the blowup of a point of affine space, in each chart; centers of positive dimension and sequences of blowups are not in Lean)

*Proof.* Induction on $i$; for $i = 0$ the inclusion is an equality. Assume $J^{(j)}_i \subseteq D^j(I_i)$ for all $j$. The center $Z_i$ lies in $\operatorname{cosupp}(I_i, m)$, so by Lemma 2.1.4 it lies in $\operatorname{cosupp}(D^j(I_i), m - j)$, and since $J^{(j)}_i \subseteq D^j(I_i)$, also in $\operatorname{cosupp}(J^{(j)}_i, m - j)$. By Lemma 2.2.4, the transform of $(J^{(j)}_i, m - j)$ is contained in the transform of $(D^j(I_i), m - j)$. Apply (2.2.1) $j$ times, to the ideals $D^{j-1}(I_i), D^{j-2}(I_i), \dots, I_i$ with the marks $m - j + 1, \dots, m$; each step is allowed because $Z_i \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(D^{l}(I_i), m - l)$. Since $D$ preserves inclusions, we get
$$\pi_i^{-1}{}_*(D^j(I_i), m - j) \subseteq D\big(\pi_i^{-1}{}_*(D^{j-1}(I_i), m - j + 1)\big) \subseteq \dots \subseteq D^j\big(\pi_i^{-1}{}_*(I_i, m)\big) = D^j(I_{i+1}). \qquad \square$$

**Corollary 2.2.8 (the maximal order does not go up).** If $\operatorname{max\text{-}ord} I \le m$, then $\operatorname{max\text{-}ord} I_i \le m$ for each blowup sequence of order at least $m$ for $(I, m)$.

Lean: `Singularity.idealOrd_chartTransformIdeal_le` (only one blowup of the origin of affine space, at the origin of a chart)

*Proof.* By Corollary 2.1.5, $D^m(I) = \mathcal{O}_X$. The transform of $(\mathcal{O}, 0)$ is $\mathcal{O}$, so $J^{(m)}_i = \mathcal{O}_{X_i}$, and Theorem 2.2.7 gives $D^m(I_i) = \mathcal{O}_{X_i}$. By Corollary 2.1.5 again, $\operatorname{max\text{-}ord} I_i \le m$. $\square$

Volume 7, Theorem 2.2.2, proves the case of a single polynomial and a point blowup in every characteristic, with the Lean theorem `Singularity.ord_le_of_pointChart` (node SG0000003A). Corollary 2.2.8 is more general in the centers, but the proof uses characteristic 0 through Lemma 2.1.4. In particular a blowup sequence of order $m$ for an ideal of maximal order $m$ stays of order $m$ as long as the cosupport of order $m$ is not empty, and order reduction for $(I, m)$ means that the maximal order drops below $m$.

The next example follows the running example of Example 2.1.8 through two blowups. It shows that the inclusion of Theorem 2.2.7 can be strict.

**Example 2.2.9.** Let $I = (y^2 - x^5)$ on $X_0 = \mathbb{A}^2$ with $m = 2$. By Example 2.1.8, $D(I) = (y, x^4)$, and $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, 2)$ is the origin. Let $X_1 \to X_0$ be the blowup of the origin. In the chart $U_1$ with coordinates $x, y_1$ and $y = y_1 x$, the pullback of $y^2 - x^5$ is $x^2(y_1^2 - x^3)$, so $I_1 = (y_1^2 - x^3)$ on $U_1$. In the chart $U_2$ with coordinates $x_2, y$ and $x = x_2 y$, the pullback is $y^2(1 - x_2^5 y^3)$, so $I_1 = (1 - x_2^5 y^3)$ on $U_2$, and this function is a unit at each point of the exceptional curve $(y = 0)$. Outside the exceptional curve $I_1$ has order at most 1. So $\operatorname{cosupp}(I_1, 2)$ is the point $p_1 = (x = y_1 = 0)$.

The transform of the marked ideal $(D(I), 1)$ on $U_1$ is
$$J^{(1)}_1 = x^{-1} \cdot (x y_1, \; x^4) = (y_1, \; x^3).$$
The derivative ideal of $I_1$ on $U_1$ is $D(I_1) = (y_1^2 - x^3, \; 2y_1, \; 3x^2) = (y_1, \; x^2)$. So $J^{(1)}_1 \subseteq D(I_1)$, as Theorem 2.2.7 says, and the inclusion is strict because $x^2 \notin (y_1, x^3)$. Both ideals have the same zero set $p_1$.

Now blow up $p_1$. In the chart with $y_1 = y_2 x$ the pullback of $y_1^2 - x^3$ is $x^2(y_2^2 - x)$, so $I_2 = (y_2^2 - x)$, of order at most 1. In the chart with $x = x_3 y_1$ the pullback is $y_1^2(1 - x_3^3 y_1)$, and $I_2 = (1 - x_3^3 y_1)$ is a unit along the new exceptional curve. So $\operatorname{max\text{-}ord} I_2 \le 1$, and the two blowups are an order reduction for $(I, 2)$. The transform $J^{(1)}_2 = x^{-1} \cdot (x y_2, x^3) = (y_2, x^2)$ lies in $D(I_2) = \mathcal{O}$.

*Example (Theorem 2.2.7 for $(x^2, y^3)$).* Let $I = (x^2, y^3)$ on $\mathbb{A}^2$ with $m = 2$. By the example at the end of Section 2.1, $D(I) = (x, y^2)$ and $D^2(I) = \mathcal{O}$. Blow up the origin. In the chart $x = x_1 y$ the transform of $(I, 2)$ is $I_1 = y^{-2} (x_1^2 y^2, y^3) = (x_1^2, y)$, and the transform of $(D(I), 1)$ is $J^{(1)}_1 = y^{-1} (x_1 y, y^2) = (x_1, y)$. The derivative ideal of $I_1$ contains $\partial_y (y) = 1$, so $D(I_1) = \mathcal{O}$, and $J^{(1)}_1 \subseteq D(I_1)$ holds. The inclusion is strict. The ideal $I_1$ has order 1 at $P = (x_1 = y = 0)$, and so the maximal order dropped from 2 to 1 after one blowup. In the chart $y = y_1 x$ the transform is $x^{-2}(x^2, x^3 y_1^3) = \mathcal{O}$. So the single blowup of the origin is an order reduction for $(I, 2)$. The marked ideal $(I, 1)$ is a different problem, with a different answer: Section 1.3 needs three blowups to principalize $I$.

### Notes and references

Definition 2.2.3 and Theorem 2.2.7 are from [Kol07, Chapter 3], Sections "Birational transforms and marked ideals" and "Birational transform of derivatives". Marked ideals are called basic objects by Encinas-Villamayor [EV98] and are related to the presentations of Bierstone-Milman [BM97]. The formulas of Lemma 2.2.6 are the computation behind all of them.
