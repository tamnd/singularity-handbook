## Order and derivative ideals

In Chapters 2 and 3, $k$ is a field of characteristic 0 and $X$ is a smooth scheme of finite type over $k$, of pure dimension $n$. An ideal sheaf $I \subseteq \mathcal{O}_X$ is always coherent and nonzero on each irreducible component of $X$. The scheme $X$ is Jacobson, so a closed subset of $X$ is determined by its closed points, and we describe closed subsets by their closed points.

### Coordinates

Let $x \in X$ be a closed point. The residue field $k(x)$ is a finite extension of $k$, and it is separable because $k$ has characteristic 0. The map $\mathfrak{m}_x / \mathfrak{m}_x^2 \to \Omega_{X/k} \otimes k(x)$, $f \mapsto df$, has cokernel $\Omega_{k(x)/k}$ [Har, Section II.8], and this module is 0 because $k(x)$ is separable over $k$. Both sides have dimension $n$, since $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is regular of dimension $n$ and $\Omega_{X/k}$ is locally free of rank $n$. So the map is an isomorphism. So there are an open set $U \ni x$ and functions $x_1, \dots, x_n \in \mathcal{O}(U)$ that vanish at $x$, such that $dx_1, \dots, dx_n$ is a basis of $\Omega_{U/k}$ and $x_1, \dots, x_n$ generate $\mathfrak{m}_x$. We call them *local coordinates* at $x$. Let $\partial_1, \dots, \partial_n$ be the dual basis of the sheaf of derivations $\operatorname{Der}_k(\mathcal{O}_U)$. Then $\partial_i(x_j) = \delta_{ij}$.

**Lemma 2.1.1 (completion).** Let $x_1, \dots, x_n$ be local coordinates at a closed point $x$, and let $K = k(x)$. Then there is a unique subfield of $\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}$ that contains $k$ and maps isomorphically to $K$, and with it $\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x} = K[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$. Each $\partial_i$ extends to a continuous derivation of $\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}$, and this extension is the partial derivative $\partial / \partial x_i$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Let $K = k(a)$ with $a$ a root of a separable irreducible polynomial $P \in k[t]$. The ring $\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}$ is complete, and $P$ has the simple root $a$ in its residue field, so by Hensel's lemma $P$ has a unique root $\tilde a$ in $\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}$ that lifts $a$. The subfield $k(\tilde a)$ maps isomorphically to $K$, and each subfield with this property contains a root of $P$ that lifts $a$, so it is $k(\tilde a)$. The elements $x_1, \dots, x_n$ form a regular system of parameters, so $\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x} = K[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$.

A derivation $\partial$ of $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ maps $\mathfrak{m}_x^r$ into $\mathfrak{m}_x^{r-1}$, so it is continuous and extends to the completion. It kills $k$. It also kills $K$: for $b \in K$ with minimal polynomial $Q$ over $k$ we have $0 = \partial(Q(b)) = Q'(b) \, \partial(b)$, and $Q'(b)$ is a nonzero element of the field $K$, hence a unit. So the extension of $\partial_i$ is a continuous $K$-linear derivation with $\partial_i(x_j) = \delta_{ij}$. Such a derivation agrees with $\partial / \partial x_i$ on polynomials, and by continuity on all power series. $\square$

### Order

**Definition 2.1.2 (order).** Let $x \in X$ be a closed point. The *order* of a function $f \in \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is $\operatorname{ord}_x f = \max \{ r : f \in \mathfrak{m}_x^r \}$, with $\operatorname{ord}_x 0 = \infty$. The order of an ideal sheaf $I$ at $x$ is $\operatorname{ord}_x I = \min \{ \operatorname{ord}_x f : f \in I_x \}$. For an integer $m$ the *cosupport of order $m$* is the set
$$\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m) = \{ x \in X \text{ closed} : \operatorname{ord}_x I \ge m \}.$$
The *maximal order* $\operatorname{max\text{-}ord} I$ is the maximum of $\operatorname{ord}_x I$ over the closed points $x$.

Since $\mathfrak{m}_x^r \hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x} \cap \mathcal{O}_{X,x} = \mathfrak{m}_x^r$, the order of $f$ is the degree of the lowest nonzero homogeneous part of $f$ in $K[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$. The set $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, 1)$ is the zero set of $I$, which we also write $\operatorname{cosupp} I$. We show below that $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$ is closed and that the maximal order is finite.

### Derivative ideals

**Definition 2.1.3 (derivative ideal).** The *derivative ideal* $D(I)$ of an ideal sheaf $I$ is the ideal sheaf generated by $I$ and by the functions $\partial f$, where $f$ is a local section of $I$ and $\partial$ a local section of $\operatorname{Der}_k(\mathcal{O}_X)$. Set $D^0(I) = I$ and $D^{j+1}(I) = D(D^j(I))$.

If $f_1, \dots, f_t$ generate $I$ on a coordinate open set $U$, then $D(I)$ is generated on $U$ by the $f_l$ and the $\partial_i f_l$, because $\partial(g f) = \partial(g) f + g \, \partial(f)$ and each derivation is a combination of $\partial_1, \dots, \partial_n$. Kollár calls $D(I)$ the derivative of $I$ and writes $D(I)$ as we do [Kol07, Chapter 3]. In the completion, $D(I) \hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}$ is generated by $I$ and the partial derivatives of its elements, by Lemma 2.1.1.

**Lemma 2.1.4 (the derivative lowers the order by one).** Let $x \in X$ be a closed point. Then
$$\operatorname{ord}_x D(I) = \max(\operatorname{ord}_x I - 1, \, 0), \qquad \operatorname{ord}_x D^j(I) = \max(\operatorname{ord}_x I - j, \, 0).$$

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Let $r = \operatorname{ord}_x I$. If $r = 0$, then $D(I) \supseteq I$ also has order 0. Let $r \ge 1$. A derivation maps $\mathfrak{m}_x^r$ into $\mathfrak{m}_x^{r-1}$, and $I \subseteq \mathfrak{m}_x^r$, so $\operatorname{ord}_x D(I) \ge r - 1$. Choose $f \in I_x$ of order $r$, and let $f_r \ne 0$ be its homogeneous part of degree $r$ in $K[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$. By Euler's identity,
$$\sum_{i=1}^n x_i \frac{\partial f_r}{\partial x_i} = r f_r \ne 0,$$
because $r$ is not zero in $K$. So some $\partial f_r / \partial x_i$ is not zero. It is the homogeneous part of degree $r - 1$ of $\partial_i f$, so $\operatorname{ord}_x \partial_i f = r - 1$. The formula for $D^j$ follows by induction on $j$. $\square$

**Corollary 2.1.5.** Let $m \ge 1$.

1. $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$ is the zero set of $D^{m-1}(I)$. In particular it is closed.
2. $\operatorname{max\text{-}ord} I \le m$ if and only if $D^m(I) = \mathcal{O}_X$.
3. The maximal order of $I$ is finite.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* (1) By Lemma 2.1.4, $\operatorname{ord}_x D^{m-1}(I) \ge 1$ if and only if $\operatorname{ord}_x I \ge m$. (2) An ideal sheaf is $\mathcal{O}_X$ if and only if it has order 0 at each closed point, and $\operatorname{ord}_x D^m(I) = 0$ if and only if $\operatorname{ord}_x I \le m$. (3) The closed sets $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$ decrease with $m$. Each closed point has finite order, because $I$ is nonzero near it and $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ is a regular local ring, so the intersection of all the $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$ is empty. Since $X$ is Noetherian, the chain is stationary, so $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, m) = \emptyset$ for large $m$. $\square$

**Example 2.1.6 (characteristic $p$).** The proof of Lemma 2.1.4 divides by $r$. Let $k$ have characteristic $p > 0$ and let $I = (x^p)$ on $\mathbb{A}^1$. Then $\partial_x (x^p) = p x^{p-1} = 0$, so $D^j(I) = I$ for all $j$. The order of $D(I)$ at the origin is $p$, not $p - 1$, and the zero set of $D^p(I)$ is the origin, while $\operatorname{cosupp}(I, p+1)$ is empty. In characteristic $p$ one replaces the derivations by the Hasse derivatives; with them, the set of points of order at least $d$ is again closed (Volume 7, Proposition 1.1.4). But the Hasse derivatives are not derivations, and the rest of this chapter does not work with them.

### Order along a smooth subvariety

Let $Z \subseteq X$ be a smooth closed subvariety, that is, a closed subscheme that is smooth over $k$, with ideal sheaf $I_Z$. Near each closed point $x \in Z$ there are local coordinates $x_1, \dots, x_n$ at $x$ such that $I_Z = (x_1, \dots, x_c)$, where $c$ is the codimension of $Z$ at $x$ [Har, Section II.8]. The next result is a form of the theorem of Zariski-Nagata. In characteristic 0 it has a short proof.

**Proposition 2.1.7 (order along a smooth subvariety).** Let $Z \subseteq X$ be a smooth closed subvariety, let $U \subseteq X$ be open and let $f \in \mathcal{O}(U)$. Assume that $\operatorname{ord}_x f \ge r$ at each closed point $x$ of $Z \cap U$. Then $f \in I_Z^r(U)$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* It suffices to show that $f \in I_Z^r \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ at each closed point $x$ of $Z \cap U$; at the other closed points $I_Z$ is the unit ideal near the point. Choose local coordinates at $x$ with $I_Z = (x_1, \dots, x_c)$, and write $x' = (x_1, \dots, x_c)$ and $x'' = (x_{c+1}, \dots, x_n)$. In $\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x} = K[[x', x'']]$ write
$$f = \sum_{\beta \in \mathbb{N}^c} f_\beta(x'') \, x'^\beta, \qquad f_\beta \in K[[x'']].$$
Let $|\beta| < r$ and $g = \partial_1^{\beta_1} \cdots \partial_c^{\beta_c} f$. Then $g \in D^{|\beta|}(f)$, so by Lemma 2.1.4 the function $g$ has order at least $r - |\beta| \ge 1$ at each closed point of $Z$ near $x$. So $g$ vanishes at all closed points of the reduced scheme $Z$ near $x$, and hence $g \in I_Z$ near $x$. In the completion, $g \in (x')$. By Lemma 2.1.1, $\partial_i$ is $\partial / \partial x_i$, and for $i \le c$ it kills $K[[x'']]$. So
$$g = \sum_\gamma f_\gamma(x'') \, \partial^\beta (x'^\gamma) \equiv \beta! \, f_\beta(x'') \pmod{(x')},$$
since $\partial^\beta(x'^\gamma)$ is zero if $\gamma \not\ge \beta$, is divisible by $x'$ if $\gamma \ge \beta$ and $\gamma \ne \beta$, and is $\beta!$ if $\gamma = \beta$. Thus $\beta! f_\beta \in (x') \cap K[[x'']] = 0$. Since $k$ has characteristic 0, $\beta! \ne 0$ and $f_\beta = 0$. Hence $f \in (x')^r \hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x} = I_Z^r \hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}$. The completion is faithfully flat over $\mathcal{O}_{X,x}$, so $I_Z^r \hat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x} \cap \mathcal{O}_{X,x} = I_Z^r \mathcal{O}_{X,x}$ [Mat, Sections 7 and 8]. $\square$

For an ideal sheaf $I$ and a smooth closed subvariety $Z$, let $\operatorname{ord}_Z I$ be the minimum of $\operatorname{ord}_x I$ over the closed points $x \in Z$. Proposition 2.1.7 says that $I \subseteq I_Z^{\operatorname{ord}_Z I}$. In particular, $Z \subseteq \operatorname{cosupp}(I, m)$ if and only if $I \subseteq I_Z^m$. This is the form in which we use it: it lets us divide the pullback of $I$ to a blowup of $Z$ by the $m$th power of the equation of the exceptional divisor.

### Two computations

The next example computes the derivative ideals in two cases. It shows how Corollary 2.1.5 finds the locus of maximal order, and that this locus can be singular even when the ideal has a simple form.

**Example 2.1.8.** (1) Let $f = y^2 - x^5$ on $\mathbb{A}^2$. The partial derivatives are $\partial_x f = -5x^4$ and $\partial_y f = 2y$. So
$$D(f) = (y^2 - x^5, \; x^4, \; y) = (y, \; x^4), \qquad D^2(f) \ni \partial_y(y) = 1.$$
By Corollary 2.1.5, $\operatorname{max\text{-}ord} f = 2$, and $\operatorname{cosupp}(f, 2) = V(y, x^4)$ is the origin. The ideal $D(f)$ has order 1 at the origin, as Lemma 2.1.4 says, and it contains the element $y$ of order 1. We use this ideal as a running example in Sections 2.2, 2.3, 3.1 and 3.2.

(2) Let $f = z^2 - x^2 y^2$ on $\mathbb{A}^3$. The partial derivatives are $-2xy^2$, $-2x^2y$ and $2z$. Since $f = z \cdot z - x \cdot xy^2$, we get
$$D(f) = (z, \; xy^2, \; x^2 y), \qquad D^2(f) = \mathcal{O}.$$
So $\operatorname{max\text{-}ord} f = 2$, and $\operatorname{cosupp}(f, 2) = V(z, xy^2, x^2y) = V(z, xy)$ is the union of the $x$-axis and the $y$-axis. This locus is singular at the origin, so we cannot blow it up as a smooth center. Proposition 2.1.7, applied to each axis, says that $f \in (y, z)^2 \cap (x, z)^2$. We can see this directly: $f = z^2 - x^2 y^2$, and both terms lie in both ideals. The two axes lie in the smooth surface $(z = 0)$, and $z \in D(f)$. Section 2.3 shows that this is not an accident.

### Notes and references

Derivatives of the equations appear in resolution since Hironaka [Hir64]. The presentation with derivative ideals follows [Kol07, Chapter 3]. In every characteristic the statement of Proposition 2.1.7 follows from the theorem of Zariski-Nagata, but the proof above needs $\beta! \ne 0$. The fact that the completion of a Noetherian local ring is faithfully flat is in [Mat] and [AM69, Chapter 10].
