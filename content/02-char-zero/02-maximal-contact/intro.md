# Maximal contact

This chapter proves the theorem that makes the induction on the dimension possible in characteristic 0. Let $I$ be an ideal sheaf on a smooth variety $X$, and let $m$ be the maximal order of $I$. Then, locally on $X$, there is a smooth hypersurface $H$ that contains all the points where $I$ has order $m$, and the birational transforms of $H$ keep this property under each sequence of blowups whose centers lie in the locus of order $m$. Such an $H$ is a hypersurface of maximal contact.

Section 2.1 defines the order and the derivative ideals, and it proves the two facts about them that need characteristic 0: the derivative ideal lowers the order by exactly 1, and a function of order at least $r$ at each point of a smooth subvariety $Z$ lies in the $r$th power of the ideal of $Z$. Section 2.2 recalls blowups and defines marked ideals and their birational transforms; its main result says that the transform of a derivative is contained in the derivative of the transform. Section 2.3 proves the theorem of maximal contact. Section 2.4 explains where characteristic 0 is used, and it shows with the example of Narasimhan that maximal contact fails in characteristic 2.

The proofs follow [Kol07, Chapter 3], Sections "Birational transform of derivatives" and "Maximal contact and going down". We give them in local coordinates, with all the computations.
