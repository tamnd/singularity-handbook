# Introduction

This volume proves local uniformization in the known cases that fit in a textbook, and it explains the other known cases with precise references. Volume 3, Chapter 4, defines local uniformization (Definition 4.1.1), reduces it to affine models (Proposition 4.1.6), proves the case of transcendence degree at most 1 (Proposition 4.1.7) and the case of divisorial valuations (Proposition 4.1.8), and states the theorems of Zariski, Abhyankar, Knaf-Kuhlmann, Cossart-Piltant and Temkin. We do not repeat that chapter. We use its definitions and its numbering, and we refer to it as Volume 3.

Chapter 1 is about surfaces and the method of Zariski. Section 1.1 studies the quadratic transform of a regular local ring of dimension 2 along a valuation. Section 1.2 proves the theorem of Abhyankar that the sequence of quadratic transforms along a valuation exhausts the valuation ring. As a consequence, each valuation of a field $k(x, y)$ of rational functions has local uniformization, over each field $k$, and on a surface one regular center is enough. Section 1.3 proves a lifting lemma: local uniformization passes from a subfield $K_0$ to a finite extension $K$ when the extension has an étale generator at the valuation. In characteristic 0 this gives local uniformization for each valuation of a surface whose value group is finitely generated. Section 1.4 states the theorem of Zariski in all dimensions. It reduces local uniformization to the valuations whose residue field is algebraic over the ground field, and it proves that a resolution of singularities gives local uniformization. So the theorem of Hironaka implies the theorem of Zariski. We also give the plan of the direct proofs with references.

Chapter 2 is about Abhyankar valuations. Section 2.1 proves local uniformization for monomial valuations in each characteristic. The core is a lemma on ordered lattices: finitely many nonnegative elements of $\mathbb{Z}^r$ lie in the cone of a basis of positive elements. Section 2.2 proves the theorem of Knaf-Kuhlmann for Abhyankar valuations with a separable residue field. The proof combines the monomial case, the lifting lemma of Section 1.3, and the generalized stability theorem of Kuhlmann [Kuh10], which we cite.

Chapter 3 is about surfaces in characteristic $p$. Section 3.1 states the theorem of Abhyankar. It deduces local uniformization of surfaces over each field from the theorem of Lipman of Volume 4, and it proves a converse of the lifting lemma: an étale generator exists if and only if the extension has ramification index 1, a separable residue extension and no defect. Section 3.2 shows that the defect depends on the choice of the subfield $K_0$, and it explains the link with the resolution of surfaces of Volume 4.

Chapter 4 is about the theorem of Temkin. Section 4.1 states inseparable local uniformization in the form of [Tem13], proves that it implies the Lean statement and the theorem of Zariski, and describes the structure of the proof. Section 4.2 explains why the theorem does not give local uniformization in dimension 4 in characteristic $p$. An example on the surface of Volume 4 shows that regularity does not descend along a purely inseparable normalization. We connect the remaining problem with Volume 9, Chapter 2.

The reader we have in mind knows the regular local rings of Volume 1 and the valuation theory of Volume 3: value groups, residue fields, the Abhyankar inequality, extensions of valuations and the defect. We use the basic theory of Noetherian local rings [Mat].

We prove these results in full: the quadratic transforms of Section 1.1, the theorem of Abhyankar on quadratic sequences (Theorem 1.2.6), local uniformization for $k(x, y)$ in each characteristic (Corollary 1.2.8), the lifting theorem (Theorem 1.3.4) and its converse (Proposition 3.1.4), local uniformization of surfaces in characteristic 0 when the value group is finitely generated (Corollary 1.3.6), the reduction to a larger ground field (Lemma 1.4.2), the fact that a resolution gives local uniformization (Proposition 1.4.5), the lattice lemma (Lemma 2.1.4) and the monomial case (Theorem 2.1.5). From the theorems of Hironaka and Lipman, which Volumes 9 and 4 treat, we deduce the theorem of Zariski (Corollary 1.4.6) and local uniformization of surfaces over each field (Proposition 3.1.2). From the theorem of Temkin we deduce the form of the Lean statement (Proposition 4.1.2). The theorem of Knaf-Kuhlmann (Theorem 2.2.1) is proved here up to two cited inputs: the generalized stability theorem [Kuh10] and standard facts on the henselization [Kuh-book]. We do not give a direct proof of the theorem of Zariski in dimension at least 3 or of the theorem of Abhyankar for surfaces in characteristic $p$, and we do not prove the theorem of Temkin. For these we give the statements, the structure of the proofs, and the references.

The Lean library states the main theorems of this volume in `Singularity/Statements/LocalUniformization.lean`: local uniformization of Abhyankar valuations (`Singularity.localUniformization_abhyankar`), inseparable local uniformization (`Singularity.localUniformization_inseparable`), and the open case of dimension 4 (`Singularity.LocalUniformizationFour`). These are statements, not proofs. The library proves the case of transcendence degree at most 1, and the facts on the charts of a blowup that Section 1.1 uses. No theorem of Chapters 1 to 4 that goes beyond Volume 3 is proved in Lean yet.
