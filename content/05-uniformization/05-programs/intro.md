# Programs in dimension at least four

Local uniformization in characteristic $p > 0$ is known for function fields of transcendence degree at most 3, by the theorem of Cossart and Piltant (Volume 3, Theorem 4.1.5). In transcendence degree 4 it is open. It is Conjecture 2.3.1 of Volume 9. Resolution of varieties of dimension 4 in characteristic $p$ is also open, and it is Conjecture 2.1.1 of Volume 9. This chapter does not prove a new case. It describes the programs that aim at these problems, and it says for each program what is proved, by whom and in which generality, where the method stops, and what the literature says about its status.

We mark three kinds of claims. A *theorem* is a result with a published proof or a proof in a preprint that the literature accepts and cites as a proof. An *announcement* is a claim of a proof that has not been checked or accepted by the community. An *open problem* is a statement that nobody claims to have proved. Numbered statements in this chapter are theorems of the literature. We do not prove them, and none of them has a Lean proof. Programs and heuristics are in unnumbered remarks.

Section 5.1 treats the programs of valuation theory: the method of Zariski, the Abhyankar valuations, the defect and the work of Kuhlmann, the results and counterexamples of Cutkosky, key polynomials, and the theorem of Temkin. Section 5.2 treats the characteristic polyhedron of Hironaka and the proofs in dimension 3 of Cossart, Jannsen, Piltant and Saito, and it explains why they do not extend directly. Section 5.3 treats the programs that look for an invariant that drops under blowups: the Rees algebras of Villamayor and his school, the idealistic filtrations of Kawanoue and Matsuki, the announced program of Hironaka, and the weighted and logarithmic methods of Abramovich, Temkin and Włodarczyk. It also collects the counterexamples to the direct extensions of the characteristic 0 proof. Section 5.4 gives a short account of the weak forms that alterations give, with references to Volume 8, and a table of the status of each program.

The chapter uses Chapters 1 to 4 of this volume, Volume 3 for valuation theory, Volume 6 for the threefold proof, Volume 7 for the residual order, Volume 8 for alterations, and Volume 9, Chapter 2, for the open problems. Where these volumes treat a result in detail, we give the reference and do not repeat it.
