# Surfaces and the method of Zariski

This chapter proves local uniformization for the valuations of surfaces that the method of Zariski reaches with a short argument, and it states the theorem of Zariski in all dimensions. The tools are the quadratic transform of a regular local ring of dimension 2 and the intersection number of two curves.

Section 1.1 defines the quadratic transform of a regular local ring $R$ of dimension 2 along a valuation, and the strict transform of an element of $R$. Section 1.2 proves the theorem of Abhyankar: the union of the sequence of quadratic transforms along a valuation $v$ is the valuation ring $\mathcal{O}_v$. The proof uses the inequality of Noether between the intersection numbers before and after a quadratic transform. As a consequence, each valuation of a field of rational functions $k(x, y)$ has local uniformization, over each field $k$. Section 1.3 proves a lifting lemma. If $K$ is a finite extension of a subfield $K_0$, and the extension has an étale generator at the valuation, then local uniformization passes from $K_0$ to $K$. In characteristic 0 this gives local uniformization for each valuation of a surface with a finitely generated value group. Section 1.4 states the theorem of Zariski and reduces it to valuations whose residue field is algebraic over the ground field. It proves that a resolution gives local uniformization, so the theorem of Hironaka implies the theorem of Zariski, and it gives the plan of the direct proofs with references.

The examples of Sections 1.1 and 1.2 show each case of a quadratic transform, and they follow the quadratic sequence of a valuation of rank 1 and of a valuation of rank 2. The proofs of Sections 1.1 to 1.3 are complete. They use Volume 1 for regular local rings and the charts of a blowup, and Volume 3 for valuations and extensions of valuations. In Section 1.3 we use standard facts on the henselization, which we cite from [Kuh-book]. The proofs of Section 1.4 are complete too, but the theorem of Zariski follows there only from the theorem of Hironaka, which this volume does not prove. Section 1.4 gives no direct proof of the theorem of Zariski.
