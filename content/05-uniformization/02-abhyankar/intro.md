# Abhyankar valuations

An Abhyankar valuation is a valuation for which the Abhyankar inequality is an equality (Volume 3, Definition 2.2.3). Its value group is $\mathbb{Z}^r$ and its residue field is finitely generated (Volume 3, Corollary 2.2.4). These valuations are the ones closest to monomial valuations, and local uniformization for them holds in each characteristic.

Section 2.1 treats monomial valuations of a field of rational functions. The proof is combinatorial. We show that finitely many positive elements of an ordered group $\mathbb{Z}^r$ lie in the cone of a basis of positive elements. A change of variables by the corresponding monomials then puts the given finite set into a polynomial ring whose center is the origin. Section 2.2 proves the theorem of Knaf-Kuhlmann [KK05] when the residue field is separable over the ground field. The valuation is monomial on a suitable subfield $K_0$, and the extension $K/K_0$ has an étale generator. Then the lifting theorem of Section 1.3 gives local uniformization. The existence of the étale generator rests on the generalized stability theorem of Kuhlmann [Kuh10], which we cite. Section 2.3 returns to monomial valuations with real independent weights. It reaches a good monomial chart by a sequence of blowups of strata of codimension 2, with an explicit rule of Jacobi-Perron type, and it proves that the rule ends.

The Lean library defines Abhyankar valuations (`Singularity.IsAbhyankarValuation`) and states their local uniformization (`Singularity.localUniformization_abhyankar`). It proves the Abhyankar inequality (`Singularity.abhyankar_inequality`). It proves the lattice lemma (Section 2.1, Lemma 2.1.4), the monomial case for value groups of each rank (Theorem 2.1.5), Lemma 2.1.2 on the value group, the residue field and the Abhyankar property of a monomial valuation, and Corollary 2.1.6 on monomial valuations over the field $k(y_1, \dots, y_t)$. It proves the theorem of Section 2.3 (`Singularity.Perron.exists_isValidRun_runExp_nonneg`), its corollaries on finite sets and on divisibility of monomials, and the facts on steps and runs that the proof uses. The other results of this chapter are not in the library.
