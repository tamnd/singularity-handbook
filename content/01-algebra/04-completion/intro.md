# Completion and the Cohen structure theorem

A point of a variety is regular when its local ring is regular. To study the local ring near the point, we often replace it by its completion. The completion keeps the dimension, the embedding dimension and the regularity of the ring (Theorem 4.3.3). It is also much simpler: by the theorem of Cohen, a complete regular local ring that contains a field is a ring of formal power series over its residue field (Theorem 4.4.7). Thus, after completion, a regular point of a variety looks like the origin of affine space, and a hypersurface through a regular point is given by one power series. The local methods of resolution in characteristic $0$ work in this setting.

Section 4.1 proves the Artin-Rees lemma and the intersection theorem of Krull. Section 4.2 defines the completion of a ring and of a module with respect to an ideal, and proves that the completion of a Noetherian ring is Noetherian and flat. Section 4.3 treats the completion of a Noetherian local ring. It proves that the completion is a Noetherian local ring with the same dimension, and that a local ring is regular if and only if its completion is regular. Section 4.4 proves the existence of coefficient fields in characteristic $0$ and for a perfect residue field, and the structure theorem of Cohen. It states the results in mixed characteristic with references.

Throughout the chapter, a ring is commutative with $1$, and we use the notation of Chapter 1. For a local ring $(R, \mathfrak{m}, \kappa)$ we write $\hat{R}$ for its $\mathfrak{m}$-adic completion and $\hat{\mathfrak{m}}$ for the maximal ideal of $\hat{R}$. We use the dimension theorem (Theorem 1.1.2) without proof, as in Chapter 1.
