## The Artin-Rees lemma and the intersection theorem of Krull

Let $R$ be a ring, let $I$ be an ideal of $R$ and let $M$ be an $R$-module. The powers of $I$ give a chain of submodules $M \supseteq IM \supseteq I^2 M \supseteq \dots$. For a submodule $N \subseteq M$ there are two natural chains in $N$: the chain $I^n N$ and the chain $I^n M \cap N$. The Artin-Rees lemma says that, for a Noetherian ring and a finitely generated module, the two chains are close to each other. This is the key to the exactness of completion in Section 4.2. A first consequence is the intersection theorem of Krull, which Section 3.2 used without proof.

### Filtrations and the Rees algebra

**Definition 4.1.1.** An *$I$-filtration* of $M$ is a chain $M = M_0 \supseteq M_1 \supseteq M_2 \supseteq \dots$ of submodules such that $I M_n \subseteq M_{n+1}$ for each $n$. The filtration is *stable* if there is $n_0$ with $I M_n = M_{n+1}$ for each $n \ge n_0$. The *$I$-adic filtration* $M_n = I^n M$ is stable, with $n_0 = 0$.

**Definition 4.1.2.** The *Rees algebra* of $I$ is the graded ring $R^* = \bigoplus_{n \ge 0} I^n$, with the product $I^m \times I^n \to I^{m+n}$ of $R$. In an equivalent form, $R^*$ is the subring $\bigoplus_n I^n t^n$ of the polynomial ring $R[t]$. For an $I$-filtration $(M_n)$ of $M$, the group $M^* = \bigoplus_{n \ge 0} M_n$ is a graded $R^*$-module, because $I^m M_n \subseteq M_{m+n}$.

**Lemma 4.1.3.** If $R$ is Noetherian, then $R^*$ is Noetherian.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Since $R$ is Noetherian, $I$ is generated by finitely many elements $a_1, \dots, a_r$. Each element of $I^n$ is a sum of products $b \, a_{i_1} \cdots a_{i_n}$ with $b \in R$. Thus $I^n t^n$ is in the subring $R[a_1 t, \dots, a_r t]$ of $R[t]$, and $R^* = R[a_1 t, \dots, a_r t]$. So $R^*$ is a quotient of the polynomial ring $R[X_1, \dots, X_r]$. This ring is Noetherian by the basis theorem of Hilbert [AM69, Theorem 7.5], so its quotient $R^*$ is Noetherian. $\square$

**Lemma 4.1.4.** Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring, let $M$ be a finitely generated $R$-module and let $(M_n)$ be an $I$-filtration of $M$. Then $M^*$ is a finitely generated $R^*$-module if and only if the filtration is stable.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* The module $M$ is Noetherian [AM69, Proposition 6.5], so each $M_n$ is a finitely generated $R$-module. For each $n$, let
$$M_n^* = M_0 \oplus M_1 \oplus \dots \oplus M_n \oplus I M_n \oplus I^2 M_n \oplus \dots,$$
where the term of degree $n + k$ is $I^k M_n$. We claim that $M_n^*$ is the $R^*$-submodule of $M^*$ that $M_0 \oplus \dots \oplus M_n$ generates. Let $i \le n$. Then $I^{n-i} M_i \subseteq M_n$, so for $k \ge 0$ we get $I^{n-i+k} M_i \subseteq I^k M_n$. For $m \le n$ we have $I^{m-i} M_i \subseteq M_m$ when $i \le m$. Thus the submodule generated by $M_0 \oplus \dots \oplus M_n$ has the term $M_m$ in degree $m \le n$ and the term $I^k M_n$ in degree $n + k$. This proves the claim. In particular, $M_n^*$ is generated by finitely many elements.

The modules $M_n^*$ form an ascending chain, and their union is $M^*$. If $M^*$ is finitely generated, then it is a Noetherian $R^*$-module, by Lemma 4.1.3. So the chain stops: $M^* = M_{n_0}^*$ for some $n_0$. In degree $n_0 + k$ this says $M_{n_0 + k} = I^k M_{n_0}$, so the filtration is stable. Conversely, if $I M_n = M_{n+1}$ for $n \ge n_0$, then $M_{n_0 + k} = I^k M_{n_0}$ for each $k$, so $M^* = M_{n_0}^*$ is finitely generated. $\square$

### The Artin-Rees lemma

**Theorem 4.1.5 (Artin-Rees).** Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring, let $I$ be an ideal, let $M$ be a finitely generated $R$-module and let $N$ be a submodule of $M$. Then there is an integer $c \ge 0$ such that
$$I^n M \cap N = I^{n-c} (I^c M \cap N) \quad \text{for each } n \ge c.$$

Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.artinRees`

*Proof.* Let $N_n = I^n M \cap N$. Then $I N_n \subseteq I^{n+1} M \cap N = N_{n+1}$, so $(N_n)$ is an $I$-filtration of $N$. The graded module $N^* = \bigoplus_n N_n$ is an $R^*$-submodule of $M^* = \bigoplus_n I^n M$. By Lemma 4.1.4, $M^*$ is a finitely generated $R^*$-module, because the $I$-adic filtration is stable. By Lemma 4.1.3, $R^*$ is Noetherian, so the submodule $N^*$ is finitely generated. By Lemma 4.1.4 again, the filtration $(N_n)$ is stable: there is $c$ with $I N_n = N_{n+1}$ for $n \ge c$. By induction, $N_{c + k} = I^k N_c$ for each $k \ge 0$. $\square$

**Corollary 4.1.6.** In the situation of Theorem 4.1.5,
$$I^n N \subseteq I^n M \cap N \subseteq I^{n-c} N \quad \text{for each } n \ge c.$$
Thus the topology on $N$ that the $I$-adic topology of $M$ induces is the $I$-adic topology of $N$.

Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.artinRees_le`

*Proof.* The first inclusion is clear. By Theorem 4.1.5, $I^n M \cap N = I^{n-c}(I^c M \cap N) \subseteq I^{n-c} N$. $\square$

### The intersection theorem of Krull

**Theorem 4.1.7 (Krull).** Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring, let $I$ be an ideal, let $M$ be a finitely generated $R$-module and let $E = \bigcap_{n \ge 0} I^n M$. Then $IE = E$, and there is $a \in I$ with $(1 + a) E = 0$.

Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.krull_intersection`

*Proof.* Apply Theorem 4.1.5 to the submodule $E$, and take $n = c + 1$. Since $E \subseteq I^n M$ for each $n$, we get
$$E = I^{c+1} M \cap E = I (I^c M \cap E) = I E.$$
The module $E$ is a submodule of the Noetherian module $M$, so it is finitely generated. By the determinant trick [AM69, Corollary 2.5], a finitely generated module $E$ with $IE = E$ is killed by an element of the form $1 + a$ with $a \in I$. $\square$

**Corollary 4.1.8.** Let $R$ be a Noetherian ring, let $I$ be an ideal in the Jacobson radical of $R$ and let $M$ be a finitely generated $R$-module. Then $\bigcap_n I^n M = 0$. In particular, if $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ is a Noetherian local ring and $I \ne R$, then $\bigcap_n I^n M = 0$.

Lean: `Ideal.iInf_pow_eq_bot_of_isLocalRing` (in Mathlib, for a local ring and $M = R$)

*Proof.* Take $a$ as in Theorem 4.1.7. Since $a$ is in the Jacobson radical, $1 + a$ is a unit [AM69, Proposition 1.9]. Thus $(1 + a) E = 0$ gives $E = 0$. In a local ring, each ideal $I \ne R$ is contained in $\mathfrak{m}$, which is the Jacobson radical. $\square$

**Corollary 4.1.9.** Let $R$ be a Noetherian domain and let $I \ne R$ be an ideal. Then $\bigcap_n I^n = 0$.

Lean: none yet

*Proof.* Take $M = R$ and $a$ as in Theorem 4.1.7. If $1 + a = 0$, then $1 = -a \in I$, and this is not the case. Since $R$ is a domain and $(1 + a) E = 0$, we get $E = 0$. $\square$

**Example 4.1.10.** The hypotheses of the two corollaries are necessary. Let $k$ be a field, let $R = k \times k$ and let $I = k \times 0$. Then $R$ is Noetherian, but it is not a domain, and $I$ is not in the Jacobson radical, which is $0$. Here $I^n = I$ for each $n \ge 1$, so $\bigcap_n I^n = I \ne 0$. The element $a = (-1, 0)$ of $I$ gives $1 + a = (0, 1)$, and $(0, 1) \cdot I = 0$, as Theorem 4.1.7 says. Section 3.2 gave an example of a local ring that is not Noetherian where $\bigcap_n \mathfrak{m}^n \ne 0$: the ring of germs of smooth functions.

### The Lean proof

Theorem 3.2.4 uses the case of Corollary 4.1.8 with $M = R$ and a local ring $R$. ### Notes and references

The lemma of Artin and Rees is [AM69, Proposition 10.9] in the language of filtrations, the form of Theorem 4.1.5 is [AM69, Corollary 10.10], and Corollary 4.1.6 is [AM69, Theorem 10.11]. Our proof with the Rees algebra follows [AM69, Chapter 10]. Matsumura proves the same results in [Mat, Section 8]. The intersection theorem of Krull is [AM69, Theorem 10.17]. Atiyah and Macdonald state it in the form that $E$ is the set of the elements of $M$ that some $1 + a$ with $a \in I$ kills, and Corollaries 4.1.8 and 4.1.9 are among the corollaries that follow it in [AM69, Chapter 10].
