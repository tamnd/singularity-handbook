# Regular local rings

A point of a variety is regular when its local ring is a regular local ring. This chapter defines regular local rings and proves their first properties. The aim is two theorems. The first theorem (Theorem 1.3.3) says that a regular local ring is a domain. Thus a regular point of a scheme lies on exactly one irreducible component. The second theorem (Theorem 1.4.2) says that a Noetherian local domain of dimension 1 is regular if and only if it is integrally closed. Thus a normal curve is regular, and this is the basis of the resolution of curves in Volume 4.

Section 1.1 recalls the dimension theory that we use. Section 1.2 gives the definition, the examples and the behaviour of regularity under the quotient by one element. Section 1.3 proves that a regular local ring is a domain. Section 1.4 treats dimension 1.

Throughout the chapter, a ring is commutative with $1$. A local ring $(R, \mathfrak{m}, \kappa)$ is a ring $R$ with exactly one maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, and $\kappa = R/\mathfrak{m}$ is its residue field. For a finitely generated ideal $I$ we write $\mu(I)$ for the least number of generators of $I$. The dimension $\dim R$ is the Krull dimension: the supremum of the lengths $d$ of the chains $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_d$ of prime ideals of $R$.
