# Regular local rings

A point of a variety is regular when its local ring is a regular local ring. This chapter defines regular local rings and proves their first properties. The aim is two theorems. The first theorem (Theorem 1.3.3) says that a regular local ring is a domain. Thus a regular point of a scheme lies on exactly one irreducible component. The second theorem (Theorem 1.4.2) says that a Noetherian local domain of dimension 1 is regular if and only if it is integrally closed. Thus a normal curve is regular, and this is the basis of the resolution of curves in Section 2.5. Sections 1.5 and 1.6 prove the theorem of Serre (Theorem 1.6.8): a Noetherian local ring is regular if and only if its residue field has finite projective dimension. As a corollary, a localization of a regular local ring is regular (Corollary 1.6.9). Section 1.7 uses the corollary to prove the criterion of Nagata for the openness of the regular locus (Theorem 1.7.7). Section 1.8 proves generic regularity in characteristic $0$, and thus the openness of the regular locus of an algebra of finite type over a field of characteristic $0$ (Theorem 1.8.10), and over a perfect field (Theorem 1.8.17). Section 1.9 removes the hypothesis on the field (Theorem 1.9.5). Section 1.10 proves that a chart of the blowup of a regular local ring at its maximal ideal is regular (Theorem 1.10.6).

Section 1.1 recalls the dimension theory that we use. Section 1.2 gives the definition, the examples and the behaviour of regularity under the quotient by one element. Section 1.3 proves that a regular local ring is a domain. Section 1.4 treats dimension 1. Section 1.5 proves that the maximal ideal is generated by a regular sequence, and computes the projective dimension of the residue field. Section 1.6 proves the converse of Serre and the localization theorem. Section 1.7 proves the criterion of Nagata. Section 1.8 proves the Jacobian criterion for a hypersurface and generic regularity over a perfect field. Section 1.9 proves the flat descent of regularity and generic regularity over an arbitrary field. Section 1.10 studies the associated graded ring and the blowup of a regular local ring.

Throughout the chapter, a ring is commutative with $1$. A local ring $(R, \mathfrak{m}, \kappa)$ is a ring $R$ with exactly one maximal ideal $\mathfrak{m}$, and $\kappa = R/\mathfrak{m}$ is its residue field. For a finitely generated ideal $I$ we write $\mu(I)$ for the least number of generators of $I$. The dimension $\dim R$ is the Krull dimension: the supremum of the lengths $d$ of the chains $\mathfrak{p}_0 \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_1 \subsetneq \dots \subsetneq \mathfrak{p}_d$ of prime ideals of $R$.
