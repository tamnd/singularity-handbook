## The blowup of a regular local ring

Each resolution algorithm blows up regular centers in a regular ambient space, and it needs that the result is again regular. In this section we prove this for the simplest center, a closed point. We work with one affine chart of the blowup at a time. The proof is algebraic, and it holds in every characteristic. The main tool is the structure of the associated graded ring of a regular local ring.

Throughout the section, $(R, \mathfrak{m}, \kappa)$ is a regular local ring of dimension $n$.

### The associated graded ring

The *associated graded ring* of $R$ is $\operatorname{gr}(R) = \bigoplus_{k \ge 0} \mathfrak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}$, with the product induced by the product of $R$. Let $x_1, \dots, x_n$ generate $\mathfrak{m}$. The images of the $x_i$ in $\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^2$ generate $\operatorname{gr}(R)$ as a $\kappa$-algebra, so there is a surjective homomorphism of graded $\kappa$-algebras
$$\varphi \colon \kappa[T_1, \dots, T_n] \to \operatorname{gr}(R), \qquad T_i \mapsto x_i + \mathfrak{m}^2.$$

**Proposition 1.10.1.** The map $\varphi$ is an isomorphism. Equivalently, let $F \in R[T_1, \dots, T_n]$ be homogeneous of degree $k$. If $F(x_1, \dots, x_n) \in \mathfrak{m}^{k+1}$, then each coefficient of $F$ is in $\mathfrak{m}$.

*Proof.* The map $\varphi$ is surjective in each degree $k$. By Theorem 3.3.13, $\ell(R/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}) = \binom{k+n}{n}$ for each $k$. By Lemma 3.1.2,
$$\dim_\kappa \mathfrak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1} = \binom{k+n}{n} - \binom{k-1+n}{n} = \binom{k+n-1}{n-1},$$
and this is the number of the monomials of degree $k$ in $n$ variables. So in each degree, $\varphi$ is a surjective $\kappa$-linear map between vector spaces of the same finite dimension, and it is bijective. For the second statement, the image of $F(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ in $\mathfrak{m}^k/\mathfrak{m}^{k+1}$ is $\varphi(\bar F)$, where $\bar F$ is the reduction of $F$ modulo $\mathfrak{m}$. $\square$

In particular, $\operatorname{gr}(R)$ is a domain. For $a \in R$ with $a \ne 0$, let $\operatorname{in}(a)$ be the image of $a$ in $\mathfrak{m}^j/\mathfrak{m}^{j+1}$, where $j$ is the largest integer with $a \in \mathfrak{m}^j$. This $j$ exists by the intersection theorem of Krull [AM69, Chapter 10]. Since $\operatorname{gr}(R)$ is a domain, $\operatorname{in}(ab) = \operatorname{in}(a) \operatorname{in}(b)$ for $a, b \ne 0$.

**Corollary 1.10.2.** Let $x \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$ and let $k, j \ge 0$. If $a \in R$ and $x^j a \in \mathfrak{m}^{k+j}$, then $a \in \mathfrak{m}^k$.

*Proof.* By induction on $j$, it is sufficient to treat $j = 1$. We can assume that $a \ne 0$. Let $a \in \mathfrak{m}^i \setminus \mathfrak{m}^{i+1}$, and assume that $i < k$. The element $\operatorname{in}(x)$ has degree $1$, and $\operatorname{in}(xa) = \operatorname{in}(x) \operatorname{in}(a) \ne 0$ has degree $i + 1$. So $xa \notin \mathfrak{m}^{i+2}$. But $xa \in \mathfrak{m}^{k+1} \subseteq \mathfrak{m}^{i+2}$. This is a contradiction, so $i \ge k$. $\square$

### The chart

**Definition 1.10.3.** Let $x \in \mathfrak{m}$. The *chart* of the blowup of $R$ at $\mathfrak{m}$ for $x$ is the $R$-subalgebra
$$R[\mathfrak{m}/x] \subseteq R_x$$
that the fractions $a/x$ with $a \in \mathfrak{m}$ generate. Its elements are the fractions $b/x^k$ with $k \ge 0$ and $b \in \mathfrak{m}^k$.

Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.blowupChart`, `Singularity.Algebra.mem_blowupChart_iff`

The blowup of $\operatorname{Spec} R$ at the closed point is covered by the spectra of the charts $R[\mathfrak{m}/x_i]$, where $x_1, \dots, x_n$ generate $\mathfrak{m}$ [Har, Section II.7]. The exceptional divisor is the closed subset $V(x_i)$ of the chart. If $R$ is the local ring of $\mathbb{A}^n$ at the origin, the chart for $x_1$ is the local version of the chart $\pi_1$ of Volume 7, Section 2.1.

The elements of the second description form a subring, because $(b/x^k)(c/x^l) = bc/x^{k+l}$ with $bc \in \mathfrak{m}^{k+l}$, and $b/x^k + c/x^l = (x^l b + x^k c)/x^{k+l}$ with $x^l b + x^k c \in \mathfrak{m}^{k+l}$. A product of $k$ fractions $a_i/x$ has this form, and conversely $b/x^k$ with $b \in \mathfrak{m}^k$ is a sum of such products. So the two descriptions agree.

Now let $x \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$. Then $x$ is a member of a minimal set of generators of $\mathfrak{m}$, by the lemma of Nakayama. So there are $y_1, \dots, y_d$ with $d = n - 1$ such that $x, y_1, \dots, y_d$ generate $\mathfrak{m}$.

Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.exists_fin_cons_span_eq_maximalIdeal`

Let $B = R[\mathfrak{m}/x]$. Since $\mathfrak{m}^k$ is generated by the monomials of degree $k$ in $x, y_1, \dots, y_d$, each element $b/x^k$ of $B$ is a polynomial in the fractions $y_i/x$ with coefficients in $R$. So the homomorphism
$$\psi \colon R[T_1, \dots, T_d] \to B, \qquad T_i \mapsto y_i/x,$$
is surjective, and $B$ is a finitely generated $R$-algebra.

**Proposition 1.10.4.** The kernel of the composite map $R[T_1, \dots, T_d] \to B \to B/xB$ is $\mathfrak{m} R[T_1, \dots, T_d]$. Thus
$$B/xB \cong \kappa[T_1, \dots, T_d].$$

Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.ker_blowupChartQuotientHom`, `Singularity.Algebra.blowupChartQuotientEquiv`

*Proof.* Let $a \in \mathfrak{m}$. Then $a = x \cdot (a/x)$ in $B$, so $a \in xB$. Thus the kernel contains $\mathfrak{m} R[T]$.

Conversely, let $G = \sum_\alpha g_\alpha T^\alpha$ with $\psi(G) \in xB$, and let $k$ be larger than the degree of $G$. Put
$$H = \sum_\alpha g_\alpha \, x^{k - |\alpha|} y^\alpha \in R.$$
Then $\psi(G) = H/x^k$ in $R_x$. Since $\psi(G) \in xB$, there are $l \ge 0$ and $c \in \mathfrak{m}^l$ with $H/x^k = x c/x^l$ in $R_x$. The ring $R$ is a domain by Theorem 1.3.3, so $R \to R_x$ is injective, and $x^l H = x^{k+1} c$ in $R$. The right side is in $\mathfrak{m}^{k + 1 + l}$. By Corollary 1.10.2, $H \in \mathfrak{m}^{k+1}$.

Now $H = F(x, y_1, \dots, y_d)$, where $F = \sum_\alpha g_\alpha T_0^{k - |\alpha|} T^\alpha$ is homogeneous of degree $k$. By Proposition 1.10.1, each $g_\alpha$ is in $\mathfrak{m}$. Thus $G \in \mathfrak{m} R[T]$. $\square$

So the exceptional divisor of the chart is the affine space $\mathbb{A}^d_\kappa$. It is a chart of the projective space $\mathbb{P}^d_\kappa = \operatorname{Proj} \operatorname{gr}(R)$.

### Regularity of the chart

**Lemma 1.10.5.** Let $A$ be a Noetherian domain and let $t \in A$ with $t \ne 0$. If $A_t$ and $A/tA$ are regular rings, then $A$ is a regular ring.

*Proof.* Let $\mathfrak{p}$ be a prime ideal of $A$. If $t \notin \mathfrak{p}$, then $A_\mathfrak{p}$ is a localization of $A_t$ at a prime ideal, and it is regular. If $t \in \mathfrak{p}$, then $t$ is a nonzerodivisor in the maximal ideal of the local domain $A_\mathfrak{p}$, and $A_\mathfrak{p}/tA_\mathfrak{p}$ is the localization of $A/tA$ at the prime ideal $\mathfrak{p}/tA$, so it is regular. By Proposition 1.2.8, $A_\mathfrak{p}$ is regular. $\square$

**Theorem 1.10.6.** Let $(R, \mathfrak{m})$ be a regular local ring and let $x \in \mathfrak{m} \setminus \mathfrak{m}^2$. Then $R[\mathfrak{m}/x]$ is a Noetherian regular domain.

Node: SG0000003C · Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.isRegularRing_blowupChart`, `Singularity.Algebra.isNoetherianRing_blowupChart`

*Proof.* Let $B = R[\mathfrak{m}/x]$. By the surjection $\psi$, $B$ is a quotient of a polynomial ring over $R$ in finitely many variables, so it is Noetherian by the basis theorem of Hilbert. The ring $R$ is a domain by Theorem 1.3.3, and $x \ne 0$. So $B$ is a subring of the domain $R_x$, and it is a domain.

We apply Lemma 1.10.5 with $A = B$ and $t = x$. Since $R \subseteq B \subseteq R_x$ and $x$ is a unit of $R_x$, we have $B_x = R_x$. Each localization of $R_x$ at a prime ideal is a localization of $R$ at a prime ideal, so it is regular by Corollary 1.6.9. Thus $B_x$ is regular. By Proposition 1.10.4, $B/xB$ is a polynomial ring over the field $\kappa$, and a polynomial ring over a field is a regular ring [Mat, Theorem 19.5]. By Lemma 1.10.5, $B$ is regular. $\square$

The theorem says that the blowup of a regular scheme at a closed point is regular, because the blowup is covered by the spectra of these charts. The blowup along a regular center of higher dimension is also regular [Hir64]. The library does not have this case yet.

### Two examples

In the next two examples we compute charts of global rings. Let $A$ be a domain, let $\mathfrak{m}$ be an ideal of $A$ and let $x \in \mathfrak{m}$ with $x \ne 0$. We define the chart $A[\mathfrak{m}/x]$ as in Definition 1.10.3: it is the subring of $A_x$ that $A$ and the fractions $a/x$ with $a \in \mathfrak{m}$ generate. If $\mathfrak{m}$ is a maximal ideal, then the localization of $A[\mathfrak{m}/x]$ at the multiplicative set $A \setminus \mathfrak{m}$ is the chart $A_\mathfrak{m}[\mathfrak{m}A_\mathfrak{m}/x]$ of the local ring.

**Example 1.10.7 (the blowup of the plane).** Let $k$ be a field, let $A = k[x, y]$ and let $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y)$. Let $u = y/x$. The chart for $x$ is $B = A[\mathfrak{m}/x] = k[x, u]$, with $y = xu$. The elements $x$ and $u$ are algebraically independent over $k$, because they generate the field $k(x, y)$ of transcendence degree $2$. So $B$ is a polynomial ring in $x$ and $u$. Also $\mathfrak{m}B = (x, xu) = xB$, and the exceptional divisor of the chart is $\operatorname{Spec} B/xB = \operatorname{Spec} k[u]$, a line. In the same way, the chart for $y$ is $k[y, w]$ with $w = x/y$ and $x = yw$. On the overlap, $uw = 1$. So the two lines glue to the projective line $\mathbb{P}^1_k$, as Proposition 1.10.4 predicts.

Let $f \in A$ have order $d$ at the origin, that is, $f = f_d + f_{d+1} + \dots$ with $f_j$ homogeneous of degree $j$ and $f_d \ne 0$. Then $f_j(x, xu) = x^j f_j(1, u)$, so
$$f(x, xu) = x^d f'(x, u), \qquad f'(x, u) = \sum_{j \ge d} x^{j-d} f_j(1, u).$$
The curve $V(f')$ in the chart is the *strict transform* of $V(f)$, and $f'(0, u) = f_d(1, u)$. So the strict transform meets the exceptional divisor at the roots of the initial form $f_d$, that is, at the tangent directions of the curve at the origin.

*The cusp.* Let $f = y^2 - x^3$. Then $d = 2$ and $f(x, xu) = x^2(u^2 - x)$. The strict transform is the curve $x = u^2$ in the plane with the coordinates $x, u$. It is regular, because it is the graph of a function of $u$. It meets the exceptional divisor $x = 0$ only at $u = 0$, and the intersection is $\operatorname{Spec} k[u]/(u^2)$, a point of length $2$. In the other chart, $f(yw, y) = y^2(1 - yw^3)$, and the strict transform $1 - yw^3 = 0$ does not meet $y = 0$. So one blowup resolves the cusp, but the strict transform is tangent to the exceptional divisor.

*The node.* Let $k$ have characteristic different from $2$ and let $f = y^2 - x^2 - x^3$. Then $f(x, xu) = x^2(u^2 - 1 - x)$. The strict transform $x = u^2 - 1$ is regular, and it meets the exceptional divisor at the two points $u = 1$ and $u = -1$. These are the two tangent directions $y = \pm x$ of the node. The blowup separates the two branches, as the normalization does in Example 1.4.5.

**Example 1.10.8 (the blowup of the cone).** Let $k$ be a field, let $A = k[x, y, z]/(xy - z^2)$ and let $\mathfrak{m} = (x, y, z)$. The ring $A$ is a domain of dimension $2$, and its local ring at $\mathfrak{m}$ is not regular (Example 1.4.6). Its field of fractions is $k(x, z)$, because $y = z^2/x$, so $x$ and $z$ are algebraically independent over $k$. We compute the three charts.

*The chart for $x$.* Let $v = z/x$. Then $y/x = xy/x^2 = z^2/x^2 = v^2$. So $A[\mathfrak{m}/x] = A[v]$, and in this ring $y = xv^2$ and $z = xv$. Thus $A[\mathfrak{m}/x] = k[x, v]$. The elements $x$ and $v$ generate the field $k(x, z)$, so they are algebraically independent, and $k[x, v]$ is a polynomial ring. Also $\mathfrak{m}A[\mathfrak{m}/x] = (x, xv^2, xv) = (x)$, and the exceptional divisor of the chart is $\operatorname{Spec} k[v]$.

*The chart for $y$.* By the symmetry of $x$ and $y$, the chart is the polynomial ring $k[y, w]$ with $w = z/y$, $x = yw^2$ and $z = yw$. The exceptional divisor of the chart is $\operatorname{Spec} k[w]$.

*The chart for $z$.* Let $a = x/z$ and $b = y/z$. Then $ab = xy/z^2 = 1$. So $A[\mathfrak{m}/z] = k[z, a, a^{-1}]$, with $x = za$ and $y = z a^{-1}$, and $z, a$ are algebraically independent. The exceptional divisor of this chart is $\operatorname{Spec} k[a, a^{-1}]$. It is contained in the two other charts.

On the overlap of the first two charts, $vw = z^2/(xy) = 1$. So the exceptional divisor is a projective line, glued from $\operatorname{Spec} k[v]$ and $\operatorname{Spec} k[w]$. Each chart is a regular ring, so the blowup of the cone at its vertex is regular.

Compare this with Proposition 1.10.4. The local ring of the cone has embedding dimension $3$. If it were regular, the exceptional divisor would be $\mathbb{P}^2_k$. In fact, the exceptional divisor is the conic $XY = Z^2$ in $\mathbb{P}^2_k$, that is, $\operatorname{Proj}$ of the graded ring $k[X, Y, Z]/(XY - Z^2)$. This conic is isomorphic to $\mathbb{P}^1_k$, but it is not all of $\mathbb{P}^2_k$. In general, the exceptional divisor of the blowup of a Noetherian local ring at its maximal ideal is $\operatorname{Proj}$ of the associated graded ring. The reason is that the blowup is $\operatorname{Proj} \bigoplus_k \mathfrak{m}^k$ [Har, Section II.7], and the ideal of the exceptional divisor is the graded ideal $\bigoplus_k \mathfrak{m}^{k+1}$. With $e$ the embedding dimension, this graded ring is a quotient of a polynomial ring in $e$ variables, so the exceptional divisor is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb{P}^{e-1}$. It is all of $\mathbb{P}^{e-1}$ when the ring is regular, by Proposition 1.10.1.

### The Lean proof

The results of this section are in `Singularity/Algebra/BlowupChart.lean`. The Lean proof follows the proofs of this section. For Proposition 1.10.1 it uses `Singularity.Algebra.length_grPiece_eq_choose`, the dimension count of Theorem 3.3.13. In the proof of Proposition 1.10.4, the Lean proof works with the fractions $b/x^k$ of Definition 1.10.3, which `Singularity.Algebra.mem_blowupChart_iff` describes. The regularity of the polynomial ring over a field comes from Mathlib. The main theorems use only the axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

### Notes and references

Proposition 1.10.1 is [AM69, Theorem 11.22]. Matsumura treats it in [Mat, Section 14]. The regularity of the blowup of a regular scheme along a regular center is a standard step of each resolution proof. Hironaka proves it in [Hir64]. For the blowup itself, see [Har, Section II.7].
