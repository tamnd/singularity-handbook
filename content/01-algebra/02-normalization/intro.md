# Integral closure and normalization

Chapter 1 showed that a Noetherian local domain of dimension 1 is regular if and only if it is integrally closed. Thus, to resolve the singularities of a curve, it is sufficient to replace its coordinate ring by the integral closure. This chapter studies that construction. The main question is finiteness. The integral closure of a domain $A$ is a ring $\tilde{A}$ with $A \subseteq \tilde{A} \subseteq \operatorname{Frac} A$, and for geometry we need $\tilde{A}$ to be a finite $A$-module. Then $\operatorname{Spec} \tilde{A} \to \operatorname{Spec} A$ is a finite morphism, and $\tilde{A}$ is again a finitely generated algebra.

Section 2.1 recalls integral dependence and gives the first examples of integrally closed rings. Section 2.2 proves the normalization lemma of Noether. Section 2.3 proves that the integral closure is finite when the field extension is separable, and in particular for each finitely generated domain over a field of characteristic 0. It ends with the application to curves.

We use the notation of Chapter 1. For a domain $A$ we write $\operatorname{Frac} A$ for its field of fractions.
