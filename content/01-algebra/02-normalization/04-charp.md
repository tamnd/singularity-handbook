## The normalization in characteristic p

In this section we prove the theorem of E. Noether over each field. Section 2.3 proved it when the field of fractions of $A$ is separable over the field of fractions of a Noether normalization. Over a field of characteristic $p > 0$ this is not always so, and the trace form can be zero. We remove the condition in two steps. First we treat a purely inseparable extension of $k(y_1, \dots, y_s)$. There we use $q$-th roots in place of the trace. Then we reduce the general case to the purely inseparable case and to the separable case with the normal closure. The proof follows [Eis95, Corollary 13.13] and [Mat, Section 33].

Throughout the section, $k$ is a field of characteristic $p > 0$, $P = k[y_1, \dots, y_s]$ is a polynomial ring, and $K = \operatorname{Frac} P = k(y_1, \dots, y_s)$. The polynomial ring $P$ is Noetherian, and it is integrally closed by Example 2.1.5.

### Purely inseparable extensions

Recall that an algebraic extension $M$ of a field $K$ of characteristic $p$ is *purely inseparable* if for each $x \in M$ there is an integer $e \ge 0$ with $x^{p^e} \in K$ [Lan02, Chapter V, Section 6]. In characteristic $p$ the map $u \mapsto u^p$ is a ring map, because the binomial coefficients $\binom{p}{i}$ with $0 < i < p$ are divisible by $p$. Thus for each power $q$ of $p$ the map $u \mapsto u^q$ is also a ring map. We call it the *Frobenius map*.

**Lemma 2.4.1.** Let $M$ be a finite purely inseparable extension of $K$. Then there is a power $q$ of $p$ such that $x^q \in K$ for each $x \in M$.

Lean: `IsPurelyInseparable.exponent_def'` (in Mathlib)

*Proof.* Let $b_1, \dots, b_n$ be a basis of $M$ over $K$, and choose $q$, a power of $p$, such that $b_j^q \in K$ for each $j$. Let $x \in M$ and write $x = \sum_j c_j b_j$ with $c_j \in K$. The Frobenius map is a ring map, so $x^q = \sum_j c_j^q b_j^q$, and each term is in $K$. $\square$

**Lemma 2.4.2.** Let $k'$ be a field extension of $k$, let $q$ be a power of $p$, and let $f = \sum_\alpha c_\alpha y^\alpha \in P$. Assume that for each coefficient $c_\alpha$ of $f$ there is an element $d_\alpha \in k'$ with $d_\alpha^q = c_\alpha$. Then in the polynomial ring $k'[Y_1, \dots, Y_s]$,
$$f(Y_1^q, \dots, Y_s^q) = \Big( \sum_\alpha d_\alpha Y^\alpha \Big)^q.$$

Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.expand_map_eq_pow`

*Proof.* The Frobenius map of $k'[Y_1, \dots, Y_s]$ is a ring map, so the right side is $\sum_\alpha d_\alpha^q Y^{q \alpha} = \sum_\alpha c_\alpha Y^{q\alpha}$. This is the left side. $\square$

**Lemma 2.4.3.** Let $k'$ be a finite field extension of $k$, let $q$ be a power of $p$, and let $T = k'[Y_1, \dots, Y_s]$. Let $\varphi : P \to T$ be the ring map that is the inclusion on $k$ and sends $y_i$ to $Y_i^q$. Then $\varphi$ is injective, and $T$ is a finite $P$-module by $\varphi$.

Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.expandMap_injective`, `Singularity.Algebra.expandMap_finite`

*Proof.* Let $f = \sum_\alpha c_\alpha y^\alpha$. Then $\varphi(f) = \sum_\alpha c_\alpha Y^{q \alpha}$. Different exponents $\alpha$ give different monomials $Y^{q\alpha}$, so $\varphi(f) = 0$ only if each $c_\alpha = 0$. Thus $\varphi$ is injective.

The ring $T$ is generated as a $P$-algebra by $Y_1, \dots, Y_s$ and a basis of $k'$ over $k$. Each $Y_i$ is integral over $P$, because it is a root of the monic polynomial $X^q - y_i$. Each element of $k'$ is integral over $k$, because $k'$ is a finite extension, and so it is integral over $P$. A finitely generated algebra that is generated by integral elements is a finite module [AM69, Corollary 5.2]. $\square$

### The purely inseparable case

**Proposition 2.4.4.** Let $M$ be a finite purely inseparable extension of $K$, and let $B$ be the integral closure of $P$ in $M$. Then $B$ is a finite $P$-module.

Node: SG00000028 · Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.finite_integralClosure_mvPolynomial_of_isPurelyInseparable`

*Proof.* *Step 1. The field $k'$.* By Lemma 2.4.1 there is a power $q$ of $p$ with $x^q \in K$ for each $x \in M$. Let $b_1, \dots, b_n$ be a basis of $M$ over $K$, and let $a_j = b_j^q \in K$. Write $a_j = f_j / g_j$ with $f_j, g_j \in P$ and $g_j \ne 0$. Let $S \subseteq k$ be the finite set of the coefficients of all the polynomials $f_j$ and $g_j$. Let $\Omega$ be an algebraic closure of $k$. For each $c \in S$, the polynomial $X^q - c$ has a root $d_c \in \Omega$. Let $k' = k(d_c : c \in S) \subseteq \Omega$. This field is generated by finitely many algebraic elements, so it is a finite extension of $k$.

*Step 2. The ring $T$.* Let $T = k'[Y_1, \dots, Y_s]$ and let $\varphi : P \to T$ be the map of Lemma 2.4.3. Then $T$ is a finite $P$-module, and $T$ is a Noetherian $P$-module, because $P$ is Noetherian [AM69, Proposition 6.5]. The ring $T$ is a unique factorization domain, so it is integrally closed (Proposition 2.1.4). Let $K' = \operatorname{Frac} T$. The map $\varphi$ is injective, so it extends to a field map $K \to K'$. Below we consider $K'$ as an extension of $K$ by this map.

*Step 3. $M$ embeds into $K'$.* By Lemma 2.4.2 there are $F_j, G_j \in T$ with $\varphi(f_j) = F_j^q$ and $\varphi(g_j) = G_j^q$. Let $z_j = F_j / G_j \in K'$. Then $a_j = z_j^q$ in $K'$. The element $b_j$ is a root of $X^q - a_j$, so its minimal polynomial over $K$ divides $X^q - a_j$. In $K'[X]$,
$$X^q - a_j = X^q - z_j^q = (X - z_j)^q,$$
because the Frobenius map of $K'[X]$ is a ring map. Thus the minimal polynomial of each $b_j$ splits in $K'$. The field $M$ is generated over $K$ by $b_1, \dots, b_n$. A $K$-embedding of $K(b_1, \dots, b_{j-1})$ into $K'$ extends to $K(b_1, \dots, b_j)$, because the minimal polynomial of $b_j$ over the smaller field divides the minimal polynomial over $K$, and so it has a root in $K'$ [Lan02, Chapter V, Section 2]. By induction on $j$ there is a $K$-embedding $\iota : M \to K'$.

*Step 4. $B$ embeds into $T$.* Let $x \in B$. Then $\iota(x)$ is a root of the same monic polynomial over $P$ as $x$, so it is integral over $P$. The map $P \to K'$ goes through $T$, so $\iota(x)$ is also integral over $T$. The ring $T$ is integrally closed, so $\iota(x) \in T$. Thus $\iota$ restricts to an injective map $B \to T$, and this map is $P$-linear. Its image is a $P$-submodule of the Noetherian $P$-module $T$, so it is a finite $P$-module. Thus $B$ is a finite $P$-module. $\square$

Note that the proof does not use the trace. It uses only the fact that the $q$-th roots of finitely many constants generate a finite extension of $k$. This fact is the reason why the theorem is true for finitely generated algebras over a field, and it fails for some other Noetherian domains (see the notes at the end of the section).

### The reduction to the purely inseparable case

**Lemma 2.4.5.** Let $L'$ be a finite normal extension of a field $K$, let $G$ be the group of the $K$-automorphisms of $L'$, and let $M = \{x \in L' : \sigma(x) = x \text{ for each } \sigma \in G\}$ be the fixed field. Then $M$ is purely inseparable over $K$, and $L'$ is a Galois extension of $M$.

Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.isPurelyInseparable_fixedField_top`, `IsGalois.of_fixed_field` (in Mathlib)

*Proof.* Let $x \in M$ and let $F$ be the minimal polynomial of $x$ over $K$. The extension $L'$ is normal, so $F$ splits in $L'$. Let $y \in L'$ be a root of $F$. There is a $K$-embedding $K(x) \to L'$ that sends $x$ to $y$, and since $L'$ is normal it extends to a $K$-automorphism $\sigma$ of $L'$ [Lan02, Chapter V, Section 3]. Then $y = \sigma(x) = x$. Thus $x$ is the only root of $F$, and $F = (X - x)^m$ in $L'[X]$ for some $m$.

Write $F(X) = H(X^{p^e})$, where $H \in K[X]$ is irreducible and separable [Lan02, Chapter V, Section 6]. The roots of $H$ are the elements $u^{p^e}$ for the roots $u$ of $F$, so $H$ has only the root $x^{p^e}$. A separable polynomial with only one root has degree 1. Thus $H = X - c$ for some $c \in K$, and $x^{p^e} = c \in K$. This proves that $M$ is purely inseparable over $K$.

The group $G$ is finite, because $L'$ is a finite extension of $K$. By the theorem of Artin, $L'$ is a Galois extension of the fixed field of a finite group of automorphisms [Lan02, Chapter VI, Theorem 1.8]. $\square$

**Theorem 2.4.6.** Let $L$ be a finite extension of $K = k(y_1, \dots, y_s)$, where $k$ is any field. Then the integral closure of $P = k[y_1, \dots, y_s]$ in $L$ is a finite $P$-module.

*Proof.* If $k$ has characteristic 0, then $L$ is separable over $K$, and Theorem 2.3.3 gives the result. Assume that $k$ has characteristic $p > 0$.

Let $L'$ be a normal closure of $L$ over $K$. It is a finite normal extension of $K$ that contains $L$ [Lan02, Chapter V, Section 3]. Let $M \subseteq L'$ be the fixed field of Lemma 2.4.5. Then $M$ is a finite purely inseparable extension of $K$, and $L'$ is a separable extension of $M$.

Let $R$ be the integral closure of $P$ in $M$. By Proposition 2.4.4, $R$ is a finite $P$-module. Thus $R$ is a Noetherian ring, because each ideal of $R$ is a $P$-submodule of a Noetherian $P$-module. The ring $R$ is integrally closed in $M$ by Lemma 2.1.2, and $\operatorname{Frac} R = M$ by Lemma 2.3.1. Thus we can apply Theorem 2.3.3 to $R$ and to the separable extension $L'$ of $M$. The integral closure $C$ of $R$ in $L'$ is a finite $R$-module. Since $R$ is a finite $P$-module, $C$ is a finite $P$-module: if $r_1, \dots, r_a$ generate $R$ over $P$ and $c_1, \dots, c_b$ generate $C$ over $R$, then the products $r_i c_j$ generate $C$ over $P$.

The ring $R$ is integral over $P$, so by Lemma 2.1.2 the integral closure of $P$ in $L'$ is $C$. The integral closure of $P$ in $L$ is contained in it. Thus the integral closure of $P$ in $L$ is a $P$-submodule of the finite $P$-module $C$. Since $P$ is Noetherian, it is a finite $P$-module. $\square$

### The theorem of Noether

**Theorem 2.4.7 (E. Noether).** Let $k$ be a field and let $A$ be a domain that is a finitely generated $k$-algebra. Then the normalization $\tilde{A}$ is a finite $A$-module.

Node: SG0000000M · Lean: `Singularity.Algebra.finite_integralClosure`

*Proof.* By Theorem 2.2.1 there are elements $y_1, \dots, y_s \in A$, algebraically independent over $k$, such that $A$ is a finite module over $P = k[y_1, \dots, y_s]$. By Step 1 of the proof of Theorem 2.3.4, $L = \operatorname{Frac} A$ is a finite extension of $K = \operatorname{Frac} P$. By Theorem 2.4.6, the integral closure $B$ of $P$ in $L$ is a finite $P$-module. By Step 2 of the proof of Theorem 2.3.4, $\tilde{A} \subseteq B$. Since $P$ is Noetherian, $B$ is a Noetherian $P$-module, so its $P$-submodule $\tilde{A}$ is a finite $P$-module. A set of generators of $\tilde{A}$ over $P$ also generates $\tilde{A}$ over $A$. Thus $\tilde{A}$ is a finite $A$-module. $\square$

This is [Har, Chapter I, Theorem 3.9A] and [Eis95, Corollary 13.13]. The same proof shows a little more. Let $L$ be any finite extension of $\operatorname{Frac} A$. Then the integral closure of $A$ in $L$ is a finite $A$-module. In the language of Nagata, a finitely generated algebra over a field is a *Japanese ring*.

**Corollary 2.4.8.** Let $k$ be a field and let $A$ be a domain that is a finitely generated $k$-algebra with $\dim A = 1$. Then $\tilde{A}$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra, it is a finite $A$-module, and for each maximal ideal $\mathfrak{n}$ of $\tilde{A}$ the local ring $\tilde{A}_\mathfrak{n}$ is regular of dimension 1.

Thus the affine part of the resolution of curves holds over each field. This is the first result of the project that is true in characteristic $p$ with no extra hypothesis. Section 2.5 glues the affine pieces and proves that the normalization of each integral curve is a resolution. In higher dimension, the normalization of a surface is not regular in general, and Volume 4 needs more tools.

### The Lean proof

The Lean proofs are in the file `Singularity/Algebra/NormalizationCharP.lean` of `tamnd/singularity-lean`. The file follows the proof above, with two changes in the form.

1. In Lean, the field $k'$ of Proposition 2.4.4 is `IntermediateField.adjoin k S'` inside `AlgebraicClosure k`, where $S'$ is the finite set of the chosen roots $d_c$. The map $\varphi$ of Lemma 2.4.3 is `expandMap`, the composite of `MvPolynomial.map (algebraMap k k')` and `MvPolynomial.expand q`. Lemma 2.4.2 is `expand_map_eq_pow`, and its proof uses the Mathlib identity `MvPolynomial.map_iterateFrobenius_expand`. The finiteness of $T$ in Lemma 2.4.3 comes from `Algebra.IsIntegral.finite`, because $T$ is integral and of finite type over $P$. For injectivity, the Lean proof applies the Frobenius map to $\varphi(f) = 0$ and gets $f^q = 0$ in $k'[Y_1, \dots, Y_s]$, in place of the argument with monomials.
2. Step 3 of Proposition 2.4.4 uses `IntermediateField.nonempty_algHom_of_adjoin_splits` of Mathlib. It gives the embedding $\iota$ in one step from the fact that the minimal polynomials of the basis elements split in $K'$. Step 4 is the lemma `finite_integralClosure_of_algHom`, which holds for each finite $P$-algebra $T$ that is a Noetherian normal domain.

The reduction of Theorem 2.4.6 is `finite_integralClosure_of_isPurelyInseparable`. It uses `IntermediateField.normalClosure` for $L'$ and `IntermediateField.fixedField` for $M$, and the lemma `finite_integralClosure_of_tower` for the step from $R$ to $C$. Theorem 2.4.7 is `finite_integralClosure`, and Corollary 2.4.8 is `Singularity.Curves.isDedekindDomain_integralClosure` in the file `Singularity/Curves/Affine.lean`. Each of these theorems uses only the standard axioms of Lean.

### Notes and references

Proposition 2.4.4 is the place where the theorem depends on the base field. Let $R$ be a Noetherian domain of characteristic $p$ with fraction field $K$. If $[K : K^p]$ is infinite, then the integral closure of $R$ in $K^{1/p}$ can fail to be finite, and Nagata used this to make his examples of Noetherian domains with a normalization that is not finite [Nag62, Appendix]. Over a field $k$, the extension $k'$ in Step 1 is finite because it is generated by finitely many roots. This is true even when $[k : k^p]$ is infinite. The rings with good finiteness properties of this type are the Nagata rings, and the excellent rings of Grothendieck [Mat, Sections 32 and 34] [Stacks]. Excellent rings are the natural setting for the resolution of singularities [Mat, Section 34].
