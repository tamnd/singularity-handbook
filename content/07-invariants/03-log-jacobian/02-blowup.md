## The log-Jacobian ideal under a point blowup

The residual order can go up under a point blowup (Section 1.4). In this section we prove that the residual order $\rho$ of the log-Jacobian ideal does not go up. The proof has two steps. The first step is an exact formula for the ideal after the blowup, at each point of the exceptional divisor. The second step uses the dichotomy of Theorem 3.1.4 at the new point. As a corollary, we get Moh's bound for $q = p$ at each point, and we see what happens at a kangaroo point.

### The setting

We use the charts of Section 2.1. Let $K$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p$, let $g \in K[x]$ be clean for the divisor $E$ of the set $\Delta$, and let $\operatorname{ord} g \ge p$. So the hypersurface $f = z^p + g$ has order $p$ at the origin. After a change of the numbering, a point of the exceptional divisor of the blowup of the origin of $\mathbb{A}^n$ is the origin of the chart $\pi = \pi_{1,t}$, with
$$\pi(x_1) = x_1, \qquad \pi(x_i) = x_1 (x_i + t_i) \ (i \ne 1).$$
With $z = x_1 z'$, the strict transform of $f$ is $z'^p + g'$, with $g' = x_1^{-p} \pi(g)$ (Proposition 2.1.1). A further translation $z' \mapsto z' + c$ adds $c^p$ to $g'$, and by Lemma 3.1.2 this does not change the log-Jacobian ideal. So we can work with $g'$.

The new divisor is the exceptional divisor $x_1 = 0$ together with the components of $E$ that pass through the new origin:
$$\Delta' = \{1\} \cup \{ i \in \Delta : i \ne 1, \ t_i = 0 \}.$$
If $i \in \Delta$ and $t_i \ne 0$, the strict transform of $x_i = 0$ is $x_i + t_i = 0$, and it does not pass through the origin of the chart. In this case the point is *translated* in the direction $i$.

We compare ideals in the power series ring $K[[x]]$ at the origin of the chart. There, $x_i + t_i$ is a unit when $t_i \ne 0$. We write $\Theta'$ for the log-Jacobian ideal for $\Delta'$, and $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$.

### The transformation law

**Definition 3.2.1.** The *log-Jacobian ideal tangent to the origin* is
$$\Theta_0(g) = \bigl( x_i \partial_i g \ (i \in \Delta) \bigr) + \mathfrak{m} \bigl( \partial_j g \ (j \notin \Delta) \bigr).$$

Lean: `Singularity.logJacobianTangent`, `Singularity.span_range_X_mul_logJacobian_le` and `Singularity.logJacobianTangent_le` (the two inclusions)

The logarithmic derivations that map $\mathfrak{m}$ into $\mathfrak{m}$ are the $K[x]$-combinations of $x_i \partial_i$ for $i \in \Delta$ and of $x_k \partial_j$ for $j \notin \Delta$ and each $k$. The ideal $\Theta_0(g)$ is the image of $g$ under these derivations. It is clear that $\mathfrak{m} \Theta(g) \subseteq \Theta_0(g) \subseteq \Theta(g)$.

**Lemma 3.2.2.** For each $h \in K[x]$,
$$\partial_i \pi(h) = x_1 \, \pi(\partial_i h) \ (i \ne 1), \qquad x_1 \partial_1 \pi(h) = \pi\Bigl( \sum_{i=1}^n x_i \partial_i h \Bigr).$$

Lean: `Singularity.pderiv_pointChart_of_ne`, `Singularity.X_mul_pderiv_pointChart`

*Proof.* Both formulas follow from the chain rule. For $i \ne 1$, the variable $x_i$ occurs in $\pi(h) = h(x_1, x_1(x_2 + t_2), \dots)$ only in the place $i$, with the factor $x_1$. For the variable $x_1$, the chain rule gives $\partial_1 \pi(h) = \pi(\partial_1 h) + \sum_{i \ne 1} (x_i + t_i) \pi(\partial_i h)$. We multiply by $x_1$ and we use $x_1 (x_i + t_i) = \pi(x_i)$. $\square$

**Theorem 3.2.3.** In $K[[x]]$,
$$\Theta'(g') = x_1^{-p} \, \pi\bigl(\Theta_0(g)\bigr) \, K[[x]].$$

*Proof.* For each logarithmic derivation $D$ for $\Delta'$, we have $D(x_1^{-p} u) = x_1^{-p} D(u)$. For $D = x_1 \partial_1$ this is $x_1 \partial_1 (x_1^{-p} u) = x_1^{-p} (x_1 \partial_1 u - p u)$, and $p = 0$ in $K$. The other basis elements do not involve $x_1$. So $\Theta'(g') = x_1^{-p} J$, where $J$ is the ideal generated by $D \pi(g)$ for the basis elements $D$. By Lemma 3.2.2, $J$ is generated by:

1. $\pi\bigl(\sum_i x_i \partial_i g\bigr)$, from $D = x_1 \partial_1$;
2. $\pi(x_i \partial_i g)$ for $i \in \Delta$, $i \ne 1$, $t_i = 0$, from $D = x_i \partial_i$, because $x_i \cdot x_1 \pi(\partial_i g) = \pi(x_i) \pi(\partial_i g)$ when $t_i = 0$;
3. $x_1 \pi(\partial_j g)$ for $j \ne 1$ and $j \notin \Delta'$, from $D = \partial_j$.

Since $\pi(\mathfrak{m}) K[[x]] = (x_1)$, the ideal $\pi(\Theta_0(g)) K[[x]]$ is generated by $\pi(x_i \partial_i g)$ for $i \in \Delta$ and by $x_1 \pi(\partial_j g)$ for $j \notin \Delta$.

First we show that $J$ contains these generators. If $i \in \Delta$, $i \ne 1$ and $t_i = 0$, the generator $\pi(x_i \partial_i g)$ is in item 2. If $i \in \Delta$, $i \ne 1$ and $t_i \ne 0$, then $i \notin \Delta'$ and $\pi(x_i \partial_i g) = (x_i + t_i) \, x_1 \pi(\partial_i g)$ is a multiple of an element of item 3. If $j \notin \Delta$ and $j \ne 1$, then $j \notin \Delta'$, and $x_1 \pi(\partial_j g)$ is in item 3. The last generator has the index $1$. It is $\pi(x_1 \partial_1 g) = x_1 \pi(\partial_1 g)$ in both cases $1 \in \Delta$ and $1 \notin \Delta$. It is the element of item 1 minus the elements $\pi(x_i \partial_i g) = x_1 (x_i + t_i) \pi(\partial_i g)$ for $i \ne 1$, and we have just shown that these are in $J$.

Next we show that $\pi(\Theta_0(g)) K[[x]]$ contains the generators of $J$. Item 1 is the image of $\sum_i x_i \partial_i g$, and each term of this sum is in $\Theta_0(g)$. Item 2 is the image of a generator. In item 3, if $j \notin \Delta$, the element is a generator. If $j \in \Delta$ and $t_j \ne 0$, then $x_1 \pi(\partial_j g) = (x_j + t_j)^{-1} \pi(x_j \partial_j g)$, and $x_j + t_j$ is a unit in $K[[x]]$. $\square$

At a translated point, the derivation $\partial_j$ of the chart comes from $x_1 \partial_j$, which is not logarithmic along $x_j = 0$. The proof absorbs this with the unit $x_j + t_j$. So the formula holds at each point. A translation changes only the divisor, from $\Delta$ to $\Delta'$, and this change is the source of the kangaroo points below.

The Lean library proves Theorem 3.2.3 in $K[x]$, over each commutative ring $K$ of characteristic $p$. In $K[x]$ the element $x_j + t_j$ is not a unit, and $x_1^{-p}$ is not an element. So the Lean statement uses $g'$ with $\pi(g) = x_1^p g'$, and the product $u = \prod (x_i + t_i)$ over the $i \in \Delta$ with $i \ne 1$ and $t_i \ne 0$. It proves two inclusions:
$$\pi\bigl(\Theta_0(g)\bigr) \subseteq x_1^p \, \Theta'(g'), \qquad x_1^p u \, \Theta'(g') \subseteq \pi\bigl(\Theta_0(g)\bigr).$$
In $K[[x]]$ the element $u$ is a unit, and the two inclusions give the theorem. The second inclusion is the second half of the proof above, with the factor $u$ in the place of the inverse of $x_j + t_j$. In Lean, $u$ is `Singularity.blowupUnit`.

*Lean.* The file `Singularity/Invariant/LogJacobianBlowupLocal.lean` proves the theorem as an equality of ideals. Let $\mathcal{O}$ be the local ring of $K[x]$ at the origin of the chart, the localization at the maximal ideal `Singularity.originIdeal` (`Singularity.OriginLocalRing`). A polynomial with a nonzero constant term is a unit in $\mathcal{O}$ (`Singularity.isUnit_algebraMap_of_constantCoeff_ne_zero`), so $u$ is a unit there (`Singularity.isUnit_algebraMap_blowupUnit`). The two inclusions then give $x_1^p \, \Theta'(g') \, \mathcal{O} = \pi(\Theta_0(g)) \, \mathcal{O}$ (`Singularity.map_logJacobian_eq_map_pointChart`), for each translation $t$ with entries in $K$. This equality gives the equality of the theorem in $K[[x]]$. Lean proves the equality in the algebraic local ring, and not in the power series ring.

*Lean.* The file `Singularity/Invariant/LogJacobianBlowupPoint.lean` proves the theorem at a point of the chart that is not its origin. Let $\pi_0$ be the chart of $x_j$ with no translation, and let $t$ with $t_j = 0$ be a $K$-rational point of its exceptional divisor. Let $\pi_0(g) = x_j^p g'$, and let $\mathcal{O}_t$ be the local ring of $K[x]$ at $t$ (`Singularity.ChartPointLocalRing`). Then $x_j^p \, \Theta'(g') \, \mathcal{O}_t = \pi_0(\Theta_0(g)) \, \mathcal{O}_t$, with $\Theta'$ the log-Jacobian ideal for the divisor $\Delta'$ of the point $t$ (`Singularity.map_logJacobian_eq_map_pointChart_zero`). The proof uses the translation $x_i \mapsto x_i + t_i$, which commutes with the partial derivatives (`Singularity.pderiv_translateBy`), and the relation of the chart at $t$ to the chart $\pi_0$ (`Singularity.pointChart_eq_translateBy_comp`). The points of the chart that are not $K$-rational are not covered.

### The residual order does not go up

**Theorem 3.2.4.** Let $g \ne 0$ be clean with $\operatorname{ord} g \ge p$, and let $\rho = \rho(g)$. Let $g'_c$ be the clean part of $g'$ for the divisor $\Delta'$. Then $g'_c \ne 0$ and $\rho(g'_c) \le \rho$.

Node: SG0000003N · Lean: `Singularity.logJacobianOrder_cleanPart_le` (for polynomials, over a field $K$ of characteristic $p$)

*Proof.* By part 2 of Theorem 3.1.4, $\Theta(g) \ne 0$. Since $\mathfrak{m} \Theta(g) \subseteq \Theta_0(g)$, also $\Theta_0(g) \ne 0$. The map $\pi$ is injective, so $\Theta'(g') \ne 0$ by Theorem 3.2.3. If $g'_c = 0$, then $g'$ is a $p$-th power, and $\Theta'(g') = \Theta'(0) = 0$ by Lemma 3.1.2. So $g'_c \ne 0$.

Write $g = x^r G$ and $d = \operatorname{ord} G$ as in Theorem 3.1.4. Since $r_j = 0$ for $j \notin \Delta$, we have $\Theta_0(g) = x^r \widetilde\Theta_0(G)$, with
$$\widetilde\Theta_0(G) = \bigl( r_i G + x_i \partial_i G \ (i \in \Delta) \bigr) + \mathfrak{m} \bigl( \partial_j G \ (j \notin \Delta) \bigr).$$
From $\mathfrak{m} \widetilde\Theta(G) \subseteq \widetilde\Theta_0(G) \subseteq \widetilde\Theta(G)$, the order $\sigma = \operatorname{ord} \widetilde\Theta_0(G)$ is $\rho$ or $\rho + 1$. Each generator of $\widetilde\Theta_0(G)$ has order at least $d$, so $\sigma \ge d$.

Each element $h$ of $\widetilde\Theta_0(G)$ has order at least $\sigma$, so $\pi(h)$ is divisible by $x_1^\sigma$. Let $W$ be the ideal of $K[[x]]$ generated by the elements $x_1^{-\sigma} \pi(h)$ with $h \in \widetilde\Theta_0(G)$. We need two facts. First, if $\operatorname{ord} h = s$, then $x_1^{-s} \pi(h)$ has order at most $s$ (Theorem 2.2.2). Second, the restriction of $x_1^{-s} \pi(h)$ to $x_1 = 0$ is $\operatorname{in}(h)(1, x_2 + t_2, \dots, x_n + t_n)$ by Proposition 2.1.1, and this polynomial is not $0$. So $W$ is not contained in $(x_1)$.

We show that $\operatorname{ord} W \le \rho$. If $\sigma = \rho$, take $h \in \widetilde\Theta_0(G)$ of order $\rho$. Then $x_1^{-\rho} \pi(h) \in W$ has order at most $\rho$. If $\sigma = \rho + 1$, take $h \in \widetilde\Theta(G)$ of order $\rho$. Then $x_1 h \in \widetilde\Theta_0(G)$, so $x_1^{-\rho - 1} \pi(x_1 h) = x_1^{-\rho} \pi(h)$ is in $W$, and it has order at most $\rho$.

Now we apply Theorem 3.2.3. Since $\pi(x^r) = x_1^{|r|} \prod_{i \ne 1} (x_i + t_i)^{r_i}$,
$$\Theta'(g') = x_1^{-p} \pi(x^r) \, x_1^\sigma \, W = u \cdot x_1^{|r| + \sigma - p} \prod_{i \in \Delta' \setminus \{1\}} x_i^{r_i} \cdot W,$$
where $u$ is the product of the units $(x_i + t_i)^{r_i}$ with $t_i \ne 0$. The exponent $|r| + \sigma - p$ is at least $|r| + d - p = \operatorname{ord} g - p \ge 0$. So $\Theta'(g') = x^b W$ for a monomial $x^b$ in the variables of $\Delta'$. Let $x^s$ be the largest monomial in the variables of $\Delta'$ that divides each element of $W$, and write $W = x^s W_0$. Then $s_1 = 0$, because $W$ is not contained in $(x_1)$.

By Lemma 3.1.2, $\Theta'(g'_c) = \Theta'(g') = x^{b + s} W_0$, and no variable of $\Delta'$ divides all of $W_0$. Theorem 3.1.4, part 1, applied to the clean $g'_c$, writes the same ideal as $x^{r'} \widetilde\Theta(G')$, where no variable of $\Delta'$ divides all of $\widetilde\Theta(G')$. Since $K[[x]]$ is a unique factorization domain, $r' = b + s$ and $\widetilde\Theta(G') = W_0$. So
$$\rho(g'_c) = \operatorname{ord} W_0 = \operatorname{ord} W - |s| \le \operatorname{ord} W \le \rho. \qquad \square$$

The proof does not use the number of variables, and it does not need the point to be non-translated. It uses only part 1 of the dichotomy, where cleanness is necessary, and the order of the weak transform. For a base of dimension $3$ the theorem is in the thesis of Cossart [Cos87]. The theorem says that $\rho$ is a candidate for the second entry of a resolution invariant. It does not say that $\rho$ goes down. The hard question is what to do at the points where $\rho$ stays the same.

The Lean proof of Theorem 3.2.4 is for $g \in K[x]$ and uses the two inclusions of Theorem 3.2.3 in $K[x]$. It does not use the ideal $W$ or unique factorization in $K[[x]]$. Let $\tau = \operatorname{ord} \Theta_0(g)$, so that $\tau = |r| + \sigma$ in the notation of the proof above. Each element of $\Theta_0(g)$ is divisible by $x^r$ and has order at least $\tau$, so each element of $\pi(\Theta_0(g))$ is divisible by $x_1^\tau x^e$, where $x^e = \prod_{i \in \Delta' \setminus \{1\}} x_i^{r_i}$. The inclusion $x_1^p u \, \Theta'(g') \subseteq \pi(\Theta_0(g))$ then gives that $x^b$ divides each element of $\Theta'(g')$, with $x^b = x_1^{\tau - p} x^e$. Here the factor $u$ cancels because its constant term is not $0$, and a monomial that divides $u \varphi$ divides $\varphi$. The other inclusion gives an element $\varphi$ of $\Theta'(g')$ with $\operatorname{ord} \varphi \le |b| + \rho$, from an element of $\Theta_0(g)$ as in the proof above. Theorem 3.1.4, part 1, applied to $g'_c$ gives $x^{r'}$ with $\Theta'(g'_c) = x^{r'} \widetilde\Theta(G')$. By Lemma 3.1.2, $\Theta'(g'_c) = \Theta'(g')$, so $x^b$ divides each element of $\Theta'(g'_c)$, and part 1 also gives $b \le r'$. So
$$|r'| + \rho(g'_c) = \operatorname{ord} \Theta'(g'_c) \le \operatorname{ord} \varphi \le |b| + \rho \le |r'| + \rho.$$
This gives $\rho(g'_c) \le \rho$.

**Corollary 3.2.5.** In the situation of Theorem 3.2.4, let $d$ be the residual order of $g$ and let $d'$ be the residual order of $g'_c$. Then
$$d' \le \rho(g'_c) + 1 \le \rho + 1 \le d + 1.$$

*Proof.* The first and the last inequalities are part 2 of Theorem 3.1.4, at the new origin and at the old origin. The middle inequality is Theorem 3.2.4. $\square$

Lean: `Singularity.residualOrderAlong_pointChart_le_logJacobianOrder` (the three inequalities) and `Singularity.residualOrderAlong_pointChart_le_add_one` (the inequality $d' \le d + 1$)

For $q = p$, Moh's bound of Theorem 1.4.1 says that the residual order goes up by at most $p^{e-1} = 1$. The corollary gives this bound at each point of the exceptional divisor, and in each chart. It also shows when the residual order goes up. This happens only if $d' = d + 1$, so each inequality is an equality: $\rho$ stays the same, $g$ has type $0$ and $g'_c$ has type $1$.

*Lean.* The file `Singularity/Invariant/LogJacobianTypeSwitch.lean` proves this last claim for polynomials. Let $\pi(g) = x_1^p g'$. If $d' = d + 1$, then $\rho(g'_c) = \rho = d$, the type of $g$ is $0$ and the type of $g'_c$ is $1$ (`Singularity.kangaroo_type_switch`, `Singularity.logJacobianType_cleanPart_eq_one_of_residualOrderAlong_eq_add_one`). The proof uses the three inequalities of `Singularity.residualOrderAlong_pointChart_le_logJacobianOrder`. Here $d'$ is the residual order in the coordinates of the chart only, and the claim is only for the $K$-rational points of the exceptional divisor.

### A kangaroo point

**Example 3.2.6.** Let $p = 2$, $n = 2$, $\Delta = \{1, 2\}$ and $g = x^3 y + x y^3 = x y (x + y)^2$. This $g$ is clean. Here $r = (1, 1)$, $G = x^2 + y^2$ and $d = 2$. Since $\partial_x (x^2) = 0$ in characteristic $2$, both generators $G + x \partial_x G$ and $G + y \partial_y G$ are equal to $G$. So $\widetilde\Theta(G) = (x^2 + y^2)$, $\rho = 2$, and the type is $0$.

Take the chart $\pi(x) = x$, $\pi(y) = x(y + 1)$, so $t = 1$ and the point is translated in the direction of $y$. Then $\Delta' = \{1\}$. In characteristic $2$, $(y + 1) + (y + 1)^3 = (y + 1)(1 + (y + 1)^2) = (y + 1) y^2$, so
$$\pi(g) = x^4 \bigl( (y + 1) + (y + 1)^3 \bigr) = x^4 y^2 (y + 1), \qquad g' = x^2 y^3 + x^2 y^2.$$
The monomial $x^2 y^2$ is a square, and the clean part is $g'_c = x^2 y^3$. For $\Delta' = \{1\}$, we get $r' = (2, 0)$, $G' = y^3$ and $d' = 3$. The generators of $\widetilde\Theta(G')$ are $2 G' + x \partial_x G' = 0$ and $\partial_y G' = 3 y^2 = y^2$, so $\rho(g'_c) = 2$.

So the residual order goes up from $2$ to $3$, and $\rho$ stays equal to $2$. Each inequality of Corollary 3.2.5 is an equality. We can also check Theorem 3.2.3 directly. Here $x \partial_x g = 3 x^3 y + x y^3 = g$ and $y \partial_y g = g$, so $\Theta_0(g) = (g)$, and $x^{-2} \pi(g) = x^2 y^2 (y + 1)$ generates the same ideal as $x^2 y^2$, because $y + 1$ is a unit. On the other side, $x \partial_x g' = 2 g' = 0$ and $\partial_y g' = x^2 (3 y^2 + 2 y) = x^2 y^2$, so $\Theta'(g') = (x^2 y^2)$.

The component $y = 0$ of $E$ does not pass through the new point, so the factor $y$ of $g$ is lost from the monomial part. The double factor $(x + y)^2$ of $G$ becomes $x^2 y^2$ in the chart, because $x + x(y + 1) = x y$ in characteristic $2$. The factor $y$ of $g$ becomes $x(y + 1)$. The product $x^2 y^2 (y + 1)$ is $x^2 y^3 + x^2 y^2$, and the cleaning removes the square $x^2 y^2$. A point where the residual order goes up in this way is a *kangaroo point* [Hau10]. The example shows that kangaroo points are compatible with a constant $\rho$. The direction $t = 1$ of the jump is the line $x = y$, where the initial form of $G$ has its double factor.

### Notes and references

Theorem 3.2.3 and Theorem 3.2.4 for a base of dimension $3$ are in the thesis of Cossart [Cos87]. The proofs above are written for each number of variables. Posva gives the transformation law in a modern language [Pos24, Appendix A]. Moh's bound is from [Moh87]. For the kangaroo points and the cycle of Hauser and Perlega, see [Hau10] and [HP19a]. For centers of positive dimension, $\rho$ does not go up under conditions on the center, and an example shows that it can go up without them (Section 3.4). Whether a sequence of blowups makes $\rho$ go down, for a base of dimension $4$, is open.
