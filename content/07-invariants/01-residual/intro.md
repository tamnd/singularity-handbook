# The order and the residual order

Let $K$ be a field and let $f$ be a power series in $K[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$. The order of $f$ is the smallest degree of a monomial of $f$, and it is the multiplicity of the hypersurface $f = 0$ at the origin. It is the first component of every resolution invariant. For a purely inseparable equation $z^{p^e} + F(x)$ in characteristic $p$, the order does not see the shape of $F$, and the second component is the residual order: the order of $F$ after we remove the monomials that are $p^e$-th powers and the factors that come from the exceptional divisor.

Section 1.1 defines the order and proves its laws. Section 1.2 defines the Newton polyhedron and the initial forms. Section 1.3 defines purely inseparable equations, cleaning and the residual order, and it describes the point blowups. Section 1.4 gives Moh's bound, an explicit increase of the residual order, and the cycle of Hauser and Perlega.

Throughout the chapter, $K$ is a field, $x = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$, $R = K[[x]]$ is the ring of formal power series and $\mathfrak{m} = (x_1, \dots, x_n)$ is its maximal ideal. For $\alpha \in \mathbb{N}^n$ we write $x^\alpha = x_1^{\alpha_1} \cdots x_n^{\alpha_n}$ and $|\alpha| = \alpha_1 + \cdots + \alpha_n$. We write a power series as $f = \sum_\alpha c_\alpha x^\alpha$, and its *support* is the set $\operatorname{supp} f$ of the $\alpha$ with $c_\alpha \ne 0$. Most of the results hold with no change for the polynomial ring $K[x]$ and the point $0$.
