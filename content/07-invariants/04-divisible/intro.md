# Multiplicities divisible by p

Chapters 1 to 3 study the purely inseparable equation $z^q + F$. Its multiplicity is the power $q = p^e$. A general point of a hypersurface in characteristic $p$ can have a multiplicity $m = p^e m_0$ with $e \ge 1$ and $p \nmid m_0$, where both factors are larger than $1$. This chapter shows what goes wrong in this mixed case, and what survives.

In characteristic $0$, the first step at a point of multiplicity $m$ is the Tschirnhausen transformation. Write the equation in the Weierstrass form $f = z^m + a_1 z^{m-1} + \dots + a_m$. The translation $z \mapsto z - a_1/m$ removes the coefficient $a_1$, and then $z = 0$ is a hypersurface of maximal contact. In characteristic $p$ with $p \mid m$, the number $m$ is $0$ in the ring, and this step is not available. Section 4.1 proves a stronger fact. No translation of $z$ changes $a_1$, and the coefficients $a_1, \dots, a_{p^e - 1}$ form a rigid layer. The first coefficient that a translation can change freely is $a_{p^e}$, and it changes by $m_0 c^{p^e}$. Section 4.2 gives a test for the blowups of the base of $z^p + g$: such a blowup comes from a permissible blowup of the hypersurface if and only if a simple membership holds. Section 4.3 shows that, for a regular center, the membership holds if and only if the hypersurface has multiplicity $p$ at the generic point of the center. It also shows that the algorithm of Cossart blows up centers that fail the test, for each $p$, and that such a center can pass through a point of multiplicity $p$.

Throughout the chapter, $R$ is a commutative ring of characteristic $p > 0$. In the applications, $R$ is $K[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$ or a regular local ring. We write $f(z + c)$ for the polynomial that we get from $f \in R[z]$ when we replace $z$ by $z + c$, with $c \in R$.
