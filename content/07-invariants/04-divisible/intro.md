# Multiplicities divisible by p

Chapters 1 to 3 study the purely inseparable equation $z^q + F$. Its multiplicity is the power $q = p^e$. A general point of a hypersurface in characteristic $p$ can have a multiplicity $m = p^e m_0$ with $e \ge 1$ and $p \nmid m_0$, where both factors are larger than $1$. This chapter shows what goes wrong in this mixed case, and what survives.

In characteristic $0$, the first step at a point of multiplicity $m$ is the Tschirnhausen transformation. Write the equation in the Weierstrass form $f = z^m + a_1 z^{m-1} + \dots + a_m$. The translation $z \mapsto z - a_1/m$ removes the coefficient $a_1$, and then $z = 0$ is a hypersurface of maximal contact. In characteristic $p$ with $p \mid m$, the number $m$ is $0$ in the ring, and this step is not available. Section 4.1 proves a stronger fact. No translation of $z$ changes $a_1$, and the coefficients $a_1, \dots, a_{p^e - 1}$ form a rigid layer. The first coefficient that a translation can change freely is $a_{p^e}$, and it changes by $m_0 c^{p^e}$. Section 4.2 gives a test for the blowups of the base of $z^p + g$: such a blowup comes from a permissible blowup of the hypersurface if and only if a simple membership holds. Section 4.3 shows that, for a regular center, the membership holds if and only if the hypersurface has multiplicity $p$ at the generic point of the center. It also shows that the algorithm of Cossart blows up centers that fail the test, for each $p$, and that such a center can pass through a point of multiplicity $p$. Section 4.4 studies the first mixed multiplicity $m = 2p$ with the residual slope $\omega$, the slope of the characteristic polyhedron minus the slopes of the boundary. It shows that $\omega$ can go up under the natural rule and that a flag does not repair this, it proves that the rule terminates when $\omega = 0$, and it states what the computations show and what is open when $\omega > 0$. Section 4.5 studies the toric branches of the rule, where the boundary is full and each new point is the origin of its chart. It proves that $\omega$ never goes up along them and that each run with $\omega$ constant is finite, so a lexicographic pair $(\omega, \ell)$ drops at each step, and it shows that no such pair works for all steps of the rule. It extends the result to each boundary and to the generic steps at points that are not chart origins, and it proves that for germs with $a_p = 0$ or $a_{2p} = 0$ the invariant $\omega$ never goes up and each branch on which no monomials cancel is finite.

Throughout the chapter, $R$ is a commutative ring of characteristic $p > 0$. In the applications, $R$ is $K[[x_1, \dots, x_n]]$ or a regular local ring. We write $f(z + c)$ for the polynomial that we get from $f \in R[z]$ when we replace $z$ by $z + c$, with $c \in R$.
