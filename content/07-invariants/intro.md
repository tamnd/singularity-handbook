# Introduction

In characteristic $0$, resolution is proved by induction on the dimension. At a point of maximal order of a hypersurface, a hypersurface of maximal contact exists, the problem descends to an ideal on it, and a string of orders in descending dimensions is a resolution invariant that drops under each blowup. In characteristic $p > 0$ this method fails at several places. Hypersurfaces of maximal contact need not exist, and the second component of the invariant, the residual order, can increase under a blowup.

This volume studies the invariants that the proposed proofs in positive characteristic use. Chapter 1 defines the order of a power series, the Newton polyhedron and the residual order of a purely inseparable equation $z^{p^e} + F(x_1, \dots, x_n)$. It gives Moh's bound for the increase of the residual order under one blowup, a short computation that shows the increase, and the cycle of Hauser and Perlega, which disproves Moh's claim that the residual order is stable. Chapter 2 proves that the order does not go up under a point blowup, and it shows that the points where the order stays the same lie in the projective space of the directrix of the initial form. Chapter 3 defines the log-Jacobian ideal of $z^p + F$, an ideal that does not change when a $p$-th power is added to $F$. Its residual order is the residual order of $F$ or one less. The chapter also gives the exact Newton criterion for $z^p + g$: the equation is Newton nondegenerate if and only if no initial form of $g$ has a critical point in the torus. Chapter 4 studies the equation $z^m + a_1 z^{m-1} + \cdots + a_m$ when $p$ divides $m$. A translation of $z$ does not change the first $p^e - 1$ coefficients, where $p^e$ is the largest power of $p$ that divides $m$, and a simple test decides when the hypersurface $z^q + g$, with $q = p^e$, has order $q$ along a smooth center. For $q = p$ the test is equivalent to multiplicity $p$ at the generic point of the center, and some centers of the algorithm of Cossart fail it.

The reader we have in mind knows Volume 1, in particular the order of an element (Section 3.1) and the Frobenius (Chapter 6). Each result that has a Lean proof gives the name of its declaration in tamnd/singularity-lean.
